Beijing Weather by Month

Beijing sits on the North China Plain at roughly the same latitude as Philadelphia or Madrid, but its continental monsoon climate produces far sharper seasonal swings than either. Winters are dry, windy, and genuinely cold; summers are hot and humid with the bulk of the year's rain; and the transitional months of spring and autumn are brief but often spectacular. Understanding this rhythm matters more in Beijing than in most destinations, because the gap between a great trip and a miserable one can come down to a few weeks — or even a few days of spring dust storms or summer downpours. The guide below breaks down typical conditions month by month, then distills them into practical advice for planning your visit.

Figures are long-term monthly averages and typical values — individual years can vary considerably, especially for rainfall. July and August together account for roughly half of Beijing's annual precipitation of about 570 mm.

Seasonal Overview

  • Winter (December–February): Sub-freezing nights are the norm, daytime highs hover near or just above freezing, and precipitation is minimal — snow is occasional rather than routine. Skies are often brilliantly clear, though the heating season (mid-November to mid-March) can bring haze. Indoor heating is excellent, so dress in layers you can shed.
  • Spring (March–May): Temperatures climb rapidly, but March and early April bring strong winds and the possibility of dust storms blowing in from the Gobi. By late April and May the city is green and pleasant, with warm days and cool evenings.
  • Summer (June–August): Hot, humid, and rainy, with July the peak of both heat and precipitation. Downpours are usually short and intense rather than all-day affairs, but the humidity can make sightseeing exhausting. This is also peak domestic tourist season.
  • Autumn (September–November): The most reliable stretch of weather: clear, dry, and comfortably warm in September and October before a rapid cooldown in November. Expect cool mornings and evenings even when afternoons are warm.

Best Months to Visit

September and October are the standout months — clear skies, low humidity, comfortable temperatures, and the famous autumn foliage around the city's parks and the Great Wall. April and May run a close second, with mild weather and spring blossoms, though March–April wind and dust are a caveat. June and November are shoulder-season compromises: June is warm but increasingly wet, while November is dry and bright but cold. July and August suit travelers who don't mind heat and humidity (and who want long daylight hours), while December through February rewards those who tolerate cold with uncrowded sights, low prices, and clear winter light — plus, if timing aligns, the Spring Festival atmosphere.

Practical Tips

  • Layer for spring and autumn: Day–night temperature swings of 10–15 °C are common in April–May and September–October. A light jacket or sweater for mornings and evenings is essential even on warm afternoons.
  • Pack for winter properly: A warm down coat, hat, gloves, and scarf are not overkill for December–February, when wind chill can push apparent temperatures well below the air temperature. Sturdy, non-slip shoes help on icy patches.
  • Carry rain gear in summer: July and August downpours arrive fast. A compact umbrella or light rain jacket beats waiting out a storm, and breathable, quick-drying clothing handles the humidity better than heavy fabrics.
  • Watch air quality: Winter heating season and still autumn days can bring haze. Check daily AQI forecasts and adjust outdoor plans — save museum and temple-interior days for poor-air days and hiking or Great Wall trips for clear ones.
  • Time your Great Wall visit: Clear autumn days offer the best views and photographs; summer haze and winter wind are less forgiving. Start early regardless of season to beat crowds and heat.
  • Book around holidays: Spring Festival (late January or February, date varies), the May Day holiday, and the October National Day week bring huge domestic travel volumes — book transport and hotels well ahead, or avoid these windows if you prefer calm.
