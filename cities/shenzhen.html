Local Character

Shenzhen's character is defined by migration: a large portion of its population comes from all over China, so the city's food, dialects, and neighborhoods are a patchwork of regional traditions rather than one inherited local culture. A visitor should care because this means you can eat Sichuan, Hakka, or northeastern Chinese cooking within a few blocks, and the city's social life is built on newcomers' networks rather than old clan ties. The city's origin as a small rural settlement turned special economic zone in 1979 gives it a remarkably young built environment — the second-largest number of skyscrapers in the world — so sightseeing here is about witnessing rapid urban transformation rather than ancient monuments. Shenzhen railway station was the last stop on the mainland Chinese section of the Kowloon–Canton Railway, a detail worth knowing because it explains the city's historic role as the gateway between Hong Kong and the mainland. Finally, its entrepreneurial, competitive culture has produced homegrown giants like Huawei, Tencent, and DJI, and visitors interested in business or design will find that corporate campuses and the China Hi-Tech Fair reveal more about daily life here than traditional attractions.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Shenzhen Stock Exchange — One of the world's largest stock exchanges by market capitalization, housed in a striking modern tower in the central business district.
  2. Port of Shenzhen — The world's fourth-busiest container port, best appreciated from waterfront viewpoints in Yantian and Shekou.
  3. Shenzhen Railway Station — The historic last stop on the mainland Chinese section of the Kowloon–Canton Railway, still a major gateway to Hong Kong.
  4. China Hi-Tech Fair — An annual national and international technology exhibition that showcases the city's role as China's Silicon Valley.
  5. Shenzhen University — A major higher education institution and a pleasant campus stop reflecting the city's rapid investment in research and education.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Chinese Yuan (CNY, ¥)
  • Language: Mandarin Chinese (Cantonese widely spoken; English signage is common in central districts)
  • Time Zone: CST (UTC+8)
  • Visa: Most international visitors need a Chinese visa arranged in advance, though some nationalities benefit from visa-free transit or short-stay policies; check current rules before booking.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Shenzhen?
October and November offer the most comfortable weather, with mild temperatures and low rainfall, while summers are hot, humid, and rainy.
How do I get to Shenzhen from Hong Kong?
Shenzhen borders Hong Kong to the south and is easily reached by cross-border rail and road links, including services that arrive at Shenzhen railway station.
Why is Shenzhen called China's Silicon Valley?
It is a global technology hub and home to major firms such as Huawei, Tencent, DJI, and Oppo, and hosts events like the China Hi-Tech Fair.
