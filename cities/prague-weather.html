Prague Weather by Month

Prague has a humid continental climate, which means it experiences four distinct seasons with cold winters and warm, sometimes stormy summers. Average daytime highs range from around 2°C (36°F) in January to roughly 25°C (77°F) in July and August, while nights can drop well below freezing in winter and stay mild in summer. Rainfall is fairly evenly distributed across the year, with total annual precipitation of about 500–550 mm (20–22 inches), peaking slightly in summer. Spring and early autumn are widely considered the best times to visit, offering comfortable temperatures, longer daylight hours, and thinner crowds than the peak summer months.

Best Months to Visit

  • May and September — the sweet spot: mild temperatures (18–22°C / 64–72°F), manageable crowds, and long daylight hours.
  • April and October — quieter shoulder months with good value, though weather can be changeable.
  • December — best for atmosphere (Christmas markets), but expect cold and higher prices around the holidays.
  • July–August — warmest and liveliest, but the busiest and priciest; book accommodation and major sights in advance.
  • January–February — cheapest and quietest, suited to museum-goers and travellers who don't mind the cold.

Practical Tips

  • Pack in layers: Prague's weather can shift quickly, especially in spring and autumn. A light waterproof jacket and comfortable walking shoes are essentials year-round.
  • Winter gear: From December to February, bring a warm coat, hat, gloves, and insulated footwear—cobblestones get cold and occasionally icy.
  • Summer rain: June–August brings short but intense thunderstorms. Carry a compact umbrella and plan indoor backups (museums, galleries, cafés).
  • Daylight planning: Days are short in winter (sunset around 4–5 pm in December), so schedule outdoor sightseeing earlier. In June–July, daylight lasts until around 9 pm.
  • Book ahead in peak season: May through September and the Christmas/New Year period see the highest demand—reserve hotels and popular attractions like Prague Castle and the Astronomical Tower in advance.
  • Check the forecast: Local forecasts (e.g., CHMI, the Czech Hydrometeorological Institute) are reliable for short-term planning, particularly for summer storms and winter snow.
