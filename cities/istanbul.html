Best Time to Visit Istanbul

Istanbul is the only major city straddling two continents, where Byzantine domes, Ottoman palaces and bustling bazaars meet the Bosphorus. Visit in spring or autumn for mild weather and manageable crowds, though the city rewards travellers year-round.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Hagia Sophia — A monumental former Byzantine cathedral and Ottoman mosque, famed for its vast dome and mosaics.
  2. Topkapi Palace — The opulent seat of Ottoman sultans, housing courtyards, harem rooms and treasured relics.
  3. Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) — A 17th-century mosque celebrated for its six minarets and thousands of blue Iznik tiles.
  4. Grand Bazaar — One of the world's oldest covered markets, with thousands of shops selling carpets, jewellery and spices.
  5. Basilica Cistern — An atmospheric underground reservoir supported by hundreds of columns and the famous Medusa heads.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Turkish Lira (TRY, ₺)
  • Language: Turkish (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: TRT (UTC+3)
  • Visa: Many nationalities can obtain an e-visa online or enter visa-free for short stays, but requirements vary by country so check before travelling.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Istanbul?
Three to four days covers the main historic sights, while five days allows time for neighbourhoods like Beyoğlu and Kadıköy.
How do I get around Istanbul?
The Istanbulkart stored-value card works on trams, metro, ferries and buses, and the tram is the easiest way to reach Sultanahmet.
What should I wear when visiting mosques?
Modest dress is required, so cover shoulders and knees, and women should carry a scarf for their heads.
