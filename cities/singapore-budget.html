Singapore Travel Cost Guide

Singapore has a reputation as one of Asia's priciest destinations, but the reality is more nuanced: the city-state offers excellent value if you know where to spend and where to save. Public transport is cheap and world-class, hawker centres serve Michelin-worthy meals for a few dollars, and many of the city's best experiences — from the Botanic Gardens to the Southern Ridges trails — are completely free. This guide breaks down typical daily costs across three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan a trip that fits your wallet. All figures are in Singapore dollars (SGD), with approximate USD equivalents (1 SGD ≈ 0.75 USD) for reference.

Key Cost Details

  • Accommodation: Prices spike during F1 season (September), major conferences, and Chinese New Year. Book 2–3 months ahead for the best rates. Hostels in Kampong Glam and Little India are the cheapest; Clarke Quay and Marina Bay command premiums.
  • Food: Hawker centres are the great equalizer — a plate of chicken rice or laksa costs S$4–7 anywhere in the city. A casual restaurant meal runs S$15–25 per person; mid-range dining S$30–60; fine dining starts around S$100 per person before drinks. Alcohol is heavily taxed: a pint costs S$10–15, cocktails S$18–25. Note that hawker centres often charge a small deposit on returnable trays.
  • Transport: The MRT is fast, clean, and cheap — most journeys cost S$1–2.50. Use a contactless credit card or an EZ-Link/SimplyGo card; single-trip tickets carry a small surcharge. Grab (ride-hailing) is widely used; airport-to-city rides run S$20–30 by taxi/Grab versus under S$3 by MRT.
  • Attractions: Prices for major sights: Gardens by the Bay conservatories ≈ S$32, Singapore Zoo ≈ S$51, Marina Bay Sands SkyPark ≈ S$32, Universal Studios ≈ S$83. Free highlights include the Botanic Gardens (UNESCO site), Merlion Park, Kampong Glam, Chinatown, and the MacRitchie Reservoir treetop walk. Many museums offer free entry for citizens/PRs and discounted days for tourists.
  • Taxes & fees: Prices generally include GST (9%). Tourist refunds on purchases over S$100 are available at participating retailers. Tipping is not customary.

Practical Money-Saving Tips

  • Eat where locals eat: Hawker centres like Maxwell, Old Airport Road, and Tiong Bahru offer exceptional food at a fraction of restaurant prices.
  • Skip taxis from the airport: The MRT connects Changi to the city in about 30–40 minutes for under S$3.
  • Buy attraction bundles: Klook, Pelago, and the official Visit Singapore site often discount combo tickets (e.g., Zoo + Night Safari).
