Local Character

Guilin's name means "Cassia Grove," chosen in 214 BC when the Qin dynasty established the Guilin Commandery for its association with exotic southern products such as cassia spice — which, ironically, does not actually grow in the Guilin area. Visitors should care because the name preserves a 2,000-year-old imperial trade logic that predates the city itself. The meaning of gui (桂) shifted during the Song dynasty and now primarily refers to the sweet osmanthus (Osmanthus fragrans), which is widely cultivated locally and is the official city flower of Guilin; a visitor in autumn can smell the osmanthus in bloom and understand why the city is named for it. The city also served as a nexus between the central government and the southwest border, where regular armies were stationed, and canals were built through the city so food supplies could be transported directly from the Yangtze plain to the empire's farthest southwestern point — a reminder that Guilin's rivers were strategic infrastructure long before they were scenic attractions. Sun Yat-sen's Northern Expeditionary Army made Guilin one of its headquarters in 1921, and the city served as Guangxi's provincial capital before 1912 and again from 1936 to 1949, giving it a political weight that its small size belies.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Li River — The river along which Guilin sits, famous for karst-peak scenery that has drawn travelers for centuries.
  2. Guilin's Karst Landscape — The limestone karst topography that earned the city its reputation as China's finest scenery.
  3. Sweet Osmanthus Groves — Cultivated osmanthus trees, the city's official flower, best appreciated in bloom during autumn.
  4. Guilin's Historic Canal Route — Canals built through the city to carry food from the Yangtze plain to the southwestern frontier.
  5. Old Guilin City Center — The area of the modern city established as Lingui County in 634 and later renamed Guilin Prefecture in 1367.

FAQ

Why is Guilin called "Cassia Grove"?
The name dates to the Qin dynasty's Guilin Commandery in 214 BC, chosen for its association with exotic southern products like cassia spice, though the spice does not actually grow in the area.
What is Guilin's official city flower?
The sweet osmanthus (Osmanthus fragrans), which is widely cultivated in the city and is the modern referent of the character gui in Guilin's name.
When is the best time to visit Guilin?
October and November offer the most comfortable conditions with low rainfall, while spring months bring mild weather but higher rain.
