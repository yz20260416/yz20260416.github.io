Tokyo Travel Budget Guide: Backpacker, Mid-Range, and Luxury Costs

Tokyo is one of the world's most rewarding cities to visit, and it can be surprisingly affordable or extraordinarily luxurious depending on how you travel. This guide breaks down realistic daily and trip costs across three travel styles—backpacker, mid-range, and luxury—covering accommodation, food, transport, and activities. Figures are in Japanese yen (¥) with approximate US dollar equivalents (based on roughly ¥150 = US$1; exchange rates fluctuate, so check current rates before you go).

Accommodation Options and Typical Rates

  • Backpacker: Hostel dorms ¥2,500–4,000/night; capsule hotels ¥3,000–5,000/night. Book early in spring and autumn.
  • Mid-Range: Business hotels (Toyoko Inn, APA) ¥10,000–18,000/night; 3-star hotels ¥15,000–25,000/night.
  • Luxury: 5-star hotels (Aman, Park Hyatt, Peninsula) ¥60,000–150,000+/night; traditional ryokan with meals ¥50,000–120,000+/night per person.

Food Cost Guidance

  • Budget: Convenience store bento ¥400–700; gyudon bowls ¥500–800; ramen ¥800–1,200.
  • Mid-Range: Lunch sets ¥1,000–2,000; izakaya dinners ¥3,000–5,000 per person; sushi lunches ¥2,000–4,000.
  • Luxury: Omakase sushi ¥15,000–40,000+; kaiseki dinners ¥20,000–50,000+; Michelin-starred tasting menus ¥30,000+.

Transport Tips and Costs

  • Subway and JR single rides typically ¥170–330; a 24-hour subway pass costs around ¥800–1,600 depending on coverage.
  • The Suica/Pasmo IC card is convenient for all trains and buses; top up as you go.
  • Airport access: Narita Express ~¥3,000; Keisei Skyliner ~¥2,570; limousine bus ~¥1,300–3,200.
  • Taxis are expensive (base fare ~¥500); use them sparingly unless traveling luxury.

Practical Tips

  • Book accommodation 1–3 months ahead during cherry blossom (late March–April) and autumn foliage (November) seasons.
  • Carry cash—many small restaurants and shops are cash-only, though card acceptance is growing.
  • Eat lunch at restaurants offering "lunch sets" for significant savings versus dinner prices.
  • Free attractions abound: Meiji Shrine, Ueno Park, Imperial Palace gardens, and neighborhood walks.
  • Consider a JR Pass only if you plan day trips beyond Tokyo; for city-only travel, IC cards are cheaper.
  • Budget travelers can eat well at depachika (department store basement food halls) near closing time for discounts.
  • Prices shown are estimates; verify current rates and opening hours before booking.
