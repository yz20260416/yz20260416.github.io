Tokyo Travel Cost Guide

Tokyo is often perceived as one of the world's most expensive cities, but the reality is far more nuanced. With a little planning, Japan's capital can be experienced on a shoestring budget or with full luxury, and everything in between. This guide breaks down typical daily costs for accommodation, food, transport, and activities across three travel styles — Budget, Mid-Range, and Luxury — so you can plan a trip that matches your wallet and your travel priorities. All figures are in Japanese Yen (¥) with approximate USD equivalents (based on ¥150 = $1 USD), and represent per-person, per-day costs unless otherwise noted.

Detailed Category Breakdown

🏨 Accommodation

  • Budget: Hostel dorms (¥2,500–4,000/night), capsule hotels (¥3,000–4,500), or budget business hotels like Toyoko Inn (¥6,000–8,000, often bookable as a budget option for couples).
  • Mid-Range: Business hotels (APA, Dormy Inn, Mitsui Garden) at ¥9,000–18,000/night, or private Airbnb rooms. Dormy Inn often includes free breakfast and late-night ramen.
  • Luxury: International five-star hotels (Park Hyatt, Aman, Mandarin Oriental) at ¥35,000–80,000+/night. Ryokan experiences in Tokyo can also fall in this range.

🍜 Food

  • Budget: Convenience store meals (¥400–600), standing soba/udon shops (¥500–700), gyudon chains like Yoshinoya/Sukiya (¥600–800), and supermarket discount bento after 8 PM (30–50% off).
  • Mid-Range: Ramen shops (¥900–1,200), teishoku set meals (¥1,000–1,500), izakaya dinners (¥2,500–4,000 with drinks), and department store basement food halls (depachika) for gourmet lunches.
