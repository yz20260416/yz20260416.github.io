Best Time to Visit Lhasa

Lhasa, meaning "place of gods" in Tibetan, is the religious and administrative capital of Tibet and one of the highest cities in the world at 3,656 metres. Its mid-17th-century old town holds the Potala Palace, Jokhang Temple and Norbulingka Palaces, making it a pilgrimage destination best visited in the warmer, clearer months from May to October.

Local Character

Lhasa's name itself carries a story worth knowing: ancient Tibetan documents call the site Rasa, or "goat's place," because it began as a herding ground, and the name shifted to Lhasa, "place of gods," only once the Jokhang temple was completed to house a holy Buddha statue. A visitor walking the Barkhor circuit around the Jokhang is retracing that transformation, since the temple's Jowo shrine is what first drew the city's sacred identity. The Jokhang and nearby Ramoche temples were built in 641 by Songtsen Gampo to house two Buddha statues brought by his wives, Princess Bhrikuti of Nepal and Princess Wencheng of the Tang court; the Ramoche's Akshobhya Vajra depicts the Buddha at age eight, while the Jokhang's Jowo Sakyamuni shows him at twelve. Knowing which image sits where helps a visitor read the two temples as a pair rather than as interchangeable stops. The city also bears scars from the 9th-century reign of Langdarma, when sacred sites were destroyed and desecrated; that history explains why so much of what stands today was rebuilt or restored rather than untouched. Lhasa appears by name in a China-Tibet treaty of 822 C.E., the first recorded use of the name for the Jowo temple area, a small fact that gives the old town's streets a documentary depth.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Potala Palace — The landmark hilltop palace on Mount Marpori, first raised by Songtsen Gampo in 637 and the seat of Tibet's religious and administrative power since the mid-17th century.
  2. Jokhang Temple — Built in 641 to house the Jowo Sakyamuni statue of the Buddha at age twelve, it anchors the old town and its pilgrim circuit.
  3. Ramoche Temple — The companion temple to the Jokhang, also built in 641, housing the Akshobhya Vajra image of the Buddha at age eight.
  4. Norbulingka Palaces — The palace complex that, with the Potala and Jokhang, forms Lhasa's core of culturally significant Tibetan Buddhist sites.
  5. Barkhor and the old town — The historic district around the Jokhang, in the city once called Rasa, where the sacred-site geography of Lhasa is most concentrated.

FAQ

How high is Lhasa and should I worry about altitude?
Lhasa sits at 3,656 metres (11,990 ft), so plan a slow first day and allow time to acclimatise before long walks or climbs.
When is the best time to visit Lhasa?
May, June and September offer the best balance of mild temperatures, lower rainfall and manageable crowds, while July and August are wetter and busier.
What does the name Lhasa mean?
Lhasa translates to "place of gods" in Tibetan; the site was earlier called Rasa, or "goat's place," when it was a herding ground.
