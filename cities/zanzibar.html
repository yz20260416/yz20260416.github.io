Local Character

Zanzibar's name derives from the Persian 'zangbâr' (black coast), a reminder of the archipelago's long role in Indian Ocean trade. The historic center, Stone Town, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose dense lanes and coral-stone buildings reflect centuries of exchange between African, Arab, Indian, and European influences. A visitor should care because Stone Town is not a museum but a living neighborhood where this layered past is visible in its architecture and street life. The islands are also known as the 'Spice Islands' for their production of clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, coconut, and black pepper; a visit to a spice farm explains why these crops shaped the region's economy and global trade. Finally, the endemic Zanzibar red colobus is found in the land ecosystem, offering a specific wildlife encounter that a generic beach guide would miss.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Stone Town — The UNESCO World Heritage-listed historic center of Zanzibar City, known for its winding alleys and coral-stone architecture.
  2. Spice farms — Working farms where visitors can see and smell the clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, coconut, and black pepper that earned the archipelago the name 'Spice Islands'.
  3. Jozani Forest — A protected area on Unguja that is home to the endemic Zanzibar red colobus.
  4. Pemba Island — The second-largest island of the archipelago, quieter than Unguja and surrounded by rich marine ecosystems.
  5. Abeid Amani Karume International Airport — The main gateway to the islands, located near Zanzibar City and capable of serving up to 1.5 million passengers per year.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Zanzibar?
The dry seasons from June to October and December to February offer the most reliable weather, with June to September being particularly comfortable.
What is Stone Town known for?
Stone Town is the historic center of Zanzibar City and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, notable for its dense lanes and coral-stone buildings that reflect the island's long history of Indian Ocean trade.
Which spices is Zanzibar known for?
Zanzibar is known as the 'Spice Islands' for its production of clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, coconut, and black pepper.
