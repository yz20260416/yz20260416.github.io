Auckland Weather by Month

Auckland enjoys a temperate maritime climate, often described as "four seasons in one day" — warm, humid summers and mild, wet winters, with rainfall spread fairly evenly across the year. Located in New Zealand's North Island, the city sits between two harbours, and sea breezes plus the surrounding hills create microclimates: the isthmus can be sunny while the Waitākere Ranges to the west are drenched. Summer (December–February) brings long daylight hours and temperatures in the low-to-mid 20s°C, while winter (June–August) is cool but rarely frosty, with daytime highs around 15°C. Rain falls on roughly 130–140 days a year, so a rain jacket is useful in every season. The table below summarises average conditions to help you plan your trip.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: February and March — the warmest, driest, and most settled weather, with sea temperatures at their peak for swimming and beaches at their best.
  • Best for budget and fewer crowds: May and September–November — shoulder-season prices, milder temperatures, and quieter attractions, though pack for showers.
  • Best for outdoor festivals and long days: December–February — daylight until late evening, but expect peak-season rates and busier roads.
  • Least ideal: June–August — cool, wet, and dark by early evening; still workable for museums, cafés, and indoor attractions.

Practical Tips

  • Pack layers and a waterproof jacket year-round. Auckland's weather can shift from sunshine to showers within an hour — an umbrella alone rarely copes with wind-driven rain.
  • Use sunscreen even on cloudy days. New Zealand's UV index is intense, especially October–March; burn times can be under 15 minutes at midday.
  • Check the forecast daily, not weekly. Local forecasts are far more reliable at 1–3 days out; plan flexible outdoor days around them.
  • Book accommodation early for December–February and around major events (e.g., Auckland Anniversary Weekend in late January, America's Cup or concert dates).
  • Expect humidity in summer. Highs of 24°C can feel warmer; air-conditioning is not universal in older accommodation.
  • Winter travellers: bring a warm mid-layer and shoes for wet pavements; many cafés and attractions are cosy and less crowded.
  • Watch for sea breezes and microclimates. West-coast beaches (Piha, Muriwai) can be windier and wetter than the sheltered Hauraki Gulf side — plan beach days accordingly.
