Los Angeles Weather by Month

Los Angeles enjoys one of the most pleasant climates in the United States: a Mediterranean-style pattern of warm, dry summers and mild, occasionally wet winters. Temperatures are moderated by the Pacific Ocean, so extreme heat and cold are rare along the coast, though inland neighborhoods and the valleys run noticeably hotter in summer. Rainfall is highly seasonal—nearly all of it falls between November and March—and "June Gloom" morning marine layer is a local institution. The table below shows typical averages for downtown LA; coastal areas (Santa Monica, Venice) are a few degrees cooler in summer, while inland spots (Pasadena, the Valley) are hotter.

Figures are long-term averages for downtown Los Angeles and vary by neighborhood—expect roughly 5–10°F (3–6°C) warmer in the San Fernando Valley and cooler, breezier conditions at the beaches.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: April–May and September–October. Warm, mostly dry, comfortable temperatures, and lighter crowds than midsummer.
  • Best for beach weather: July–September. Ocean temperatures peak (mid-to-upper 60s°F) and the marine layer is least persistent.
  • Best for budget and crowds: November–early December and January–February (outside holiday peaks), accepting a higher chance of rain.
  • Busiest and priciest: late June–August and major holiday weekends—book lodging and attractions well ahead.

Practical Tips

  • Dress in layers. Even in summer, coastal mornings and evenings can be 15–20°F cooler than midday, and the marine layer can linger until noon.
  • Rain is concentrated, not constant. Winter storms are usually short-lived; a compact umbrella and waterproof layer are enough. Check forecasts before canyon or mountain hikes, where rain brings mudslide and flash-flood risk.
  • Respect the summer sun. UV index runs high April–September; use sunscreen, hats, and hydration, especially inland where highs can exceed 100°F (38°C) during heat waves.
  • Watch for Santa Ana winds (fall–early winter). These dry, gusty winds bring hot, clear days but also elevated wildfire risk—check air quality and fire alerts before outdoor plans.
  • Microclimates are real. A single day might mean foggy 65°F in Santa Monica, 85°F downtown, and 95°F in Pasadena—plan outfits and itineraries accordingly.
  • Book outdoor attractions (Griffith Observatory, studio tours, theme parks) for mornings in late spring and summer to beat both crowds and afternoon heat or haze.
