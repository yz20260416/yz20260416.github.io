Melbourne Weather by Month

Melbourne has a temperate oceanic climate famous for its "four seasons in one day" reputation—warm northerly winds can push temperatures into the 40s°C in summer, while cold fronts bring rapid drops and showers within hours. Overall, the city enjoys a mild climate year-round: summers are warm to hot and relatively dry, winters are cool and damp, and spring and autumn are transitional with changeable conditions. Rainfall is fairly evenly distributed across the year (averaging around 600–650 mm annually in the city), so there's no true "dry season"—but February–March and September–October typically offer the most comfortable balance of temperature and sunshine for sightseeing.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: March–April and October–November — mild temperatures, manageable crowds, and good conditions for both city exploring and regional day trips.
  • Best for warm weather & events: January–February — hottest and driest, ideal for beaches and major events, though book accommodation early and prepare for heatwaves.
  • Best for budget travelers: June–August — winter means lower hotel rates and fewer crowds, with plenty of indoor cultural attractions.
  • Avoid if you dislike rain: October is statistically the wettest month, though showers are rarely prolonged anywhere in the year.

Practical Tips

  • Layer up, always: Melbourne's weather can shift from sunny and 25°C to windy and 14°C within hours. Carry a light jacket, a compact umbrella, and sunglasses year-round.
  • Check the forecast daily (or hourly): Local apps like the Bureau of Meteorology (BOM) are more reliable than long-range predictions; plan outdoor activities flexibly.
  • Summer heatwaves: January and February can bring days above 40°C. Schedule outdoor sightseeing for mornings, stay hydrated, and use the free City Circle tram or air-conditioned galleries as respite.
  • Winter comfort: June–August days are cool but rarely freezing in the city (lows around 6–7°C). A warm coat, scarf, and waterproof shoes will serve you well; snow is confined to the nearby alpine regions.
  • UV exposure: Even on cloudy or cool days, UV levels in Melbourne can be high from September to April—sun protection is recommended by public health authorities year-round.
  • Rain strategy: Showers are often brief. Rather than cancelling plans, duck into a laneway café, arcade, or museum—Melbourne's covered arcades and gallery scene make rainy days easy.
  • Book around events: Major events (Australian Open in January, Grand Prix in March, AFL finals in September, Melbourne Cup in November) drive up accommodation prices—reserve well ahead or plan around them.
