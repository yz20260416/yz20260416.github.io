Bora Bora Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Bora Bora, a small island in French Polynesia's Society Islands, is famous for its turquoise lagoon, overwater bungalows, and the dramatic silhouette of Mount Otemanu. While the island is compact—about 30 km in circumference—its attractions range from mountaintop viewpoints and coral gardens to pearl farms and vanilla plantations. Most visitors arrive by air at Bora Bora Airport (BOB) on Motu Mute, then transfer by boat to their resort. Below are the top 10 attractions with practical details to help you plan your visit.

  1. Mount Otemanu Viewpoints

    Mount Otemanu, the island's iconic 727-meter volcanic peak, is best experienced from designated viewpoints rather than a summit hike (the summit is not accessible to tourists). Popular vantage points include the lookout near Faanui and the coastal road along the island's western side. The changing light at sunrise and sunset makes for spectacular photography.

    Ticket PriceFree (public viewpoints)
    Opening HoursOpen 24 hours; daylight visits recommended
    Visiting TipsBring water and sun protection; the best light is early morning or late afternoon. Combine with a drive around the island's perimeter road.

  2. Matira Beach

    Matira Beach is Bora Bora's most famous public beach, located at the southern tip of the main island. It features soft white sand, shallow calm water, and a gentle slope ideal for swimming and snorkeling. Public access is free, and there are nearby snack bars and small shops.

    Ticket PriceFree
    Opening HoursOpen 24 hours (public beach)
    Visiting TipsArrive early to secure shade; the beach can get busy midday. Snorkel near the rocky points at either end for better fish life.

  3. Lagoonarium (Le Lagoonarium)

    This family-run marine park on a motu (islet) offers snorkeling in a natural lagoon enclosure with sharks, rays, and tropical fish. It is a popular half-day excursion that includes boat transfer and guided feeding sessions.

    Ticket PriceApproximately 6,000–8,000 XPF (about US$55–75) including transfer; prices vary by operator
    Opening HoursTypically 9:00–16:00, Monday–Saturday; confirm with operator
    Visiting TipsBook in advance through your resort or a local tour operator. Bring reef-safe sunscreen and an underwater camera.

  4. Coral Gardens Snorkeling Sites

    The lagoon's Coral Gardens are shallow, protected snorkeling areas with vibrant coral formations and abundant fish. These sites are usually visited as part of lagoon tours, which may also include stops at shark and ray feeding spots.

    Ticket PriceIncluded in lagoon tours, typically 8,000–15,000 XPF (US$70–140) per person for a half or full day
    Opening HoursTour-dependent; most departures between 8:00 and 9:00
    Visiting TipsChoose a small-group tour to reduce crowding. Wear water shoes and use only reef-safe sunscreen.

  5. Bloody Mary's Restaurant

    A legendary waterfront restaurant and bar on the main island, Bloody Mary's is known for its fresh seafood, sand floor, and celebrity guest list. It is a popular lunch and dinner spot and a cultural landmark in its own right.

    Ticket PriceNo entry fee; meals typically 3,000–6,000 XPF (US$25–50) per person
    Opening HoursGenerally 11:00–15:00 and 18:00–21:00; reservations recommended
    Visiting TipsBook ahead for dinner, especially in high season (June–August). The fish of the day is displayed for selection.

  6. Bora Bora Pearl Farms

    French Polynesia is renowned for cultured black pearls, and several pearl farms on Bora Bora offer guided tours explaining the grafting process and pearl grading. Tours often include a demonstration and the opportunity to purchase jewelry.

    Ticket PriceFree tours at most farms; purchases optional
    Opening HoursTypically 9:00–17:00, Monday–Saturday; hours vary by farm
    Visiting TipsAsk about certification and grading before buying. Tours last 30–60 minutes and are a good rainy-day activity.

  7. Vanilla Plantation Tours

    Small vanilla plantations on the main island offer insight into one of French Polynesia's signature crops. Guided visits explain cultivation, pollination, and curing, and often include tastings of vanilla products.

    Ticket PriceUsually free or a small fee (under 1,000 XPF); purchases optional
    Opening HoursVaries by plantation; typically weekday mornings
    Visiting TipsCall ahead to confirm tour times. Vanilla products make good gifts but check customs rules for your home country.

  8. World War II Relics (Faanui and Surroundings)

    Bora Bora hosted a U.S. military base during World War II, and remnants such as coastal gun emplacements and a small museum display can be found around Faanui Bay. These sites offer a different perspective on the island's history.

    Ticket PriceFree for outdoor relics; small museum fees may apply
    Opening HoursOutdoor sites open 24 hours; museum hours vary
    Visiting TipsCombine with a circle-island drive. Respect any barriers or private property signs.

  9. Circle Island Road Tour

    A drive or guided tour around the 30 km perimeter road showcases coastal villages, churches, viewpoints, and small shops. It is an efficient way to see the main island's non-lagoon attractions in a few hours.

    Ticket PriceSelf-drive rental car approximately 5,000–8,000 XPF per day; guided tours 6,000–12,000 XPF per person
    Opening HoursRoad accessible 24 hours; tours typically depart mornings
    Visiting TipsDrive slowly and watch for scooters and pedestrians. Fuel up in Vaitape, the main town.

  10. Mount Pahia Hiking Trail

    For adventurous hikers, guided hikes toward Mount Pahia (661 m) offer panoramic views of the lagoon and Mount Otemanu. The trail is steep and often muddy, and a local guide is strongly recommended.

    Ticket PriceGuided hikes typically 8,000–12,000 XPF (US$70–110) per person
    Opening HoursDepartures usually early morning (6:00–7:00) to avoid heat
    Visiting TipsWear sturdy shoes and bring water, insect repellent, and a rain jacket. Not suitable for young children or those with mobility issues.

Practical Tips for Visiting Bora Bora

  • Currency: The local currency is the CFP franc (XPF). Credit cards are widely accepted at resorts and larger restaurants, but carry cash for small shops, markets, and roadside vendors.
  • Language: French and Tahitian are official; English is widely spoken in tourism settings.
  • Best time to visit: May–October is the drier, cooler high season. November–April is warmer and wetter, with lower prices and fewer crowds.
  • Transport: There are no public buses on the main island; options include rental cars, scooters, bicycles, and resort shuttles. Boat transfers connect the airport motu to the main island.
  • Reef safety: Use reef-safe sunscreen, avoid touching coral, and respect shark and ray feeding guidelines set by tour operators.
  • Budget: Bora Bora is an expensive destination; plan for resort dining costs and book tours in advance during peak season.
  • Respect local customs: Dress modestly in villages, ask permission before photographing people, and avoid removing coral or shells from the lagoon.
