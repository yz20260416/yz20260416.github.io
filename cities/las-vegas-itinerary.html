Las Vegas: The Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Las Vegas packs an astonishing range of experiences into a compact strip of desert: world-class dining, headline entertainment, over-the-top casino resorts, and easy access to dramatic natural landscapes just outside the city. This three-day itinerary balances the classic Vegas highlights with a break from the neon, structuring each day into morning, afternoon, and evening blocks. It assumes you are staying on or near the Las Vegas Strip and have access to a car or rideshare for the off-Strip excursions. Adjust start times to your energy levels—Vegas rewards both early risers and night owls.

Day 1: The Strip Essentials

Time Activity Notes
Morning Walk the central Strip from your hotel toward the Bellagio and Caesars Palace Start early (8–9 a.m.) to beat the heat and crowds. Explore the Bellagio Conservatory & Botanical Gardens (free) and the hotel's lobby Chihuly glass ceiling.
Afternoon Lunch, then explore the Venetian and the Grand Canal Shoppes The indoor canal, gondola rides, and street performers make this a good midday air-conditioned stop. Wander the Forum Shops at Caesars nearby.
Evening Fountain show at the Bellagio, dinner, and a show The Bellagio fountains run every 15–30 minutes from late afternoon. Book a production show (Cirque du Soleil, a residency, or a comedy club) in advance.

Day 1 dining picks:

  • Breakfast: Eggslut at The Cosmopolitan (counter service, famous for its egg sandwiches).
  • Lunch: Lotus of Siam (Thai) or a casual bite at the Grand Canal Shoppes food hall.
  • Dinner: Carbone at ARIA or Hell's Kitchen at Caesars Palace—reserve weeks ahead.

Day 2: Downtown, Arts District, and a Change of Pace

Time Activity Notes
Morning Old Las Vegas and the Neon Museum Visit the Neon Museum's Boneyard (guided tours recommended; book online) to see retired casino signs. Pair with a walk down Fremont Street Experience before it gets crowded.
Afternoon Arts District (18b) lunch and galleries Browse independent galleries, vintage shops, and breweries. This is a relaxed, walkable contrast to the Strip.
Evening Fremont Street at night and a downtown dinner See the Viva Vision light canopy shows and live music. Downtown casinos (Golden Nugget, Circa) offer a more classic Vegas atmosphere.

Day 2 dining picks:

  • Breakfast: Eat at your hotel or grab coffee at a local roaster in the Arts District.
  • Lunch: Esther's Kitchen (Italian, Arts District) or a brewery lunch on Main Street.
  • Dinner: Andiamo Steakhouse at The D or a downtown taco spot; finish with a cocktail at a craft bar on Fremont East.

Day 3: Nature Escape and a Grand Finale

Time Activity Notes
Morning Red Rock Canyon or Hoover Dam Red Rock Canyon (about 30 minutes west) offers a scenic 13-mile drive and hiking trails; arrive early for parking. Hoover Dam (about 45 minutes southeast) suits history buffs. Either makes a half-day trip.
Afternoon Return to the Strip: pool time or a spa Recover from the morning excursion. Many resorts sell day passes to their pools and spas.
Evening High-end dinner and a final show or observation deck Consider the Eiffel Tower Viewing Deck or the High Roller observation wheel at sunset, then a celebratory dinner.

Day 3 dining picks:

  • Breakfast: Pack snacks for the excursion or eat before leaving.
  • Lunch: A casual stop en route back, or a late lunch at a Strip restaurant.
  • Dinner: Joël Robuchon (high-end) or a more accessible option like Best Friend at Park MGM; reserve in advance.

Practical Tips

  • Book ahead: Popular restaurants, shows, and Neon Museum tours sell out. Reserve at least a few days—often weeks—in advance.
  • Beat the heat: Summer daytime temperatures regularly exceed 100°F (38°C). Do outdoor activities in the morning and plan indoor breaks midday. Carry water.
  • Walking distances are deceptive: The Strip looks compact but blocks are long. Wear comfortable shoes and use the free tram or rideshares between distant resorts.
  • Resort fees and taxes: Most hotels charge nightly resort fees not included in the room rate. Budget for them.
  • Transportation: Rideshares (Uber/Lyft) are convenient but have designated pickup zones at each resort. The Las Vegas Monorail and free trams connect many Strip properties.
  • Budget for extras: Drinks, tips, and casino spending add up quickly. Set a daily budget and use cash for gambling.
  • Dress code: Upscale restaurants and clubs enforce dress codes—check before you go. Comfortable walking shoes are essential everywhere else.
  • Hydration and sun: The desert climate is dry. Drink more water than you think you need and use sunscreen, even in winter.
  • Timing: Weekdays are cheaper and less crowded than weekends. Avoid major convention weeks and major holidays for better rates.
