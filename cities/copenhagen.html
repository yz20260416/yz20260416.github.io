Best Time to Visit Copenhagen

Copenhagen is a compact, cycle-friendly Scandinavian capital known for its harbour baths, design heritage, and world-class dining. Visit between May and September for long daylight hours and outdoor life, or in December for cosy Christmas markets and hygge.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Nyhavn — A 17th-century waterfront canal lined with brightly coloured townhouses, historic wooden ships, and lively cafés.
  2. Tivoli Gardens — One of the world's oldest amusement parks, offering vintage rides, gardens, and evening illuminations in the heart of the city.
  3. The Little Mermaid — A small bronze statue on the Langelinie promenade honouring Hans Christian Andersen's famous fairy tale.
  4. Christiansborg Palace — A working royal and parliamentary palace whose towers and reception rooms can be visited.
  5. Torvehallerne Market — A glass-roofed food market with dozens of stalls selling Danish smørrebrød, pastries, and fresh produce.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Danish Krone (DKK, kr)
  • Language: Danish (English is widely spoken)
  • Time Zone: CET (UTC+1), CEST (UTC+2) in summer
  • Visa: Citizens of the EU/EEA and many Western countries can enter visa-free for up to 90 days, while other nationalities should check Schengen visa requirements before travelling.

FAQ

Is Copenhagen easy to explore without a car?
Yes, the city is compact and flat, with an extensive cycling network, efficient metro, and walkable central districts.
Is Copenhagen expensive to visit?
It is one of Europe's pricier cities, but budget travellers can save with supermarket meals, free walking tours, and the Copenhagen Card for transport and attractions.
What is the best way to see the city like a local?
Rent a bicycle and explore neighbourhoods such as Nørrebro and Vesterbro, stopping for coffee and smørrebrød along the way.
