Best Time to Visit Shanghai

Shanghai is China's most populous city and a global hub for finance, trade, and culture, bisected by the Huangpu River on the southern estuary of the Yangtze. Visit in spring or autumn for mild temperatures and clear skies, when the Bund and the Lujiazui skyline are at their most photogenic.

Local Character

Shanghai's character was shaped by its 19th-century role as one of five treaty ports forced open after the First Opium War, which led to the establishment of the Shanghai International Settlement and the French Concession. Visitors can still trace this layered past in the architecture of those former concession areas, where Western-style buildings sit alongside local lanes. The city's distinctive shikumen stone-gate houses, a hybrid architectural style born in this era, are a specific local detail worth seeking out; they reveal how everyday residents blended Chinese courtyard living with Western row-house density, and they remain one of the most tangible links to old Shanghai. Equally specific is the City God Temple, a historic site that anchors the old quarter near Yu Garden and shows how local folk religion and market life have long coexisted in the city center. Shanghai's local language and cuisine are also central to its identity, and its cosmopolitan culture reflects decades of domestic and foreign trade. The city's re-emergence as a financial center, marked by the Shanghai Stock Exchange and the Pudong New Area's redevelopment from the 1990s, gives the skyline its dramatic contrast with the historic Bund across the river.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. The Bund — A historic waterfront promenade lined with early 20th-century buildings facing the Lujiazui skyline across the Huangpu River.
  2. Lujiazui Skyline — Pudong's cluster of landmark skyscrapers, the symbol of Shanghai's redevelopment since the 1990s.
  3. Yu Garden — A classical garden in the old quarter, adjacent to the City God Temple and its surrounding market streets.
  4. City God Temple — A historic temple anchoring the old city, reflecting local folk religion and traditional market life.
  5. Shanghai Museum — A major museum showcasing Chinese art and artifacts in the city center.

FAQ

How do I get around Shanghai?
The Shanghai Metro, opened in 1993, is the largest metro network in the world by route length and connects most major sights.
When is the best time to visit?
April, May, and October offer the most comfortable temperatures and clear skies, though crowds are high.
What local architecture should I look for?
Look for shikumen stone-gate houses and Art Deco buildings, especially in the former International Settlement and French Concession areas.
