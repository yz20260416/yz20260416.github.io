Best Time to Visit Shanghai

Shanghai is China's most cosmopolitan city, a dazzling mix of 1920s Bund architecture, futuristic Pudong skylines, and leafy former French Concession lanes. Visit in spring (March–May) or autumn (September–November) for mild temperatures and clear skies, though the city buzzes year-round with world-class dining, museums, and nightlife.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. The Bund — A riverside promenade lined with historic colonial buildings facing the futuristic Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River.
  2. Yu Garden — A classical Ming-dynasty garden with rockeries, koi ponds, and pavilions set amid the bustling Old Town bazaar.
  3. Shanghai Museum — A free, world-class museum housing vast collections of ancient Chinese bronzes, ceramics, calligraphy, and jade.
  4. Former French Concession — Leafy streets of plane trees, Art Deco villas, boutiques, and cafés ideal for leisurely walking and people-watching.
  5. Shanghai Tower Observation Deck — The world's second-tallest building offers a 118th-floor lookout with sweeping views over the entire metropolis.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Chinese Yuan (CNY, ¥)
  • Language: Mandarin Chinese (English is limited outside major tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: CST (UTC+8)
  • Visa: Many nationalities need a visa arranged in advance, though China's visa-free transit and unilateral visa-free schemes now cover numerous countries for short stays.

FAQ

How do I get around Shanghai?
The extensive, affordable metro system is the fastest way to travel, supplemented by taxis, ride-hailing apps like DiDi, and the magnetic levitation train to Pudong Airport.
What payment methods are accepted?
Mobile payments via Alipay and WeChat Pay dominate, so link an international card to these apps; cash is accepted but increasingly inconvenient.
Is tap water safe to drink?
No, tap water should not be drunk without boiling; bottled water is cheap and widely available everywhere.
