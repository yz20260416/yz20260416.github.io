Local Character

Sydney's character is rooted in the clans of the Darug, Dharawal and Eora, the traditional custodians of the land, whose engravings and cultural sites remain common across the region — visitors should seek them out to understand a history stretching back at least 30,000 years, long before the First Fleet arrived in 1788. That convict settlement, led by Arthur Phillip, gave the city its origins as a British penal colony, a quirk that still shapes the sandstone lanes of the old quarter. Today locals call themselves "Sydneysiders," and the city's nickname the Emerald City reflects its green setting: over 1,000,000 hectares of nature reserves and parks, including Royal National Park and Ku-ring-gai Chase. More than 40 per cent of residents were born overseas, with mainland China, India, the UK, Vietnam and the Philippines the largest sources, so a walk through the suburbs is a tour of global food and custom. Greater Sydney's 658 suburbs across 33 local government areas mean each neighbourhood has its own flavour, from harbourfront to the fringes of the Blue Mountains.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Sydney Opera House — The World Heritage-listed harbourfront performing arts centre with its iconic sail-shaped shells.
  2. Sydney Harbour Bridge — The steel arch bridge spanning the harbour, offering climbs and walks with sweeping city views.
  3. Royal National Park — The world's second-oldest national park, south of the city, with coastal tracks and secluded beaches.
  4. Ku-ring-gai Chase National Park — A vast bushland reserve north of the harbour featuring Aboriginal engravings and river scenery.
  5. Central Station — The historic hub of Sydney's suburban train, metro and light rail networks, and a grand starting point for exploring.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Sydney?
April, May, September and October offer mild temperatures and moderate crowds, making them ideal for harbour walks and national park trips.
How do I get around Sydney?
Central Station is the hub of the suburban train, metro and light rail networks, and Kingsford Smith Airport is the main gateway for international arrivals.
What should I know about Sydney's Aboriginal heritage?
The Darug, Dharawal and Eora are the traditional custodians of the land, and engravings and cultural sites dating back at least 30,000 years are common across the region.
