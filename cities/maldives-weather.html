Maldives Weather by Month

The Maldives enjoys a warm tropical climate year-round, with average temperatures hovering between 26°C and 31°C (79°F–88°F) and sea temperatures rarely dipping below 27°C (81°F). The country's weather is shaped by two monsoon seasons: the dry northeast monsoon (iruvai), which runs roughly from December to April and brings sunny skies, low humidity, and calm seas, and the wet southwest monsoon (hulhangu), which runs from May to November and brings higher rainfall, occasional storms, and choppier waters. Because the islands are scattered across the equator, conditions can vary from atoll to atoll, and short, intense downpours are possible even during the "dry" season. The guide below breaks down typical monthly conditions to help you plan your trip.

Best months to visit: The optimal window is January through April, when the northeast monsoon delivers the most sunshine, lowest rainfall, calmest seas, and best underwater visibility. February and March are especially prized for diving and honeymoons. Travelers seeking lower prices and don't mind occasional showers should consider May–July or November, which offer good value and fewer crowds. September and October are the wettest and least predictable months, though rain typically arrives in short bursts rather than all-day downpours.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for both sun and rain: Lightweight, breathable clothing, reef-safe sunscreen, a wide-brim hat, and a compact rain jacket or umbrella are essentials regardless of season.
  • Book early for the dry season: December through April—especially the Christmas/New Year period—sees the highest demand and steepest resort rates. Reserve 6–12 months ahead for the best selection.
  • Embrace the wet season's advantages: May–November brings lower rates, fewer crowds, and excellent manta ray and whale shark sightings in areas like Hanifaru Bay (Baa Atoll, roughly June–October).
  • Plan water activities for mornings: Even in the wet season, mornings tend to be calmer and clearer; afternoon storms are common and usually brief.
  • Check inter-atoll transfers: Rough seas during the southwest monsoon can affect speedboat and domestic flight schedules—build buffer time into your itinerary, especially in September and October.
  • Stay hydrated and sun-safe: The equatorial sun is intense year-round; UV levels are high even on cloudy days, so reapply sunscreen frequently.
  • Consider seaplane timing: Seaplanes operate only in daylight; if arriving on a late flight, plan an overnight in Malé before transferring to your resort.
  • Watch for humidity: The wet season brings higher humidity—choose resorts with good air conditioning and pack quick-dry fabrics for comfort.
