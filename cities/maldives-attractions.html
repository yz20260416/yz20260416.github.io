Maldives: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

The Maldives is an archipelago of nearly 1,200 coral islands scattered across the Indian Ocean, famed for its turquoise lagoons, white-sand beaches, and vibrant marine life. While many visitors spend their entire trip within a single resort, the country offers a surprising range of cultural, natural, and adventure attractions—from the historic mosques of Malé to whale shark encounters in South Ari Atoll. This guide covers ten standout attractions, with practical details on tickets, hours, and tips to help you plan. Note that the Maldives is a Muslim nation: alcohol is generally unavailable on local islands, and modest dress is expected in inhabited areas outside resorts.

  1. Male' City & the Old Friday Mosque (Hukuru Miskiy)

    Malé, the compact capital, packs markets, colorful harbors, the Tsunami Monument, and the coral-stone Old Friday Mosque (built 1658) into a few walkable square kilometers. The mosque, a UNESCO-listed site, features intricate lacquered carvings and coral masonry.

    • Ticket price: Mosque entry free (donations welcome); guided city walks from about US$25–40 per person.
    • Opening hours: Mosque open to non-Muslims outside prayer times, roughly 9:00–16:00 (closed during prayers); the city itself is always accessible.
    • Tips: Dress modestly (covered shoulders and knees; women should carry a headscarf). Avoid Friday midday prayers. The fish market is liveliest in the early morning.

  2. Banana Reef

    One of the Maldives' most famous dive sites, Banana Reef lies north of Malé and is known for its coral-covered overhangs, schools of fusiliers and snappers, and occasional reef sharks and turtles. It suits intermediate divers.

    • Ticket price: Two-tank dive trips typically US$80–150, including equipment and boat.
    • Opening hours: Dive operators usually run morning and afternoon trips, roughly 8:00–17:00.
    • Tips: Currents can be strong; dive with a certified operator and follow guide briefings. Book ahead in peak season (December–April).

  3. Maldives Whale Shark Research Area, South Ari Atoll

    South Ari Atoll is one of the few places worldwide where whale sharks can be seen year-round. Snorkelers and divers can join guided excursions to observe these gentle giants in the protected marine area.

    • Ticket price: Snorkeling excursions typically US$60–120 per person, depending on distance and inclusions.
    • Opening hours: Tours generally depart 7:00–9:00 and return by early afternoon.
    • Tips: Maintain a respectful distance (at least 3 meters), never touch or chase the sharks, and choose operators affiliated with conservation programs. Morning trips often have calmer seas.

  4. Vaadhoo Island (Sea of Stars)

    This Raa Atoll island is famous for its bioluminescent plankton, which make the shoreline glow blue at night, especially during the dry season. The "sea of stars" effect is best seen on dark, moonless nights.

    • Ticket price: No entry fee; access is via guesthouse stays or resort excursions (excursion prices vary, often US$50–100).
    • Opening hours: Best viewing after sunset, roughly 20:00–23:00.
    • Tips: Check moon phases—new moon offers the darkest skies. Avoid flash photography and bright lights, which diminish the effect. The phenomenon is natural and not guaranteed.

  5. HP Reef (Himmafushi Protected Marine Area)

    Located near Himmafushi in North Malé Atoll, HP Reef is a protected marine area celebrated for dense soft corals, colorful reef fish, and good visibility. It is popular with both divers and snorkelers.

    • Ticket price: Snorkeling trips typically US$40–80; dive trips US$80–150.
    • Opening hours: Trips generally run 8:00–16:00.
    • Tips: The site is a protected area—no anchoring or touching coral. Visit on weekdays for fewer boats.

  6. Fuvahmulah (Tiger Shark Diving)

    Fuvahmulah, in the southern Gnaviyani Atoll, is the Maldives' premier destination for tiger shark encounters, along with thresher sharks and other pelagics. Its unique geography—a single large island with steep drop-offs—creates exceptional diving.

    • Ticket price: Dive packages typically US$100–180 per day; multi-day packages offer better value.
    • Opening hours: Dive shops operate daily, generally 7:00–17:00.
    • Tips: This is advanced diving with strong currents and large sharks; certification and experience are required. Book accommodation and dives well in advance, as capacity is limited.

  7. Utheemu Ganduvaru (Utheemu, Haa Alifu Atoll)

    This historic wooden palace was the childhood home of Sultan Mohamed Thakurufaanu, a national hero who led resistance against Portuguese rule in the 16th century. The site offers a rare glimpse into traditional Maldivian architecture and history.

    • Ticket price: Entry typically around US$5–10 (confirm locally; fees vary).
    • Opening hours: Generally 9:00–16:00, closed Fridays.
    • Tips: Dress modestly. The island is reached by domestic flight plus speedboat; arrange transfers through a local guesthouse. Combine with a visit to the nearby beach.

  8. Addu Atoll (British Loyalty Wreck & Cycling)

    The southernmost atoll offers WWII history, including the wreck of the British Loyalty tanker (torpedoed in 1942), plus scenic cycling routes, wetlands, and the laid-back city of Hithadhoo. It is one of the few Maldivian atolls with a network of paved roads linking islands by causeway.

    • Ticket price: Wreck dives typically US$50–90; bicycle rentals about US$5–10 per day.
    • Opening hours: Dive shops generally 8:00–17:00; cycling routes accessible at all hours.
    • Tips: Addu is reached by domestic flight from Malé (about 1 hour 20 minutes). The wreck is suitable for advanced divers; check visibility and currents with local operators.

  9. Hanifaru Bay (Baa Atoll)

    A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, Hanifaru Bay is famous for seasonal aggregations of manta rays and whale sharks feeding on plankton. Between roughly June and October, dozens of mantas can gather in the bay.

    • Ticket price: Ranger-guided snorkeling permits typically around US$25–30 per person, plus excursion costs (often included in resort packages).
    • Opening hours: Access is regulated; snorkeling typically permitted 8:00–16:00 during the season, with limited daily visitors.
    • Tips: Only snorkeling is allowed—no diving or swimming with fins in the bay. Book through a licensed operator and expect strict rules on distance and group size. Peak manta season is June–October.

  10. Dhigurah Island & South Ari Marine Protected Area

    Dhigurah, a long, narrow local island in South Ari Atoll, is a budget-friendly base for whale shark snorkeling, diving, and beach time. The surrounding marine protected area hosts reef sharks, rays, and vibrant coral gardens.

    • Ticket price: No island entry fee; whale shark snorkeling excursions typically US$60–100; dives US$50–90.
    • Opening hours: Excursions generally 7:00–15:00; beaches accessible all day.
    • Tips: Local islands require modest dress outside the beach "bikini zones." Guesthouses offer affordable stays and can arrange excursions. Bring reef-safe sunscreen.

Practical Tips for Visiting the Maldives

  • Best time to visit: The dry northeast monsoon (December–April) brings sunny skies and calm seas; the wet season (May–November) offers lower prices and excellent manta and whale shark sightings in some atolls.
  • Getting around: Inter-island travel relies on speedboats, domestic flights, and seaplanes. Seaplane transfers operate only during daylight hours, so plan arrivals accordingly.
  • Currency and payments: The local currency is the rufiyaa (MVR), but US dollars are widely accepted at resorts. Carry small bills for local islands; ATMs are limited outside Malé.
  • Culture and dress: The Maldives is a Muslim country. On inhabited local islands, cover shoulders and knees and avoid alcohol; resorts permit swimwear and alcohol.
  • Responsible tourism: Use reef-safe sunscreen, never touch coral or marine life, and choose operators with conservation credentials.
  • Health and safety: Check vaccination requirements before travel, purchase comprehensive travel insurance (including evacuation coverage), and stay hydrated in the tropical heat.
  • Book ahead: Popular dive sites, whale shark excursions, and seaplane transfers fill up in peak season—reserve early.
