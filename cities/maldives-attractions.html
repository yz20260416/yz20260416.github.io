Maldives Top 10 Attractions

The Maldives is a nation of 1,192 coral islands scattered across the Indian Ocean, famous for its impossibly turquoise lagoons, powder-white sandbanks, and world-class marine life. While many visitors never leave their resort island, the country rewards those who explore further — from the bustling capital of Malé to UNESCO-listed biosphere reserves and manta-ray cleaning stations. This guide covers the ten attractions that best capture the Maldives' natural and cultural character, with realistic price ranges, practical logistics, and tips to help you plan around monsoon seasons, transfer costs, and local customs.

# Attraction Description Ticket / Entry Price (approx.) Visiting Tips
1 Banana Reef (North Malé Atoll) One of the Maldives' oldest protected dive sites, named for its curved banana-shaped reef. Famous for overhangs, caves, and dense schools of fusiliers, plus regular sightings of turtles, napoleon wrasse, and reef sharks. Suitable for intermediate divers and strong snorkelers on calm days. Dive trip: $60–$120 per person (2-tank trip, gear extra ~$25–$40). Snorkel excursion: $40–$70. Best November–April (dry season, calmer seas). Currents can be strong — dive with a licensed operator and check your certification level. Book through your resort or a Malé-based dive center.
2 Manta Point (Hanifaru Bay, Baa Atoll) A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and the world's largest known feeding aggregation site for reef manta rays. Between roughly June and October, plankton blooms draw dozens — sometimes over a hundred — mantas and occasionally whale sharks into the bay. Ranger-guided snorkel tour: $75–$150 per person (permit + guide). Liveaboard day trips from nearby resorts vary. Strictly regulated: no scuba diving, limited snorkelers at a time, timed slots. Book well ahead in peak season (July–September). Only licensed operators may enter — avoid "informal" boat offers.
3 Malé Old Friday Mosque (Hukuru Miskiy) A 17th-century coral-stone mosque with intricate lacquered woodwork and carved coral walls — the finest example of Maldivian craftsmanship. Nearby, the Sultan Park and National Museum (housed in the former Sultan's palace) add historical context. Mosque entry: free; donations welcome. National Museum: ~$5 (foreign adults). Dress modestly (covered shoulders and knees; women should cover their hair). Closed to non-Muslims during prayer times. Combine with a guided walking tour of Malé (~$25–$40).
4 Vaadhoo Island "Sea of Stars" (Raa Atoll) A natural phenomenon where bioluminescent plankton (typically Noctiluca scintillans) light up the shoreline in electric blue after dark. Best seen on moonless nights when the water is disturbed. Beach access is generally free; reaching Vaadhoo requires a domestic flight (~$200–$300 round trip from Malé) plus speedboat transfer, or a stay at a nearby resort. Not guaranteed — depends on plankton blooms, tides, and darkness. Avoid full-moon nights. Check with your resort's marine team for recent sightings before committing to the trip.
5 Maafushi Island (South Malé Atoll) The most established local (guesthouse) island, offering a budget-friendly alternative to resorts. Public "bikini beach," souvenir shops, and a hub for day excursions — snorkeling, sandbank trips, dolphin cruises, and night fishing. Island access: free. Public ferry from Malé: ~$2 (3.5 hrs); speedboat: $25–$40 (30 min). Day excursions: $25–$60. Alcohol is not available on local islands (resorts are exempt). Respect local dress codes outside the designated bikini beach. Guesthouses are excellent value — book early in high season (Dec–Mar).
6 Ari Atoll (Rasdhoo & Fish Head) A diving and snorkeling heartland, with legendary sites like Fish Head (Mushimasmingili Thila) — a submerged thila famous for grey reef sharks, tuna, and schooling fish. Rasdhoo is a popular local island base for dive safaris. Dive trips: $55–$100 per 2-tank trip; guesthouse stays from $50–$120 per night. Best for certified divers — some sites have strong currents. Rasdhoo is reachable by public ferry (~$5, slow) or speedboat (~$35–$50). Combine with a liveaboard for the widest site access.
7 Fuvahmulah (Gnaviyani Atoll) A unique single-island atoll with freshwater lakes, wetland mangroves, and — most famously — year-round tiger shark encounters at "Tiger Zoo." A bucket-list destination for experienced divers. Dive packages: $100–$180 per day (2–3 dives). Domestic flight from Malé: ~$250–$350 round trip. Requires advanced certification and solid buoyancy control. Tiger shark dives are strictly guided — follow all safety briefings. Best year-round, with calmer seas Dec–Apr.
8 Hulhumalé & Artificial Beach (Malé area) A modern reclaimed island city with a man-made beach, watersports, cafés, and easy access to the airport. A convenient half-day stop for transit travelers or a low-cost base near the capital. Beach access: free. Watersports: $
