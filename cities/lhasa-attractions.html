Lhasa Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Lhasa, the spiritual heart of Tibet, sits at an altitude of about 3,650 meters and blends centuries-old monasteries with a growing modern city. Its landmarks are deeply tied to Tibetan Buddhism and the history of the Dalai Lamas, so visitors should plan for acclimatization, permit requirements, and respectful behavior at religious sites. Below are ten essential attractions with practical details to help you plan your visit. Prices and hours can change seasonally and around Tibetan holidays, so confirm locally before you go.

  1. Potala Palace

    The iconic hilltop palace complex was the winter residence of the Dalai Lamas and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It contains the White Palace (administrative and living quarters) and the Red Palace (chapels and stupas), with thousands of rooms, murals, and religious artifacts. Visits are timed and strictly regulated.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 200 in high season (May–Oct); around CNY 100 in low season. Foreign visitors often book through a tour agency.
    • Opening hours: Typically 09:00–16:00; entry slots are assigned and may close earlier in winter.
    • Visiting tips: Book tickets in advance, especially in summer. Expect steep stairs and thin air; climb slowly. Photography is prohibited inside the palace. The visit is usually limited to about one hour.

  2. Jokhang Temple

    The most sacred temple in Tibetan Buddhism and the spiritual center of Lhasa. Pilgrims circumambulate the temple along Barkhor Street, and the interior houses the revered Jowo Shakyamuni statue. The architecture reflects Tibetan, Nepalese, and Tang Chinese influences.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 85.
    • Opening hours: Generally 09:00–18:00; morning hours are reserved for pilgrims, with tourist access often from late morning.
    • Visiting tips: Walk clockwise around the temple and Barkhor. Dress modestly and remove hats inside chapels. Avoid pointing your feet at statues or monks.

  3. Barkhor Street

    A historic circular pilgrimage route and market district surrounding the Jokhang. It is the best place to observe daily Tibetan life, buy prayer wheels, jewelry, and handicrafts, and join the flow of pilgrims spinning prayer wheels.

    • Ticket price: Free.
    • Opening hours: Open all day; liveliest in the morning and late afternoon.
    • Visiting tips: Always walk clockwise. Bargain politely in shops, and ask before photographing pilgrims. Watch for pickpockets in crowded sections.

  4. Norbulingka

    The former summer palace of the Dalai Lamas, set in a large garden complex with palaces, pavilions, and ponds. It is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a pleasant place to stroll, especially during the Shoton Festival.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 60 in high season; lower in low season.
    • Opening hours: Typically 09:00–18:00 (may close earlier in winter).
    • Visiting tips: Allow 1–2 hours. Combine with a nearby visit to the Tibet Museum if time permits. The gardens are a good low-effort activity during acclimatization.

  5. Sera Monastery

    One of the great Gelug monasteries, founded in the 15th century. It is famous for its lively monk debates held in the courtyard in the afternoon and for its chapels and thangka collections.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 50.
    • Opening hours: Generally 09:00–17:00; debates usually take place in the afternoon (around 15:00), except on certain days.
    • Visiting tips: Keep a respectful distance during debates and avoid blocking the monks. Photography may be restricted in some halls. The monastery is a short taxi ride north of central Lhasa.

  6. Drepung Monastery

    Once the largest monastery in the world, Drepung sprawls across a hillside west of Lhasa. It was the seat of the Dalai Lamas before the Potala Palace and offers sweeping views over the valley.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 60.
    • Opening hours: Typically 09:00–16:00.
    • Visiting tips: The altitude is higher than central Lhasa, so take it slowly. Wear sturdy shoes for the uneven paths. Combine with a visit to Nechung Monastery nearby if time allows.

  7. Ganden Monastery

    One of the three great Gelug monasteries, located on a ridge about 45 km east of Lhasa. It offers dramatic views and an important kora (circuit) around the hilltop.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 50.
    • Opening hours: Generally 09:00–16:00.
    • Visiting tips: Plan a half-day trip. The drive involves winding roads, and the site is at about 4,300 meters, so acclimatize first. The kora takes 1–2 hours.

  8. Ramoche Temple

    A major temple in central Lhasa, traditionally associated with the Jowo Mikyoe Dorje statue brought by Princess Wencheng. It is smaller and quieter than the Jokhang and offers an intimate look at Tibetan worship.

    • Ticket price: About CNY 30.
    • Opening hours: Typically 09:00–17:00.
    • Visiting tips: Visit in the morning when pilgrims are most active. Dress conservatively and avoid loud conversation in prayer halls.

  9. Tibet Museum

    A modern museum covering Tibetan history, culture, and art, with collections of thangkas, manuscripts, and everyday objects. It provides useful context for the religious sites you will visit.

    • Ticket price: Free (bring your passport for registration).
    • Opening hours: Generally 10:00–17:00, closed Mondays.
    • Visiting tips: Allow 1–2 hours. Some exhibits have limited English labeling. It is a good rainy-day or acclimatization-day option.

  10. Chakpori Hill

    A small hill opposite the Potala Palace, traditionally used for carving mani stones and prayer stones. It offers one of the best photo spots of the Potala and a short, pleasant walk.

    • Ticket price: Free (small donation appreciated at the carving areas).
    • Opening hours: Open all day; best light is in the late afternoon.
    • Visiting tips: Walk clockwise around the hill and prayer stones. Bring water and sun protection. Combine with a visit to the Potala or the nearby Tibetan medicine hospital.

Practical Tips for Visiting Lhasa

  • Permits: Foreign travelers generally need a Tibet Travel Permit arranged through a licensed tour agency, plus a Chinese visa. Rules vary by nationality and route; confirm current requirements well in advance.
  • Acclimatization: Spend your first day resting and hydrating. Avoid alcohol and strenuous activity. Consider a doctor's advice about altitude medication before travel.
  • Best time to visit: April to October offers milder weather; July and August are busiest. Winter is cold but quieter and often cheaper.
  • Etiquette: Walk clockwise around monasteries and stupas. Ask before photographing monks or pilgrims. Dress modestly, remove hats in chapels, and do not touch religious statues or artifacts.
  • Money and tickets: Carry cash for smaller sites and donations; some ticket offices may not accept foreign cards. Prices and hours can change, especially around Tibetan New Year and other festivals.
  • Health and safety: Use sun protection and lip balm, drink bottled or boiled water, and carry any prescription medicine you need. Travel insurance that covers high-altitude activities is recommended.
