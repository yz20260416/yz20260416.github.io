Lhasa Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Lhasa, the spiritual heart of Tibet, sits at an altitude of roughly 3,650 meters and blends centuries-old monasteries, palaces, and pilgrimage routes with a growing modern city. This guide covers the ten essential sites for first-time visitors, including ticket information, opening hours, and practical advice for navigating high-altitude travel, permits, and local customs.

Top 10 Attractions in Lhasa

  1. Potala Palace

    The iconic 13-story palace complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and former residence of the Dalai Lamas, dominates the Lhasa skyline. The White Palace contains living quarters and offices, while the Red Palace holds chapels and stupas. Visits are timed and strictly limited, so book well in advance.

    • Tickets: 200 CNY (peak season, May–Oct); 100 CNY (off-peak, Nov–Apr)
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–16:00 (timed entry; closed on some administrative days—confirm locally)
    • Tips: Book tickets online or through your tour operator days ahead; entry slots are limited to about one hour inside. Climb slowly due to altitude and the many stairs. Photography is prohibited inside the palace.
  2. Jokhang Temple

    Tibet's most sacred temple, founded in the 7th century, houses the revered Jowo Shakyamuni statue. Pilgrims circumambulate the temple along Barkhor Street throughout the day, making it the spiritual center of Lhasa.

    • Tickets: 85 CNY
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–17:30 (morning hours reserved for pilgrims; tourist visits typically from late morning)
    • Tips: Visit in the afternoon when tourist entry is permitted. Walk clockwise around the temple and inside chapels. Remove hats inside and avoid pointing feet at statues.
  3. Barkhor Street

    The circular pilgrimage route around Jokhang Temple is lined with shops, stalls, and tea houses. It is the best place to observe Tibetan daily life, buy prayer beads, thangkas, and handicrafts, and join the flow of pilgrims.

    • Tickets: Free
    • Hours: Open all day (most shops roughly 09:00–20:00)
    • Tips: Always walk clockwise. Bargain politely at shops but avoid disrupting pilgrims. Keep valuables secure in crowded sections.
  4. Sera Monastery

    One of the great Gelug monasteries, founded in 1419, Sera is famous for its lively monk debates held in the courtyard most afternoons. The monastery's chapels and colleges are worth a slow exploration.

    • Tickets: 50 CNY
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–16:00 (debates typically around 15:00, weekdays; schedules vary)
    • Tips: Arrive in the afternoon to watch the debates. Photography of monks may require permission or a small fee. Dress modestly.
  5. Drepung Monastery

    Once the largest monastery in the world, Drepung sprawls across a hillside west of Lhasa. Its whitewashed buildings and the Ganden Palace offer sweeping views and a sense of the monastic scale of old Tibet.

    • Tickets: 60 CNY
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–16:00
    • Tips: Allow 2–3 hours for the climb and exploration. Bring water and sun protection; the altitude makes the uphill walk strenuous.
  6. Norbulingka Palace

    The former summer palace of the Dalai Lamas is a garden complex with palaces, ponds, and shaded paths—an easy, pleasant contrast to the city's busy sites.

    • Tickets: 60 CNY (peak season); 40 CNY (off-peak)
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–17:00
    • Tips: Best visited in warm months when the gardens are green. Combine with a nearby stroll along the river.
  7. Ramoche Temple

    A major temple traditionally associated with the Jowo Mikyö Dorje statue, Ramoche is smaller and quieter than Jokhang but historically significant and central to Tibetan Buddhism.

    • Tickets: 30 CNY
    • Hours: Approximately 09:00–17:00
    • Tips: A short visit of 30–45 minutes suffices. It pairs well with nearby Barkhor and Jokhang on a walking day.
  8. Ani Tsangkung Nunnery

    A small, atmospheric nunnery tucked into the old town, known for its peaceful courtyards and a popular on-site tea house where visitors can sit with locals.

    • Tickets: Free (donations welcome)
    • Hours: Approximately 08:00–17:00
    • Tips: Keep voices low and respect prayer areas. The tea house is a good spot to rest and warm up.
  9. Chagpori (Iron Hill) and the Tibetan Medical College

    The hill south of the Potala offers short walks, views over the city, and a glimpse of traditional Tibetan medicine at the nearby college and clinic.

    • Tickets: Free for the hill; clinic visits vary
    • Hours: Daylight hours
    • Tips: A gentle climb suitable for acclimatized travelers. Avoid strenuous exertion in your first days at altitude.
  10. Lhasa River and Riverside Walks

    The riverbanks south of the city provide open views, local life, and a relaxed way to spend an afternoon away from crowded sites. Several bridges and promenades are accessible by taxi.

    • Tickets: Free
    • Hours: Open all day
    • Tips: Go in the late afternoon for softer light. Bring layers—winds pick up quickly along the water.

Practical Tips for Visiting Lhasa

  • Permits: Foreign travelers need a Tibet Travel Permit arranged through a licensed tour operator, plus a Chinese visa. Group tours are generally required; confirm current regulations before booking.
  • Acclimatization: Spend your first day or two resting and hydrating. Avoid alcohol and strenuous activity. Consider arriving by air with a slow schedule or via a lower-altitude route if time allows.
  • Best time to visit: April to October offers milder weather and clearer skies; winter is colder and quieter with lower ticket prices at some sites.
  • Etiquette: Walk clockwise around temples and stupas, remove hats indoors, ask before photographing monks or pilgrims, and avoid touching religious objects.
  • Money and connectivity: Carry cash for smaller sites and markets; ATMs exist but can be unreliable. Mobile coverage is good in the city.
  • Health: Sun protection is essential at altitude. Consult a doctor about altitude sickness precautions and carry any personal medication.
  • Opening hours: Hours can change seasonally or for religious events—confirm with your hotel or guide on the day of your visit.
