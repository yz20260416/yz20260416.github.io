Singapore Weather by Month

Singapore sits just 1° north of the equator, so it doesn't have four seasons — it has two monsoon seasons and a whole lot of humidity. Temperatures stay remarkably consistent year-round, hovering between 24°C (75°F) and 32°C (90°F) most days, with highs occasionally nudging 34°C (93°F). What changes month to month is rainfall: the Northeast Monsoon (roughly November–January) brings the wettest, coolest conditions, while the Southwest Monsoon (June–September) tends to be drier, though "dry" in Singapore is a relative term. Afternoon thunderstorms are a near-daily feature in many months, usually blowing through within an hour or two. The good news for travelers: there's no truly bad month to visit, just different trade-offs between rain, crowds, and heat.

Figures are long-term averages and vary year to year; rainfall can be highly localized, with one district drenched while another stays dry.

Best Months to Visit

  • February–April: Generally the sweet spot — relatively lower rainfall (especially February), warm but not oppressive, and fewer monsoon-driven downpours. February in particular is one of the driest months on average.
  • June–September: The Southwest Monsoon period brings comparatively stable, drier weather and is a popular window for visitors, though haze from regional fires can occasionally affect air quality (most often June–October).
  • November–January: The wettest and coolest stretch. Expect frequent, sometimes prolonged showers — but also lower prices, thinner crowds outside the December holidays, and comfortable evening temperatures. December is festive and lively despite the rain.
  • Avoid if rain-sensitive: November and December, when monthly rainfall peaks near 250–290 mm.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for rain and heat equally: A compact umbrella or packable rain jacket is essential year-round; so are breathable, quick-dry fabrics. Leave heavy layers at home.
  • Plan outdoor activities for mornings: Afternoon thunderstorms are most common between roughly 2 pm and 6 pm. Front-load sightseeing, gardens, and walking tours before lunch.
  • Embrace the indoors: Singapore's malls, museums, hawker centres, and MRT network are air-conditioned and connected — a sudden downpour is rarely more than a few steps from shelter.
  • Stay hydrated and sun-safe: UV index runs high most days; sunscreen, a hat, and water are non-negotiables. Humidity makes heat feel stronger than the thermometer suggests.
  • Check the radar app: Local weather apps (e.g., NEA's myENV) give short-term rain forecasts — storms here are intense but usually short-lived.
  • Book around events: Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb), National Day (Aug 9), and the year-end holidays drive up prices and crowds — book accommodation early if visiting then.
  • Don't fear the monsoon labels: "Monsoon" here means more rain, not washed-out days — most travelers still get plenty of sunshine even in the wettest months.
