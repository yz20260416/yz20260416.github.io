Best Time to Visit Dali

Dali is a historic city in Yunnan, China, once the capital of the Kingdom of Dali and the earlier Kingdom of Nanzhao. Set between mountains and a large lake, it is known for its Bai minority culture and relaxed atmosphere. Visit in spring or autumn for mild weather and clear skies.

Local Character

Dali was the center of the Kingdom of Dali (937–1253 AD), a powerful state that controlled much of modern Yunnan. Visitors can explore the remains of this kingdom, including the ancient city walls, which offer a glimpse into the region's medieval past. The city is also home to the Bai people, an ethnic minority with distinct traditions, architecture, and cuisine. A notable local quirk is the existence of Dali Town and Xiaguan (Dali New Town), which are often confused by travelers; Xiaguan is the modern administrative center, while Dali Town is the historic old town. Understanding this distinction helps visitors navigate the area and appreciate the contrast between old and new.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Dali Ancient City — The historic old town with well-preserved city walls and traditional Bai architecture.
  2. Erhai Lake — A large alpine lake offering scenic views and opportunities for cycling and boat trips.
  3. Cangshan Mountains — A mountain range with hiking trails and cable cars providing panoramic views of the region.
  4. Three Pagodas — An iconic trio of ancient pagodas dating back to the Kingdom of Dali.
  5. Xizhou Town — A nearby town known for its traditional Bai courtyards and local markets.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Dali?
Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) offer the most pleasant weather with low rainfall and moderate crowds.
How do I get to Dali?
Dali is accessible by train or bus from Kunming, the capital of Yunnan, and has an airport with domestic flights.
What local food should I try in Dali?
Bai cuisine features dishes like rushan (cheese), xizhou baba (flatbread), and various wild mushroom dishes.
