Florence in 3 Days: A Complete Itinerary

Florence packs an extraordinary concentration of Renaissance art, architecture, and cuisine into a compact, walkable historic center. This three-day itinerary balances the essential sights—the Duomo, the Uffizi, Michelangelo's David—with neighborhood wandering, market food, and evening aperitivo. Book major museums in advance, wear comfortable shoes for cobblestones, and plan to start early to beat the crowds.

Day 1: The Duomo, Piazza della Signoria, and the Uffizi

TimePlan
MorningStart at the Piazza del Duomo. Climb Brunelleschi's Dome (reserve a timed ticket; 463 steps) or Giotto's Campanile for city views. Visit the Baptistery and its gilded bronze doors, then the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo to see the original sculptures.
AfternoonWalk to Piazza della Signoria to see the Palazzo Vecchio and the open-air sculpture loggia. Lunch nearby, then enter the Uffizi Gallery (book a timed entry; allow 2–3 hours). Focus on Botticelli's Birth of Venus, Leonardo's Annunciation, and the Caravaggio rooms.
EveningCross the Ponte Vecchio at sunset. Enjoy an aperitivo in the Oltrarno district (Santo Spirito area), then dinner at a trattoria serving ribollita or pappa al pomodoro.

Restaurant picks: Trattoria Mario (lunch, near San Lorenzo market; no reservations), Osteria Santo Spirito (Oltrarno), All'Antico Vinaio (quick schiacciata sandwich).

Day 2: Accademia, San Lorenzo Market, and Santa Croce

TimePlan
MorningVisit the Galleria dell'Accademia early (timed ticket) to see Michelangelo's David and the unfinished Prisoners. Then explore the San Lorenzo Market—the outdoor leather and souvenir stalls plus the covered Mercato Centrale food hall.
AfternoonWalk to the Basilica of Santa Croce (burial place of Michelangelo, Machiavelli, and Galileo). Wander the surrounding Santa Croce neighborhood for artisan workshops. Optional: the Bargello museum for Renaissance sculpture.
EveningDinner in the Santa Croce or Sant'Ambrogio area. Try a bistecca alla fiorentina if you're a group, or fresh pasta at a smaller osteria. End with gelato at a traditional gelateria.

Restaurant picks: Mercato Centrale (casual lunch stalls), Cibrèo Caffè (Sant'Ambrogio), Il Santo Bevitore (Oltrarno dinner).

Day 3: Pitti Palace, Boboli Gardens, and Piazzale Michelangelo

TimePlan
MorningCross the river to the Pitti Palace (Palazzo Pitti) and choose one or two museums—the Palatine Gallery or the Costume and Fashion Museum. Then stroll the Boboli Gardens behind the palace (allow 1–2 hours).
AfternoonExplore the Oltrarno artisan district—Via Maggio, Santo Spirito, and the Brancacci Chapel in Santa Maria del Carmine (reserve a slot). Lunch in Santo Spirito square.
EveningWalk or take bus 12/13 up to Piazzale Michelangelo for the classic panoramic sunset view over the city and Arno. Return to the center for a final dinner.

Restaurant picks: Osteria del Cinghiale Bianco (Oltrarno), La Casalinga (Santo Spirito, budget-friendly), Gelateria dei Neri (gelato).

Practical Tips

  • Book ahead: Reserve timed tickets for the Uffizi, Accademia, Brunelleschi's Dome, and Brancacci Chapel online—often weeks in advance in peak season.
  • Museum pass: The Firenze Card can be worthwhile for heavy sightseeing, but compare against individual timed tickets, which now often require advance booking anyway.
  • Dress code: Shoulders and knees should be covered when entering churches, including the Duomo and Santa Croce.
  • Walking: The historic center is compact and best explored on foot; wear sturdy shoes for cobblestones.
  • Meal times: Lunch is typically 12:30–14:30 and dinner from 19:30; many kitchens close between service. Reserve popular trattorias.
  • Water: Carry a refillable bottle—public fountains (fontanelle) offer free drinking water.
  • Crowds and heat: Start major sights at opening; July–August afternoons are hot—plan indoor museum time then.
  • Pickpockets: Watch belongings in crowded areas like the Ponte Vecchio, markets, and around the Duomo.
  • Tipping: Not obligatory; a small round-up or "coperto" (cover charge) is common in restaurants.
