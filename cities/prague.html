Best Time to Visit Prague

Prague, the Czech capital, is a fairy-tale city of Gothic spires, cobbled lanes, and a hilltop castle that has survived centuries of history intact. It is compact, walkable, and affordable, with world-class beer and a lively cultural calendar. Visit in late spring or early autumn for mild weather and thinner crowds, or in December for its celebrated Christmas markets.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Prague Castle — The world's largest ancient castle complex, home to St. Vitus Cathedral and sweeping views over the city.
  2. Charles Bridge — A 14th-century stone bridge lined with baroque statues that connects the Old Town with Malá Strana.
  3. Old Town Square — The city's historic heart, featuring the astronomical clock's hourly show and colorful baroque facades.
  4. Josefov (Jewish Quarter) — A preserved district of synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery that documents centuries of Prague's Jewish heritage.
  5. Vyšehrad — A peaceful hilltop fortress with a basilica, cemetery of notable Czechs, and panoramic views along the Vltava.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Czech Koruna (CZK, Kč)
  • Language: Czech (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: CET (UTC+1), CEST (UTC+2) in summer
  • Visa: Citizens of the EU, US, UK, Canada, and many other countries can enter visa-free for up to 90 days, while other nationalities should check Schengen requirements in advance.

FAQ

How many days do I need in Prague?
Two to three days cover the main sights comfortably, while four or five allow day trips and a slower pace.
Is Prague expensive?
Prague is moderately priced by Western European standards, with affordable public transport, beer, and meals, though central tourist areas cost more.
How do I get around Prague?
The city is best explored on foot, supported by an efficient and inexpensive tram and metro network; taxis and ride-hailing apps are also available.
