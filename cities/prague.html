Best Time to Visit Prague

Prague is the capital of the Czech Republic and historical capital of Bohemia, a Vltava River city whose historic centre has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992. Visit in late spring or early autumn for mild weather and manageable crowds, though its Romanesque-to-Baroque architecture rewards any season.

Local Character

Prague's name likely derives from the old Czech verb pražiti ('to burn'), referring to a burned forest cleared for settlement, or from práh ('threshold' or 'ford') tied to the Vltava crossing where the city began — a linguistic quirk that explains why locals see their home as a place of passage and clearing. The city is nicknamed the 'City of a Hundred Spires,' a count attributed to 19th-century mathematician Bernard Bolzano; visitors should care because this nickname frames Prague's skyline as a measurable, historical claim rather than tourist poetry, and it guides you to look up at church towers across the Old Town. Prague was the residence of Holy Roman Emperors Charles IV and Rudolf II, whose patronage left the Gothic and Renaissance layers visible today, and Charles University, the oldest in Central Europe, still anchors the city's scholarly character.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Prague Castle — The historic castle complex founded on shale bedrock, long the seat of Bohemian kings and Holy Roman Emperors.
  2. Charles Bridge — The iconic stone bridge crossing the Vltava, linking the Old Town with the castle side of the city.
  3. Old Town Square and the Astronomical Clock — The medieval square whose astronomical clock draws crowds for its hourly display.
  4. Jewish Quarter — The historic Josefov district preserving synagogues and a cemetery central to Prague's Jewish heritage.
  5. Petřín Hill and Vyšehrad — Two elevated historic sites offering green walks and views over the Vltava and the city's spires.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Prague?
May, June, and September offer mild weather and lively streets, while April and October balance fewer crowds with comfortable temperatures.
How do I get around Prague?
An extensive modern public transportation system connects the city, supplemented by walking routes through the compact UNESCO-listed historic centre.
What is Prague known for architecturally?
Prague preserves Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, and Baroque buildings, earning its nickname the 'City of a Hundred Spires.'
