San Francisco Travel Cost Guide

San Francisco is one of the most expensive cities in the United States, but it is also one of the most rewarding to visit. From the Golden Gate Bridge and Alcatraz to world-class food, cable cars, and neighborhood culture, the city offers an unforgettable experience at nearly every price point. The key to managing costs is choosing the right travel style and knowing where your money goes. This guide breaks down typical daily and trip costs across three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan a San Francisco trip that fits your wallet.

How Much Does a San Francisco Trip Cost? (Daily Estimates per Person)

Figures below are approximate 2025 costs in US dollars, assuming double occupancy for accommodation and a moderate season (spring/fall). Summer and major event weekends run higher; winter (excluding holidays) can be somewhat cheaper.

Sample Trip Budgets (3 Days / 2 Nights, per Person)

  • Budget: roughly $350–$550 total — hostel or budget lodging, transit passes, free attractions, casual food.
  • Mid-Range: roughly $900–$1,500 total — central hotel, Alcatraz and museum tickets, a mix of casual and nicer meals.
  • Luxury: roughly $2,500–$4,500+ total — premium hotel, fine dining, private tours, and car service.

Cost Breakdown by Category

Accommodation

  • Hostels: Dorm beds typically $50–$90 per night; private hostel rooms $120–$200.
  • Budget hotels/motels: $120–$200, often near Fisherman's Wharf, the Marina, or outside the city center (e.g., near SFO or in Oakland/Berkeley with BART access).
  • Mid-range hotels: $180–$320; book well in advance for spring and summer.
  • Luxury hotels: $450–$900+; rates spike during conventions, Pride, and major sporting events.
  • Tip: Hotel taxes in San Francisco are high (around 14–16% plus tourism assessments), so factor that into your nightly rate.

Food

  • Budget: Mission District taquerias ($8–$15 burritos), dim sum in Chinatown, food trucks, and grocery stores (Trader Joe's, Safeway) keep daily costs near $30–$50.
  • Mid-range: Expect $20–$35 per sit-down entrée in most neighborhoods; a nice dinner for two with drinks runs $100–$180.
  • Luxury: Tasting menus at acclaimed restaurants commonly start around $150–$350 per person before wine.
  • Tip: San Francisco sales tax is about 8.6%, and tipping of 18–20% is standard at sit-down restaurants.

Transportation

  • Muni (buses, light rail, historic streetcars): Single rides about $2.75–$3; a 1-day visitor passport is around $13–$14, and a 3-day pass around $33–$34 (cable cars included on passport products).
  • Cable car single ride: About $8 — scenic but pricey; the Powell-Hyde line is the classic experience.
  • BART: Useful for airport transfers (SFO to downtown roughly $10) and East Bay trips.
  • Rideshares/taxis: Short city trips typically $12–$25; airport runs $35–$60.
  • Rental car: Generally not recommended for city sightseeing — parking can cost $40–$80 per day at hotels. Rent only for day trips (Marin, Napa, Monterey).
  • Clipper card: A reloadable transit card that works across Muni, BART, and ferries — convenient and slightly discounted.

Activities and Attractions

  • Free: Golden Gate Bridge walk, Lands End and Sutro Baths, Golden Gate Park (including the Japanese Tea Garden on free-entry mornings), Palace of Fine Arts, Ferry Building, Coit Tower exterior, neighborhood walking tours (self-guided).
  • Moderate: Alcatraz ferry and tour (~$45–$50), California Academy of Sciences (~$40–$45), de Young Museum (~$20), Exploratorium (~$40), SFMOMA (~$30), cable car rides (~$8 each).
  • Splurge: Bay cruises and sailing ($60–$150), helicopter tours ($250–$400+), Napa/Sonoma wine tours ($150–$300), fine dining experiences, theater and live music ($50–$200+).
  • Tip: Book Alcatraz weeks in advance — it sells out, especially in summer. CityPASS or Go City passes can save money if you plan to visit several major attractions.

Practical Tips for Saving Money in San Francisco

  • Book early and travel shoulder season: Spring and fall offer good weather and lower rates than summer. Avoid major convention weeks and holiday weekends.
  • Stay near transit, not necessarily downtown: Neighborhoods along Muni and BART lines (Inner Sunset, Richmond, Mission, Noe Valley) can offer better value and local flavor.
  • Skip the rental car: Parking is expensive and scarce; transit, walking, and occasional rideshares are cheaper and less stressful.
  • Eat like a local: Farmers markets (Ferry Building on Saturdays), taquerias, dim sum, and happy hours deliver excellent value.
