Rio de Janeiro 3-Day Itinerary

Rio de Janeiro packs an extraordinary amount into one city: granite peaks rising straight from the Atlantic, the world's largest urban rainforest, golden beaches framed by favelas and five-star hotels, and a laid-back carioca rhythm that rewards travelers who slow down. Three days is enough to hit the essential icons — Christ the Redeemer, Sugarloaf Mountain, Copacabana and Ipanema — while still leaving room for samba, street food, and a sunset or two. This itinerary is organized geographically to minimize transit time, front-loads the headline sights, and builds in realistic buffers for Rio's traffic and weather. Start early, book major attractions in advance, and leave evenings free for the city's best restaurants and nightlife.

Day 1 — Copacabana, Ipanema & Leblon

Morning: Ease into Rio with a walk along Copacabana's iconic black-and-white wave promenade. Continue to Forte de Copacabana (open Tue–Sun, small entry fee), a historic military fort with excellent views back toward the beach and up to Sugarloaf — a great low-effort orientation point. Grab a fresh coconut water or açaí from a kiosk.

Afternoon: Head to Ipanema. Wander the streets around Praça General Osório (home to a famous Sunday craft fair if your visit aligns), then claim a spot on Ipanema Beach — Posto 9 is the classic people-watching stretch. Continue on foot to quieter Leblon Beach.

Evening: Walk to Arpoador Rock for the sunset, a beloved carioca ritual with applause when the sun dips behind the Two Brothers peaks. Dinner in Leblon.

  • Lunch — Bar Astor (Ipanema): Relaxed, stylish beach-bar fare; excellent bolinhos (codfish fritters) and cold beer.
  • Dinner — CT Boucherie (Leblon): Celebrated steakhouse with unlimited sides; book ahead.

Day 2 — Christ the Redeemer, Santa Teresa & Lapa

Early morning: Book the first train slot (around 8:00 a.m.) up Corcovado via the Trem do Corcovado through Tijuca Forest — the earliest departure means fewer crowds and clearer views before afternoon clouds build. Allow 2–2.5 hours round trip including time at the statue.

Late morning: Descend and visit Parque Lage at the foot of Corcovado — a lush, free park with a photogenic mansion, courtyard café, and trails.

Afternoon: Taxi or rideshare to Santa Teresa, Rio's hilltop bohemian quarter. Ride the historic bondinho tram if running, browse galleries and antique shops, and walk down to the Escadaria Selarón (Selarón Steps), the mosaic staircase linking Santa Teresa and Lapa. Continue to the Arcos da Lapa aqueduct.

Evening: Lapa comes alive at night. Catch live samba at a traditional venue (e.g., Carioca da Gema or Rio Scenarium — reserve in advance) with caipirinhas and petiscos.

  • Lunch — Bar do Mineiro (Santa Teresa): Beloved neighborhood boteco; order the feijoada if visiting on a weekend.
  • Dinner — Aprazível (Santa Teresa): Garden-terrace restaurant with panoramic city views and refined Brazilian dishes; reserve ahead.

Day 3 — Sugarloaf Mountain & Farewell

Morning: Arrive at Praia Vermelha in Urca and take the two-stage cable car (bondinho) up Sugarloaf Mountain — go early to beat queues and heat. The views over Guanabara Bay, Copacabana, and the city are arguably Rio's finest. Active travelers can instead (or additionally) hike the Pão de Açúcar trail from Praia Vermelha (moderate, ~1–1.5 hours, free) and ride the cable car down.

Lunch: Bar Urca — a Rio institution where locals sit on the seawall with empadas and beers overlooking the bay.

Afternoon options: Choose based on energy and interest —

  • Culture: Explore Centro's Museu do Amanhã (Museum of Tomorrow) and the revitalized Porto Maravilha waterfront, or the fine collection at the Museu de Arte do Rio (MAR).
  • Relaxation: Return to Ipanema or Copacabana for a final beach
