Lima Weather by Month

Lima sits on a coastal desert at roughly 12° south of the equator, yet its climate is anything but tropical. The Humboldt Current chills the Pacific offshore, and the city's persistent marine layer—locally called la garúa—creates one of the most unusual weather patterns of any world capital. The result is a mild, almost rainless climate split into two distinct seasons: a gray, damp, cool winter from roughly May to November, and a bright, warm summer from December to April. Temperatures rarely drop below 12°C (54°F) or climb above 28°C (82°F), and measurable rainfall is so rare that many Limeños don't own an umbrella. What varies dramatically by month is not the thermometer but the sky: the famous "donkey's belly" (panza de burro) overcast of winter versus the clear blue of summer. Figures are long-term monthly averages for coastal Lima (Miraflores/Jorge Chávez Airport area). Rainfall near the coast is negligible year-round; the "damp" of winter arrives as mist and drizzle rather than true rain. Microclimates matter: districts closer to the sea (Barranco, Miraflores, San Isidro) are cooler and foggier, while inland districts (La Molina, Ate) run several degrees warmer and sunnier.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: April–May and October–November. Warm-to-mild temperatures, returning or lingering sunshine, and thinner crowds than high summer make these the sweetest spots on the calendar.
  • Best for sunshine and beaches: December–March. Clear skies, warm sea breezes, and reliable sun—ideal if you plan to surf at Punta Hermosa or lounge on the Costa Verde. February is the warmest month.
  • Best for budget and culture: June–September. Gray skies and cool air keep prices and crowds down, while museums, ceviche lunches, and colonial architecture are unaffected by the garúa. This is also prime time to escape to the Andes (Cusco, Huaraz), which are dry and sunny in these months.
  • Avoid if you crave sun: July–September, when Lima can go weeks under a thick, drizzly overcast.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for two seasons in one trip. Even in summer, coastal evenings can be breezy and cool; a light jacket or sweater is useful year-round.
  • Don't expect rain—but expect damp. Winter garúa leaves a fine mist that can make streets slick and clothes feel clammy. A packable windbreaker beats an umbrella.
  • Layer for microclimates. It can be foggy and 16°C in Miraflores while sunny and 24°C in La Molina. If you're heading inland or uphill, dress in layers.
  • Plan winter mornings accordingly. From June to September, the sun often doesn't break through until midday or later. Schedule outdoor sightseeing for afternoons and save mornings for indoor attractions.
  • Watch the UV. Lima sits near the equator, and summer UV levels are extreme even when it feels mild. Sunscreen is essential December–April.
