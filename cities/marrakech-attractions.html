Marrakech: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Marrakech, known as the "Red City" for its ochre walls and buildings, is one of Morocco's most visited destinations. Its medina—a UNESCO World Heritage Site—packs palaces, gardens, souks, and mosques into a dense, walkable historic core. Below are the ten attractions most worth your time, with practical details on tickets, hours, and how to visit them well. Prices and hours change periodically, so confirm before you go.

  1. Jemaa el-Fnaa

    The city's main square and beating heart, Jemaa el-Fnaa transforms through the day: juice stalls and snake charmers in the morning, storytellers and musicians in the afternoon, and a sprawling open-air food market at night. It's free to enter and always open.

    • Tickets: Free (food, drinks, and photos with performers cost extra)
    • Hours: Open 24 hours; liveliest after sunset
    • Tips: Agree on a price before any photo with performers; visit twice—once in daylight, once at night.

  2. Koutoubia Mosque

    The city's tallest landmark, a 12th-century minaret rising about 77 meters, visible across Marrakech. Non-Muslims cannot enter the mosque, but the surrounding gardens and exterior are worth a look, especially at sunset.

    • Tickets: Free (exterior and gardens only)
    • Hours: Gardens generally open daily during daylight; mosque closed to non-Muslims
    • Tips: Best photographed from the gardens or from a rooftop café nearby; dress respectfully.

  3. Bahia Palace

    A 19th-century palace with ornate tilework, carved cedar ceilings, and courtyard gardens. It's one of the most photogenic sites in the medina and a manageable 1–1.5 hour visit.

    • Tickets: Around 70 MAD for adults (varies)
    • Hours: Typically 9:00–17:00 daily; may close early during Ramadan
    • Tips: Arrive at opening to beat tour groups; combine with the nearby Saadian Tombs.

  4. Saadian Tombs

    A royal necropolis from the Saadian dynasty, rediscovered in 1917. The highlight is the Chamber of the Twelve Columns, with intricate stucco and marble work.

    • Tickets: Around 70 MAD for adults
    • Hours: Typically 9:00–17:00 daily
    • Tips: The site is small and gets crowded—go early; it's a 10-minute walk from Bahia Palace.

  5. Majorelle Garden (Jardin Majorelle)

    A vivid blue villa and botanical garden designed by Jacques Majorelle and later owned by Yves Saint Laurent. Expect cacti, palms, and bold color—plus queues.

    • Tickets: Around 100 MAD for the garden; combined tickets with the YSL Museum available
    • Hours: Generally 8:00–18:00 (hours shift seasonally)
    • Tips: Book timed tickets online in advance; visit at opening for cooler temperatures and fewer crowds.

  6. Ben Youssef Madrasa

    A former Islamic college (madrasa) dating to the 14th century and rebuilt in the 16th, famous for its carved cedar, stucco, and zellige tilework around a central courtyard.

    • Tickets: Around 50 MAD for adults
    • Hours: Typically 9:00–17:00 daily
    • Tips: Check for restoration closures before visiting; pair with the nearby souks and Almoravid Koubba.

  7. El Badi Palace

    The ruins of a 16th-century palace built by Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur. Though stripped of its former riches, the vast courtyards, sunken gardens, and stork nests on the walls give a sense of scale.

    • Tickets: Around 70 MAD for adults
    • Hours: Typically 9:00–17:00 daily
    • Tips: Bring water and sun protection—there's little shade; combine with the Saadian Tombs next door.

  8. The Souks of the Medina

    A labyrinth of market streets selling leather, textiles, lanterns, spices, and crafts. The souks are free to wander—the experience itself is the attraction—but bring patience and bargaining skills.

    • Tickets: Free to enter
    • Hours: Roughly 9:00–19:00, though individual shops vary; many close on Fridays
    • Tips: Start bargaining around half the asking price; a guide can help you navigate, but agree on a fee first.

  9. Le Jardin Secret

    A restored historic garden complex in the medina with two distinct gardens (exotic and Islamic), a tower with views, and a café. A calm counterpoint to the busy souks.

    • Tickets: Around 80 MAD for adults (tower access may cost extra)
    • Hours: Typically 9:30–18:00 daily
    • Tips: Good midday escape from the heat; check the tower's separate entry fee.

  10. Menara Gardens

    A large olive grove and garden with a central pavilion and reflecting pool, set against the Atlas Mountains. Popular with locals for picnics and evening walks.

    • Tickets: Free (small fee may apply for the pavilion)
    • Hours: Gardens open daily during daylight
    • Tips: Best in late afternoon for mountain views; it's a short taxi ride from the medina.

Practical Tips for Visiting Marrakech

  • Best time to visit: Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer mild temperatures; summer can exceed 40°C.
  • Currency and payments: The Moroccan dirham (MAD) is the currency. Carry cash for souks, tips, and small vendors; larger sites and hotels accept cards.
  • Dress respectfully: Cover shoulders and knees, especially at religious sites and in the medina.
  • Getting around: The medina is best explored on foot. Use petit taxis for longer trips and agree on the fare or ensure the meter is running.
  • Bargaining: Expected in souks; fixed prices apply at museums and gardens.
  • Safety: Marrakech is generally safe for tourists, but watch for pickpockets in crowded areas and avoid unlicensed guides.
  • Book ahead: Popular sites like the Majorelle Garden and YSL Museum sell timed tickets online—reserve in peak season.
  • Hydration and sun: Carry water and sun protection; many sites have limited shade.
