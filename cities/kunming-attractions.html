Top Attractions in Kunming

Kunming, the "Spring City" of Yunnan, blends ancient Buddhist and Taoist heritage with dramatic highland scenery. From bronze temples and thousand-year-old pagodas to forested mountains rising above Dian Lake, the city offers a compact mix of culture and nature. This guide highlights ten standout sights most likely to appeal to international visitors, based on their historical, architectural, or scenic significance.

  1. Golden Temple Park (Jindian Park) — This Taoist temple in the Mingfeng Mountains of Panlong District was built during the late Ming Dynasty and is the largest bronze temple in China, as well as one of the oldest. Its gleaming bronze-tiled halls make it a striking and unusual sight.
  2. Yuantong Temple — An ancient Buddhist temple set in a protected natural depression in Kunming, notable for its expansion funded by Thailand in recent years. In the 1950s it hosted a grand ceremony for the sacred teeth of the Buddha, giving it importance across Southeast Asia.
  3. Qiongzhu Temple (Bamboo Temple) — Situated on Yu'an Mountain northwest of Kunming, this temple was established during the Yuan dynasty as the first Zen Buddhist temple in Yunnan. Though rebuilt several times after fires, its present structures and forested setting remain a peaceful retreat.
  4. Huating Temple — A Buddhist temple located in Xishan District, easily combined with a visit to the surrounding Western Mountains. It is one of the notable religious sites within the Xishan Forest Park area.
  5. Xishan Forest Park — This national forest park about 15 kilometers west of Kunming occupies roughly 442 hectares in the Western Mountains above Dian Lake, with Mt. Luohan rising to 2,507.5 meters. Scenic spots include the Dragon Gate (Long Men), Sanqing Ge, and Huating Temple.
  6. Daguan Park — A lakeside park in the southwestern suburbs where locals gather to sit, drink tea, fly kites, and go boating. Its focal point is Daguan Lou, a square, three-storeyed pavilion built for the Kangxi Emperor's enjoyment of the distant Western Hills and now a storehouse of calligraphy.
  7. Eastern and Western Pagodas — These two pagodas stand about 200 meters apart in Xishan District and were constructed in the late eighth or early ninth century under the Kingdom of Nanzhao. They are among Kunming's oldest surviving monuments.
  8. Yunnan Ethnic Village — This ethnographic village and theme park next to Dian Lake displays the folklore, culture, and architecture of 26 ethnic groups in Yunnan Province. Covering 89 hectares, it offers a concentrated introduction to the region's cultural diversity.
  9. World Horti-Expo Garden — A major botanical garden center that hosted the 1999 Kunming International Horticulture Exposition. It spans 218 hectares and includes five indoor exhibition halls and six theme gardens, making it a significant city landmark.
  10. Dadieshui Waterfall — Located on the Bajiang River in Kunming, this waterfall is 87.8 meters high and 30 meters wide, making it the largest in Yunnan and known as "the first waterfall on Pearl River." It also appeared in the Jackie Chan film The Myth.

Practical tips

  • Visit temples and pagodas in the morning for cooler temperatures and quieter grounds; dress modestly at active religious sites.
  • Allow a full day for Xishan Forest Park and its Dragon Gate, and wear comfortable shoes for the mountain paths.
  • Dadieshui Waterfall lies outside the city center, so plan transport in advance and check water flow conditions before traveling.
  • Combine Daguan Park and the Yunnan Ethnic Village, both near Dian Lake, into a single lakeside day.
  • Kunming sits at about 1,890 meters elevation, so take it easy on your first day and stay hydrated.
