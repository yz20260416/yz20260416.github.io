Shanghai Weather by Month

Shanghai sits on the eastern coast of China at roughly 31°N latitude, giving it a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons. Summers are hot, muggy, and wet, winters are chilly and damp (though snow is rare), and spring and autumn are the most comfortable windows for sightseeing. Because the city is coastal and low-lying, humidity is high year-round, which makes both summer heat and winter cold feel more intense than the thermometer suggests. The guide below breaks down typical temperatures, rainfall, and what each month means for travelers.

Best months to visit: April, May, October, and November offer the most comfortable temperatures, lower humidity, and generally clear skies. March and September are good shoulder-season alternatives, though both can bring abrupt shifts in weather. Travelers seeking lower prices and fewer crowds should consider November through February, keeping in mind the winter chill.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for humidity: Even in cooler months, the damp air makes temperatures feel sharper. Moisture-wicking layers work better than thick single garments.
  • Summer heat and typhoons: July–September can bring temperatures above 35°C (95°F) with high humidity, and August is the peak typhoon month. Check forecasts and build flexibility into outdoor plans.
  • Plum rain season: Late June through mid-July brings persistent drizzle and high humidity. A compact umbrella and quick-dry shoes are essential.
  • Winter comfort: Many buildings lack central heating, so indoor spaces can feel cold. Bring warm indoor layers, and note that January–February is the coldest stretch.
  • Air quality: Winter months occasionally see higher pollution levels. Sensitive travelers may want to monitor AQI readings and carry a mask.
  • Seasonal highlights: Cherry and plum blossoms peak in March; osmanthus blooms scent the city in October; December brings festive lights along Nanjing Road and the Bund.
  • Rainfall reality: Annual precipitation averages around 1,100 mm, concentrated in summer. Rain often arrives in short bursts rather than all-day drizzle, so a rain jacket beats an umbrella in windy conditions.
