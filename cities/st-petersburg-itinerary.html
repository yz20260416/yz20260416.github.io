St. Petersburg 3-Day Itinerary

St. Petersburg, Russia's imperial capital on the Neva River, packs more than 200 museums, dozens of palaces, and a UNESCO-listed historic center into a compact, walkable core. Three days is enough to see the essentials — the Hermitage, Peterhof, the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood — without rushing, provided you plan around opening days, book timed tickets in advance, and cluster sights by neighborhood. This itinerary assumes you're staying in the historic center (Nevsky Prospekt area), traveling on foot and by metro, and visiting between May and September, when the White Nights bring long daylight hours. In winter, shift outdoor blocks earlier and swap in more indoor museums.

Day 1 — Imperial Heart: Hermitage, Palace Square & the Neva

TimeActivityDetails
9:30 Palace Square & Alexander Column Start with the grandest square in Russia. Arrive before the Hermitage opens for unobstructed photos of the Winter Palace facade.
10:00–14:00 State Hermitage Museum Book a timed entry online (closed Mondays). Focus on the State Rooms, Rembrandt, the Italian Renaissance (Leonardo, Raphael), and the Gold Rooms if pre-booked. Four hours is a solid first pass — don't try to see everything.
14:00–15:00 Lunch near Palace Square Palkin (upscale Russian classics, Nevsky Prospekt 47) or Stolovaya #1 for a fast, budget-friendly canteen-style meal.
15:15–16:30 Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood Walk 15 minutes along the Griboyedov Canal. The mosaic interior is the highlight — allow 45–60 minutes. Closed Wednesdays.
16:45–18:00 Russian Museum (optional) or Mikhailovsky Garden If energy permits, the Russian Museum next door holds the best collection of Russian art. Otherwise, stroll the adjacent gardens.
19:00 Dinner Teplo (cozy, reliable Russian-European, Bolshaya Morskaya 45) or Mansarda for rooftop views over St. Isaac's Cathedral.
21:00 Evening walk: Nevsky Prospekt & Kazan Cathedral In summer, take a late Neva river boat cruise from the Admiralteyskaya embankment to see the city under the White Nights sky.

Day 2 — Peterhof, St. Isaac's & the Bronze Horseman

TimeActivityDetails
8:30 Depart for Peterhof Take the Meteor hydrofoil from the Admiralteyskaya embankment (≈30–40 min, most scenic) or the suburban train from Baltiysky Station to Novy Peterhof plus a short bus. Book hydrofoil tickets ahead in high season.
9:30–13:00 Peterhof Palace & Grand Cascade The fountains switch on at 11:00 — time your arrival for the spectacle. The Grand Palace requires a separate timed ticket; the Lower Park alone justifies the trip. Closed Mondays.
13:00–14:00 Lunch at Peterhof Casual park cafés near the Lower Park; pack snacks as options are limited and pricey.
14:30–15:30 Return to the city Hydrofoil back to the center, or train to Baltiysky Station.
16:00–17:15 St. Isaac's Cathedral & colonnade Climb the colonnade for the best panorama of the historic center. Closed Wednesdays.
17:30–18:15 Bronze Horseman & Decembrists' Square Walk through Alexander Garden to Falconet's famous equestrian statue of Peter the Great on the Neva bank.
19:00 Dinner Restoran (modern Russian, Dumskaya 2) or Gogol (literary-themed Russian cooking, Malaya Morskaya 8).
20:30 Optional: Mariinsky Theatre Book ballet or opera tickets well in advance — a quintessential St. Petersburg evening.
