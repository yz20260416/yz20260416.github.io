Local Character

Lisbon's identity is layered by successive settlers and rulers — pre-Celtic tribes, Phoenician founders, Romans who made it the municipium of Felicitas Julia, Visigoths, and Moors — before Afonso Henriques conquered it in 1147 and it replaced Coimbra as Portugal's capital in 1255. A visitor walking its old quarters is moving through that chronology rather than reading about it. The city's name itself likely stems from Proto-Celtic Olisippo or Lissoppo, adapted by Phoenician, Greek and Roman visitors, and linked to the pre-Roman name for the Tagus, Lisso or Lucio; knowing this turns the riverfront from scenery into the root of the city's very name. Because Lisbon has been the political, economic and cultural centre of Portugal since the 13th century, its daily life carries the weight of a national capital rather than a provincial port — something felt in its institutions, its diplomatic presence and its role as home to major Portuguese companies. The city's position fronting the Tagus on the western Iberian Peninsula shaped its outlook toward the Atlantic and the wider world.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. River Tagus waterfront — The northern shore of the Tagus defines Lisbon's geography and is best explored on foot along its banks.
  2. Historic centre — The old quarters preserve layers of Phoenician, Roman, Visigothic and Moorish settlement beneath later Portuguese building.
  3. Castelo de São Jorge area — The hill associated with Afonso Henriques's 1147 conquest offers a vantage over the city he took from the Moors.
  4. Baixa and the 1255 capital legacy — The central districts reflect Lisbon's role as Portugal's capital since 1255, replacing Coimbra.
  5. Belém riverside — The western riverside district points toward the Atlantic, the waterway that shaped Lisbon's maritime identity.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Euro (EUR, €)

  • Time Zone: WET (UTC+0), WEST (UTC+1) in summer
  • Visa: Citizens of the EU, EEA and many other countries can enter Portugal visa-free for short stays, while other nationalities should check Schengen visa requirements before travelling.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Lisbon?
May and September offer mild temperatures, low rainfall and manageable crowds, making them the strongest months for sightseeing.
How old is Lisbon?
It is one of the world's oldest cities and the second-oldest European capital after Athens, with settlement layers from pre-Celtic, Phoenician, Roman, Visigothic and Moorish periods.
What currency and language should I expect?
Portugal uses the euro, and the local language is Portuguese, though English is common in tourist areas.
