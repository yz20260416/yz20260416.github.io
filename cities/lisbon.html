Best Time to Visit Lisbon

Lisbon is Portugal's hilly, sun-washed capital, known for its tiled facades, historic trams, and pastel de nata. Visit in spring or early autumn for mild weather and manageable crowds, though summer brings lively festivals and warm beach days nearby.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Belém Tower — A 16th-century Manueline fortress on the Tagus waterfront that once guarded Lisbon's harbor.
  2. Jerónimos Monastery — A UNESCO-listed monastery famed for its ornate Manueline cloisters and maritime-themed stonework.
  3. Alfama District — Lisbon's oldest quarter, a maze of steep cobbled lanes, tiled houses, and fado bars below the castle.
  4. São Jorge Castle — A hilltop Moorish castle offering sweeping views over the city and the Tagus estuary.
  5. Tram 28 — A vintage yellow tram that rattles through the historic neighborhoods of Graça, Alfama, and Estrela.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Euro (EUR, €)
  • Language: Portuguese (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: WET (UTC+0), WEST (UTC+1) in summer
  • Visa: EU, EEA, and many Western nationals can visit visa-free for up to 90 days, while other travelers should check Schengen visa requirements in advance.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Lisbon?
Three to four days covers the main sights, with an extra day for a day trip to Sintra or Cascais.
Is Lisbon expensive to visit?
Lisbon is moderately priced for Western Europe, with affordable meals, transit, and accommodation outside peak summer.
How do I get around Lisbon?
The metro, trams, funiculars, and buses cover the city well, and a rechargeable Viva Viagem card is the easiest way to pay.
