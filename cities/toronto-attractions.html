Toronto Top 10 Attractions

Toronto is Canada's largest city and one of the most multicultural destinations in the world, blending world-class museums, iconic architecture, waterfront green space, and vibrant neighbourhoods. Whether you have two days or a full week, these ten attractions represent the essential Toronto experience — from the soaring heights of the CN Tower to the historic lanes of the Distillery District. Below you'll find descriptions, current ticket prices (in Canadian dollars, subject to change), and practical tips to help you plan efficiently and avoid common pitfalls. Prices reflect adult general admission as of the most recent season; always confirm on official websites before booking.

Detailed Descriptions

1. CN Tower

Location: 290 Bremner Blvd (downtown core)  |  Ticket: ~$43–$48 adult; combo tickets with Ripley's Aquarium save money

Once the world's tallest free-standing structure, the 553-metre CN Tower remains Toronto's defining landmark. The LookOut Level (346 m) offers panoramic views of the city and Lake Ontario, while the Glass Floor lets visitors stand above a 342-metre drop. Thrill-seekers can add the EdgeWalk, an outdoor walk around the tower's exterior ledge, and the 360 Restaurant rotates slowly above the city.

Tips: Book timed-entry tickets online to skip queues. Visit at sunset for the best light and fewer crowds; clear-day visibility can reach 160 km. Skip the tower on hazy or overcast days.

2. Royal Ontario Museum (ROM)

Location: 100 Queens Park  |  Ticket: ~$26–$30 adult; discounts for students/seniors; free for members

Canada's largest museum of art, culture, and natural history, the ROM houses more than 13 million objects across galleries covering dinosaurs, ancient Egypt, Indigenous cultures, minerals, and world art. The striking Michael Lee-Chin Crystal addition is an attraction in itself.

Tips: Allow at least half a day — the museum is enormous. Check the website for Friday-evening or discounted admission windows. The dinosaur gallery is a hit with kids.

3. Art Gallery of Ontario (AGO)

Location: 317 Dundas St W  |  Ticket: ~$30 adult; free for visitors under 25 (annual pass program)

One of North America's largest art museums, the AGO holds a celebrated collection of Canadian and Indigenous art, European masterworks, and the world's largest public collection of Henry Moore sculptures. The Frank Gehry–designed renovation made the building itself a draw.

Tips: Wednesday evenings often feature reduced or free admission — verify current offers. Combine with a stroll through nearby Chinatown or Kensington Market.

4. Ripley's Aquarium of Canada

Location: 288 Bremner Blvd (base of the CN Tower)  |  Ticket: ~$44–$48 adult; combo deals with CN Tower available

Home to sharks, rays, jellyfish, and a mesmerizing Dangerous Lagoon tunnel, Ripley's is one of Canada's most popular indoor attractions and a perfect rainy-day option. Interactive touch pools and a Pacific kelp forest exhibit appeal to all ages.

Tips: Book online and arrive at opening to avoid school-group crowds. A CN Tower + Aquarium combo ticket is significantly cheaper than buying separately.

5. St. Lawrence Market

Location: 93 Front St E (Old Town)  |  Ticket: Free entry
