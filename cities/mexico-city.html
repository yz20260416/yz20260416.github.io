Best Time to Visit Mexico City

Mexico City is a sprawling high-altitude capital where Aztec ruins, colonial architecture, and world-class museums sit alongside vibrant street food and markets. At 2,240 meters elevation, its mild, spring-like climate makes it a year-round destination, though the dry months from November to April offer the clearest skies and most comfortable sightseeing.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Zócalo and Templo Mayor — The vast main square is flanked by the Metropolitan Cathedral and the excavated Aztec temple ruins at its edge.
  2. Museo Nacional de Antropología — Mexico's premier museum houses the Aztec Sun Stone and an unrivaled collection of pre-Hispanic artifacts.
  3. Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul) — The cobalt-blue Coyoacán home where the artist lived and worked displays her personal belongings and paintings.
  4. Chapultepec Park and Castle — A huge urban park with museums, a lake, and a hilltop castle offering sweeping city views.
  5. Teotihuacán — The monumental ancient city of pyramids lies just outside the capital and is easily visited on a day trip.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Mexican Peso (MXN, $)
  • Language: Spanish (English is common in tourist areas and upscale hotels)
  • Time Zone: CST (UTC-6)
  • Visa: Most international visitors receive a 180-day tourist permit on arrival, but check your nationality's specific requirements before travel.

FAQ

Is Mexico City safe for tourists?
Main tourist areas like Roma, Condesa, Polanco, and Coyoacán are generally safe by day and night, though normal big-city precautions against pickpocketing and unlicensed taxis apply.
How does the altitude affect visitors?
At 2,240 meters, mild altitude sickness is possible, so take it easy on arrival, stay hydrated, and limit alcohol for the first day or two.
What is the best way to get around?
Use ride-hailing apps or authorized taxis for safety and convenience, and the inexpensive Metro for longer trips during daylight hours.
