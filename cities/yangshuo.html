Local Character

Yangshuo's character is shaped by the karst landscape and the Li River, which have supported farming and fishing communities for generations. A tradition most visitors miss is cormorant fishing, practiced on the Li and Yulong rivers for over a thousand years: fishermen tie a ring around a cormorant's neck so it can catch fish but not swallow them, then haul the bird back to retrieve its catch. Though largely a demonstration today, it reveals how locals adapted to a river economy rather than merely posing for tourists. The town also sits in a Zhuang and Han cultural borderland; nearby villages keep stilt-house architecture and rice-cultivation customs tied to the terraced fields. Visitors should care because understanding this working-river heritage changes how you see the scenery: the peaks are not just a backdrop but a livelihood. Wander away from West Street into villages like Xingping or the Yulong River valley, where farmers still dry grain on stone courtyards and elders gather at riverside pavilions, and the region's rhythm becomes legible beyond the postcard view.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Li River Cruise (Yangshuo to Xingping) — A scenic boat ride past the most famous karst peaks, including the scene printed on the 20-yuan note.
  2. Yulong River — A quieter tributary ideal for bamboo rafting and cycling through rice paddies and village lanes.
  3. Xingping Old Town — A small riverside town with Ming-era streets and panoramic viewpoints over the Li River bend.
  4. Moon Hill — A limestone arch reached by a steep stair climb, rewarding hikers with sweeping valley views.
  5. West Street — Yangshuo's historic main lane, now lined with cafes, shops, and guesthouses mixing old facades with tourist bustle.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Chinese Yuan (CNY, ¥)
  • Language: Mandarin Chinese (English is common in tourist areas and hotels)
  • Time Zone: CST (UTC+8)
  • Visa: Most international visitors need a Chinese visa arranged in advance, though some nationalities qualify for visa-free transit or short-stay entry schemes.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Yangshuo?
Three to four days lets you cruise the Li River, cycle the Yulong River, climb Moon Hill, and explore Xingping without rushing.
What is the best way to get around Yangshuo?
Bicycles and e-bikes are the best way to reach countryside villages, while taxis and shuttle buses connect the town with the Li River docks and Xingping.
When is the best time to visit Yangshuo?
April to May and September to October offer the most comfortable temperatures and clear river views, though spring and summer bring the greenest rice terraces.
