Top Attractions in Paris

Paris draws around 30 million foreign visitors a year, and its appeal lies in grand monuments, riverside promenades and lively historic quarters. From the sweep of the Champs-Élysées to the medieval halls of the Conciergerie, the city rewards both first-time visitors and returning travellers. The ten highlights below are drawn from across the arrondissements and pair iconic sights with a few less-obvious stops.

  1. Champs-Élysées
    This 1.9-kilometre avenue runs between Place de la Concorde and Place Charles de Gaulle, where the Arc de Triomphe stands, and is known for its theatres, cafés and luxury shops. It also serves as the finish line of the Tour de France.
    Tip: Walk it early in the morning for fewer crowds, and climb the Arc de Triomphe for the classic view straight down the avenue.
  2. Place de la Concorde
    At 7.6 hectares, this is the largest square in Paris, sitting at the eastern end of the Champs-Élysées. Originally the Place Louis XV, it was the site of notable public executions during the Revolution, including those of Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette.
    Tip: Combine it with a walk through the Tuileries Garden toward the Louvre; the fountains are especially photogenic in late afternoon light.
  3. Conciergerie
    A former courthouse and prison on the Île de la Cité, originally part of the royal Palais de la Cité, with two large medieval halls surviving from the palace. During the French Revolution, 2,781 prisoners including Marie Antoinette were held, tried and sentenced here.
    Tip: Book tickets online and visit in the morning; the same ticket often covers the neighbouring Sainte-Chapelle, so allow extra time.
  4. Bouquinistes of the Seine
    These booksellers of used and antiquarian books and vintage postcards line the banks of the Seine, from Pont Marie to the Quai du Louvre on the right bank and from the Quai de la Tournelle to Quai Voltaire on the left. The river is famously described as "the only river in the world that runs between two bookshelves."
    Tip: Stroll the Left Bank stretch at sunset, when the stalls are open and the light on the river is best.
  5. Bateaux Mouches
    These long, open, often glass-covered excursion boats give visitors a view of central Paris from along the Seine, and also operate on canals such as the partly subterranean Canal Saint-Martin.
    Tip: Take an evening departure to see the monuments illuminated; arrive early to secure an open-air seat on the upper deck.
  6. Ballon Generali
    A tethered helium balloon installed in the Parc André-Citroën since 1999, used both as a tourist attraction and as an air-quality awareness tool. It has lifted more than 500,000 passengers into the sky since opening.
    Tip: Flights depend on weather and wind, so check conditions on the day; the 15th-arrondissement park is a pleasant place to wait.
  7. Avenue George V
    This avenue in the 8th arrondissement runs from Place de l'Alma to 99 avenue des Champs-Élysées and marks the western limit of Paris's "golden triangle." It was renamed in 1918 in honour of the British monarch George V.
    Tip: Pair a walk here with the nearby Champs-Élysées and the Seine bridges at Alma; it is a good area for window-shopping and architecture.
  8. Café des 2 Moulins
    A café in Montmartre at the junction of Rue Lepic and Rue Cauchois, named after the nearby Moulin Rouge and Moulin de la Galette. It gained considerable fame from its appearance in the 2001 film Amélie.
    Tip: Visit mid-afternoon to avoid the lunch rush, then wander Rue Lepic up toward the Sacré-Cœur.
  9. Cent Quatre
    A public cultural centre that opened in 2008 on the site of a former municipal undertaker's at 104 rue d'Aubervilliers in the 19th arrondissement. It hosts exhibitions, performances and artist residencies in a vast industrial space.
    Tip: Check the programme online before visiting, as events vary; it pairs well with a walk along the nearby Canal de l'Ourcq.
  10. Cité de la mode et du design
    A building on the site of the old general storehouses on the Quai d'Austerlitz in the 13th arrondissement, opened to the public in 2010 and home to the Institut Français de la Mode. Its riverside setting and design-focused programme make it a modern counterpoint to the city's historic sights.
    Tip: Combine it with a stroll along the Seine's Left Bank in the 13th, and check opening hours as some spaces are tied to exhibitions.

Practical tips

  • Buy museum and monument tickets online in advance to skip queues, especially at the Conciergerie and the Arc de Triomphe.
  • Start major sights like the Champs-Élysées and Place de la Concorde early in the morning to avoid peak crowds.
  • Use the Seine as your route: walking the quays links the Bouquinistes, Bateaux Mouches departures and many central landmarks.
  • Weather-dependent attractions such as the Ballon Generali can be cancelled at short notice, so keep a flexible plan.
  • Allow a full afternoon for neighbourhoods like Montmartre and the 13th arrondissement, where the best experiences are unplanned strolls.
