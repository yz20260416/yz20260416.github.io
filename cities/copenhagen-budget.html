Copenhagen Travel Cost Guide

Copenhagen is one of Scandinavia's most captivating capitals — famous for its harbor baths, cycling culture, New Nordic cuisine, and the fairy-tale charm of Tivoli Gardens. But it's also known as one of Europe's pricier destinations. The good news? With smart planning, Copenhagen can fit a range of budgets. This guide breaks down realistic daily costs across accommodation, food, transport, and activities for three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan with confidence. All figures are in Danish kroner (DKK) with approximate USD/EUR equivalents; note that 1 USD ≈ 6.9 DKK and 1 EUR ≈ 7.5 DKK (rates fluctuate).

Accommodation Breakdown

  • Budget: Hostels such as Generator or Copenhagen Downtown Hostel charge roughly 150–350 DKK per dorm bed. Budget guesthouses and Airbnb rooms in outer neighborhoods (Nørrebro, Amager) run 400–600 DKK.
  • Mid-range: Central 3–4★ hotels (e.g., near Vesterbro or the lakes) typically cost 700–1,400 DKK per night. Book 4–8 weeks ahead for the best rates, especially in summer.
  • Luxury: Five-star properties like Hotel d'Angleterre or Nimb Hotel start around 2,000 DKK and climb well beyond 5,000 DKK for suites. Expect premium pricing during design fairs and holidays.

Food & Drink Breakdown

  • Budget: Supermarkets (Netto, Rema 1000, Lidl) are your friend — a self-catered day costs 100–150 DKK. Street food at Reffen or Torvehallerne runs 60–100 DKK per dish; a budget hot dog is ~35 DKK.
  • Mid-range: Lunch specials (dagens ret) at cafés cost 90–150 DKK; dinner at a casual bistro runs 150–250 DKK per main. A beer at a bar is 50–70 DKK; coffee 35–50 DKK.
  • Luxury: Copenhagen's Michelin-starred scene (Noma, Geranium, Kadeau) offers tasting menus from 1,500–3,500 DKK per person before wine. Even upscale mid-tier dinners can reach 500–800 DKK per person.

Transport Breakdown

  • Budget: Copenhagen is exceptionally walkable and bike-friendly. Rent a bike for ~100–150 DKK/day or use the city's Donkey Republic app. Single-zone metro/bus tickets cost 24 DKK; a 24-hour City Pass Small is 80 DKK.
  • Mid-range: A 72-hour City Pass Small (200 DKK) covers unlimited metro, bus, and harbor bus travel in the central zones — ideal for museum-hopping.
