Huangshan: The Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Huangshan (Yellow Mountain) is one of China's most celebrated landscapes, famous for its granite peaks, twisted pine trees, sea of clouds, and hot springs. Located in Anhui Province, the scenic area sits at roughly 1,800 meters at its highest developed points and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This 3-day guide balances the classic mountain highlights with the charming nearby villages, with morning, afternoon, and evening schedules, restaurant suggestions, and practical advice to help you plan a smooth trip.

Day 1: Arrival and the Mountain's Eastern Approach

Time Activity
Morning Arrive at Huangshan Tunxi International Airport or Huangshan North Railway Station. Transfer by bus or taxi (about 1 hour) to the Tangkou transfer center at the mountain's base. Store large luggage at your hotel or the transfer center; bring only a small overnight pack up the mountain.
Afternoon Take the shuttle bus to the Yungu (Cloud Valley) Cableway and ride up to the White Goose Ridge area. Check into your mountain hotel (options include the Beihai Hotel and Shilin Hotel). Begin an easy walk to the Beginning-to-Believe Peak and the Black Tiger Pine, two classic viewpoints near the Beihai area.
Evening Watch sunset from the Refreshing Terrace (Qingliangtai) or a nearby west-facing viewpoint. Dinner at your hotel restaurant — mountain hotel buffets are convenient but pricey; expect simple Anhui-style dishes such as bamboo shoots and preserved vegetables.

Restaurant notes: Mountain hotels serve set meals and buffets (roughly 80–150 CNY per person). For better value and variety, eat a hearty lunch in Tangkou before ascending — local restaurants near the transfer center serve Hui cuisine specialties like stinky mandarin fish (臭鳜鱼) and hairy tofu.

Day 2: Sunrise, Grand Canyon, and the Western Peaks

Time Activity
Morning Rise early (check the posted sunrise time, typically 5:00–6:30 depending on season) and walk to Dawn Pavilion (Shuguang Ting) or the Refreshing Terrace for sunrise. Return for breakfast, then hike to the West Sea Grand Canyon. The full loop, including the steep steps and the monorail option, takes 3–4 hours. The canyon's sculpted cliffs and pine-clad ravines are a highlight of the trip.
Afternoon Continue to the Flying-Over Stone and the Bright Summit (Guangming Ding), at 1,860 meters one of the highest points in the developed area. On clear days the panorama extends across dozens of named peaks. Rest at the summit area or descend toward the Yuping (Jade Screen) area if time allows.
Evening Return to your hotel for dinner and rest. If you are staying near the Jade Screen area, the sunset view toward the Lotus Peak is excellent. Keep an eye on weather forecasts — cloud inversions often form after rain.

Restaurant notes: The Baiyun Hotel near the Bright Summit has a restaurant with decent stir-fried dishes; the Shilin Hotel is another reliable option. Packed lunches and instant noodles are available at small shops along the trails at marked-up prices.

Day 3: Jade Screen, Greeting-Guest Pine, and Hongcun Village

Time Activity
Morning Hike down (or take the Yuping Cableway) toward the Jade Screen area to see the iconic Greeting-Guest Pine (Yingke Song), the Welcome Pine that appears on countless postcards. Visit the nearby Lotus Peak trailhead if open (seasonal closures apply). Descend via the Yuping Cableway or on foot via the Western Steps, which take about 2.5–3 hours.
Afternoon Return to Tangkou by shuttle bus, retrieve your luggage, and transfer by bus or taxi (about 40 minutes) to Hongcun, a UNESCO-listed Ming- and Qing-era village. Wander the white-walled, black-tiled lanes, the Moon Pond, and the South Lake. The village is compact and easily explored in 2–3 hours.
Evening Stay overnight in Hongcun or return to Tunxi. Dinner in Hongcun features Hui cuisine at family-run guesthouses; try the bamboo shoot stew and locally brewed rice wine. Evening strolls along the Moon Pond are atmospheric once tour groups depart.

Restaurant notes: In Hongcun, guesthouse kitchens such as those along the South Lake serve home-style Hui dishes. In Tunxi, the historic Old Street (Lao Jie) has restaurants serving stinky mandarin fish and fried hairy tofu — a good final meal before departure.

Practical Tips

  • Best seasons: Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–November) offer the clearest skies and mild temperatures. Summer is crowded and humid; winter is quiet but icy, and some trails close.
  • Book mountain hotels in advance: Accommodation on the summit is limited and fills up, especially on weekends and holidays. Prices are high for basic rooms.
  • Pack light but smart: Bring layers (temperatures drop sharply at altitude), rain gear, sturdy shoes, a headlamp for sunrise hikes, and cash for small vendors.
  • Cableways: The Yungu, Yuping, and Taiping cableways save hours of climbing. Check operating hours and maintenance closures before you go.
  • Weather: The "sea of clouds" is most likely after rain and in early morning. Check the forecast and be flexible with your schedule.
  • Physical fitness: Trails involve thousands of stone steps; pace yourself, use trekking poles, and avoid overpacking.
  • Transport: Shuttle buses connect Tangkou to the cableway stations. High-speed trains serve Huangshan North Station, with direct connections to Shanghai and Hangzhou.
  • Etiquette and safety: Stay on marked paths, do not feed wildlife, and carry out your trash. Smoking is restricted on the mountain.
  • Language: English signage is limited; download a translation app and save key place names in Chinese characters.
