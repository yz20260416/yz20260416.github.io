Jiuzhaigou Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Jiuzhaigou Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Sichuan Province, China, is famous for its turquoise lakes, multi-tiered waterfalls, and colorful forests. The park is shaped like a Y, with three main valleys: Shuzheng, Rize, and Zechawa. Most visitors spend one full day inside the park, using the shuttle bus system to cover the highlights. Below are the top 10 must-see attractions, with practical details to help you plan your visit.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions

  1. Five Flower Lake (Wuhua Hai)

    One of Jiuzhaigou's most photographed spots, this shallow lake is known for its vivid blues, greens, and yellows created by submerged fallen trees and mineral-rich water. The colors are most intense in the morning light.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket (see Practical Tips for park pricing).
    • Opening hours: Same as park hours, typically 07:00–18:00 (varies by season).
    • Visiting tips: Arrive early to avoid crowds and catch the best reflections. The lake is a short walk from the shuttle stop on the Rize Valley route.
  2. Long Lake (Chang Hai)

    The largest and deepest lake in Jiuzhaigou, sitting at an elevation of about 3,100 meters. Its deep blue water is framed by snow-capped mountains, and it is a popular starting point for exploring Zechawa Valley.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Bring warm layers, as temperatures here are cooler than in the lower valleys. The shuttle bus from the park entrance goes directly here.
  3. Nuorilang Waterfall

    A wide, curtain-like waterfall about 320 meters wide and 24 meters high, located at the junction of the three valleys. It is one of the widest high-altitude waterfalls in China and a convenient transfer point for shuttle buses.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Best viewed from the viewing platform near the shuttle stop. Combine with a visit to nearby Shuzheng Valley sites.
  4. Pearl Shoal Waterfall (Zhenzhu Tan)

    A dramatic waterfall where water cascades over a wide, terraced cliff, creating a curtain of white spray. It is one of the most powerful waterfalls in the park and a favorite for photographers.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Visit in the late morning for good light. The walkway from the shuttle stop offers multiple viewpoints.
  5. Mirror Lake (Jing Hai)

    Known for its glassy surface that reflects the surrounding mountains and sky, Mirror Lake is especially beautiful on calm, clear mornings. It sits in Rize Valley near Five Flower Lake.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Go early before wind ripples the water. The reflection is best on still days.
  6. Shuzheng Lakes and Waterfalls

    A series of terraced lakes and small waterfalls in Shuzheng Valley, including the well-known Shuzheng Waterfall. The area is easy to explore on foot and is close to the park entrance.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: A good area for a relaxed walk. Combine with a visit to the nearby Tibetan village.
  7. Panda Lake (Xiongmao Hai)

    A small, colorful lake named after the giant pandas that once lived in the area. Its clear water and surrounding forest make it a peaceful stop in Rize Valley.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Walk the boardwalk between Panda Lake and Five Flower Lake for a scenic connection.
  8. Arrow Bamboo Lake (Jian Zhu Hai)

    A large, deep-blue lake surrounded by arrow bamboo, which was used in the film "Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon." The lake is a highlight of the Rize Valley route.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Stop here on the way to or from Five Flower Lake. The boardwalk offers good views of the lake and surrounding peaks.
  9. Colorful Pond (Wucai Chi)

    A small, intensely colored pond near Long Lake in Zechawa Valley. Its vivid greens and blues come from mineral deposits and algae, and it is often less crowded than the main lakes.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Combine with Long Lake, as they are close together. The walk between them is short and scenic.
  10. Tibetan Villages (Zharu and Shuzheng)

    Traditional Tibetan villages inside the park offer a glimpse of local culture, with wooden houses, prayer flags, and small shops. They are a good place to rest and learn about the area's heritage.

    • Ticket: Included in the main park ticket; food and souvenirs cost extra.
    • Opening hours: Park hours, typically 07:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Respect local customs and ask before taking photos of residents. Try local snacks such as yak meat and butter tea.

Practical Tips

  • Park ticket: The entrance ticket is typically around CNY 190 in peak season and CNY 80 in low season, with a separate shuttle bus fee (about CNY 90 peak, CNY 80 low). Prices change by season and policy, so confirm current rates before you go.
  • Opening hours: The park generally opens around 07:00 and closes around 18:00, but hours vary by season. Check official notices before your visit.
  • Best time to visit: Autumn (October–November) offers the most colorful foliage, while spring and summer are greener and less crowded. Winter has fewer visitors but some areas may be restricted.
  • Altitude: Elevations range from about 2,000 to 3,100 meters. Take it slow, stay hydrated, and avoid strenuous activity on your first day.
  • Getting around: Use the shuttle bus system to cover long distances, then walk the boardwalks between nearby attractions.
