Jiuzhaigou Valley: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Jiuzhaigou Valley (九寨沟), a UNESCO World Heritage Site in northern Sichuan Province, China, is famous for its terraced travertine lakes, cascading waterfalls, and colorful forests. The valley is shaped like a Y, with three main branches: Shuzheng Valley (entrance to Nuorilang), Rize Valley (Nuorilang to Long Lake area), and Zechawa Valley (Nuorilang to Long Lake). Visitors travel by eco-bus between stops and walk along boardwalks. The following ten attractions are the park's highlights, listed roughly in the order most visitors encounter them from the entrance.

  1. Shuzheng Lakes (树正群海)

    A chain of terraced lakes and small waterfalls near the park entrance, framed by Tibetan-style water mills and prayer wheels. It offers an easy introduction to Jiuzhaigou's signature turquoise water and travertine dams.

    Tickets: Included in park admission (peak season approx. CNY 190 + mandatory eco-bus CNY 90; off-season approx. CNY 80 + bus CNY 80 — confirm current prices before travel).

    Hours: Park typically open 07:00–18:00 in peak season (Apr–Nov) and 08:30–17:00 off-season; last entry is earlier than closing.

    Tips: Visit early morning for calm water and fewer crowds; walk the boardwalk between Shuzheng and Sparkling Lake for the best views.

  2. Nuorilang Waterfall (诺日朗瀑布)

    One of China's widest high-altitude travertine waterfalls (about 270 m wide, 20 m high), located at the junction of the valley's three branches. A classic photo stop and a natural transfer point for eco-buses.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Best flow after summer rains (July–September); the viewing platform fills quickly at midday, so visit before 10:00 or after 15:00.

  3. Five Flower Lake (五花海)

    Often called the most beautiful lake in Jiuzhaigou, with submerged fallen trees visible through water that shifts between emerald, turquoise, and gold. The surrounding forest is spectacular in autumn.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Climb to the upper viewing point on the hillside trail for the panoramic view; arrive early as this is the busiest stop in Rize Valley.

  4. Pearl Shoal Waterfall (珍珠滩瀑布)

    A broad, curtain-like waterfall where water tumbles over a shallow travertine shoal, scattering like pearls. It gained fame as a filming location for the 1986 TV adaptation of Journey to the West.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Walk the loop trail from Pearl Shoal down to the waterfall base; the boardwalk can be slippery, so wear grippy shoes.

  5. Mirror Lake (镜海)

    A still lake that reflects the surrounding mountains and forest like a mirror, especially in the early morning before wind picks up.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Go at opening time (before 9:00) for the clearest reflections; it is a short walk from the Nuorilang area.

  6. Long Lake (长海)

    The largest and deepest lake in the park, sitting at about 3,100 m elevation at the head of Zechawa Valley. Its deep blue water is backed by snow-capped peaks.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: It is the highest major stop, so pace yourself and bring a layer — it is noticeably colder here. Combine with Five-Color Pond on the walk down.

  7. Five-Color Pond (五彩池)

    A small but intensely colored pond near Long Lake, fed by mineral-rich water that produces vivid blues and greens even in winter.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Reachable via a short but steep staircase from the Long Lake area; take your time at altitude and photograph from the lower viewing deck.

  8. Arrow Bamboo Lake (箭竹海)

    A serene lake surrounded by arrow bamboo, with clear reflections and a boardwalk loop. It is also associated with scenes from the film Hero.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: The full boardwalk loop takes about 40–60 minutes; it is a good spot to escape midday crowds in Rize Valley.

  9. Panda Lake (熊猫海)

    Named for the giant pandas once said to visit its shores, this deep blue-green lake sits below a cliff and connects to Panda Lake Falls.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Combine with Arrow Bamboo Lake on foot via the connecting trail; water color is richest on sunny mornings.

  10. Zechawa Valley & Seasonal Lakes (季节海)

    The Zechawa branch offers quieter scenery, including the Seasonal Lakes that swell in the wet season and shrink in dry months, plus Tibetan village viewpoints along the way.

    Tickets: Included in park admission.

    Hours: Same as park hours.

    Tips: Fewer visitors ride this branch — a good option for a calmer afternoon; check with park staff which stops are open, as some close seasonally.

Practical Tips for Visiting Jiuzhaigou

  • Book ahead: Entry is capped daily; reserve tickets online (official WeChat mini-program or authorized platforms) and bring your passport, which is required for foreign visitors.
  • Best seasons: Autumn (late September–October) for foliage color; summer for full waterfalls; winter is quiet and cheaper but some trails and lakes may be closed.
  • Altitude: The park ranges from about 2,000 m to over 3,100 m. Move slowly, stay hydrated, and avoid strenuous climbing on your first day.
  • Getting around: Eco-buses are included in the mandatory bus ticket; ride uphill to the farthest stops first, then walk downhill between attractions.
  • What to bring: Sun protection, rain gear, warm layers, comfortable walking shoes, and cash/phone payment for food at visitor centers.
  • Access: Fly to Jiuzhai Huanglong Airport (about 1.5–2 hours by road) or take a long-distance bus from Chengdu (roughly 8–9 hours).
  • Rules: Stay on boardwalks, do not touch or swim in the water, and carry out trash — the ecosystem is fragile and strictly protected.
