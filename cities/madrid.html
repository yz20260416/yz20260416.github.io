Local Character

Madrid's character was forged when it became the permanent seat of the Hispanic Monarchy's court in 1561, and that courtly legacy still shapes daily life: the city functions as an administrative and ceremonial centre, and its central plazas and official buildings reflect centuries of state-building around the monarchy. A visitor should care because the city's identity is inseparable from its role as a seat of power, which explains the density of monumental architecture and museums in its core. The city's origins are even older: its primitive core began as a walled military outpost in the late 9th century under the Emirate of Córdoba, before being conquered by Christians in the 1080s. That layered past rewards travellers who look beyond the main squares. Madrid is also a centre for Spanish linguistic prescriptivism through the Royal Spanish Academy, so the Spanish spoken and written here carries a certain authority — a detail language-minded visitors will appreciate. The city's cultural calendar leans heavily on fairs such as FITUR, ARCO, SIMO TCI and Madrid Fashion Week, while football loyalties split between Real Madrid and Atlético Madrid, a rivalry that colours everyday conversation in bars and on terraces.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Prado Museum — Spain's flagship art museum and the anchor of the UNESCO-listed Golden Triangle of Art along the Paseo del Prado.
  2. Reina Sofía Museum — A modern art museum forming the second point of the Golden Triangle of Art.
  3. Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum — The third museum of the Golden Triangle, complementing the holdings of the Prado and Reina Sofía.
  4. Royal Palace of Madrid — The city's principal royal landmark and one of Madrid's best-known sights.
  5. Buen Retiro Park — A large park founded in 1631 and one of the city's most beloved green spaces.

FAQ

What is the Golden Triangle of Art?
It is a UNESCO World Heritage grouping of three art museums along the Paseo del Prado: the Prado, the Reina Sofía and the Thyssen-Bornemisza.
When did Madrid become Spain's capital?
Madrid became the permanent seat of the court of the Hispanic Monarchy in 1561, which drove its development as an administrative centre.
How high is Madrid above sea level?
The city lies at about 660 metres (2,170 ft) above mean sea level on the River Manzanares.
