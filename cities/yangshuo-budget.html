Yangshuo Travel Budget Guide

Yangshuo, set amid the dramatic karst peaks of Guangxi, China, is one of the country's most popular destinations for scenery, cycling, rock climbing, and river trips on the Li and Yulong rivers. Costs vary widely by travel style: backpackers can get by on hostel dorms, street food, and rented bicycles, while mid-range travelers enjoy boutique guesthouses and guided activities, and luxury visitors can book riverside resorts and private tours. The table below summarizes typical daily costs per person in Chinese yuan (CNY), with approximate US dollar equivalents (at roughly 7.2 CNY per USD). Prices are indicative and vary by season, with October holidays and summer weekends being the most expensive.

Cost Notes by Category

  • Accommodation: West Street (Xi Jie) has the densest concentration of hostels and guesthouses. Book ahead during Chinese public holidays (especially the first week of October) and the summer high season, when prices can double.
  • Food: Local specialties include beer fish (pijiu yu), stuffed snails, and Guilin rice noodles. Street stalls and small eateries are the cheapest option; cafés along West Street cater to foreign visitors at higher prices.
  • Transport: Yangshuo town is walkable. Bicycles and e-bikes are the standard way to explore the countryside; taxis and ride-hailing are available but limited in rural areas. The nearest major rail hub is Yangshuo Station (about 30–40 minutes from town by bus or taxi), with high-speed trains to Guilin and Guangzhou.
  • Activities: The Li River cruise from Guilin to Yangshuo is a classic but can be pricey; shorter bamboo raft sections on the Yulong River are cheaper. Rock climbing at sites like Moon Hill and the Egg is popular, and cooking classes and tai chi lessons are widely offered.

Practical Tips

  • Carry cash for small vendors and rural areas; mobile payment (WeChat Pay, Alipay) is widespread but requires a Chinese bank account or supported foreign card setup.
  • Prices rise significantly during Chinese New Year, the October Golden Week, and summer weekends — book transport and lodging early.
  • Bargain politely at markets and for private tours; fixed prices apply at most hostels and official ticket counters.
  • Rent bicycles from your guesthouse for convenience and to avoid deposit hassles; inspect the bike before renting.
  • Weather affects costs and plans: the rainy season (April–June) can limit rafting and climbing, while autumn (September–November) offers the best conditions at peak prices.
  • Entrance fees to scenic areas (such as Moon Hill) are modest but add up; check whether your activity price includes them.
