Top Attractions in Singapore

Singapore packs an extraordinary range of experiences into a small island, from historic riverside quays and colonial-era landmarks to world-class wildlife parks and vibrant ethnic enclaves. Whether you're drawn to the city's rich cultural heritage, its green spaces and nature reserves, or its modern architectural icons, the city-state offers something for every kind of traveler. Here are ten attractions that should top any international visitor's itinerary.

  1. Singapore Zoo (Ah Meng legacy) — Home to a large orangutan enclosure, the zoo is famously associated with Ah Meng, a Sumatran orangutan who became a beloved tourism icon of Singapore. She lived at the zoo from 1971 until her death in 2008 and led a clan of about twenty orangutans.
    Tip: Visit in the cooler morning hours when animals are most active; the zoo is part of the Mandai Wildlife Reserve, so consider combining it with nearby parks.
  2. Bird Paradise — Opened in May 2023 in Mandai, this 17-hectare aviary replaced Jurong Bird Park and features larger, double-the-size walk-in aviaries. It forms part of the Mandai Wildlife Reserve alongside the Singapore Zoo, Night Safari, and River Wonders.
    Tip: Book tickets online in advance and allow at least half a day; the Mandai precinct is best reached by shuttle or car.
  3. Boat Quay — This historical quay on the southern bank of the Singapore River was once the busiest part of the old Port of Singapore. Its crowded shophouses were built here because the river's shape resembles the belly of a carp, a symbol of wealth and prosperity in Chinese belief.
    Tip: Come in the late afternoon to stroll the riverside promenade, then stay for dinner with views of the skyline; it's an easy walk from Raffles Place MRT.
  4. Clarke Quay — A historical riverside quay located upstream from the river mouth and Boat Quay, Clarke Quay is a lively waterfront district within the Singapore River Planning Area. Its restored warehouses and riverside setting make it a favorite for evening walks and dining.
    Tip: Visit after dark when the quay is at its liveliest; riverboat cruises depart nearby and offer a relaxed way to see the waterfront.
  5. CHIJMES — This historic complex began life as a Catholic convent, the Convent of the Holy Infant Jesus, dating from 1852, and includes the 1840–41 Caldwell House. Today the beautifully restored buildings house dining and event spaces in the heart of the Civic District.
    Tip: Stop by in the late afternoon for photos of the whitewashed chapel and cloisters, then enjoy dinner in the courtyard; it's a short walk from City Hall MRT.
  6. CHIJMES Hall — The former convent chapel, designed by Father Charles Benedict Nain, now serves as an elegant function hall for weddings and corporate events. Its neo-Gothic interior is one of the most photographed spaces in the CHIJMES complex.
    Tip: Check whether the hall is open to visitors on your dates, as it is primarily an event venue; the exterior and complex grounds are freely accessible.
  7. Cathedral of the Good Shepherd — Built in 1847, this is the oldest Catholic church in Singapore, set within shaded grounds in the Civic District. Its architecture recalls famous London churches such as St Paul's, Covent Garden and St Martin-in-the-Fields.
    Tip: Visit on a weekday morning for quiet contemplation; appropriate dress is expected, and it's a short walk from Bras Basah MRT.
  8. Central Fire Station — Singapore's oldest fire station, on Hill Street, is a gazetted national monument dating from the colonial era. It currently houses the Civil Defence Heritage Gallery, the official museum of the Singapore Civil Defence Force.
    Tip: The heritage gallery is a good rainy-day stop; check opening hours before visiting, as they may be limited.
  9. Chinatown — One of Singapore's most historically and culturally significant districts, Chinatown is an ethnic enclave in the Outram area encompassing five precincts including Kreta Ayer, Telok Ayer, and Tanjong Pagar. It features distinctly Chinese cultural elements and a historically concentrated ethnic Chinese population.
    Tip: Go early in the morning to see the wet market and temples before the crowds, or visit during Chinese New Year for festive decorations; Chinatown MRT puts you in the heart of it.
  10. Chek Jawa — These 100-hectare wetlands on the south-eastern tip of Pulau Ubin are among the last places in Singapore with a natural rocky shore. Several ecosystems—sandy and rocky beaches, seagrass lagoon, coral rubble, mangroves, and coastal forest—can be observed in one area.
    Tip: Take a bumboat from Changi Point Ferry Terminal to Pulau Ubin, then rent a bicycle; check tide tables, as the best intertidal life is visible at low tide.

Practical tips

  • Getting around: Singapore's MRT is fast, cheap, and air-conditioned; buy an EZ-Link card or use contactless payment for buses and trains.
  • Best time to visit: Early mornings are cooler and less crowded for outdoor sights like Boat Quay, Chinatown, and the Mandai wildlife parks; evenings suit riverside districts like Clarke Quay.
  • Tickets: Book wildlife park tickets (Singapore Zoo, Bird Paradise) online in advance for savings and timed entry, especially on weekends and holidays.
  • Dress code: Wear modest clothing when visiting religious sites such as the Cathedral of the Good Shepherd and temples in Chinatown.
  • Island trips: For Pulau Ubin and Chek Jawa, allow a full day, bring water and sun protection, and check tide and ferry schedules before setting out.
