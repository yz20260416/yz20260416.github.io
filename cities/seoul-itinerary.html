Seoul 3-Day Itinerary

Seoul is a city where ancient palaces stand beside glass skyscrapers, where centuries-old tea houses share alleys with K-pop dance studios, and where street food sizzles around the clock. With three days, you can experience the essential contrasts that make Seoul one of Asia's most exciting capitals: royal history in the morning, modern design and shopping in the afternoon, and vibrant night markets after dark. This itinerary balances must-see landmarks with neighborhood exploration and carefully chosen restaurants, so you can taste, see, and feel the real Seoul without rushing. Each day is organized by area to minimize transit time, with realistic timing and practical tips included at the end.

Day 1 – Royal Seoul & Historic Center

Time Activity Details & Restaurant Recommendations
8:30 – 11:30 Gyeongbokgung Palace & Changing of the Guard Arrive early to beat crowds. Watch the royal guard ceremony at the main gate (usually 10:00 and 14:00; check schedule). Wear comfortable shoes — the grounds are large.
Breakfast: Grab a quick toast & coffee at a local café near Gwanghwamun, or try Isaac Toast (a Seoul institution) for a Korean-style toasted sandwich.
11:30 – 12:30 National Folk Museum of Korea Located inside the palace grounds; free admission and a great introduction to Korean daily life and traditions. A good indoor break if the weather is hot or rainy.
12:30 – 14:00 Lunch in Insadong Walk 10 minutes to Insadong, a traditional arts and tea district.
Recommended: Gwangjang Market is a short taxi ride away for bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes) and mayak gimbap. Alternatively, dine at Insadong Sujebi for handmade noodle soup, or Osegyehyang for excellent vegetarian Korean temple cuisine.
14:00 – 16:00 Bukchon Hanok Village Wander the preserved traditional hanok houses on the hillside between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung. Respect residents — keep voices low and avoid entering private homes. Great photo spots along Bukchon-ro 11-gil.
16:00 – 17:30 Changdeokgung Palace & Secret Garden A UNESCO World Heritage site. The Huwon (Secret Garden) requires a separate timed ticket — book in advance, especially in spring and autumn.
18:00 – 19:30 Dinner near Jongno Recommended: Imun Seolnongtang (rich beef bone soup, a Seoul classic since 1904) or Myeongdong Kyoja for hand-cut noodles and dumplings (a short walk or taxi from Jongno).
20:00 – 22:00 Cheonggyecheon Stream Evening Walk Stroll the restored urban stream, beautifully lit at night. In winter, the Seoul Lantern Festival often takes place here. End with a drink in the nearby Euljiro area, famous for hidden hof (pub) spots.

Day 2 – Modern Seoul, Shopping & Nightlife

Time Activity Details & Restaurant Recommendations
9:00 – 10:30 N Seoul Tower & Namsan Park Take the Namsan cable car or hike up for panoramic city views. Go early for clearer skies and fewer crowds. Love-lock fences and observation deck are highlights.
Breakfast: Café at the base of Namsan or a bakery in Myeongdong.
10:30 – 12:00 Myeongdong Shopping District Korea's beauty and fashion hub. Stock up on cosmetics (tax refund available), and snack on street food: hotteok (sweet pancake), ttekbokki, and egg bread.
