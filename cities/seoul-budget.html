Seoul is one of Asia's most dynamic capitals, where cutting-edge technology, centuries-old palaces, and a legendary food scene sit side by side. The good news for travelers is that South Korea's capital is remarkably flexible on budget: you can live well on a backpacker's dime, travel comfortably in the mid-range, or splurge on luxury hotels and Michelin-starred dining. This guide breaks down typical costs across accommodation, food, transport, and activities for three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan a trip that matches your wallet. All figures are in US dollars (with Korean won equivalents where helpful) and are based on per-person, per-day estimates for a trip of roughly one week.

Budget Breakdown by Travel Style

The table below summarizes typical daily costs. Note that accommodation is priced per room per night (assuming two people sharing in the budget and mid-range tiers), while food, transport, and activities are per person per day.

Accommodation in Detail

  • Budget: Hostel dorms in Hongdae and Insadong run $18–$30 per bed; private rooms in guesthouses (many are hanok-style) start around $40–$60. Look for stays near subway line 2 for easy access.
  • Mid-range: Business hotels and 3–4★ properties in Myeongdong, Jongno, and Hongdae typically cost $90–$180 per night. Book 4–6 weeks ahead for the best rates, especially in spring (cherry blossom season) and autumn.
  • Luxury: Five-star hotels (Shilla, Signiel, Park Hyatt, Josun Palace) range from $300 to well over $700 per night. Gangnam and central Seoul command the highest prices; off-peak weekday rates can drop 20–30%.

Food in Detail

  • Budget: Street food (tteokbokki, hotteok, gimbap) costs $2–$5 per item; a bowl of noodles or a set meal at a local eatery is $6–$10. Convenience store meals are $3–$6.
  • Mid-range: Korean BBQ for two runs $30–$50 total; a casual restaurant lunch is $10–$15; café coffee is $4–$6. Expect $35–$60 per person per day with a mix of meals.
  • Luxury: Tasting menus at top restaurants (e.g., Jungsik, Mingles) start around $150–$250 per person before drinks. Hotel buffets and premium hanwoo beef dinners easily exceed $100 per person.

Transport in Detail

  • Subway: Base fare is about $1.10–$1.40 (₩1,400–₩1,500) per ride, with small distance surcharges. A T-money card costs ~$2 and can be reloaded at convenience stores and station machines.
  • Buses: Similar fares to the subway; transfers between buses and subway within 30 minutes are free or discounted with T-money.
  • Taxis: Base fare ~$3.50; a cross-town ride typically costs $8–$15. Late-night surcharges apply after midnight.
  • Airport transfer: AREX express train from Incheon is ~$8; all-stop train ~$4; airport limousine bus $12–$15; taxi $50–$70.
  • Money-saver: A 1-day or 2-day subway pass or the Korea Tour Card can pay off if you ride frequently; otherwise T-money pay-as-you-go is simplest.

Activities in Detail

  • Free and cheap: Gyeongbokgung Palace admission is ~$2.20 (free if wearing hanbok); many museums (National Museum of Korea, War Memorial) are free; Namsan hike and Cheonggyecheon stream walk cost nothing; free walking tours tip-based.
  • Mid-range: Hanbok rental $10–$20 for 2 hours; N Seoul Tower observation deck ~$15; DMZ tours $50–$70; cooking classes $40–$80; baseball game tickets $10–$30.
  • Luxury: Private guided palace and food tours $100–$200+; spa and jjimjilbang premium packages $50–$150; helicopter tours and private DMZ experiences $300+.

Sample Weekly Budgets (per person)

  • Budget traveler: ~$400–$600 for 7 days (shared dorm, street food, subway, free sights).
  • Mid-range traveler: ~$1,100–$1,900 for 7 days (private hotel room shared, restaurant meals, some tours and shows).
  • Luxury traveler: ~$3,400–$8,000+ for 7 days (5★ hotel, fine dining, private transfers and tours).

Practical Tips

  • Cash vs. card: Korea is highly card-friendly — even street stalls often accept cards. Carry a small amount of cash (₩50,000–₩100,000) for markets and small vendors.
  • T-money is essential: Buy a card at the airport or any convenience store; it works on subways, buses, and many taxis, and saves time at ticket machines.
  • Book accommodation early in peak seasons: Cherry blossom season (early April), autumn foliage (late October–early November), and major holidays (Chuseok, Lunar New Year) see prices spike and rooms sell out.
  • Eat where locals eat: Restaurants in side streets and near universities (Hongdae, Sinchon) are cheaper and often better than tourist-district spots. Lunch sets (jeomsim) offer great value.
  • Tax refunds: Look for "Tax Free" signs; purchases over ₩30,000 at participating stores qualify for VAT refunds at the airport.
