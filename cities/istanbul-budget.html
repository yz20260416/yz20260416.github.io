Istanbul Travel Cost Guide

Istanbul is one of the rare cities where a backpacker, a mid-range traveler, and a luxury seeker can all have a memorable trip without feeling out of place. Straddling Europe and Asia, the city offers everything from street-side simit and ferry rides for pocket change to rooftop fine dining overlooking the Bosphorus. Costs have risen in recent years due to inflation and currency fluctuations, but Istanbul remains relatively affordable compared to Western European capitals—especially if you pay attention to how and where you spend. This guide breaks down typical daily budgets by travel style, with realistic 2024–2025 figures in Turkish lira (TRY) and approximate US dollars (USD). Note that exchange rates move quickly; treat dollar figures as rough reference points and check current rates before you go.

Accommodation Breakdown

  • Hostels: Dorms in Sultanahmet, Beyoğlu, and Kadıköy run TRY 700–1,200 per bed. Private hostel rooms start around TRY 1,800.
  • Budget hotels and guesthouses: Simple double rooms in central areas: TRY 1,500–2,500.
  • Mid-range hotels: Comfortable 3–4★ properties, often with breakfast: TRY 2,500–4,500. Book early in spring and autumn.
  • Boutique stays: Galata, Karaköy, and Nişantaşı offer design-forward boutique hotels from TRY 4,000–7,000.
  • Luxury: Bosphorus-facing 5★ hotels (Beşiktaş, Ortaköy, Şişli) typically start around TRY 9,000 and climb steeply in peak season.

Tip: Prices in Sultanahmet skew tourist-high; Beyoğlu, Karaköy, and Kadıköy often offer better value and more local atmosphere.

Food Budget Breakdown

  • Street food: Simit (TRY 15–25), balık ekmek (TRY 80–150), döner (TRY 100–180), lahmacun (TRY 80–150). A street-food day can stay under TRY 500.
  • Local lokantas: Home-style plate meals (ev yemeği) for TRY 150–300 per dish; a full meal with tea around TRY 400–600.
  • Mid-range restaurants: Mains TRY 350–700; a meyhane dinner with mezes and drinks for two: TRY 2,000–3,500.
  • Coffee culture: Turkish coffee TRY 60–120; specialty espresso in Beyoğlu or Kadıköy TRY 100–180.
  • Fine dining: Tasting menus at top restaurants run TRY 3,000–6,000+ per person before drinks.

Tip: Lunch menus (günün menüsü) at lokantas are the best value in the city—often a soup, main, and bread for under TRY 300.

Transport Costs

  • İstanbulkart: Reusable transit card (TRY 70–130 deposit/issuance depending on type). Single rides on metro, tram, bus, and ferries cost roughly TRY 20–35 each with discounts for transfers.
  • Ferries: Cross-continental commuter ferries are among the world's great travel bargains—often TRY 25–50 per ride.
  • Taxis: Metered; short cross-town trips typically TRY 200–400. Use ride-hailing apps (BiTaksi, Uber) for transparency.
  • Airport transfers: Havalist buses ~TRY 100–150; metro (M11 to Gayrettepe, then connections) cheap but slower; private transfer TRY 1,200–2,000.
  • Car rental: From TRY 1,500–2,500/day, but traffic and parking make it unnecessary for most city trips.

Activities and Attractions

  • Free: Blue Mosque, Süleymaniye Mosque, Gülhane Park, walking the Galata Bridge, İstiklal Avenue, and the Kadıköy market streets.
  • Paid highlights: Hagia Sophia (entry fee applies for the visitor section), Topkapı Palace (~TRY 1,500–2,000), Basilica Cistern (~TRY 800–1,200), Galata Tower (~TRY 1,000).
  • Museum Pass Istanbul: Around TRY 3,500–4,500 for multi-day access to major sites—worth it if you plan to visit four or more paid attractions.
  • Bosphorus cruise: Public ferry version TRY 100–200; tourist cruises TRY 800–1,500.
  • Hammam: Historic baths (Çemberlitaş, Kılıç Ali Paşa) TRY 1,200–2,500 for scrub and wash; luxury spa hammams TRY 4,000+.
  • Guided tours: Group walking tours TRY 500–1,000; private guides from TRY 3,000–5,000 per half day.
