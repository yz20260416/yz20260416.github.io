Prague is one of Europe's most affordable capital cities, offering a remarkable blend of medieval charm, world-class beer, and rich cultural heritage at prices that won't break the bank. While the city has become increasingly popular with tourists, it remains significantly cheaper than Western European capitals like Paris, London, or Vienna. Your daily budget in Prague can range from as little as €35 for a frugal backpacker to €250+ for travelers seeking luxury experiences. The key to managing costs lies in understanding where your money goes — accommodation, food, transport, and activities — and choosing the travel style that matches your priorities. This guide breaks down realistic costs across three travel styles: budget, mid-range, and luxury, so you can plan your Prague adventure with confidence.

Accommodation Costs in Detail

  • Hostels: Dorm beds range from €15–30 per night in high season (May–September), dropping to €10–20 in winter. Private rooms in hostels cost €40–70. Look for hostels in Vinohrady or Žižkov for better value than Old Town.
  • Mid-range hotels: Three- and four-star hotels typically run €60–120 per night. Apartments via Airbnb or local agencies offer good value for stays of 3+ nights, especially in neighborhoods like Karlín or Holešovice.
  • Luxury hotels: Prague's five-star properties (e.g., in Malá Strana or along the Vltava) cost €180–400+ per night. Boutique hotels in converted palaces offer unique character at similar price points.
  • Seasonal note: Prices spike during Christmas markets (December) and major events; book well in advance. January–March and November offer the best accommodation deals.

Food and Drink Costs

  • Budget: Supermarket meals (Lidl, Albert, Billa) cost €5–8 per day. Street food like klobása (sausage) or trdelník runs €2–5. Cheap hospody (pubs) serve hearty lunches for €6–10.
  • Mid-range: A sit-down lunch at a casual restaurant costs €8–15; dinner for two with drinks at a mid-tier restaurant runs €40–70. A half-liter of local Pilsner costs €2–3 in most pubs — beer is often cheaper than water!
  • Luxury: Fine dining restaurants (several with Michelin recognition) charge €60–120 per person for tasting menus. Wine pairings add €30–60. Expect €150+ for a full upscale dinner for two.
  • Tip: Lunch menus (polední menu) at restaurants offer excellent value — often a soup and main for €6–10, available weekdays until about 2 PM.

Transport Costs

  • Public transit: Prague's metro, tram, and bus network is excellent and affordable. A 30-minute ticket costs 30 CZK (~€1.20), a 24-hour pass is 120 CZK (~€4.80), and a 3-day pass is 330 CZK (~€13). Validate your ticket — inspectors do fine tourists.
  • Airport transfers: Bus 119 + metro (~€2) is the budget option; Airport Express bus is ~€3; taxis/Uber run €15–25 to the center. Avoid unlicensed taxis at the airport.
  • Walking: Central Prague is highly walkable — most major sights are within 30 minutes of each other on foot.
  • Ride-hailing: Bolt and Uber are widely available and typically cheaper than street taxis.

Activities and Attractions

  • Free and cheap: Charles Bridge, Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock, Vyšehrad fortress, Letná Park, and the John Lennon Wall cost nothing. Free walking tours (tip-based, €5–10 tip expected) are excellent introductions.
  • Paid attractions: Prague Castle circuit tickets run €10–15; Jewish Museum €15–20; Petřín Lookout Tower €6; river cruises €15–30. Most museums charge €5–12.
  • Discounts: The Prague Card (€60–75 for 2–3 days) bundles public transport and 50+ attractions — worthwhile only if you plan to visit many paid sites. Many museums offer free entry on the first Monday of the month or have discounted evening hours.
  • Cultural experiences: Concerts in churches cost €10–25; black light theater shows €20–30; opera at the National Theatre €15–50 (standing room is cheaper).

Practical Money-Saving Tips

  • Pay in Czech koruna (CZK), not euros. Always choose to be charged in the local currency when paying by card to avoid unfavorable dynamic currency conversion rates.
  • Use ATMs wisely. Withdraw from bank ATMs (Česká spořitelna, Komerční banka) rather than standalone machines, which charge high fees. Decline ATM conversion offers.
  • Eat away from Old Town Square. Restaurants within 200 meters of the main tourist squares charge double or triple. Walk 10 minutes into Vinohrady, Žižkov, or Karlín for authentic, affordable food.
  • Book accommodation early for peak season (May–September, December) and consider neighborhoods outside the historic center — they're cheaper, quieter, and well-connected by tram.
  • Take advantage of free sights and parks — Prague's beauty is largely in its streets, architecture, and viewpoints, which cost nothing to enjoy.
  • Buy a transit pass only if you'll use it. For a compact 2–3 day visit centered on the old town, walking may suffice.
  • Avoid currency exchange kiosks in tourist areas — their rates and commissions are notoriously poor. Use ATMs or reputable exchange offices (e.g., in banks).
