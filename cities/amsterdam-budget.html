Amsterdam Travel Cost Guide

Amsterdam is one of Europe's most popular city-break destinations, famous for its picturesque canals, world-class museums, and vibrant cycling culture. However, the Dutch capital has a reputation for being expensive — and while it certainly isn't a budget backpacker's paradise, careful planning can make a visit surprisingly affordable. Costs vary dramatically depending on your travel style, the season, and how far in advance you book. This guide breaks down the real costs of accommodation, food, transport, and activities across three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan a trip that fits your wallet. All figures are per person per day, based on 2024–2025 prices in euros (€), with rough USD equivalents for reference.

Detailed Cost Breakdown

1. Accommodation

  • Hostels: Dorm beds range from €30–€55 in high season; private hostel rooms start around €80–€120. Book 2–3 months ahead for the best rates.
  • Budget hotels & guesthouses: €90–€140 per night for a double room, often outside the canal ring (e.g., Amsterdam-Noord or near Sloterdijk station).
  • Mid-range hotels: €110–€180 per night for 3–4★ properties; aparthotels and Airbnb (note: short-term rentals are heavily regulated) can offer good value for stays of 3+ nights.
  • Luxury hotels: €300–€600+ per night at canal-side 5★ properties such as the Pulitzer, Conservatorium, or Waldorf Astoria.
  • Money-saving tip: Staying in nearby Haarlem or Utrecht (20–30 minutes by train) can cut accommodation costs by 30–50%.

2. Food & Drink

  • Budget: Albert Heijn and Jumbo supermarkets are everywhere — self-catering breakfast and lunch costs €8–€12/day. Street snacks like frikandel or stroopwafels run €2–€5. A basic dinner at a budget eatery (e.g., a broodjes shop or FEBO) is €8–€15.
  • Mid-range: Café lunches €10–€18; a main course at a casual restaurant €18–€28; a coffee €3–€4; a beer at a brown café €4–€6. Expect €55–€90/day including a sit-down dinner.
