Lhasa: A Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Lhasa, the spiritual heart of Tibet, sits at an altitude of roughly 3,650 meters (12,000 feet) and rewards travelers who pace themselves carefully. This three-day itinerary balances Lhasa's most sacred sites—the Potala Palace, Jokhang Temple, and Barkhor Street—with acclimatization time, local food, and practical logistics. Because altitude sickness is a real risk, the plan front-loads lighter activities and reserves the most demanding climbs for when your body has adjusted. Book Potala Palace tickets in advance, carry your passport at all times (it is required at many sites), and expect to move slowly.

Day 1: Acclimatize and Explore the Old City

Time Activity Notes
Morning Arrive and rest at hotel; light breakfast Drink water, avoid alcohol and strenuous activity. Many hotels offer oxygen.
Late Morning Gentle walk around Barkhor Street Join the clockwise kora (pilgrim circuit) with locals; keep it slow.
Afternoon Visit Jokhang Temple Tibet's holiest temple; arrive early afternoon to avoid peak crowds. Modest dress required.
Evening Dinner near Barkhor; early night Rest is essential for acclimatization.

Restaurant recommendations: Try Snowland Restaurant (Tibetan and Nepali dishes, rooftop views over Barkhor) or Tashi Restaurant for momos, thukpa (noodle soup), and butter tea. Both are casual and central.

Day 2: Potala Palace and the Great Monasteries

Time Activity Notes
Morning Potala Palace (timed entry) Book tickets online in advance; entry is timed and visits inside are limited to about one hour. Climb the steps slowly—this is the most demanding altitude test.
Afternoon Drepung Monastery (west of the city) Once the world's largest monastery; allow 1.5–2 hours. A short taxi ride from central Lhasa.
Late Afternoon Sera Monastery debate courtyard Monk debates typically occur in the afternoon (roughly 3–5 pm, weekdays); confirm times locally.
Evening Dinner and rest Keep the evening light after a full day at altitude.

Restaurant recommendations: Makye Ame (near Barkhor, popular for Tibetan cuisine and views) or Dunya Restaurant for a mix of Western and local dishes.

Day 3: Culture, Views, and Departure Prep

Time Activity Notes
Morning Norbulingka (Summer Palace) or Tibet Museum Norbulingka is a relaxed garden complex; the museum offers context on Tibetan history and culture.
Afternoon Barkhor shopping and Chakpori viewpoint Pick up prayer flags, thangka paintings, and handicrafts. Bargain politely.
Evening Farewell dinner; pack for onward travel Confirm permits and transport for the next leg (e.g., Everest Base Camp or Shigatse).

Restaurant recommendations: Namaste Restaurant for Indian and Nepali food, or a local tian (sweet tea house) for an authentic Tibetan snack experience.

Practical Tips

  • Permits: Foreign travelers need a Tibet Travel Permit arranged through a licensed agency; you generally cannot travel independently. Carry your passport and permit at all times.
  • Altitude: Arrive by air only if you can rest on day one. Consider Diamox (consult your doctor), stay hydrated, and avoid alcohol for the first 48 hours. Seek help for severe headache, vomiting, or breathlessness.
  • Potala tickets: Book well in advance; entry is timed and limited. Bring your passport—it is checked against the booking.
  • Dress code: Shoulders and knees covered at monasteries and temples; remove hats inside. Walk clockwise around shrines and kora routes.
  • Photography: Ask before photographing monks or pilgrims; some chapels prohibit photos (look for signs).
  • Money: Carry cash (Chinese yuan) for small vendors and monastery donations; ATMs exist but can be unreliable.
  • Weather: Sun is intense at altitude—use sunscreen, sunglasses, and a hat. Mornings and evenings are cool even in summer.
  • Etiquette: Do not touch religious objects or point feet at altars; accept tea with both hands if offered.
