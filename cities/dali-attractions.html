Dali Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Dali, nestled between the Cangshan Mountains and Erhai Lake in Yunnan Province, is one of China's most rewarding destinations for travelers seeking a blend of natural scenery, Bai ethnic culture, and ancient history. The old town's stone-paved lanes, the lakeside villages, and the dramatic mountain backdrop make it easy to spend several days exploring. Below are the top 10 attractions in and around Dali, with practical details to help you plan your visit. Note that ticket prices and opening hours can change seasonally, so confirm current information locally or through official channels before you go.

Top 10 Attractions

  1. Dali Ancient City (大理古城)

    The historic heart of Dali, founded in the Ming dynasty and enclosed by restored city walls and four gate towers. Inside, you'll find cobblestone streets lined with Bai-style courtyard houses, cafés, craft shops, and temples such as the Wuhua Tower and the Confucian Temple. It's the best base for exploring the region and is lively in the evenings.

    • Tickets: Free to enter the town; small fees apply to individual sites (e.g., Wuhua Tower around ¥20–30).
    • Hours: Open all day; shops and sites generally 9:00–22:00.
    • Tips: Visit early morning or after 18:00 to avoid tour groups. Rent a bike to explore the quieter side streets and the nearby Three Pagodas.
  2. Three Pagodas of Chongsheng Temple (崇圣寺三塔)

    Dali's iconic landmark: three brick pagodas dating back over 1,000 years, with the central Qianxun Pagoda standing about 69 meters tall. The surrounding Chongsheng Temple complex has been rebuilt in grand style and offers excellent views of Erhai Lake and the Cangshan range.

    • Tickets: Around ¥75 (includes temple complex).
    • Hours: Roughly 8:00–18:00 (may extend in summer).
    • Tips: Allow 2–3 hours. The reflection pond near the entrance is the classic photo spot. Combine with a visit to the nearby ancient city.
  3. Erhai Lake (洱海)

    A large alpine lake ringed by Bai villages, wetlands, and cycling paths. The "Erhai Ecological Corridor" offers well-maintained bike and walking routes with lake views. Popular stops include Xizhou, Shuanglang, and the small peninsula at Caicun.

    • Tickets: Free to access the lake and corridor; boat cruises typically ¥100–200 depending on route.
    • Hours: Accessible all day; cruises usually depart 9:00–16:00.
    • Tips: Rent an e-bike or bicycle for the corridor (around ¥30–60 per day). The loop road is long—plan a section rather than the whole lake in one day.
  4. Cangshan Mountains (苍山)

    A dramatic range rising to over 4,000 meters behind Dali. Cable cars and hiking trails lead to viewpoints, waterfalls, and the Zhonghe Temple area. The mountains shelter the old town and offer some of the region's best scenery.

    • Tickets: Cable car fares vary by route (roughly ¥120–280 round trip); park entrance around ¥30–40.
    • Hours: Cable cars typically 8:30–17:00; check seasonal schedules.
    • Tips: Bring layers—temperatures drop with altitude. The Zhonghe cable car is popular for shorter visits; the Gantong route suits those heading to the large Gantong Temple.
  5. Xizhou Ancient Town (喜洲古镇)

    A well-preserved Bai town north of Dali known for its ornate courtyard mansions, market square, and traditional foods such as Xizhou baba (flatbread). It's a great place to see Bai architecture and daily life away from the busiest tourist zones.

    • Tickets: Free to enter; some courtyard homes charge ¥10–30 for entry.
    • Hours: Town open all day; shops and sites roughly 9:00–18:00.
    • Tips: Try the local baba and visit the morning market. Combine with a stop at nearby Butterfly Spring or the lakeside.
  6. Shuanglang (双廊)

    A lakeside village on Erhai's eastern shore with boutique guesthouses, waterfront cafés, and views toward Cangshan. It has become a fashionable spot for sunset views and relaxed stays, though it can get crowded in peak season.

    • Tickets: Free to enter the village.
    • Hours: Open all day.
    • Tips: Book accommodation in advance during holidays. The Nanzhao Fengqing Island (a small island offshore) charges a modest entry fee and is worth a short visit.
  7. Butterfly Spring Park (蝴蝶泉)

    A scenic park northwest of Dali famous for its spring and the legend of the butterfly gathering, celebrated in Bai folklore. The park includes a butterfly museum and pleasant gardens, and it's a common stop on the way to Xizhou.

    • Tickets: Around ¥40–60.
    • Hours: Roughly 8:00–18:00.
    • Tips: The actual butterfly season is limited (spring–early summer); the park is still pleasant year-round. Combine with Xizhou for a half-day trip.
  8. Chongsheng Temple (崇圣寺)

    The large Buddhist temple complex at the foot of Cangshan, rebuilt in the 2000s on the site of an ancient temple. It's a grand, terraced complex with halls, statues, and views, usually visited together with the Three Pagodas.

    • Tickets: Included in the Three Pagodas ticket (around ¥75).
    • Hours: Roughly 8:00–18:00.
    • Tips: The complex is large and involves stairs—allow time and wear comfortable shoes. A shuttle may be available to the upper halls.
  9. Dali March Street Festival (三月街) — seasonal

    An annual Bai ethnic festival held around the third lunar month (usually April) on the plain west of the old town. It features horse racing, markets, dancing, and food stalls, and is one of Yunnan's most important cultural events.

    • Tickets: Free to attend the public festival grounds.
    • Hours: Runs for about a week; daytime activities and evening performances.
    • Tips: Dates follow the lunar calendar, so check the exact year's schedule. Expect crowds and book accommodation early.
  10. Caicun and the Erhai Ecological Corridor (才村与洱海生态廊道)

    A lakeside area just north of the old town, ideal for cycling and walking along the ecological corridor. Caicun has a small pier and waterfront paths, and the corridor connects several villages with wetlands and birdlife.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Open all day; best in daylight.
    • Tips: Rent a bike near Caicun or the old town. Early morning offers calm water and fewer visitors; bring sun protection as shade is limited on parts of the path.
