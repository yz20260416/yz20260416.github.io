Dali Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Dali, nestled between the Cangshan Mountains and Erhai Lake in Yunnan Province, is one of China's most rewarding destinations for travelers seeking a blend of natural beauty, Bai ethnic culture, and ancient history. Once the capital of the Nanzhao and Dali kingdoms, the region is packed with temples, old towns, and scenic landscapes. This guide covers the top 10 attractions with practical details on tickets, hours, and tips to help you plan an efficient visit.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions in Dali

  1. Dali Ancient Town (大理古城)

    The historic heart of Dali, founded in the Ming Dynasty and enclosed by restored city walls. Cobblestone streets are lined with Bai-style architecture, shops, cafes, and courtyards. Key sights include the Wuhua Tower and the Foreigner Street area.

    • Tickets: Free to enter the town; Wuhua Tower admission around ¥20–40.
    • Hours: Open all day; shops typically 9:00–22:00.
    • Tips: Visit early morning or evening to avoid crowds. The South Gate is the most photogenic entrance.

  2. Erhai Lake (洱海)

    A vast alpine lake stretching roughly 40 km, ringed by villages, wetlands, and cycling paths. Popular stops include Xizhou, Shuanglang, and the lakeside eco-corridor.

    • Tickets: Free to access the lake; boat cruises ¥100–200; some scenic spots charge ¥10–30.
    • Hours: Accessible 24 hours; boat services generally 8:30–17:00.
    • Tips: Rent an e-bike or join a loop drive. The west shore eco-corridor is best for cycling; respect local fishing and wetland protection rules.

  3. Chongsheng Temple & Three Pagodas (崇圣寺三塔)

    Dali's iconic landmark, a triad of pagodas dating to the 9th–10th centuries, backed by the Cangshan range. The temple complex behind them was rebuilt in the 2000s and is extensive.

    • Tickets: Around ¥75 (combined temple and pagoda entry).
    • Hours: Approximately 7:30–18:30 (seasonal variation).
    • Tips: The classic photo spot is the reflection pool near the entrance. Allow 2–3 hours; the site involves considerable walking.

  4. Cangshan Mountains (苍山)

    A dramatic mountain range rising over 4,000 m behind Dali. Cable cars and hiking trails lead to viewpoints, waterfalls, and the Cloud Traveler's Path (Yudai Yunyou Road).

    • Tickets: Cable car fares vary by route, roughly ¥120–280 round trip; park entrance around ¥40.
    • Hours: Cable cars typically 8:30–17:00; confirm seasonal schedules.
    • Tips: Weather changes fast—bring layers and rain gear. The Zhonghe Cable Car is a popular mid-mountain option; the Gantong route serves the large Zhonghe Temple area.

  5. Xizhou Ancient Town (喜洲古镇)

    A well-preserved Bai village north of Dali known for ornate courtyard homes, the Xizhou morning market, and traditional tie-dye and batik crafts.

    • Tickets: Free to enter; some courtyard houses charge ¥20–60 for tours.
    • Hours: Town open all day; market busiest 7:00–11:00.
    • Tips: Try local Xizhou baba (flatbread). Visit the Yan Family Compound for classic Bai architecture.

  6. Shuanglang Town (双廊)

    A lakeside town on Erhai's east shore famed for sunset views, boutique guesthouses, and access to Nanzhao Fengqing Island.

    • Tickets: Free to enter; boat to Fengqing Island around ¥50.
    • Hours: Open all day; boats roughly 8:30–17:30.
    • Tips: Stay overnight for quieter evenings. Book accommodation ahead in peak season.

  7. Three Pagodas Reflection Park & Dali University Area

    While the main pagoda site charges admission, the nearby reflection park and lakeside spots offer free views of the pagodas framed by water and mountains.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Daylight hours.
    • Tips: Great for photography at sunrise; combine with a walk along the ancient town's outskirts.

  8. Butterfly Spring Park (蝴蝶泉)

    A scenic park northwest of Dali famous for its spring and the legend of the butterfly gathering, set against forested hills.

    • Tickets: Around ¥40.
    • Hours: Approximately 8:00–17:30.
    • Tips: Best combined with a Cangshan or Xizhou day trip; the butterfly season peaks in spring.

  9. Shaxi Ancient Town (沙溪古镇)

    A historic caravan town on the Tea Horse Road, about 2 hours from Dali, with a beautifully preserved Ming-era market square and temple.

    • Tickets: Free to enter; Sideng Market and Xingjiao Temple free or small donations.
    • Hours: Open all day.
    • Tips: Worth an overnight stay; arrange transport in advance as buses are limited.

  10. Jizu Mountain (鸡足山)

    A sacred Buddhist mountain east of Erhai with temples, pilgrimage routes, and summit views. A cable car and hiking trails serve the upper sections.

    • Tickets: Entrance around ¥60; cable car additional ¥50–80.
    • Hours: Roughly 7:00–18:00; cable car hours seasonal.
    • Tips: Start early—it's a full-day trip from Dali. Dress respectfully at temples and carry water.

Practical Travel Tips

  • Best time to visit: March–May and September–November offer mild, clear weather. July–August is rainy season; winters are cool but sunny.
  • Getting around: Dali Ancient Town is walkable. E-bikes and bicycles are ideal for Erhai Lake; taxis and ride-hailing apps cover longer trips. Buses connect major towns.
  • Tickets and hours: Prices and opening times can change seasonally—confirm at official counters or your hotel before setting out.
  • Altitude and sun: Dali sits around 2,000 m; UV is strong. Use sunscreen, a hat, and stay hydrated.
  • Etiquette: Dress modestly at temples; ask before photographing locals, especially in Bai villages.
  • Money and connectivity: Mobile payment (WeChat/Alipay) is widespread; carry some cash for small vendors. Download a translation app for menus and signage.
  • Suggested itinerary: Day 1 Ancient Town and Three Pagodas; Day 2 Erhai loop with Xizhou and Shuanglang; Day 3 Cangshan or a day trip to Shaxi or Jizu Mountain.
