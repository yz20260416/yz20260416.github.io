Local Character

The Old City's four quarters—Armenian, Christian, Jewish and Muslim—were laid out in the 19th century within walls rebuilt in 1538 under Suleiman the Magnificent of the Ottoman Empire; a visitor can walk between them in minutes and sense how distinct communities have shared a single square kilometer for centuries. The City of David, just outside those walls, shows the city's first signs of settlement in the 4th millennium BCE as encampments of nomadic shepherds, and radiocarbon dating of 103 Iron Age samples traces westward expansion and fortification until the Babylonian destruction in 586 BCE—worth knowing because the archaeology beneath the modern streets is part of the sightseeing itself. The name Jerusalem appears on ancient Egyptian tablets of the 14th century BCE as Urusalim, likely meaning 'foundation of the god Shalem', a Canaanite deity. That the city has been destroyed at least twice, besieged 23 times and captured and recaptured 44 times explains the layered, compact feel of its streets, where each era's stones sit atop the last.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Old City Walls and Quarters — The 1538 Ottoman walls of Suleiman the Magnificent enclose the Armenian, Christian, Jewish and Muslim quarters, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.
  2. City of David — The archaeological core of ancient Jerusalem, with settlement traces dating to the 4th millennium BCE and Iron Age remains confirmed by radiocarbon dating.
  3. Western Wall — The surviving retaining wall of the Temple Mount and the most visited Jewish prayer site in the city.
  4. Church of the Holy Sepulchre — The church in the Christian Quarter traditionally held to be the site of the crucifixion and burial of Jesus.
  5. Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Compound — The gold-domed shrine and mosque complex on the Temple Mount, among the oldest surviving Islamic structures in the world.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Israeli New Shekel (ILS, ₪)
  • Language: Hebrew and Arabic (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: IST (UTC+2), with daylight saving in summer
  • Visa: Most Western visitors receive a visa on arrival or enter visa-free for short stays, but entry rules are strict and travelers should verify current requirements with their embassy before booking.

FAQ

How many days do I need in Jerusalem?
Three to four days covers the Old City quarters, the City of David and the main holy sites at a comfortable pace.
When is the best time to visit?
April, May and October offer mild, mostly dry weather, though spring and autumn also bring the heaviest crowds.
Is the Old City walkable?
Yes—the walled Old City is about one square kilometer and its four quarters are easily crossed on foot, though the stone lanes are uneven and steep in places.
