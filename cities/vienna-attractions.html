Top Attractions in Vienna

Vienna blends imperial grandeur with modern culture, from the sprawling Hofburg Palace to quirky landmarks like the Hundertwasserhaus. The city's historic center, ringed by the grand Ringstraße, holds many of its finest sights, while green spaces and the Danube Canal offer breathing room between museum visits. Whether you're drawn to Habsburg history, contemporary art, or family-friendly aquariums, these ten attractions capture the essence of Austria's capital.

  1. Hofburg Palace — The former principal imperial palace of the Habsburg dynasty, built in the 13th century and expanded over the centuries, served as the imperial winter residence. Since 1946 it has been the official residence and workplace of the Austrian president.
    Tip: Allow at least half a day; the complex includes multiple museums, so check combined tickets.
  2. Austrian Parliament Building — Located on the Ringstraße in the first district near Hofburg Palace, this grand building houses the two chambers of Austria's parliament. It was originally built for the Imperial Council of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.
    Tip: Visit early morning for the best light on the facade and fewer crowds along the Ringstraße.
  3. Haus des Meeres — This public aquarium in Mariahilf district houses over ten thousand aquatic creatures across roughly 4,000 square meters inside a converted World War II flak tower. It drew a record 650,000 visitors in 2012.
    Tip: Book tickets online in advance; it's a popular family destination, especially on weekends.
  4. Hundertwasserhaus — This whimsical apartment house, completed in 1985, was built on the idea and concept of Austrian artist Friedensreich Hundertwasser. Its irregular forms and colorful facade make it one of Vienna's most photographed buildings.
    Tip: It's a residential building, so admire from outside; combine with a stroll through the nearby streets.
  5. Imperial Crypt (Kaisergruft) — Founded in 1618 beneath the Capuchin Church near Hofburg Palace, this burial chamber has served since 1633 as the principal place of entombment for the House of Habsburg. It holds the remains of 145 Habsburg royalty.
    Tip: A modest entrance fee applies; visit on a weekday morning for a quieter experience.
  6. Ducal Crypt — This burial chamber beneath the chancel of Stephansdom holds 78 containers with the bodies, hearts, or viscera of 72 members of the House of Habsburg. It offers a fascinating, if macabre, glimpse into imperial burial traditions.
    Tip: Combine with a visit to St. Stephen's Cathedral above; guided tours are limited, so check times in advance.
  7. Herzgruft (Heart Crypt) — Located behind the Loreto Chapel in the Augustinian Church within the Hofburg complex, this crypt protects 54 urns containing the hearts of Habsburg family members. The first heart was placed here in 1654.
    Tip: It's a small, easily missed site—ask at the church entrance for directions.
  8. Botanical Garden of the University of Vienna — Covering 8 hectares and immediately adjacent to the Belvedere gardens, this botanical garden is part of the University of Vienna. It's a peaceful green escape in the city.
    Tip: Pair with a visit to the Belvedere; spring and early summer are best for blooms. Entry is generally free.
  9. Donaukanal — This former arm of the Danube, regulated since 1598, runs 17.3 kilometers and borders the city center, where the Wien River flows into it. Today its banks are lined with murals, bars, and promenades.
    Tip: Go in the late afternoon or evening for the lively riverside atmosphere and street art.
  10. Bohemian Prater — This small amusement park at the edge of Vienna in Favoriten dates to the second half of the 19th century, and some rides are over 100 years old. It was almost completely destroyed by a bomb attack in 1944 and later rebuilt.
    Tip: Ideal for families with children; check seasonal opening hours before making the trip.

Practical tips

  • Many major sights cluster in the Innere Stadt and along the Ringstraße—plan walking routes to minimize transit time.
  • Purchase a Vienna City Card or transit pass for discounts on museums and unlimited public transport.
  • Book popular attractions like Haus des Meeres online to skip ticket queues, especially in summer.
  • Visit outdoor sites such as the Donaukanal and Hundertwasserhaus in the morning for fewer crowds and better photos.
  • Note that several crypts have limited opening hours—confirm schedules before your visit.
