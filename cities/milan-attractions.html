Milan Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Milan is Italy's financial and fashion capital, but it is also a city rich in art, history, and architecture. From the soaring spires of the Duomo to Leonardo da Vinci's masterpiece The Last Supper, the city offers a compact but rewarding set of sights. Most attractions are concentrated in the historic center and are easily reached on foot or by metro. Below are the ten must-see attractions, with practical details on tickets, opening hours, and tips for making the most of your visit.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions

  1. Duomo di Milano (Milan Cathedral)

    The city's iconic Gothic cathedral, begun in 1386 and completed only in the 20th century, features more than 3,000 statues and 135 spires. The rooftop terraces offer panoramic views over the city and, on clear days, the Alps.

    • Tickets: Cathedral only around €10; rooftop terraces by stairs around €15, by lift around €20; combined tickets available. Book online to skip queues.
    • Hours: Cathedral generally 9:00–19:00 (last entry around 18:10); rooftop terraces typically 9:00–19:00, with variations by season.
    • Tips: Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered); security checks apply. Visit the terraces early morning or late afternoon for the best light and smaller crowds.
  2. Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

    A spectacular 19th-century shopping arcade linking Piazza del Duomo to Piazza della Scala, with a glass-vaulted roof, mosaic floors, and historic cafés and luxury boutiques. It is free to enter.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Public passage generally open 24 hours; shops typically 10:00–19:00 or later.
    • Tips: Look up at the mosaics and the central dome. Spin on the bull mosaic (a local tradition) with your heel—but be gentle, as it is a historic floor.
  3. Teatro alla Scala and Museo Teatrale

    One of the world's most famous opera houses, inaugurated in 1778. The adjacent museum displays costumes, instruments, and portraits, and visitors can often peek into the auditorium from a box.

    • Tickets: Museum around €12; guided tours available at additional cost; opera tickets vary widely.
    • Hours: Museum generally 9:30–17:30, closed some holidays; check ahead as hours vary with the performance schedule.
    • Tips: Book opera tickets well in advance. The museum is a good alternative if you cannot attend a performance.
  4. Pinacoteca di Brera

    A major art gallery in the Brera district housing masterpieces by Raphael, Caravaggio, Mantegna, and Bellini, set in a former Jesuit college and palace complex.

    • Tickets: Around €15; reductions available; free on certain first Sundays of the month (check current policy).
    • Hours: Generally 8:30–19:15, closed Mondays.
    • Tips: Combine with a stroll through the charming Brera neighborhood, known for galleries, boutiques, and cafés.
  5. Castello Sforzesco and Parco Sempione

    A vast 15th-century fortress built by the Sforza dukes, now housing several museums (including Michelangelo's unfinished Rondanini Pietà) and opening onto the green expanse of Parco Sempione.

    • Tickets: Courtyards free; museum ticket around €5–€10 depending on exhibitions.
    • Hours: Castle grounds generally 7:00–19:30; museums typically 10:00–17:30, closed Mondays.
    • Tips: Enter through the main gate and walk through to the park, ending at the Arco della Pace—a pleasant half-day route.
  6. Santa Maria delle Grazie and The Last Supper

    A UNESCO-listed Dominican church and refectory housing Leonardo da Vinci's The Last Supper (1495–1498), one of the most celebrated paintings in the world.

    • Tickets: Around €15 plus booking fee; timed-entry only.
    • Hours: Generally Tuesday–Sunday, with timed slots throughout the day; closed Mondays.
    • Tips: Tickets sell out weeks or months in advance—book as early as possible through the official channel. Visits are limited to about 15 minutes, and photography is restricted.
  7. Basilica di Sant'Ambrogio

    One of Milan's oldest churches, founded by St. Ambrose in the 4th century, with a Romanesque facade, a serene atrium, and the tomb of the city's patron saint.

    • Tickets: Free entry; small charge for some areas such as the crypt or museum.
    • Hours: Generally open daily with a midday closure (roughly 10:00–12:00 and 14:30–18:00); hours vary.
    • Tips: Visit on a weekday morning for quiet reflection; the church is a short walk from the Duomo area.
  8. Navigli District

    A picturesque canal district (Naviglio Grande and Naviglio Pavese) lined with restaurants, bars, and vintage shops. It is the heart of Milan's evening scene.

    • Tickets: Free to explore; canal boat cruises typically €10–€20.
    • Hours: Always accessible; best visited in the late afternoon and evening.
    • Tips: The last Sunday of each month hosts a large antiques market along the Naviglio Grande. Enjoy an aperitivo by the water.
  9. Museo del Novecento

    A modern art museum in Piazza del Duomo tracing 20th-century Italian art, with works by Futurists and major Italian painters, plus a top-floor terrace with views of the cathedral.

    • Tickets: Around €5–€10; free on certain first Sundays.
    • Hours: Generally 10:00–19:30, with later closing on some days; closed Mondays.
    • Tips: The rooftop view of the Duomo is a lesser-known photo spot. Combine with a visit to the Duomo next door.
  10. Cimitero Monumentale

    An ornate 19th-century cemetery filled with elaborate tombs, sculptures, and mausoleums—an open-air museum of funerary art that surprises many visitors.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Generally Tuesday–Sunday around 8:30–18:00; closed Mondays.
    • Tips: Pick up a free map at the entrance to locate notable monuments. It is a peaceful break from the city center, reachable by metro.

Practical Tips for Travelers

  • Book ahead: Timed-entry attractions such as The Last Supper and the Duomo terraces sell out quickly, especially in peak season (April–October).
  • Museum closures: Many museums close on Mondays; check individual opening hours before planning your day.
  • Dress code: Churches require modest dress—cover shoulders and knees.
  • Transport: The metro, trams, and buses are efficient; a single ticket is valid for a set period, and day passes offer good value. The historic center is very walkable.
  • Combined tickets: Look for city museum passes and combined tickets (e.g., Duomo complexes) to save money.
  • Timing: Visit major sights early morning or late afternoon to avoid crowds and heat; many attractions have last entry about an hour before closing.
  • Security: Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas such as the Duomo square, the Galleria, and on public transport.
  • Verify details: Prices and hours change seasonally—confirm on official websites before you go.
