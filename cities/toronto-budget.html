Toronto Travel Cost Guide

Toronto is Canada's largest city and one of its most expensive — but it's also one of the most rewarding. Whether you're a backpacker stretching every dollar, a mid-range traveler balancing comfort and cost, or a luxury visitor seeking the best the city offers, Toronto can be done well at nearly any budget. This guide breaks down realistic daily and trip costs across three travel styles, covering accommodation, food, local transport, and activities, with all figures in Canadian dollars (CAD) and based on current typical prices. Use it to plan a trip that fits your wallet without missing what makes Toronto great.

Note: Prices are estimates and vary by season — summer (June–August) and major events command the highest rates. Add 13% HST to most purchases (usually already included in listed prices).

Accommodation

  • Budget: Hostels downtown (e.g., near Kensington Market or the Entertainment District) run $40–$70 per dorm bed. A few budget motels on the city fringe offer private rooms from ~$90–$120. Book early in summer.
  • Mid-Range: 3–4★ hotels and well-located Airbnb/rental apartments typically cost $150–$280 per night. Staying slightly outside the core (e.g., near St. Lawrence Market, Leslieville, or Bloor West) can shave 20–30% off.
  • Luxury: Downtown five-star properties (Yorkville, Financial District, harbourfront) range from $350 to $700+ per night, higher during film festival and convention weeks.

Food & Drink

  • Budget: Expect $30–$45/day by mixing grocery stores, food courts, and cheap eats — Toronto's diverse food scene makes this easy (e.g., Kensington Market, Chinatown, shawarma shops). A sit-down meal at a basic restaurant runs $15–$22.
  • Mid-Range: $60–$100/day covers casual breakfasts, a lunch out, and dinner at a mid-priced restaurant, with mains around $22–$35. Add $8–$14 per drink at bars.
  • Luxury: Fine dining in Yorkville or the Financial District easily reaches $150–$300+ per day, with tasting menus at top restaurants running $120–$250 per person before wine.
  • Money-saver: Look for lunch specials, happy hours, and the many affordable ethnic restaurants — some of the city's best food is its cheapest.

Local Transport

  • TTC (subway, streetcar, bus): A single fare is about $3.35 (Presto card / contactless); a day pass is roughly $13 and a weekly pass around $47 — excellent value if you ride more than three times a day.
  • From the airport: UP Express train to Union Station costs about $12.35 (adult) and takes ~25 minutes — far cheaper than a $60–$70 taxi or rideshare from Pearson.
  • Bike share: Short trips on the city's bike-share network cost a few dollars and are a great way to explore the waterfront and downtown.
  • Taxis/rideshare: Downtown trips typically run $12–$25; budget travelers should rely on transit, while luxury travelers can budget $40–$80/day for cars.

Activities & Attractions

  • Free & cheap: Walking the waterfront, High Park, the Distillery District, Kensington Market, graffiti alleys, and free walking tours cost little or nothing. Many museums offer free or discounted admission on certain evenings (check current schedules).
  • Mid-range: CN Tower (about $40–$45), Royal Ontario Museum (~$25–$30), Art Gallery of Ontario (~$25), Casa Loma (~$35), and harbour cruises (~$35–$50) are the classic paid attractions. A CityPASS-style bundle can save 20–30% if you plan to hit several.
  • Luxury: Private guided tours ($100–$250+), theatre and live shows ($60–$200+), helicopter tours, and premium sporting events (Maple Leafs, Raptors, Blue Jays) can run $80–$300+ per ticket depending on the game.
