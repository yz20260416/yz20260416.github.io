Chengdu in Three Days: A Complete Itinerary

Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan Province, is a city of tea houses, pandas, and some of the most flavorful food in China. Famous for its relaxed pace of life, spicy cuisine, and the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, the city rewards travelers who slow down and explore. This guide lays out a practical three-day itinerary balancing major sights, neighborhood wandering, and serious eating, with morning, afternoon, and evening plans for each day.

Day 1: Pandas, People's Park, and Hotpot

Time Plan Notes
Morning (7:00–11:30) Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding (熊猫基地) Arrive at opening (around 7:30) when pandas are most active. Take a taxi or DiDi from the city center (about 30–40 minutes). Book tickets in advance online. Allow 3–4 hours.
Afternoon (12:30–17:00) People's Park (人民公园) and a tea house Lunch near the park, then join locals at Heming Teahouse for bottomless jasmine tea and people-watching. Watch the "matchmaking corner" and ear-cleaning (candling) vendors.
Evening (18:00 onward) Sichuan hotpot dinner and Jinli Street stroll Try a classic hotpot (see restaurant list below), then walk off dinner along Jinli Ancient Street, lit up at night.

Day 2: Temples, Wenshu, and the Wide and Narrow Alleys

Time Plan Notes
Morning (8:30–12:00) Wenshu Monastery (文殊院) One of Chengdu's best-preserved Buddhist monasteries. Explore the halls, then have a vegetarian lunch at the monastery's own restaurant.
Afternoon (13:30–17:30) Wide and Narrow Alleys (宽窄巷子) and Renmin Road area Restored Qing-era lanes with teahouses, snacks, and shops. Combine with a walk toward Tianfu Square to see the Mao statue and city center.
Evening (18:30 onward) Sichuan opera at a teahouse theater Book a face-changing (bian lian) show, such as at the Shufeng Yayun theater in the Culture Park. Arrive early for tea and snacks.

Day 3: Museums, Markets, and a Final Feast

Time Plan Notes
Morning (9:00–12:00) Sanxingdui Museum (三星堆博物馆) or Jinsha Site Museum Sanxingdui (in Guanghan, about 1 hour by car) is a must for Bronze Age artifacts; Jinsha is closer and covers the same culture. Choose one based on time.
Afternoon (13:30–17:00) Local market and neighborhood walk Visit a wet market such as Yulin or Qingyang for a taste of daily life, then wander the leafy Yulin neighborhood with its cafés and small bars.
Evening (18:00 onward) Final Sichuan dinner and a riverside walk Try mapo tofu, kung pao chicken, and dan dan noodles at a local restaurant, then stroll along the Jinjiang River.

Restaurant Recommendations

  • Hotpot: Huangcheng Laoma (皇城老妈) for a polished experience; Shu Jiu Xiang (蜀九香) for a local favorite; or any busy neighborhood hotpot for authenticity.
  • Sichuan classics: Chen Mapo Tofu (陈麻婆豆腐) for mapo tofu; Yu's Family Kitchen (喻家厨房) for refined Sichuan dishes.
  • Snacks and street food: Long Chao Shou (龙抄手) for wontons; Zhong Shui Jiao (钟水饺) for dumplings; try dan dan noodles and rabbit head (兔头) if adventurous.
  • Vegetarian: Wenshu Monastery's vegetarian restaurant is excellent and affordable.
  • Tea houses: Heming Teahouse in People's Park and Pengzhen Teahouse for a classic Chengdu afternoon.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: The metro is cheap, clean, and extensive; use DiDi (China's ride-hailing app) for taxis. Have addresses written in Chinese for drivers.
  • Panda base timing: Go early. Pandas nap through the midday heat, and crowds build quickly after 9:00.
  • Spice levels: "微辣" (wei la) means mildly spicy — still spicy by most standards. Ask for "不辣" (bu la) if you need no chili, though it will limit your options.
  • Payments: Mobile payment (WeChat Pay, Alipay) is nearly universal; set up one before arrival. Carry some cash for small vendors.
  • Weather: Chengdu is humid and often overcast. Pack layers, an umbrella, and comfortable walking shoes. Summers are hot and sticky; winters are cool and damp.
  • Language: English is limited outside major hotels. A translation app and screenshots of Chinese place names go a long way.
  • Etiquette: At teahouses, refills are free — signal staff for more hot water. Tipping is not customary.
  • Book ahead: Panda base tickets, opera shows, and popular restaurants (especially on weekends) are best reserved in advance.
