Petra Travel Cost Guide

Petra, the ancient Nabataean city carved into rose-red sandstone cliffs, is Jordan's crown jewel and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. While the experience is priceless, a visit to Petra involves several costs that travelers should plan for carefully. This guide breaks down realistic budgets for accommodation, food, transport, and activities across three travel styles—budget backpacker, mid-range traveler, and luxury visitor—so you can plan your trip with confidence. All figures are in US dollars (JOD is pegged to the dollar at roughly 1 JOD = $1.41) and reflect typical 2024–2025 prices. Note that Petra entry itself is significantly cheaper if you hold a Jordan Pass, which bundles the visa fee with admission to Petra and dozens of other sites.

Detailed Cost Notes

  • Accommodation: Almost all visitors stay in Wadi Musa, the town at Petra's entrance. Prices spike during peak season (March–May, September–October) and on weekends—book ahead. The village of Taybet, 10 minutes away, offers quieter boutique options.
  • Food: Inside Petra, only a few cafés operate (near the Visitor Center and Basin restaurant); expect inflated prices, so carry water and snacks. Wadi Musa has excellent cheap eats along the main tourist street.
  • Transport: The JETT bus (Amman–Petra) runs daily, ~$15, and is the best mid-range option. Shared minibuses from Amman's Wahdat station cost ~$10 but are slower. Taxis within Wadi Musa should cost $3–5; agree on the fare first.
  • Activities: Donkey and camel rides inside Petra are negotiable ($10–30) but controversial—consider walking instead. Petra by Night runs Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday (~$17). The Jordan Pass must be purchased online before arrival to waive the visa fee.
  • Hidden costs: Tipping (10% at restaurants, small tips for guides/drivers), bottled water ($1–2 per bottle—bring a refillable filter bottle), and the Petra Visitor Center's optional golf-cart shuttle.

Practical Tips

  • Buy the Jordan Pass: For nearly every traveler staying 3+ nights in Jordan, the Jordan Pass (70 JOD for 1-day Petra) saves money versus paying the visa fee (~$56) plus Petra entry separately.
  • Stay two days if budget allows: The 2-day Petra ticket costs only ~$8 more than 1-day, and Petra is vast—one day means rushing past the High Place of Sacrifice and Monastery trails.
  • Start early: Gates open at 6 a.m. (6:30 in winter). Early entry beats crowds and heat, and lets you cover the Siq, Treasury, and Monastery before lunch.
  • Carry cash: ATMs exist in Wadi Musa but can run dry; many small vendors and taxi drivers prefer cash (JOD).
  • Pack smart: Sturdy shoes (trails are rocky), sun protection, and 2+ liters of water per person. The Monastery hike involves ~800 steps.
  • Combine with Wadi Rum: Most travelers pair Petra with a Wadi Rum desert camp ($50–150/night depending on style)—budget an extra day and ~$100–200 for transport and camp.
  • Avoid hidden fees: Confirm whether your hotel's "breakfast included" rate is real, and check if taxis quote per person or per vehicle.
