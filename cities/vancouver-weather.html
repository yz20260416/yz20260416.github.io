Vancouver Weather by Month

Vancouver sits in a temperate rainforest climate on Canada's Pacific coast, which means mild winters, warm summers, and plenty of rain — most of it between October and March. Unlike the rest of Canada, Vancouver rarely sees deep freezes or heavy snow at sea level, but the city's reputation for drizzle is well earned: it receives roughly 1,200 mm (47 in) of precipitation annually, with November being the wettest month. The flip side is that summer (June–September) is genuinely spectacular, with long daylight hours, low humidity, and some of the driest, sunniest weather in the country. Understanding the seasonal rhythm is key to packing right and choosing when to visit.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall (weather): July and August — warm, dry, sunny, and ideal for beaches, hiking, and patios. Expect peak crowds and higher hotel rates.
  • Best value & balance: June and September — nearly summer-like conditions with fewer crowds and lower prices. September is a local favourite.
  • Best for blossoms & gardens: March to May — cherry blossoms peak in late March, and May brings lush parks and comfortable temperatures.
  • Best for skiing & winter sports: December to March — Grouse Mountain, Cypress, and Mount Seymour offer local skiing while the city stays mild and green.
  • Best for budget travellers: November and early December — the wettest, quietest stretch, but with the lowest accommodation rates (aside from holiday weeks).

Practical Tips

  • Pack layers and rain gear year-round. Even in summer, evenings can be cool, and a light waterproof shell is never wasted in Vancouver.
  • Don't fear the rain forecasts. Winter "rain" often means intermittent drizzle rather than all-day downpours — a hooded jacket beats an umbrella in windy coastal weather.
  • Book summer accommodation early. July–August and cruise-season weekends fill up fast; reserve hotels and popular restaurants well in advance.
  • Use the shoulder seasons for savings. Late April–May and September–early October offer good weather at noticeably lower prices.
  • Check mountain conditions separately. The North Shore mountains can be snowy and cold while downtown is mild and rainy — dress accordingly if you're heading up for skiing or sightseeing.
  • Embrace indoor options on wet days. Museums, galleries, Granville Island Market, and the Aquarium are ideal rainy-day backups.
  • Watch daylight hours. Winter days are short (sunset before 4:30 pm in December), so plan outdoor sightseeing for midday.
