Local Character

Tokyo's character is layered by its transformation from Edo, a fishing village that became the seat of the Tokugawa shogunate in 1603 and grew past one million residents by the mid-18th century. Visitors should care because this shogunal legacy shaped the city's concentric layout and merchant culture that still echo in older districts. After the Meiji Restoration of 1868, the imperial capital moved from Kyoto and the city was renamed Tokyo, literally 'Eastern Capital' — a rename that explains why the Imperial Palace sits at the historic center in Chiyoda. The 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and World War II bombing raids destroyed much of the city, so today's skyline is largely postwar reconstruction; knowing this helps visitors understand why so few pre-20th-century buildings survive. The 23 special wards each retain distinct identities: Chiyoda holds the National Diet Building and Imperial Palace, Shinjuku serves as the administrative center, and Shibuya is a commerce and business hub. Tokyo's 1964 hosting of the Summer Olympics made it the first Asian city to do so, and the 1927 opening of the Tokyo Metro Ginza Line gave Asia its oldest underground metro — a detail that reframes the city's efficient subway as a piece of living history.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Tokyo Imperial Palace — The Emperor's residence in Chiyoda, built on the site of the former Tokugawa shogunate castle, with East Gardens open to visitors.
  2. Shibuya Crossing — The famously busy scramble intersection in Shibuya, a commerce and business hub where crowds surge in all directions at once.
  3. Shinjuku Station — The world's busiest train station, serving as the city's administrative center and a maze of rail and retail.
  4. Tokyo Skytree — The world's tallest tower, offering observation decks with sweeping views over the Kantō region.
  5. Tokyo Station — The central hub for the Shinkansen high-speed railway network and a landmark gateway to the city.

FAQ

How do I get around Tokyo?
Use the extensive rail network: Tokyo Station is the Shinkansen hub, Shinjuku Station is the world's busiest train station, and the Tokyo Metro Ginza Line, opened in 1927, is Asia's oldest underground metro line.
When is the best time to visit Tokyo?
April is excellent for mild temperatures and high-season energy, while October and May offer comfortable weather; June and September are the rainiest months.
What are the must-see districts?
Chiyoda for the Imperial Palace and National Diet Building, Shinjuku for the administrative center and busiest station, and Shibuya for commerce, business, and its famous crossing.
