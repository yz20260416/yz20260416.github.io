Best Time to Visit Beijing

Beijing, China's capital, blends imperial grandeur with modern ambition, from the Forbidden City and Great Wall to hutong alleys and futuristic skylines. Visit in spring or autumn for mild temperatures and clear skies, though each season offers its own character. Allow at least four days to cover the major sights and sample the city's celebrated cuisine.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Forbidden City — The vast imperial palace complex at the heart of Beijing, home to 24 Ming and Qing emperors and now a UNESCO-listed museum.
  2. Great Wall at Mutianyu — A beautifully restored and less crowded section of the Great Wall, set amid forested hills about 70 km from the city.
  3. Temple of Heaven — A serene Ming-dynasty ritual complex where emperors prayed for good harvests, surrounded by a lively public park.
  4. Summer Palace — An expansive imperial garden of lakes, pavilions and hillside temples that served as the Qing court's summer retreat.
  5. Tiananmen Square and the Hutongs — The vast ceremonial square anchors the city center, while nearby hutong lanes reveal traditional courtyard life.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Chinese Yuan (CNY, ¥)
  • Language: Mandarin Chinese (English is limited outside major tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: CST (UTC+8)
  • Visa: Most international visitors need a visa arranged in advance, though some nationalities qualify for visa-free transit or short stays.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Beijing?
Four to five days lets you cover the Forbidden City, Great Wall, Temple of Heaven and Summer Palace at a comfortable pace.
What is the best way to get around Beijing?
The subway is fast, cheap and English-signed; use taxis or ride-hailing apps for late nights and outlying sights like the Great Wall.
What should I know about visiting the Forbidden City?
Tickets must be booked online in advance with a passport, and the palace is closed on Mondays except for public holidays.
