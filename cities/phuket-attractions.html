Phuket's Top 10 Must-See Attractions: A Complete Travel Guide

Phuket, Thailand's largest island, blends turquoise Andaman Sea beaches with Sino-Portuguese heritage, hilltop temples, and lush rainforest. Whether you're a first-time visitor or returning for a slower pace, these ten attractions cover the island's essential experiences—from the iconic Big Buddha viewpoint to the limestone karsts of Phang Nga Bay. Below you'll find descriptions, current ticket prices, opening hours, and practical tips to help you plan each visit efficiently.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions in Phuket

  1. Big Buddha (Phra Phuttha Ming Mongkol Akenakkiri)

    A 45-meter white marble-clad Buddha statue seated atop Nakkerd Hill between Chalong and Kata, offering 360-degree views across Chalong Bay, Kata Beach, and on clear days the Phi Phi islands. The site is an active temple, so dress respectfully (shoulders and knees covered).

    • Ticket price: Free entry; donations welcome (suggested 20–100 THB)
    • Opening hours: Daily, roughly 6:00 AM–7:30 PM
    • Visiting tips: Arrive before 9:00 AM or after 4:00 PM to avoid tour bus crowds and midday heat. Bring water and sun protection—the hilltop has little shade. Combine with a stop at Wat Chalong nearby.
  2. Wat Chalong (Chalong Temple)

    Phuket's largest and most revered Buddhist temple, home to the Grand Pagoda (Chedi) said to contain a splinter of the Buddha's bone. The temple complex is ornate and busy with merit-making locals, especially during festivals.

    • Ticket price: Free entry; donations accepted
    • Opening hours: Daily, approximately 8:00 AM–5:00 PM
    • Visiting tips: Remove shoes before entering temple buildings. Do not point feet toward Buddha images or monks. Photography is allowed in most areas but avoid posing disrespectfully with statues.
  3. Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island

    A day-trip highlight featuring dramatic limestone karsts rising from emerald water, sea caves, and the famous "James Bond Island" (Koh Tapu) from The Man with the Golden Gun. Most tours include canoeing through caves at Koh Hong or Koh Panak.

    • Ticket price: National park entry 300 THB for adults (plus tour cost, typically 1,200–2,500 THB depending on operator and inclusions)
    • Opening hours: Day trips generally depart 7:30–9:00 AM and return by late afternoon; the national park is open daylight hours
    • Visiting tips: Book a small-group or early-morning tour to beat peak crowds at James Bond Island. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a dry bag, and cash for park fees. Sea conditions can be rough in monsoon season (May–October).
  4. Old Phuket Town (Sino-Portuguese Quarter)

    A charming historic district of colorful Sino-Portuguese shophouses along Thalang Road, Soi Romanee, and Dibuk Road. The area is packed with cafés, galleries, boutique hotels, and street art—best explored on foot.

    • Ticket price: Free to walk; individual museums and galleries vary
    • Opening hours: Public streets accessible 24/7; shops and cafés typically 9:00 AM–9:00 PM
    • Visiting tips: Visit on Sunday evening for the Lard Yai Walking Street Market (approx. 4:00–10:00 PM). Go early morning for cooler temperatures and better photos of the architecture.
  5. Kata Beach & Karon Viewpoint

    Kata Beach offers calm swimming in high season and a relaxed family-friendly vibe, while nearby Karon Viewpoint (also called Kata Viewpoint) delivers one of the island's classic postcard panoramas over Kata Noi, Kata, and Karon beaches.

    • Ticket price: Free (beach access and viewpoint)
    • Opening hours: Beaches open 24/7; lifeguards generally on duty 9:00 AM–5:00 PM in high season
    • Visiting tips: Swim only where flags indicate safe conditions—rip currents are common in the southwest monsoon (May–October). Visit the viewpoint at sunrise or late afternoon for the best light.
  6. Phi Phi Islands (day trip from Phuket)

    World-famous archipelago of limestone cliffs, Maya Bay (from The Beach), and vibrant snorkeling sites. Most day tours depart from Rassada Pier or Chalong Pier and include stops at Phi Phi Don, Phi Phi Leh, and Bamboo Island.

    • Ticket price: National park fee 400 THB for adults (plus tour cost, typically 1,500–3,500 THB depending on speedboat vs. ferry and inclusions)
    • Opening hours: Tours typically depart 8:00–9:00 AM and return 4:00–6:00 PM; Maya Bay has seasonal closures for coral recovery (check current status before booking)
    • Visiting tips: Choose speedboat tours for shorter travel time, or overnight on Phi Phi Don to experience the islands after day-trippers leave. Bring motion sickness tablets if prone—crossings can be bumpy.
  7. Wat Phra Thong (Golden Buddha Temple)

    A lesser-visited temple in Thalang district famous for a half-buried golden Buddha image emerging from the earth—legend says attempts to excavate it brought misfortune. A peaceful, authentic alternative to busier temples.

    • Ticket price: Free entry; donations accepted
    • Opening hours: Daily, approximately 7:00 AM–5:00 PM
    • Visiting tips: Dress modestly and remove shoes before entering buildings. Combine with a visit to the nearby Thalang National Museum for local history.
  8. Khao Phra Thaeo National Park & Bang Pae Waterfall

    One of Phuket's last remaining rainforest areas, with trails to Bang Pae and Ton Sai waterfalls and the Gibbon Rehabilitation Project at the park entrance. A great half-day escape from the beaches.

    • Ticket price: Park entry 200 THB for adults; Gibbon Rehabilitation Project entry by donation (suggested 200 THB)
    • Opening hours: Daily, roughly 8:00 AM–5:00 PM
    • Visiting tips: Wear sturdy shoes—trails can be muddy after rain. Bring insect repellent. Visit the Gibbon Project in the morning when the animals are most active; do not feed or touch wildlife.
  9. Promthep Cape

    The southernmost tip of Phuket and the island's most famous sunset spot, with sweeping views over the Andaman Sea and nearby islands. A lighthouse and small shrine mark the viewpoint.

    • Ticket price: Free entry
    • Opening hours: Accessible 24/7; best visited late afternoon to sunset
    • Visiting tips: Arrive 45–60 minutes before sunset to secure a good spot and parking. Bring a light layer—it can be breezy. Combine with a stop at Nai Harn Beach or Rawai seafood restaurants nearby.
  10. Sirinat National Park & Nai Yang Beach

    A tranquil national park near the airport protecting casuarina-lined beaches, coral reefs, and sea turtle nesting grounds. Nai Yang Beach is ideal for plane-spotting, long walks, and low-key sunset dinners.

    • Ticket price: 200 THB for adults (park entry)
    • Opening hours: Daily, approximately 8:00 AM–5:00 PM for park facilities; beach accessible at all hours
    • Visiting tips: Best visited in the dry season (November–April) for clear water and snorkeling. Keep a respectful distance from turtle nesting areas and follow local signage. It's a convenient stop before or after a flight.

Practical Tips for Visiting Phuket's Attractions

  • Best time to visit: November to April offers dry, calm weather ideal for beaches and island trips. May–October is monsoon season—expect occasional rough seas, lower prices, and fewer crowds.
  • Dress code: Temples require covered shoulders and knees; sarongs are often available for rent or loan at major sites. Remove shoes before entering temple buildings.
  • Transport: Grab and Bolt are widely used and often cheaper than metered taxis. Renting a scooter is popular but requires an international driving permit and caution on hilly roads.
  • Tickets and tours: Book island tours through reputable operators and confirm whether national park fees are included. Prices listed here are indicative and can change—verify locally or on official park websites.
  • Cash: Many smaller attractions, park entrances, and donation boxes accept cash only. Carry small notes (20–100 THB).
  • Sun and sea safety: Use reef-safe sunscreen, stay hydrated, and swim only in flagged areas. Rip currents are a real hazard during the southwest monsoon.
  • Respect local culture: Avoid touching monks (especially women), do not climb on Buddha statues
