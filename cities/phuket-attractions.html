Phuket Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Phuket, Thailand's largest island, blends white-sand beaches, Sino-Portuguese heritage, and Buddhist temples with a lively resort scene. Most attractions cluster along the west coast and in Phuket Town, so planning by area saves both time and taxi fares. Below are ten standout sights with practical details on tickets, hours, and visiting tips. Prices and hours change periodically, so confirm locally before you go.

  1. Big Buddha (Phra Phuttha Ming Mongkol Akenakkiri)

    A 45-meter white marble-clad Buddha statue seated atop Nakkerd Hill between Kata and Chalong, offering panoramic views over the south of the island. It is both a landmark and an active religious site.

    Ticket priceFree entry; donations welcome
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 6:00–19:00
    Visiting tipsDress modestly (cover shoulders and knees); sarongs are available at the entrance. Go early morning or late afternoon for cooler temperatures and softer light. The hilltop road is steep—agree on a taxi fare or use a ride-hailing app.

  2. Wat Chalong (Chalong Temple)

    Phuket's largest and most revered Buddhist temple complex, with several ornate halls and a tall chedi said to house a fragment of the Buddha's bone. It is a working temple and a pilgrimage site.

    Ticket priceFree entry; donations accepted
    Opening hoursDaily, approximately 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsRemove shoes before entering buildings and dress respectfully. Avoid pointing feet at Buddha images. Firecrackers are sometimes set off near the grounds—expect noise. Weekday mornings are quietest.

  3. Old Phuket Town (Sino-Portuguese Quarter)

    A historic district of colorful Sino-Portuguese shophouses along Thalang Road and nearby lanes, home to cafés, galleries, and museums. The area reflects the island's tin-mining and Chinese immigrant heritage.

    Ticket priceFree to walk; individual museums charge separate entry fees
    Opening hoursPublic streets open 24 hours; shops and cafés typically 9:00–18:00
    Visiting tipsVisit on a Sunday evening for the Lard Yai walking street market on Thalang Road. Go early morning for photos without crowds and cooler weather. Wear comfortable shoes for uneven sidewalks.

  4. Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island

    A dramatic bay of limestone karsts northeast of Phuket, famous for Khao Phing Kan (James Bond Island) and the floating Muslim village of Koh Panyee. Most visitors arrive by organized boat tour.

    Ticket priceNational park entry around 300 THB for adults (foreign visitors); tour prices vary widely by operator and inclusions
    Opening hoursPark sites generally accessible during daylight; tours depart in the morning
    Visiting tipsChoose a small-group or early-morning tour to beat crowds at James Bond Island. Bring sun protection and a dry bag. Sea conditions can be rough in the monsoon season (roughly May–October)—check forecasts.

  5. Phi Phi Islands

    A world-famous island group southeast of Phuket known for turquoise water, snorkeling, and Maya Bay (made famous by the film "The Beach"). Day trips by speedboat are the most common way to visit.

    Ticket priceNational park fees apply (Maya Bay area); speedboat day tours typically range from about 1,200–2,500 THB depending on operator
    Opening hoursDay tours usually depart 8:00–9:00 and return late afternoon
    Visiting tipsMaya Bay has had periodic closures for reef recovery—check current status before booking. Book with licensed operators and confirm life jackets are provided. Bring reef-safe sunscreen and cash for park fees.

  6. Kata and Kata Noi Beaches

    Two adjoining west-coast beaches with calm swimming conditions in high season and a relaxed, family-friendly atmosphere. Kata Beach has restaurants and surf schools along the sand.

    Ticket priceFree (public beach)
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; swimming safest during daylight
    Visiting tipsSwim only where lifeguards flag safe zones—rip currents occur in the monsoon season. Rent sun loungers only from operators with clear pricing. Kata Noi is quieter and better for a low-key afternoon.

  7. Patong Beach & Bangla Road

    Phuket's busiest resort area, with a long beach by day and a dense entertainment strip on Bangla Road by night. It is the island's most commercial and energetic zone.

    Ticket priceFree (beach and street access)
    Opening hoursBeach open 24 hours; Bangla Road nightlife typically 20:00–late
    Visiting tipsKeep valuables secure and watch for pickpockets in crowds. Agree on prices before buying drinks or services. Families may prefer daytime visits; the beach is busiest in the late afternoon.

  8. Phuket Elephant Sanctuary

    An ethical sanctuary in the hills of Thalang where rescued elephants roam in forested enclosures. Visits are guided and focus on observation rather than riding.

    Ticket priceHalf-day visits typically around 3,000–4,000 THB per adult (varies by program)
    Opening hoursGuided visits by advance booking; morning and afternoon sessions
    Visiting tipsBook ahead—spots are limited. Wear closed shoes and clothes you don't mind getting muddy. Avoid any venue offering elephant rides or bathing shows, which are considered unethical.

  9. Khao Phra Thaeo National Park & Bang Pae Waterfall

    A rainforest park in northern Phuket with nature trails, a waterfall, and the Gibbon Rehabilitation Project near the park entrance. It offers a cool green escape from the beaches.

    Ticket pricePark entry around 200 THB for adults (foreign visitors); gibbon project accepts donations
    Opening hoursDaily, approximately 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsWear sturdy shoes for slippery trails and bring insect repellent. The waterfall flows best in the rainy season. Do not feed or approach wildlife, and follow signage on trail closures.

  10. Promthep Cape

    The southernmost viewpoint on Phuket, overlooking the Andaman Sea and nearby islands. It is the island's classic sunset spot, with a lighthouse and small shrine nearby.

    Ticket priceFree entry
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; best visited around sunset
    Visiting tipsArrive 30–45 minutes before sunset for parking and a good vantage point. Bring a light layer—it can be breezy. Combine the visit with nearby Nai Harn Beach or Rawai seafood restaurants.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Ride-hailing apps and metered taxis are convenient; songthaews (shared buses) are cheaper for Phuket Town routes. Agree on fares before entering unmetered taxis.
  • Dress code: Temples require covered shoulders and knees. Carry a light sarong or scarf.
  • Seasonality: High season (November–April) brings calm seas and higher prices; monsoon season (May–October) means rougher water, occasional closures, and lower rates.
  • Cash: Many smaller vendors and park entrances accept cash only. ATMs are widespread but charge fees.
  • Safety: Swim only in flagged areas and heed rip-current warnings. Use licensed tour operators and wear life jackets on boats.
  • Etiquette: Avoid touching people's heads or pointing feet at sacred images; treat Buddha images with respect.
  • Verify details: Ticket prices and opening hours change—confirm with official sources or your hotel before visiting.
