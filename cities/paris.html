Local Character

Paris is nicknamed the "City of Light" mainly for its leading role in the Age of Enlightenment, a label that still shapes how the city presents its intellectual and architectural heritage; visitors notice it in the emphasis on public institutions and grand civic spaces. The city's modern layout comes from Haussmann's renovation, which created new boulevards, parks, and public works and gave birth to a city known as the "capital of the 19th century" — the wide, uniform boulevards a visitor walks today are a direct product of that project, not an organic medieval street pattern. The Art Nouveau-decorated Métro has become a symbol of the city, so even a routine ride between arrondissements is a chance to see period design that a generic guide might skip. Parts of the city along the Seine have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991, which explains the protected riverbank setting that defines so much of daily life and strolling.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Louvre — The largest and most-visited museum in the world, housed in a former royal palace on the Right Bank.
  2. Musée d'Orsay — A noted collection of French Impressionist art set in a converted railway station.
  3. Musée de l'Orangerie — An Impressionist-focused museum known for its intimate scale in the Tuileries area.
  4. Seine riverbanks — Parts of the city along the Seine have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991.
  5. Musée Rodin — A museum noted for its modern and contemporary art collections, dedicated to the sculptor.
