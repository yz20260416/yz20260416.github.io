Xi'an Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Xi'an, the capital of Shaanxi Province, served as the capital of 13 Chinese dynasties and is one of the oldest cities in China. Best known as the eastern terminus of the Silk Road and home to the Terracotta Army, Xi'an blends ancient heritage with a lively modern food scene. Most major sights are concentrated inside or near the Ming-era city wall, making the city easy to explore on foot, by metro, or by bicycle. The list below covers the essential attractions, with practical details to help you plan your visit. Prices and hours can change seasonally, so confirm before you go.

Top 10 Attractions

  1. Terracotta Army (Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum)

    One of the world's greatest archaeological discoveries: thousands of life-size clay warriors, horses, and chariots buried to guard the tomb of China's first emperor. Three pits are open to visitors, with Pit 1 being the largest and most impressive.

    • Tickets: About CNY 120 (adult); discounts for students and seniors; children under 1.2 m free.
    • Hours: Roughly 8:30–17:00 (last entry around 16:30); longer hours in peak summer season.
    • Tips: Located about 35 km east of the city; take metro Line 9 to Huaqing Pool Station then a shuttle bus, or join a tour. Arrive at opening to beat tour groups. Hire a guide or audio guide for context. Combine with Huaqing Palace nearby.
  2. Xi'an City Wall

    One of the best-preserved ancient city walls in China, dating to the Ming dynasty. The 13.7 km rectangular circuit offers superb views and is a favorite spot for cycling.

    • Tickets: About CNY 54; bicycle rental extra (roughly CNY 45 for 2 hours, deposit required).
    • Hours: Approximately 8:00–22:00 (varies by gate and season).
    • Tips: Enter at the South Gate (Yongningmen), the most atmospheric. Cycling the full loop takes 1.5–2 hours. Visit near sunset for cooler weather and illuminated views.
  3. Shaanxi History Museum

    A leading provincial museum with a vast collection spanning prehistoric to Tang dynasty artifacts, including Tang tri-color glazed ceramics and ancient bronzes. Free general admission with limited daily tickets; the Treasures Gallery requires a separate paid ticket.

    • Tickets: Free (reservation required); special exhibitions around CNY 30–300.
    • Hours: About 9:00–17:30, closed Mondays (except public holidays).
    • Tips: Book free tickets online in advance (passport number needed); slots sell out quickly in peak season. Arrive early and head to popular galleries first.
  4. Big Wild Goose Pagoda (Dayan Pagoda)

    A 7th-century Buddhist pagoda built to house sutras brought from India by the monk Xuanzang. The surrounding Da Ci'en Temple complex and the north square fountain show make it a lively area day and night.

    • Tickets: Temple about CNY 40–50; extra fee (around CNY 30) to climb the pagoda.
    • Hours: Roughly 8:00–17:30 (temple); the square is open all day.
    • Tips: The North Square musical fountain show (evenings) is free and popular. Combine with the nearby Shaanxi History Museum.
  5. Muslim Quarter (Huimin Jie)

    A bustling neighborhood centered on the Great Mosque, famous for street food and Hui Muslim culture. Best explored in the evening when the food stalls are at full swing.

    • Tickets: Free to enter the quarter; Great Mosque admission about CNY 25.
    • Hours: Streets open all day; food stalls busiest roughly 17:00–late.
    • Tips: Try roujiamo (Chinese "burger"), yangrou paomo (lamb soup with bread), and biangbiang noodles. Bring cash or use mobile payment; watch belongings in crowds.
  6. Bell Tower and Drum Tower

    Twin Ming-dynasty towers marking the historic center of the city. The Bell Tower sits at the crossroads of the four main streets; the Drum Tower is a short walk northwest. Both are illuminated at night.

    • Tickets: About CNY 30 each, or a combined ticket around CNY 50.
    • Hours: Approximately 8:30–21:00 (varies by season).
    • Tips: Drum performances are held several times daily. View them from street level at night for free; climb for city views if time allows.
  7. Huaqing Palace (Huaqing Pool)

    A royal hot-spring resort at the foot of Mount Li, associated with the Tang dynasty and the famous love story of Emperor Xuanzong and Yang Guifei. The evening "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" outdoor performance is a highlight.

    • Tickets: About CNY 120; evening show tickets separate and higher.
    • Hours: Roughly 7:00–19:00 (seasonal).
    • Tips: Pairs naturally with the Terracotta Army on a day trip east of the city. Book show tickets in advance during holidays.
  8. Giant Wild Goose Pagoda (Jianfu Temple)

    A quieter, older pagoda south of the city wall, built in the Tang dynasty. Less crowded than its "Big" counterpart and set in a peaceful temple compound.

    • Tickets: Temple around CNY 50; pagoda climb extra (about CNY 30).
    • Hours: Approximately 8:00–17:00.
    • Tips: A good stop for travelers seeking a calmer temple experience; combine with the nearby Xi'an Museum if time permits.
  9. Tang Paradise (Datang Furong Garden)

    A large reconstructed Tang-style garden and cultural park with lakes, pavilions, and evening light-and-water shows. Popular with families and photographers.

    • Tickets: About CNY 120 (varies by season and show).
    • Hours: Roughly 9:00–22:00 in peak season.
    • Tips: Visit in late afternoon to catch the gardens in daylight and stay for the evening performances. Check show schedules on arrival.
  10. Mount Hua (Huashan)

    One of China's Five Great Mountains, famed for dramatic granite peaks, cliffside plank walks, and cable cars. A long but rewarding day trip from Xi'an (about 120 km east).

    • Tickets: About CNY 160 (peak season); cable cars and shuttle buses extra (roughly CNY 80–140 each way depending on route).
    • Hours: Roughly 7:00–19:00 (seasonal; last cable car times vary).
    • Tips: Take the high-speed train from Xi'an North Station to Huashan North (about 30–40 minutes), then a shuttle. Wear sturdy shoes, carry water, and avoid weekends and holidays. The West Peak cable car is the easiest ascent.
