Top Attractions in Xi'an

Xi'an, the ancient capital of Chang'an, is one of China's most historically rich cities, home to imperial tombs, Buddhist temples, and the world-famous Terracotta Army. From Ming-dynasty towers in the city center to Tang-era pagodas and lively Muslim Quarter streets, the city offers a compact journey through thousands of years of Chinese civilization.

  1. Terracotta Army — This collection of terracotta sculptures depicts the armies of Qin Shi Huang, China's first emperor, and was buried with him around 210–209 BCE to protect him in the afterlife. Discovered in 1974 by local farmers in Lintong County, the figures vary in height by rank, with generals the tallest.
    Tip: Arrive early in the morning to beat tour groups; the site is about an hour's drive east of central Xi'an and pairs well with the Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum.
  2. Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang — This vast tomb complex was built over 38 years (246–208 BC) for the first Qin emperor, beneath a 76-metre-tall tomb mound shaped like a truncated pyramid. Its layout was modeled on Xianyang, the Qin capital.
    Tip: Combine with the Terracotta Army on the same trip; the mound itself is largely unexcavated, so focus on the surrounding pits and exhibits.
  3. Giant Wild Goose Pagoda — This seven-storey Buddhist pagoda in the Daci'en Temple complex was first built in 652 under the monk Xuanzang, whose pilgrimage to India inspired Journey to the West. Its present brick exterior reflects major Ming-dynasty repairs.
    Tip: Visit in the late afternoon, then stay for the fountain show on the North Square in the evening.
  4. Daci'en Temple — Located in Yanta District, this temple is considered the cradle of East Asian Yogācāra Buddhism and is notable as the home of the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda. It was one of the three great sutra translation sites of the Tang dynasty.
    Tip: Entry to the temple and pagoda is ticketed separately from the surrounding square; allow about two hours.
  5. Bell Tower of Xi'an — Built in 1384 during the early Ming dynasty, this tower is a symbol of the city and one of the grandest of its kind in China, positioned on the axis of the Imperial Way. A replica of the Tang-dynasty Jingyun Bell now hangs in the tower.
    Tip: Go after dark when the tower is illuminated; it sits at the heart of the city center, steps from the Drum Tower.
  6. Drum Tower of Xi'an — Erected in 1380 during the Ming dynasty, the Drum Tower stands above the city center and offers sweeping views of Xi'an. It sits in the predominantly Muslim district known as the Drum Tower Muslim District.
    Tip: Pair with the Bell Tower and the Muslim Quarter below; evening drum performances are a highlight.
  7. Xi'an Muslim Quarter — This snack and commercial street in central Xi'an sits close to the Bell and Drum Towers and contains ten mosques, the largest being the Great Mosque. It is the city's liveliest district for street food and local culture.
    Tip: Come hungry in the evening for the best street food atmosphere; it is a short walk from the Drum Tower.
  8. Great Mosque of Xi'an — Also known as the Huajue Xiang Mosque for its location on Huajue Lane, it is one of the largest premodern mosques in China, with traditional accounts dating its founding to 742. It is a state-protected heritage site prized for its traditional architecture.
    Tip: Located inside the Muslim Quarter; dress modestly and visit outside prayer times.
  9. Small Wild Goose Pagoda — This Tang-dynasty brick pagoda was built between 707 and 709 in the former Jianfu Temple precinct, when Xi'an was the imperial capital Chang'an. Earthquake damage destroyed the upper two of its original fifteen storeys, leaving the present thirteen-storey structure about 43 metres tall.
    Tip: Quieter than its larger counterpart; combine with a stroll in the surrounding park.
  10. Banpo — Discovered in 1953, this Neolithic archaeological site in the Yellow River valley represents the first phase of the Yangshao culture (c. 5000–3000 BCE) and features well-organized settlements surrounded by a defensive ditch. It is one of the earliest examples of planned village life in China.
    Tip: East of central Xi'an and easy to reach by bus; the on-site museum displays artifacts from the excavations.

Practical tips

  • Visit the Terracotta Army and Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum on a weekday morning to avoid the biggest crowds.
  • Many central sights — the Bell Tower, Drum Tower, Muslim Quarter, and Great Mosque — are within walking distance of each other; plan them as one loop.
  • Book tickets for major sites like the Terracotta Army and Giant Wild Goose Pagoda in advance, especially in peak season.
  • Evenings are ideal for the illuminated Bell and Drum Towers and for street food in the Muslim Quarter.
  • Wear comfortable shoes and carry water; temple and tomb complexes involve considerable walking.
