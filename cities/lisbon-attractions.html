Top Attractions in Lisbon

Lisbon rewards visitors with a blend of maritime history, grand monuments, and lively neighbourhoods. From the Manueline splendour of Belém to the medieval lanes beneath São Jorge Castle, the city packs centuries of discovery into a compact, walkable core. This guide highlights ten standout sights most likely to appeal to an international traveller, each drawn from Lisbon's rich heritage of fortresses, monasteries, museums, and gardens.

  1. Belém Tower (Torre de Belém) — This 16th-century fortification served as a point of embarkation and disembarkation for Portuguese explorers and as a ceremonial gateway to Lisbon. The tower symbolises Portugal's maritime and colonial power in early modern Europe, built during the height of the Portuguese Renaissance.
  2. Jerónimos Monastery — A former monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome near the Tagus in Belém, it became the necropolis of the Portuguese royal dynasty of Aviz in the 16th century. It is one of Lisbon's most celebrated monuments and a masterpiece of Manueline architecture.
  3. São Jorge Castle — A historic castle in the Santa Maria Maior parish whose hill has been occupied since at least the 8th century BC, with the oldest fortifications dating to the 2nd century BC. The site has played an important part in Lisbon's history and offers sweeping views over the city.
  4. Lisbon Oceanarium (Oceanário de Lisboa) — Located in Parque das Nações, the former Expo '98 exhibition grounds, it is one of the largest indoor aquariums in Europe. Approximately one million people visit each year.
  5. Belém Cultural Center (Centro Cultural de Belém) — A complex of artistic venues in Belém and the largest building with cultural facilities in Portugal, with over 140,000 m² of usable space. It was initially built for Portugal's 1992 Presidency of the European Council and now provides permanent venues for conferences, exhibitions, and performance arts.
  6. Church of Santa Engrácia (National Pantheon) — A 17th-century monument in Alfama, originally a church, converted into the National Pantheon where important Portuguese personalities are buried. It stands close to the Monastery of São Vicente de Fora.
  7. Casa dos Bicos — A historical house in Alfama built in the early 16th century, with a curious spiked façade influenced by Italian Renaissance palaces and Portuguese Manueline styles. It survived the 1755 earthquake and now houses the José Saramago Foundation.
  8. Lisbon Zoo (Jardim Zoológico de Lisboa) — Founded in 1884 and now located at Quinta das Laranjeiras in Sete Rios, it houses about 2,000 animals of more than 300 species. Its mission includes conservation and breeding programmes.
  9. Águas Livres Aqueduct — A historic aqueduct system considered one of the most remarkable examples of 18th-century Portuguese engineering and one of Lisbon's best-known landmarks. The main aqueduct extends about 14.2 km, while the complete network reaches nearly 58 km.
  10. Estufa Fria — A greenhouse containing three distinct gardens, located in Edward VII Park between Alameda Engenheiro Edgar Cardoso and Alameda Cardeal Cerejeira. It offers a lush, sheltered escape within the city.

Practical tips

  • Visit Belém's Tower and Jerónimos Monastery early in the morning to avoid the longest queues, as both are among the city's busiest sights.
  • Many Belém-area attractions are close together, so plan a single day combining the tower, monastery, and Belém Cultural Center on foot.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for São Jorge Castle and Alfama, whose steep, cobbled lanes reward slow exploration.
  • The Lisbon Oceanarium and Parque das Nações are easily reached by metro and make a good half-day outing away from the historic centre.
  • Check opening days in advance for cultural venues such as the Belém Cultural Center and Casa dos Bicos, which may close on certain days.
