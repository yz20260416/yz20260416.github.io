New York City: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

New York City packs an extraordinary concentration of iconic sights into five boroughs, from world-class museums and soaring observation decks to leafy parks and historic landmarks. This guide covers the ten attractions most travelers should prioritize, with current-style ticket prices (always verify on official websites, as prices change), opening hours, and practical tips to help you plan efficiently and avoid common pitfalls.

  1. Statue of Liberty & Ellis Island

    The Statue of Liberty National Monument, reached by ferry from Battery Park, combines the iconic 1886 copper statue with the Ellis Island National Museum of Immigration, where millions of arrivals were processed. Allow at least half a day for both islands.

    • Tickets: Ferry and grounds access from about $25 (adult); pedestal access roughly $25–$30; crown access about $26–$27 (crown tickets sell out far in advance). Children and seniors discounted.
    • Hours: Ferries typically run 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. (extended in summer); first departure from Battery Park around 9:00 a.m.
    • Tips: Book crown/pedestal tickets weeks ahead on the official operator's site (City Cruises is the authorized ferry provider). Arrive at security 30–45 minutes before your departure. Liberty State Park in New Jersey offers shorter lines.

  2. Central Park

    The 843-acre Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux masterpiece offers lakes, meadows, woodland trails, Bethesda Terrace, Belvedere Castle, and the Central Park Zoo. It is free and open daily from 6:00 a.m. to 1:00 a.m.

    • Tickets: Free entry; paid add-ons include the zoo (about $20 adult), rowboat rentals (about $20/hour), and carriage or pedicab tours.
    • Hours: 6:00 a.m.–1:00 a.m. daily.
    • Tips: Enter at Grand Army Plaza (59th St) or Columbus Circle for the classic experience. Rent a bike or join a guided walking tour. Avoid the southern loop on weekend afternoons when crowds peak.

  3. Metropolitan Museum of Art

    One of the world's great encyclopedic museums, the Met holds over two million works spanning 5,000 years, from Egyptian temples to European masters and the Costume Institute. The Cloisters branch in Fort Tryon Park focuses on medieval art.

    • Tickets: About $30 for adults; free for New York State residents and students (pay-what-you-wish). Children under 12 free.
    • Hours: Typically Sunday–Tuesday and Thursday 10:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.; Friday–Saturday 10:00 a.m.–9:00 p.m.; closed Wednesday (verify current schedule).
    • Tips: Enter via the 81st Street entrance for shorter lines. Focus on two or three wings rather than trying to see everything. Friday evenings are lively but crowded.

  4. Empire State Building

    The 1931 Art Deco skyscraper's 86th-floor open-air observatory and 102nd-floor glass-enclosed deck deliver classic Manhattan views. The building is also a National Historic Landmark.

    • Tickets: 86th floor from about $44–$48; 102nd floor combo roughly $75–$80. Discounts for children and seniors; skip-the-line options cost more.
    • Hours: Generally 9:00 a.m.–midnight (last elevator around 11:15 p.m.); extended summer hours.
    • Tips: Go at sunset for day and night views in one visit. Buy timed tickets online to avoid ticket-line waits. Security screening is mandatory.

  5. Top of the Rock (Rockefeller Center)

    The 70th-floor observation decks at 30 Rockefeller Plaza offer the best views of the Empire State Building and Central Park, plus a skyline panorama south to One World Trade Center.

    • Tickets: From about $40–$45 for adults; combo and express passes higher. Children and seniors discounted.
    • Hours: Typically 9:00 a.m.–midnight (last entry around 11:00 p.m.).
    • Tips: Book a sunset slot for the classic photo of the Empire State Building glowing against the sky. The Beam experience (photo op) costs extra.

  6. Times Square & Broadway

    The neon-lit crossroads of the world at Broadway and Seventh Avenue is free to wander day or night, and the surrounding Theater District hosts the world's largest concentration of Broadway shows.

    • Tickets: Times Square free; Broadway shows typically $80–$250+, with rush, lottery, and TKTS discount tickets (same-day, 20–50% off) available.
    • Hours: Times Square open 24/7; theaters generally curtain at 7:00–8:00 p.m. with Wednesday and weekend matinees.
    • Tips: Visit after dark for the full effect. Use the TKTS booth in Duffy Square for same-day discounts. Avoid costumed characters who demand payment for photos.

  7. Brooklyn Bridge

    Completed in 1883, this Gothic-arched suspension bridge offers a free, 1.3-mile pedestrian walkway with sweeping views of the Manhattan skyline and harbor. Walk from Manhattan to Brooklyn for the best photo angles.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Pedestrian walkway open 24 hours.
    • Tips: Go at sunrise or early morning to beat crowds; the walkway is narrow and busy midday. Combine with a stroll through Brooklyn Bridge Park and DUMBO. Wear comfortable shoes.

  8. 9/11 Memorial & Museum

    The memorial plaza's twin reflecting pools mark the footprints of the Twin Towers, while the underground museum documents the events of September 11, 2001, and their aftermath with artifacts, recordings, and exhibits.

    • Tickets: Memorial plaza free; museum about $33–$36 adults, with discounts for students, seniors, and free admission on select evenings (verify).
    • Hours: Memorial plaza typically 8:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m. (daily); museum generally 9:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m. (last entry 5:30 p.m.).
    • Tips: Book museum tickets online for a timed entry. The museum is emotionally intense; allow 2–3 hours. Combine with nearby One World Observatory and the Oculus.

  9. High Line & Chelsea Market

    The High Line is a 1.45-mile elevated park built on a former freight rail line, threading gardens, public art, and skyline views from Gansevoort Street to 34th Street. Chelsea Market, beneath it, is a food hall in a former factory.

    • Tickets: High Line free; Chelsea Market free to enter (food costs vary).
    • Hours: High Line typically 7:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m. (seasonal); Chelsea Market roughly 7:00 a.m.–9:00–10:00 p.m. daily.
    • Tips: Walk south to north and exit near Hudson Yards for the Vessel and Edge observation deck. Weekday mornings are quietest. The High Line gets crowded on sunny weekends.

  10. American Museum of Natural History

    Facing Central Park on the Upper West Side, this museum houses the blue whale, dinosaur halls, Hayden Planetarium, and the Rose Center for Earth and Space—an excellent choice for families and science lovers.

    • Tickets: General admission about $28–$30 adults (includes pay-what-you-wish for New York residents); planetarium and special exhibitions extra.
    • Hours: Generally 10:00 a.m.–5:30 p.m. daily (closed Thanksgiving and Christmas).
    • Tips: Enter at the 81st Street/Rose Center entrance for shorter lines. Book the planetarium show in advance. Allow 3–4 hours; the dinosaur halls are busiest midday.

Practical Tips for Visiting New York

  • Get an MTA MetroCard or OMNY tap: The subway runs 24/7 and is the fastest way around; OMNY contactless payment is now standard. Weekly unlimited passes offer good value for multi-day trips.
  • Book major attractions online: Observation decks, the Statue of Liberty, and the 9/11 Museum sell out; timed tickets save hours of queueing.
  • Consider a city pass: The New York CityPASS or Sightseeing Pass can save 20–40% if you plan to visit four or more paid attractions.
  • Watch for hidden fees: Restaurants add tax and tips; many attractions have security screening (no large bags at museums and observation decks).
  • Walk smart: Distances between neighborhoods are long; combine walking with the subway. Comfortable shoes are essential.
  • Check official websites: Hours and prices change seasonally and after renovations—always confirm before you go.
  • Best seasons: Spring and fall offer the most comfortable weather; summer is hot and crowded, and winter brings holiday crowds and cold winds.
