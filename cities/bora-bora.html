Local Character

Bora Bora's local character is steeped in Polynesian heritage and a history of powerful chiefs and kings. The island was known as 'Porapora mai te pora'—'created by the gods'—a name that reflects its sacred status in ancient Tahitian culture. Visitors should care because this origin story is still echoed in local legends and ceremonies, offering a glimpse into the island's spiritual roots. Another distinctive detail is that Bora Bora was an independent kingdom until 1888, when France annexed it and forced Queen Teriimaevarua III to abdicate. This relatively recent royal history explains the presence of traditional marae (stone temples) and the continued respect for royal lineage among locals. The island's economy historically relied on copra production from its abundant coconut trees, and you can still see remnants of this industry in the form of drying coconut flesh along the roadsides. Understanding this copra heritage helps visitors appreciate the resourcefulness of the islanders beyond the luxury resorts. The main settlement, Vaitape, sits on the western side opposite the main lagoon channel, and its laid-back pace contrasts with the polished resorts, giving travelers a taste of everyday Polynesian life.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Mount Otemanu — The highest peak on Bora Bora at 727 meters, offering challenging hikes and spectacular panoramic views of the lagoon.
  2. Lagoonarium — A natural aquarium on a motu where visitors can snorkel with sharks, rays, and colorful fish in a protected lagoon.
  3. Vaitape — The main settlement on the western side, home to the island's shops, restaurants, and the historic Protestant church founded in 1890.
  4. World War II Relics — Remnants of the U.S. military base from Operation Bobcat, including eight 7-inch coastal defense guns that remain in place around the island.
  5. Coral Gardens — A popular snorkeling site with vibrant coral formations and abundant marine life in the shallow lagoon.

Practical Information

  • Currency: CFP Franc (XPF)
  • Language: Tahitian and French (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: TAHT (UTC-10)
  • Visa: Visitors from most Western countries can stay visa-free for up to 90 days; check French Polynesia entry requirements.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Bora Bora?
The dry season from May to October offers the most reliable weather, with lower rainfall and comfortable temperatures.
Do I need to speak French to get around?
Not necessarily; English is commonly spoken in tourist areas, though learning a few Tahitian or French phrases is appreciated.
What currency is used in Bora Bora?
The CFP Franc (XPF) is the official currency, and major credit cards are accepted at most resorts and restaurants.
