Top Attractions in Kyoto

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, and its landscape is layered with UNESCO-listed temples, imperial residences, and living festival traditions. From the bamboo groves and monkey park of Arashiyama to the gold-standard Zen gardens of Daisen-in, the city rewards slow exploration. This guide highlights ten attractions that best capture Kyoto's historic and scenic character for the first-time international visitor.

  1. Arashiyama

    This district on Kyoto's western outskirts is a nationally designated historic site and place of scenic beauty, with the mountain across the Ōi River forming its backdrop. It is the classic base for riverside strolls and mountain views.

    Tip: Arrive early in the morning to beat the crowds, and pair it with the nearby monkey park across the river.

  2. Iwatayama Monkey Park

    Located atop Mount Arashiyama opposite the train station, this park is home to a troop of over 120 wild Japanese macaques. Visitors can feed the monkeys with food purchased on site while enjoying views over the district.

    Tip: Wear comfortable shoes for the uphill walk, and keep food out of sight until inside the feeding enclosure.

  3. Daisen-in

    A sub-temple of Daitoku-ji in the Rinzai Zen school, Daisen-in was built between 1509 and 1513 and is famed for its screen paintings and dry landscape (kare-sansui) garden. Both are attributed to the artist Sōami.

    Tip: Visit on a weekday morning for quieter contemplation of the garden; it sits within the Daitoku-ji temple complex in northern Kyoto.

  4. Kyoto Imperial Palace

    The former residence of the Emperor of Japan from 1331 to 1869, the palace is located in Kamigyō-ku and its grounds are now open to the public. The Imperial Household Agency hosts public tours of the buildings several times a day.

    Tip: Combine your visit with a walk through the surrounding Kyoto Gyoen National Garden; check tour times in advance.

  5. Kyoto Gyoen National Garden

    This 65-hectare national garden was established after the capital moved from Kyoto to Tokyo and surrounds the Kyoto Imperial Palace. It offers broad lawns and tree-lined paths in the heart of the city.

    Tip: It is free to enter and especially pleasant during cherry blossom season and autumn foliage.

  6. Katsura Imperial Villa

    An imperial residence with gardens and outbuildings on the western bank of the Katsura River, about 8 km from the main Kyoto Imperial Palace. The villa and gardens are nationally recognized as an Important Cultural Property.

    Tip: Advance reservations are typically required for guided tours, so book well ahead of your trip.

  7. Fushimi Castle

    Also known as Momoyama Castle, it was originally constructed from 1592 to 1594 by Toyotomi Hideyoshi as a retirement residence. The castle was destroyed by the 1596 earthquake, rebuilt, and later demolished in 1623.

    Tip: The current reconstruction sits in Fushimi Ward; combine it with a visit to the nearby sake-brewing district.

  8. Kyoto Tower

    Now officially renamed Nidec Kyoto Tower, this steel tower is the tallest structure in Kyoto, with an observation deck at 100 metres and a spire reaching 131 metres. It stands atop a nine-story building opposite Kyoto Station.

    Tip: Go up around sunset for city views, and use it as an easy orientation stop on arrival day.

  9. Kyoto Botanical Garden

    A major 240,000 m² botanical garden with a conservatory, located next to the Kamo River in Sakyō-ku and first established in 1924. It is open daily and charges a general admission fee, with an extra charge for the conservatory.

    Tip: Allow at least half a day, and check seasonal bloom schedules before visiting.

  10. Gion Matsuri

    One of Japan's largest and most famous festivals, held annually throughout July in central Kyoto and at Yasaka Shrine in the Gion district. Formally a Shinto festival, its original purpose was purification and pacification of disease-causing entities.

    Tip: The grand procession and street festivities draw huge crowds—arrive early and expect road closures.

Practical tips

  • Festival timing: Kyoto's three major festivals are spread across the year—Aoi Matsuri (May 15), Gion Matsuri (July), and Jidai Matsuri (October 22)—so plan your trip around one if festivals interest you.
  • Book ahead: Imperial properties such as Katsura Imperial Villa and the Kyoto Imperial Palace require advance reservations or timed tours.
  • Start early: Popular districts like Arashiyama and Gion are far calmer before mid-morning.
  • Use the rivers as guides: The Kamo River links the Botanical Garden and central Kyoto, while the Ōi River frames Arashiyama—handy for orienting yourself.
  • Seasonal events: Gozan no Okuribi on August 16 lights five giant bonfires on the surrounding mountains, a spectacular Obon finale worth planning around.
