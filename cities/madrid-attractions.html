Top Attractions in Madrid

Madrid rewards visitors who look beyond the obvious, blending royal history, world-class art, and green escapes across the city. From the Habsburg-era lanes of the old centre to glass palaces in Retiro Park and a buzzing Sunday flea market, the Spanish capital offers something for every kind of traveller. Here are ten attractions most likely to appeal to an international visitor.

  1. El Madrid de los Austrias
    This is the name given to Madrid's old centre, built during the reign of the Habsburg Dynasty (1516–1700), located south of Calle Mayor between the Sol and Ópera metro stations. Wandering its historic streets is the best way to feel the city's imperial past.
    Tip: Visit in the early morning to enjoy the plazas and narrow lanes before the crowds arrive.
  2. Royal House of the Post Office (Real Casa de Correos)
    This eighteenth-century building on Puerta del Sol was originally built for the postal service and now serves as the office of the President of the Community of Madrid. It anchors the city's most famous square and is a natural starting point for any walking tour.
    Tip: Combine your visit with Puerta del Sol at dusk, when the square is at its liveliest.
  3. Market of San Miguel (Mercado de San Miguel)
    Originally built in 1916, this covered market was purchased by private investors in 2003, who renovated its iron structure and reopened it in 2009. Today it is a stylish spot for tapas and local produce near Plaza Mayor.
    Tip: Arrive mid-morning or late afternoon to avoid the peak lunchtime crush.
  4. El Rastro
    El Rastro is Madrid's most popular open-air flea market, held every Sunday and public holiday along Plaza de Cascorro and Ribera de Curtidores, just south of La Latina metro station. A huge variety of new and used goods is on offer, and nearby antique shops also open on Sundays.
    Tip: Go early in the morning for the best browsing and keep an eye on your belongings in the crowds.
  5. Teatro de la Comedia
    Inaugurated on Calle del Príncipe in 1875 and rebuilt in 1915 after a devastating fire, this theatre became the headquarters of the National Classical Theater Company in 1986. It reopened in 2015 after a long renovation and remains a landmark of Madrid's literary quarter.
    Tip: Check the programme in advance and pair a show with a stroll through the nearby Barrio de las Letras.
  6. Real Jardín Botánico de Madrid
    This 8-hectare Royal Botanical Garden is entered from Plaza de Murillo, right next to the Prado Museum. It offers a peaceful counterpoint to the city's art museums and is ideal for a shaded break between gallery visits.
    Tip: Visit in spring for peak blooms, and combine it with a morning at the Prado next door.
  7. Palacio de Cristal del Retiro
    This 19th-century conservatory in Buen Retiro Park is shaped like a Greek cross and made almost entirely of glass set in an iron framework on a brick base, topped by a cupola over 22 metres high. It is currently used for art exhibitions.
    Tip: Entry to the exhibitions is generally free; go on a weekday morning for quieter photos.
  8. Palacio de Velázquez
    Built in 1881–83 for the National Mining Exhibition by architect Ricardo Velázquez Bosco, this exhibition hall in Buen Retiro Park now functions as an arts and crafts gallery and is listed as a Bien de Interés Cultural. Its striking interior makes it a worthwhile stop within the park.
    Tip: Pair it with the Palacio de Cristal on a single loop through Retiro Park.
  9. Forest of Remembrance (Bosque del Recuerdo)
    This memorial garden in Buen Retiro Park commemorates the 191 civilian victims of the 2004 Madrid train bombings and the special forces agent who died during the subsequent siege. It is a solemn, beautifully planted corner of the park.
    Tip: Visit respectfully and quietly; it sits within easy walking distance of the park's main paths.
  10. El Capricho Park
    Created by María Josefa Pimentel, Duchess of Osuna (1752–1834), on her estate at Alameda de Osuna, this park is landscaped in eighteenth-century style with formal and naturalistic features and some English influence. It is recognised as one of the most beautiful parks in the city.
    Tip: Check opening days before you go, as the park has limited hours, and allow time for the journey to Alameda de Osuna.

Practical tips

  • Many of Madrid's best experiences — the Habsburg old centre, Puerta del Sol, Retiro Park and El Rastro — are free to explore on foot.
  • Plan El Rastro for a Sunday or public holiday, and note that many nearby antique shops open only on Sundays.
  • Retiro Park deserves at least half a day: the Palacio de Cristal, Palacio de Velázquez and Forest of Remembrance are all within its grounds.
  • Pair the Royal Botanical Garden with the Prado Museum, since the garden entrance is at Plaza de Murillo right beside it.
  • Check opening hours in advance for El Capricho and the theatres, as schedules can be limited or seasonal.
