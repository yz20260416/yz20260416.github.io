Madrid Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Madrid combines royal palaces, world-class art museums, and lively plazas in a compact, walkable center. The city rewards both first-time visitors and repeat travelers, with major sights clustered around the Paseo del Prado, the Royal Palace, and the historic Austrias quarter. Below are the ten attractions most worth your time, with current ticket guidance, opening hours, and practical tips. Prices and hours change seasonally, so always confirm on official websites before visiting.

  1. Museo del Prado

    Spain's flagship art museum and one of the world's great collections, the Prado holds masterpieces by Velázquez (Las Meninas), Goya (The Third of May 1808), El Greco, Titian, and Bosch. The collection spans roughly 8,000 paintings, with several hundred on display at any time.

    Ticket priceGeneral admission around €15; reduced prices for students and seniors; free entry during limited evening hours (typically the last two hours daily and Sunday afternoons—verify current schedule)
    Opening hoursMonday–Saturday 10:00–20:00; Sunday and holidays 10:00–19:00; closed January 1, May 1, and December 25
    Visiting tipBook timed tickets online to skip the queue. Focus on one floor per visit—the museum is enormous. The Goya Black Paintings and Velázquez rooms are highlights; go early or late for quieter galleries.

  2. Museo Reina Sofía

    Spain's national museum of 20th-century art, home to Picasso's monumental Guernica as well as major works by Dalí, Miró, and Juan Gris. The collection covers Cubism, Surrealism, and postwar Spanish art in a converted 18th-century hospital building.

    Ticket priceGeneral admission around €12; free entry during limited evening hours and Sunday afternoons (verify current schedule)
    Opening hoursMonday and Wednesday–Saturday 10:00–21:00; Sunday 10:00–19:00; closed Tuesday; shortened hours on some holidays
    Visiting tipGuernica is in a dedicated gallery—check its location on the museum map first. The Sabatini and Nouvel buildings are connected; allow at least two to three hours.

  3. Royal Palace of Madrid (Palacio Real)

    The official residence of the Spanish monarchy and the largest royal palace in Western Europe by floor area, with more than 3,000 rooms. Visitors tour the opulent state rooms, the Royal Armoury, and the Royal Pharmacy. The changing of the guard takes place on select days.

    Ticket priceGeneral admission around €13–15; reduced for children, students, and seniors; free entry during limited afternoon hours on some weekdays (verify current schedule)
    Opening hoursTypically daily 10:00–18:00 in winter and 10:00–20:00 in summer; closed on official state occasions—check ahead
    Visiting tipArrive at opening or book online; queues build quickly. Combine with the nearby Almudena Cathedral and the Sabatini Gardens for a half-day in the Austrias quarter.

  4. Plaza Mayor

    Madrid's grandest square, built in the 17th century and enclosed by arcaded buildings with frescoed facades. Once the site of markets, festivals, and bullfights, it is now a lively hub of cafés and terraces beneath the Casa de la Panadería's painted exterior.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; cafés and shops keep their own hours
    Visiting tipVisit early morning for photos without crowds. Café prices on the square are high—walk a block or two away for better value. The San Miguel Market is a short stroll southwest.

  5. Puerta del Sol

    The geographic and symbolic heart of Madrid, where the city's kilometer-zero marker sits and where Spaniards traditionally gather on New Year's Eve to eat twelve grapes at midnight. The square is home to the famous El Oso y el Madroño (Bear and Strawberry Tree) statue and the Tío Pepe neon sign.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours
    Visiting tipIt is a major metro interchange and always busy—watch your belongings. Use it as a starting point for walks to Plaza Mayor, the Royal Palace, and the Gran Vía.

  6. Retiro Park (Parque del Retiro)

    A 125-hectare former royal retreat and Madrid's most beloved park, with tree-lined avenues, a boating lake, the Crystal Palace (Palacio de Cristal), and the elegant rose garden. It is a UNESCO-listed landscape as part of the Paseo del Prado and Buen Retiro ensemble.

    Ticket priceFree; small fee for rowboat rental on the lake
    Opening hoursGenerally daily from 06:00 until 22:00–24:00 depending on season
    Visiting tipEnter from the Puerta de Alcalá side and walk to the lake and Crystal Palace. Rent a rowboat in the afternoon, and combine the visit with the Prado and Thyssen museums nearby.

  7. Museo Thyssen-Bornemisza

    One of the "Golden Triangle of Art" museums, housed in the Palacio de Villahermosa. Its collection traces European art from the 13th to the 20th century, with works by Caravaggio, Rembrandt, Van Gogh, Monet, and Hopper.

    Ticket priceGeneral admission around €13; reduced rates available; free entry on certain evenings (verify current schedule)
    Opening hoursTuesday–Sunday 10:00–19:00 (Saturdays until 21:00 in some seasons); closed Monday
    Visiting tipIdeal for travelers who want a manageable, chronological overview of European art in two to three hours. A combined Paseo del Prado art ticket can cover multiple museums.

  8. Plaza de Cibeles and Cibeles Palace

    One of Madrid's most photographed landmarks, centered on the Fountain of Cybele and flanked by the ornate Cibeles Palace (now Madrid City Hall) with its rooftop viewing terrace. The square anchors the Paseo del Prado and is a short walk from the Retiro and the art museums.

    Ticket priceSquare free; rooftop terrace of Cibeles Palace around €3–5
    Opening hoursSquare open 24 hours; rooftop terrace typically daily 10:30–20:30 (seasonal)
    Visiting tipGo up to the rooftop for panoramic views over the Paseo del Prado and Gran Vía. The square is best seen at dusk when the palace is illuminated.

  9. Mercado de San Miguel

    A historic iron-and-glass market hall near Plaza Mayor, now a gourmet food market with tapas bars, jamón ibérico, olives, seafood, and Spanish wines. It is a convenient, lively spot for a casual lunch or an evening snack.

    Ticket priceFree entry; pay per item at stalls
    Opening hoursTypically Sunday–Thursday 10:00–24:00, Friday–Saturday 10:00–01:00 (verify current hours)
    Visiting tipIt gets very crowded at peak meal times—visit mid-afternoon or late evening. Prices are higher than neighborhood bars but the variety is excellent for first-timers.

  10. Temple of Debod

    An authentic ancient Egyptian temple gifted to Spain in the 1960s and rebuilt in Madrid's Parque del Oeste. The temple's terraces and reflecting pool offer some of the best sunset views in the city, especially over the Royal Palace and the Casa de Campo.

    Ticket priceFree (interior access may require a free timed ticket; verify current procedure)
    Opening hoursGrounds generally open daily; temple interior typically Tuesday–Sunday with limited hours, closed Monday
    Visiting tipCome about an hour before sunset for the best light and photos. Bring a light layer—the park is exposed and breezy. It pairs well with a walk through the nearby Plaza de España.

Practical Tips for Visiting Madrid

  • Book online: For the Prado, Reina Sofía, Thyssen, and Royal Palace, buy timed tickets in advance to avoid long queues, especially in spring and autumn.
