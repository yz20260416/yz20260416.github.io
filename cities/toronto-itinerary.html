Toronto is Canada's largest city and one of the most multicultural places on earth, where more than 200 ethnic origins are represented across its neighbourhoods. In three days you can climb the CN Tower, wander the historic Distillery District, ferry to the Toronto Islands, and eat your way through Chinatown, Kensington Market, and St. Lawrence Market. This itinerary balances the city's headline attractions with local favourites, and it's designed to be walkable and transit-friendly — you won't need a car. Distances are compact, the TTC (subway, streetcar, and bus) is reliable, and most major sights sit within a few kilometres of the downtown core.

Day 1 — Downtown Icons & the Waterfront

Morning: Start early at the CN Tower (301 Front St W) to beat the crowds — book a timed ticket online in advance. The Glass Floor and the 447-metre-high LookOut level deliver the classic Toronto panorama; the EdgeWalk is available for thrill-seekers. Next door, Ripley's Aquarium of Canada is an excellent 60–90 minute stop, especially with kids, thanks to its underwater tunnel and Pacific kelp forest.

Lunch: Walk 10 minutes to Union Station and grab lunch at the food hall, or head to Momofuku Noodle Bar (190 University Ave) for pork buns and ramen.

Afternoon: Stroll down to Harbourfront Centre and the waterfront promenade. From Jack Layton Ferry Terminal (Bay St at Queens Quay), take the 13-minute ferry to the Toronto Islands — Centre Island is best for first-timers. Rent a bike or quadricycle, walk to Hanlan's Point, and enjoy skyline views back toward the city. Allow 2–3 hours round trip including the ferry.

Evening: Return downtown for dinner. Pai Northern Thai Kitchen (18 Duncan St) serves outstanding khao soi and is a local favourite — reserve ahead. For a Mediterranean-leaning night out, Byblos (11 Duncan St) offers excellent shared plates in a stylish room.

Day 2 — History, Markets & Culture

Morning: Begin at St. Lawrence Market (93 Front St E), consistently ranked among the world's best food markets. Go hungry: the peameal bacon sandwich at Carousel Bakery is a Toronto institution, and the Saturday farmers' market (5 a.m.–3 p.m.) is worth planning around. Then walk 15 minutes east to the Distillery District, a pedestrian-only Victorian industrial district now filled with galleries, boutiques, and cafés — the Christmas Market here is famous if you visit in December.

Lunch: Stay in the Distillery District — El Catrin Destileria does excellent tacos and guacamole in a colourful courtyard setting. Cluny Bistro is a solid alternative for French-inspired fare.

Afternoon: Take the subway to Museum Station and spend 2 hours at the Royal Ontario Museum (100 Queens Park), Canada's largest museum — the dinosaur galleries and First Peoples exhibits are standouts. From there, walk or take the streetcar to Kensington Market, a bohemian neighbourhood of vintage shops, produce stalls, and street art. Grab a coffee at a local café and wander.

Evening: Dinner at Gusto 101 (101 Portland St) for Italian small plates, or explore Kensington's casual eats — Rasta Pasta and the many taquerias are reliable and budget-friendly.

Day 3 — Neighbourhoods & Green Space

Morning: Head to Casa Loma (1 Austin Terrace), Toronto's castle, and tour the grand rooms, secret passages, and gardens — arrive at opening (9:30 a.m.) to avoid tour groups. From there, take transit to High Park, the city's largest green space, for a walk through the Grenadier Pond area; in late April the cherry blossoms draw crowds.

Lunch: Stop at Bar Isabel (797 College St) for Spanish tapas, or keep it casual with a sandwich from a local deli on College Street.

Afternoon: Explore Queen West, Toronto's design and shopping strip, then visit the Art Gallery of Ontario (317 Dundas St W) — the Frank Gehry-designed building houses a strong Group of Seven collection and major touring exhibitions. End with a walk through Chinatown on Spadina Avenue for bubble tea and bakery stops.

Evening: Finish with dinner at Mother Tongue (100 Yonge St area) for creative cocktails and modern plates, or Pizzeria Libretto (multiple locations) for wood-fired Neapolitan pizza — a dependable, crowd-pleasing final meal.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Buy a PRESTO card or tap a contactless credit card on the TTC. A day pass (or the weekend family pass) pays off if you're making 3+ trips per day. The PATH underground network connects much of downtown in winter.
  • Book ahead: CN Tower, Casa Loma, and the ROM all sell timed tickets online — reserve for the first morning slot to avoid queues.
  • Budget: Expect roughly CAD $150–250 per person per day for attractions, meals, and transit, excluding accommodation. Many museums offer discounted or free admission on certain evenings (check current schedules).
  • Weather: Toronto has four distinct seasons. Summer (June–August) is best for the Islands; winter (December–February) is cold but the Distillery District Christmas Market and indoor attractions shine. Pack layers in spring and fall.
  • Tipping: 15–20% at restaurants is standard; taxis and bars follow the same convention.
  • Safety: Toronto is generally very safe. Standard city precautions apply — stay aware on transit at night and keep valuables secure in crowded areas.
  • Accessibility: Most major attractions and the TTC are wheelchair accessible; check individual venue websites for details.
