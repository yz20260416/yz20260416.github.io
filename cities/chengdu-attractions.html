Chengdu Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan Province, blends ancient teahouse culture with modern urban energy and is home to China's most famous giant pandas. The city is compact enough to explore in a few days, with attractions ranging from historic temples and museums to lively pedestrian streets. Below are the ten essential stops for first-time visitors, with practical details to help you plan.

  1. Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding

    The city's signature attraction, where giant pandas and red pandas roam large semi-wild enclosures. Pandas are most active in the early morning, so arrive at opening time for the best viewing and feeding sessions.

    Ticket priceCNY 55 (adults); discounts for children and seniors
    Opening hoursApproximately 7:30–18:00 (entry until ~17:00); hours vary by season
    Visiting tipsGo before 9:00; buy tickets online to skip queues; allow 3–4 hours; wear comfortable shoes.

  2. Wuhou Shrine (Wuhou Ci)

    A memorial complex dedicated to Zhuge Liang and Liu Bei of the Three Kingdoms period, set in tranquil gardens with ancient cypress trees. The adjacent Jinli Street is a convenient add-on for snacks and souvenirs.

    Ticket priceCNY 50
    Opening hoursRoughly 8:00–18:00 (seasonal variations)
    Visiting tipsPair with Jinli Street in the evening; hire a guide or audio tour to understand the Three Kingdoms history.

  3. Jinli Ancient Street

    A restored Qing-style commercial street beside Wuhou Shrine, packed with Sichuan snack stalls, teahouses, and souvenir shops. It is lively and photogenic, especially after dark.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen all day; shops typically ~9:00–22:00
    Visiting tipsVisit in the evening for lanterns and atmosphere; try local snacks like san da pao; expect crowds on weekends.

  4. Du Fu Thatched Cottage

    A serene park and museum commemorating the Tang dynasty poet Du Fu, who lived here. The grounds include reconstructed thatched buildings, bamboo groves, and a museum of his life and work.

    Ticket priceCNY 50
    Opening hoursApproximately 8:00–18:00 (seasonal)
    Visiting tipsBest in spring and autumn; combine with a stroll along the nearby Huanhua Creek area.

  5. People's Park (Renmin Gongyuan)

    The heart of traditional Chengdu life, with teahouses, a lake for boating, and the famous Heming Teahouse. Locals gather here for tea, mahjong, and ear-cleaning services.

    Ticket priceFree (teahouse and activities cost extra)
    Opening hoursRoughly 6:00–21:00
    Visiting tipsSpend a slow morning at Heming Teahouse; try the traditional ear cleaning if curious; bring cash for small vendors.

  6. Kuanzhai Alley (Wide and Narrow Alleys)

    Three parallel Qing-era lanes — Wide, Narrow, and Well Alleys — restored into a district of teahouses, courtyard restaurants, and boutiques. It offers a polished glimpse of old Chengdu architecture.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen all day; businesses roughly 10:00–22:00
    Visiting tipsGo on a weekday to avoid crowds; the Narrow Alley has the best food options; combine with a walk to People's Park.

  7. Qingyang Palace

    One of China's most important Taoist temples, with ornate halls, bronze statues, and a peaceful garden atmosphere in the middle of the city.

    Ticket priceCNY 10
    Opening hoursApproximately 8:00–18:00
    Visiting tipsDress respectfully; the temple is a short walk from Kuanzhai Alley and makes a good cultural pairing.

  8. Sichuan Museum

    A major provincial museum covering Sichuan's history, archaeology, and ethnic cultures, including artifacts from the Sanxingdui and Jinsha cultures.

    Ticket priceFree with reservation (special exhibitions may charge)
    Opening hoursTypically 9:00–17:00, closed Mondays
    Visiting tipsBook online in advance; closed on Mondays; allow 2–3 hours; check for temporary exhibitions.

  9. Jinsha Site Museum

    An archaeological site and museum preserving relics of the ancient Shu civilization, including gold masks and jade artifacts. It complements a visit to Sanxingdui outside the city.

    Ticket priceCNY 70
    Opening hoursApproximately 8:00–18:00 (last entry ~17:00)
    Visiting tipsVisit the excavation hall first; allow 2 hours; combine with a trip to the panda base if time is tight.

  10. Chunxi Road Pedestrian Street

    Chengdu's busiest shopping and entertainment district, with department stores, local brands, street food, and the nearby Taikoo Li open-air complex. A good spot to experience the city's modern side and evening energy.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen all day; shops roughly 10:00–22:00
    Visiting tipsBest in the evening; explore Taikoo Li for dining; keep an eye on belongings in crowded areas.

Practical Tips

  • Best time to visit: Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer mild weather; summers are hot and humid, winters cool and grey.
  • Getting around: The metro is efficient and inexpensive; use a transport card or mobile payment. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are widely available.
  • Tickets: Book popular sites (panda base, museums) online in advance, especially on weekends and holidays. Carry your passport for museum reservations.
  • Timing: Start early — the panda base and major sites are best before midday crowds. Many museums close on Mondays.
  • Food: Chengdu is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy; try hotpot, mapo tofu, and dan dan noodles, but ask for mild spice if needed.
  • Currency and payments: Mobile payments (Alipay/WeChat Pay) are standard; keep some cash for small vendors and temples.
  • Etiquette: Dress modestly at temples; avoid loud behavior in museums and shrines; tipping is not customary.
  • Verify details: Ticket prices and opening hours can change seasonally — confirm on official sites or at your hotel before visiting.
