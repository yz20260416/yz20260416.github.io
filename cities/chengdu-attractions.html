Top Attractions in Chengdu

Chengdu blends ancient Shu history, living Buddhist traditions, and modern urban energy into one of China's most rewarding destinations. Visitors can meet giant pandas, trace the footsteps of Tang poets, wander Qing-style lanes, and marvel at 2,000-year-old hydraulic engineering. This guide highlights ten standout attractions that capture the city's character, from sacred temples and archaeological treasures to lively historic districts and skyline landmarks.

  1. Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding
    Founded in 1987 with six rescued giant pandas, this government-funded non-profit base has grown into the city's most famous attraction, breeding giant pandas, red pandas, and other rare animals. Its success is remarkable, with 124 panda births recorded by 2008.
    Tip: Arrive early in the morning when pandas are most active and feeding; the base is north of the city center, so allow extra travel time.
  2. Dujiangyan
    This ancient hydraulic engineering system was originally built around 256 BC by the State of Qin for irrigation and flood control, and it is still in use today. Set on the Min River between the Chengdu Plain and the Tibetan Plateau, it is a masterpiece of ancient engineering.
    Tip: It lies in Dujiangyan City west of Chengdu, making it a good day trip; wear comfortable shoes for walking the site.
  3. Jinsha Site
    Jinsha is one of China's major archaeological discoveries of the 21st century, listed on the UNESCO World Cultural Heritage Tentative List and protected at the national level. The Jinsha Museum, built in 2007, displays finds from this ancient Shu site in Qingyang, Chengdu.
    Tip: Combine your visit with nearby attractions in Qingyang; check the museum's opening hours before you go.
  4. Du Fu Thatched Cottage
    This 24-acre park and museum honors the Tang dynasty poet Du Fu, located on Chengdu's western outskirts beside Flower Rinsing Creek. It was designated a National Heritage site in 1961.
    Tip: The garden setting is pleasant year-round; pair it with a stroll along the adjacent Huanhua Xi area.
  5. Jinli
    This roughly 550-meter street traces its history back more than 1,800 years to the pre-Qin era and was ranked first among the "world's most beautiful streets" by CNN Travel in 2019. Its Qing Dynasty-style buildings, themed around Three Kingdoms culture, house bars, inns, snack stores, and souvenir shops.
    Tip: Visit in the evening for lantern-lit atmosphere, and sample local snacks along the way.
  6. Baoguang Temple
    Founded during the Tang dynasty in Xindu district, about 18 km north of Chengdu, this temple covers 100,000 square meters with more than 400 large stone pillars. It is listed as national heritage architecture and houses cultural treasures including a tilted Tang dynasty sharira stupa and a Liang dynasty jade Buddha.
    Tip: As a working temple, dress respectfully; it makes a worthwhile half-day excursion north of the city.
  7. Tianfu Square
    Occupying about 88,000 square meters at the intersection of Chengdu's main north–south and east–west axes, Tianfu Square is one of the city's principal civic landmarks. Its design incorporates a taijitu-inspired landscape featuring the Sun and Immortal Birds motif from the Jinsha site, along with fountains and a sunken plaza.
    Tip: It is centrally located and easy to reach by metro; visit in the evening when the fountains and lighting are at their best.
  8. Anshun Bridge
    This covered bridge crosses the Jin River and contains a relatively large restaurant, making it a popular eating location in the city. Its name means "Bridge of the Peaceful and Fluent."
    Tip: Come at night to see the bridge illuminated over the river; it is a short walk from the city center.
  9. Tianfu Panda Tower
    Also known as the 339 TV Tower, this 339-meter broadcasting tower in Chenghua stands by the Jin River and is the tallest tower in western China. It contains observation areas, dining facilities, and event spaces alongside its telecommunications role.
    Tip: Head up to the observation deck for skyline views, ideally around sunset.
  10. Taikoo Li Chengdu
    This mixed-use development in Jinjiang District near the Chunxi Road shopping area spans over 2.86 million square feet, with an open-air, lane-driven mall, the boutique Temple House hotel, and a Grade-A office tower. Its low-rise lanes make it a stylish place to wander.
    Tip: Combine a visit with nearby Daci Temple and Chunxi Road for a full afternoon of shopping and sightseeing.

Practical tips

  • Use the Chengdu Metro to reach most central sights; Tianfu Square and Chunxi Road are well connected on Lines 2 and 3.
  • Visit the Panda Base first thing in the morning, as pandas are most active before the midday heat.
  • Allow a full day trip for Dujiangyan, which lies outside the city proper.
  • Dress respectfully at active temples such as Baoguang Temple, and check opening hours for museums like the Jinsha Museum in advance.
  • Evenings are ideal for Jinli, Anshun Bridge, and Tianfu Square, when lighting and atmosphere are at their best.
