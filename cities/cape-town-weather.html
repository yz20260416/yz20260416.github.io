Cape Town Weather by Month

Cape Town sits in the Mediterranean climate zone at the southwestern tip of Africa, which means its seasons run opposite to Europe and North America: summer falls from December to February, and winter from June to August. Summers are warm, dry, and windy (the famous "Cape Doctor" southeaster), while winters are mild, wet, and changeable, with cold fronts sweeping in off the Atlantic. Because the city is wrapped around Table Mountain and two oceans, weather can vary noticeably between neighbourhoods — the Atlantic Seaboard and City Bowl catch more rain, while the winelands to the east are drier and warmer. The table below gives average daytime highs, overnight lows, and monthly rainfall to help you plan around the conditions you actually want.

Figures are long-term monthly averages for the City Bowl and are approximate; coastal areas are cooler and windier, while inland suburbs and the winelands run a few degrees warmer. Rainfall also varies sharply across the peninsula — the Atlantic Seaboard can receive noticeably more than the southern suburbs.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: March–May and September–November. Shoulder seasons offer warm, settled weather, thinner crowds, and lower accommodation prices than peak summer.
  • Best for beaches and outdoor living: December–February. Hot, dry, and sunny, though the southeaster can be fierce and prices peak over the holidays.
  • Best for budget travel: June–August. Winter brings the lowest rates, dramatic stormy skies, and excellent whale watching around Hermanus and False Bay — but expect rain and pack layers.
  • Best for whale watching: June–November, peaking around August–October.
  • Best for hiking Table Mountain and Lion's Head: October–April, when trails are driest and views clearest (start early to beat heat and wind).

Practical Tips

  • Layer up year-round. Even in summer, temperatures drop quickly after sunset and the wind can feel cool; a light jacket is always useful.
  • Respect the wind. The summer southeaster ("Cape Doctor") can gust strongly, especially on the Atlantic Seaboard and atop Table Mountain — check the cableway status before heading up.
  • Plan around winter rain. June–August bring passing cold fronts rather than all-day drizzle; mornings are often clear, so build flexibility into outdoor plans.
  • Book peak season early. December–February and the Easter period fill up fast; reserve accommodation, Table Mountain cableway tickets, and Robben Island ferries in advance.
  • Watch the UV. The South African sun is strong even on mild days — sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses are essentials.
  • Check microclimates. It can be raining in the City Bowl while sunny in Stellenbosch or Simon's Town; a short drive often escapes the weather.
  • Pack for both oceans. Atlantic beaches (Camps Bay, Clifton) are bracingly cold; False Bay (Muizenberg, Boulders) is warmer — a wetsuit helps either way.
