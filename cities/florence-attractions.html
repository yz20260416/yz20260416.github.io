Top Attractions in Florence

Florence, the cradle of the Renaissance, packs an extraordinary concentration of art, architecture, and history into its compact historic centre. From Brunelleschi's soaring dome to the sculpture-filled piazzas and the green escapes across the Arno, the city rewards both first-time visitors and returning travellers. This guide highlights ten standout attractions that capture the essence of Florence, each paired with a practical tip to help you plan your visit.

  1. Florence Cathedral (Duomo di Firenze)
    Commenced in 1296 in the Gothic style to a design of Arnolfo di Cambio and completed by 1436, the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore is crowned by Filippo Brunelleschi's pioneering dome and faced with polychrome marble panels. It remains the symbolic heart of the city and the epicentre of its historic centre.
    Tip: Book dome-climb tickets online in advance and arrive early to avoid the longest queues.
  2. Loggia dei Lanzi
    This open-air loggia on the south corner of Piazza della Signoria, adjoining the Uffizi, consists of three very high and wide arches resting on cluster columns with Corinthian capitals. It functions as a free public gallery of classical and Renaissance sculpture right in the political heart of the city.
    Tip: Visit in the early morning or evening for cooler temperatures and fewer crowds in the piazza.
  3. Fountain of Neptune
    Situated in Piazza della Signoria near the corner of the Palazzo Vecchio, the fountain was commissioned by Cosimo I de' Medici in 1559 to celebrate his gift of clean water to the city. Designed by Baccio Bandinelli and executed by Bartolomeo Ammannati, it is a beloved landmark of the square.
    Tip: Combine your visit with the nearby Palazzo Vecchio and the Loggia dei Lanzi in a single piazza walk.
  4. Boboli Gardens
    This large park was originally designed as the garden of the Medici family's Pitti Palace in the mid-16th century and is an important early Italian garden that influenced aristocratic gardens across Europe. It houses statues of various styles and periods, large fountains, and artificial grottoes.
    Tip: Enter from the Pitti Palace side and allow at least two hours to explore the terraces and viewpoints.
  5. Oltrarno District
    The Oltrarno ("Beyond the Arno") lies south of the river and contains part of Florence's historic centre along with notable sites such as Santo Spirito, the Palazzo Pitti, the Belvedere, and Piazzale Michelangelo. It is the city's artisan quarter and an atmospheric neighbourhood to wander.
    Tip: Explore the side streets around Santo Spirito for workshops and a more local pace than the north bank.
  6. Palazzo Medici Riccardi
    This 15th-century Renaissance palace was built for the Medici family, who dominated the politics of the Republic of Florence, and was designed by Michelozzo di Bartolomeo for Cosimo de' Medici between 1444 and 1484. It now serves as a museum and the seat of the Metropolitan City of Florence administration.
    Tip: Don't miss the interior Chapel of the Magi with its celebrated frescoes; check opening hours before you go.
  7. Great Synagogue of Florence
    Completed in 1882 and designed in the Italian and Moorish Revival styles, this Orthodox synagogue on Via Luigi Carlo Farini is one of the largest in south-central Europe. It was among the most important synagogues built in Europe during the age of Jewish emancipation.
    Tip: Dress respectfully and check security and visiting hours in advance, as entry policies can vary.
  8. Orto Botanico di Firenze (Giardino dei Semplici)
    Established on December 1, 1545, by Cosimo I de' Medici, this botanical garden maintained by the University of Florence is Europe's third oldest, behind Pisa and Padua. It covers 2.3 hectares and is known as the "Garden of simples."
    Tip: The garden is typically open weekday mornings, so plan it as a quiet start to your day.
  9. Giardino delle Rose
    Located in the Oltrarno below Piazzale Michelangelo, this garden park offers a commanding view of the city. Once open to the public only briefly in spring, it is now open daily from 9 am until sunset, except on Christmas and New Year's Day.
    Tip: Pair it with a sunset visit to Piazzale Michelangelo just above for one of Florence's best panoramas.
  10. Giardino dell'Iris
    This botanical garden specializes in the cultivation of iris flowers, the symbol of Florence since 1251, and sits at the corner of Viale dei Colli and Piazzale Michelangelo. It is open daily without charge from May 2–20 every year.
    Tip: Time your trip to late April or May to catch the iris blooms during the garden's short annual opening.

Practical Tips

  • Book major sites such as the Florence Cathedral dome and Boboli Gardens online in advance to secure entry times.
  • Visit popular piazzas like Piazza della Signoria early in the morning or after dusk to avoid peak crowds.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: Florence's historic centre is compact but cobbled, and the Oltrarno and garden terraces involve hills.
  • Check seasonal opening hours carefully — gardens such as Giardino dell'Iris open only for a few weeks each year.
  • Dress respectfully when visiting religious sites such as the Great Synagogue and the cathedral.
