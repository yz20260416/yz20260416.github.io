Florence Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Florence, the cradle of the Renaissance, packs an extraordinary concentration of art, architecture, and history into a compact historic center that is easily explored on foot. From Michelangelo's David to Brunelleschi's soaring dome, the city rewards visitors who plan ahead, as its most famous sites draw long lines year-round. This guide covers the ten essential attractions, with practical details on tickets, hours, and tips to help you make the most of your visit. Note that prices and hours can change seasonally, so always confirm with official websites before you travel.

  1. Galleria dell'Accademia (Michelangelo's David)

    Home to Michelangelo's marble masterpiece David, carved between 1501 and 1504, this gallery also houses unfinished "Slaves" by Michelangelo and a collection of Renaissance paintings and musical instruments. It is smaller than the Uffizi, and most visitors come specifically for the David.

    Ticket priceApproximately €16 (plus €4 reservation fee if booked online); reduced and free categories apply
    Opening hoursTuesday–Sunday, roughly 8:15 AM–6:50 PM; closed Mondays and some public holidays

    Tip: Book a timed-entry ticket online well in advance. Early morning or late afternoon slots are quieter. Allow 1–1.5 hours.

  2. Uffizi Gallery

    One of the world's great art museums, the Uffizi holds masterpieces by Botticelli (The Birth of Venus, Primavera), Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael, Titian, and Caravaggio. The collection traces the arc of Italian and European painting across centuries, and the building itself—a 16th-century Medici office complex—is a work of art.

    Ticket priceApproximately €25 in high season (plus reservation fee online); reduced rates available
    Opening hoursTuesday–Sunday, roughly 8:15 AM–6:30 PM; closed Mondays

    Tip: Reserve online to skip the general admission line. The gallery is huge—budget at least 3 hours, or focus on the second floor (Botticelli, Leonardo) if time is short.

  3. Florence Cathedral (Duomo) & Brunelleschi's Dome

    The Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore, with its iconic red-tiled dome engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi in the 15th century, dominates the skyline. The cathedral interior is free to enter, while climbing the 463 steps of the dome offers the best close-up view of the frescoed interior and a panorama of the city.

    Ticket priceCathedral entry free; Brunelleschi's Dome climb requires the "Giotto Pass" or combined "Brunelleschi Pass" (roughly €30, includes dome, baptistery, bell tower, and museum)
    Opening hoursCathedral roughly 10:15 AM–4:30 PM (varies); dome climb by timed reservation

    Tip: Dome climbs must be booked in advance with a timed slot and sell out quickly. The climb is narrow and steep—not recommended for those with claustrophobia or mobility issues.

  4. Ponte Vecchio

    The oldest and most famous bridge in Florence, dating to 1345, is lined with jewelry shops and characterized by the Vasari Corridor running above it. It is best appreciated at sunset, when the Arno River and the bridge glow in warm light.

    Ticket priceFree to walk across
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; shops typically 10 AM–7 PM

    Tip: Visit early morning for photos without crowds, or view it from the nearby bridges (Ponte Santa Trinita) for the classic postcard shot.

  5. Piazza della Signoria & Palazzo Vecchio

    Florence's civic heart, this lively square features the Palazzo Vecchio (the city's town hall), a replica of Michelangelo's David, the Fountain of Neptune, and the open-air sculpture gallery of the Loggia dei Lanzi. It is a free public space and a natural starting point for exploring the historic center.

    Ticket priceSquare free; Palazzo Vecchio museum roughly €12.50 (tower climb extra)
    Opening hoursPalazzo Vecchio roughly 9 AM–7 PM (Fridays longer); square always open

    Tip: The tower offers excellent views for a lower price than the Duomo dome, with shorter lines. Combine with a visit to the nearby Uffizi.

  6. Santa Croce Basilica

    This Franciscan basilica is the burial place of some of Italy's greatest figures, including Michelangelo, Galileo, Machiavelli, and Rossini—earning it the nickname "Temple of the Italian Glories." It also features frescoes by Giotto and a peaceful cloister.

    Ticket priceApproximately €8–€10 (combined ticket with museum and cloisters)
    Opening hoursMonday–Saturday roughly 9:30 AM–5:30 PM; Sunday afternoons only; closed some holidays

    Tip: Dress respectfully (shoulders and knees covered). The adjoining leather school and cloister are worth a look.

  7. Bargello National Museum

    Housed in a former prison and police headquarters, the Bargello is a sculpture museum with works by Michelangelo, Donatello (including his bronze David), Cellini, and Giambologna. It is less crowded than the major galleries and a favorite among art lovers.

    Ticket priceApproximately €10; reduced rates available
    Opening hoursTuesday–Sunday roughly 8:45 AM–1:30 PM (hours vary); closed Mondays

    Tip: Check current hours before visiting, as they are limited. It pairs well with a walk to Santa Croce.

  8. Boboli Gardens & Pitti Palace

    The sprawling Boboli Gardens behind the Pitti Palace offer terraced paths, fountains, sculptures, and lovely city views—an ideal break from museum-going. The Pitti Palace houses several museums, including the Palatine Gallery with works by Raphael and Titian, and the Costume Gallery.

    Ticket priceBoboli Gardens roughly €10; combined tickets with Pitti museums available (€16–€38 depending on access)
    Opening hoursGardens roughly 8:15 AM–6:30 PM (seasonal); closed first and last Monday of the month

    Tip: Wear comfortable shoes—the gardens are hilly. Bring water in summer, as shade is limited in some areas.

  9. San Lorenzo Market & Medici Chapels

    The bustling San Lorenzo Market offers leather goods, souvenirs, and a lively food hall (Mercato Centrale) with local specialties. Nearby, the Medici Chapels, attached to the Basilica of San Lorenzo, contain the magnificent New Sacristy designed by Michelangelo, with tombs of the Medici family.

    Ticket priceMarket free to browse; Medici Chapels roughly €9–€10
    Opening hoursMarket roughly 7 AM–2 PM (food hall longer); Medici Chapels Tuesday–Sunday roughly 8:15 AM–1:50 PM

    Tip: Visit the market in the morning for the best selection and freshest food. Bargain politely on leather goods.

  10. Piazzale Michelangelo

    This hillside terrace south of the Arno offers the most famous panoramic view of Florence, including the Duomo, Ponte Vecchio, and the surrounding hills. It is especially popular at sunset.

    Ticket priceFree
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours

    Tip: Walk up via the scenic route through the Giardino delle Rose (Rose Garden) or take bus 12/13. Arrive 30–45 minutes before sunset to secure a good spot, and bring a light layer for the evening breeze.

Practical Tips for Visiting Florence

  • Book major sites online: The Accademia, Uffizi, and Duomo dome climb sell out in peak season (April–October). Timed-entry reservations save hours of waiting.
  • Consider a city pass: The Firenze Card can be worthwhile if you plan to visit many museums in a short time, though it no longer includes some sites—compare against individual ticket costs.
  • Dress code: Churches require covered shoulders and knees. Carry a light scarf or shawl for impromptu visits.
  • Walk and use public transport: The historic center is compact and best explored on foot; the center is largely pedestrianized. Buses serve outlying areas like Piazzale Michelangelo.
