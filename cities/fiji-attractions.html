Top 10 Must-See Attractions in Fiji

Fiji is an archipelago of more than 330 islands in the South Pacific, known for its coral reefs, white-sand beaches, volcanic peaks, and vibrant Fijian culture. Whether you base yourself in Nadi, Denarau, the Mamanuca Islands, or the Yasawas, these ten attractions cover the country's best natural and cultural highlights. Prices and hours below are typical at the time of writing and can change seasonally, so confirm locally before you go.

  1. Mamanuca Islands

    A chain of about 20 islands southwest of Nadi, the Mamanucas are Fiji's most accessible island escape, featured in films such as Cast Away. Expect turquoise lagoons, fringing reefs, and resorts ranging from backpacker to luxury. Day cruises depart from Denarau and Port Denarau Marina.

    Ticket priceDay cruises typically FJD 150–350 per adult; island resort day passes vary
    Opening hoursDay trips usually depart 8:00–9:00 and return 16:00–18:00; resorts operate daily
    Visiting tipsBook in advance in peak season (June–September); bring reef-safe sunscreen, cash for island fees, and a light rain jacket.

  2. Yasawa Islands

    A volcanic archipelago northwest of Nadi, the Yasawas offer dramatic peaks, caves, and some of Fiji's clearest water. The Yasawa Flyer ferry links the islands daily, making island-hopping easy for independent travelers.

    Ticket priceYasawa Flyer passes from around FJD 250–400 depending on route; resort day passes FJD 50–150
    Opening hoursFerry departs Port Denarau daily in the morning; resort facilities generally 7:00–22:00
    Visiting tipsBuy a multi-day ferry pass for flexibility; carry cash as ATMs are scarce; village visits require modest dress (sarongs recommended).

  3. Sabeto Hot Springs and Mud Pool

    Near Nadi, this natural hot spring and mud pool is a popular half-day excursion. Visitors coat themselves in mineral-rich mud, let it dry, then rinse off in the warm spring pools. It is a relaxed, family-friendly experience.

    Ticket priceAround FJD 30–50 per adult; children discounted
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsBring a towel and a change of clothes; wear dark swimwear as mud can stain; combine with a Nadi sightseeing tour.

  4. Garden of the Sleeping Giant

    Founded by actor Raymond Burr near Nadi, this lush botanical garden sits at the foot of the Sleeping Giant mountain. It features orchids, native plants, and shaded walking trails with mountain views.

    Ticket priceAround FJD 18–25 per adult; children reduced
    Opening hoursMonday–Saturday 9:00–17:00; often closed Sunday
    Visiting tipsGo early to avoid heat; bring insect repellent; the garden pairs well with Sabeto Hot Springs on the same road.

  5. Sri Siva Subramaniya Temple, Nadi

    The largest Hindu temple in the Southern Hemisphere, this colorful Dravidian-style temple in Nadi is a striking cultural landmark built by Indian indentured laborers' descendants. Photography inside the main temple is restricted.

    Ticket priceFree entry; donations welcome
    Opening hoursDaily, approximately 5:30–19:00 (closed during certain prayer times)
    Visiting tipsDress modestly (shoulders and knees covered); remove shoes before entering; be respectful during worship.

  6. Denarau Island and Port Denarau

    Fiji's main resort and marina hub, Denarau offers golf, spas, restaurants, and the departure point for most island cruises. The marina is the gateway to the Mamanucas and Yasawas.

    Ticket priceFree to walk the marina; activities and cruises priced separately
    Opening hoursMarina shops and restaurants generally 9:00–22:00
    Visiting tipsUse the Bula Bus for easy transport around the island; book cruises at the marina; sunset is best viewed from the waterfront.

  7. Sigatoka Sand Dunes National Park

    Fiji's first national park protects a dramatic coastal dune system on the Coral Coast, with archaeological sites and endemic forest. Walking trails range from short loops to longer hikes with ocean views.

    Ticket priceAround FJD 10–20 per adult; guided walks extra
    Opening hoursDaily, approximately 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsWear sturdy shoes and a hat; carry water; the dunes are exposed—avoid midday heat; combine with Sigatoka town and market.

  8. Kula Wild Adventure Park, Coral Coast

    A wildlife park near Sigatoka focused on conservation of Fiji's native species, including the crested iguana and Fiji's endemic birds. It also offers zipline and adventure activities for families.

    Ticket priceEntry around FJD 40–60 per adult; zipline and combo packages extra
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 9:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsAllow 2–3 hours; good for families with children; check combo ticket prices online for savings.

  9. Bouma National Heritage Park, Taveuni

    On the "Garden Island" of Taveuni, Bouma protects rainforest, waterfalls, and coastal trails. The Lavena Coastal Walk and Tavoro (Bouma) Falls are highlights, with guided options available.

    Ticket pricePark entry around FJD 20–30; guided walks and boat transfers extra
    Opening hoursDaily, approximately 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsBring reef shoes for river crossings; hire a local guide for the coastal walk; Taveuni is reached by short domestic flight from Nadi or Suva.

  10. Suva Municipal Market and Fiji Museum

    Fiji's capital offers a cultural counterpoint to the resorts. The bustling municipal market sells produce, kava, and handicrafts, while the Fiji Museum in Thurston Gardens documents the islands' history and anthropology.

    Ticket priceMarket free; Fiji Museum entry around FJD 5–10
    Opening hoursMarket Monday–Saturday roughly 7:00–17:00; museum Monday–Saturday about 9:30–16:30, closed Sunday
    Visiting tipsVisit the market in the morning for the best atmosphere; ask before photographing vendors; Suva is walkable in the central district.

Practical Tips for Visiting Fiji

  • Best time to visit: May to October is the dry season with cooler, drier weather; November to April is wetter and cyclone season, with lower prices.
  • Currency and payments: The Fijian dollar (FJD) is the local currency. Cards are widely accepted at resorts, but carry cash for markets, villages, and island fees.
  • Getting around: Nadi International Airport is the main gateway. Domestic flights, ferries, and catamarans connect the islands; taxis and rental cars serve Viti Levu.
  • Culture and etiquette: Dress modestly in villages, remove hats and sunglasses when entering a village, and ask permission before photographing people. Kava ceremonies are a customary welcome.
  • Reef safety: Use reef-safe sunscreen, avoid touching coral, and check tide and current conditions before snorkeling or diving.
  • Health: Drink bottled or filtered water on outer islands, use insect repellent, and consider travel insurance covering water activities.
  • Prices and hours: Confirm admission fees and opening times directly with operators, as they vary by season and are subject to change.
