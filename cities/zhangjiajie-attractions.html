Top Attractions in Zhangjiajie

Zhangjiajie, in northwestern Hunan Province, is famous for its towering quartzite sandstone pillars, dramatic canyons, and vertiginous viewpoints. The core scenery lies within the Wulingyuan Scenic Area, a UNESCO-listed landscape of peaks, gorges, streams, and caves. Beyond the mountains, visitors can explore glass elevators, skywalks, and centuries-old Buddhist temples. This guide covers the highlights most likely to appeal to international travelers.

  1. Wulingyuan Scenic Area
    This vast scenic and historical site is noted for more than 3,000 quartzite sandstone pillars and peaks, many over 200 metres tall, along with ravines, gorges, streams, pools, lakes, rivers, and waterfalls. It also features 40 caves and a natural bridge named Tianqiaoshengkong.
    Tip: Allow at least two full days to cover the different sections of the scenic area.
  2. Zhangjiajie National Forest Park
    This national forest park is one of several national parks within the Wulingyuan Scenic Area and is the classic entry point for the region's pillar landscapes. Its trails and viewpoints offer some of the most photographed scenery in China.
    Tip: Arrive early in the morning to avoid crowds at the main viewpoints.
  3. Bailong Elevator
    This glass double-deck elevator is built onto the side of a cliff in Wulingyuan, ascending 326 metres in just one minute and 32 seconds after a 2015 upgrade. It saves visitors a two-hour hike to the top of one of the area's towering pillars.
    Tip: Ride it early or late in the day, as queues can be long at peak times.
  4. Tianmen Mountain
    Located within Tianmen Mountain National Park, this mountain is one of Zhangjiajie's signature landmarks and is known for its dramatic cliff scenery and the famous "Heaven's Gate" arch. It offers some of the region's most spectacular high-altitude views.
    Tip: Check the weather before going up; cloud cover can hide the views.
  5. Zhangjiajie Glass Bridge
    This transparent, glass-bottomed skywalk bridge spans the canyon between two cliffs above the Wulingyuan area. When it opened on 20 August 2016, it was the longest and tallest glass-bottomed bridge in the world, measuring 430 metres in total length and suspended about 300 metres above the ground.
    Tip: Book tickets in advance, as visitor numbers are controlled.
  6. Tianmenshan Temple
    This Buddhist temple is located on Tianmen Mountain and is built within grounds of some 20,000 square metres. It makes a peaceful cultural stop amid the mountain's dramatic scenery.
    Tip: Combine your visit with the Tianmen Mountain cableway for a full day out.
  7. Puguang Temple
    Also known as Puguang Chan Temple, this Buddhist temple is located in Yongding District of Zhangjiajie. It offers a quieter, more traditional counterpoint to the region's natural attractions.
    Tip: It is convenient to visit while exploring Zhangjiajie's city center.

Practical tips

  • Visit between April and October for the best weather, but expect crowds during Chinese public holidays.
  • Wear comfortable walking shoes; many attractions involve steep stairs and long trails.
  • Book tickets for the Bailong Elevator and Glass Bridge in advance where possible.
  • Carry layers and rain gear, as mountain weather changes quickly.
  • Allow extra time for transfers between the city, Tianmen Mountain, and the Wulingyuan area.
