Zhangjiajie Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Zhangjiajie, in Hunan Province, China, is famous for its towering quartz-sandstone pillars, which inspired the floating mountains in the film Avatar. The region centers on Zhangjiajie National Forest Park (part of Wulingyuan Scenic Area, a UNESCO World Heritage Site), with additional attractions in nearby Wulingyuan, Baofeng Lake, and the city itself. Most sites are reached via the Wulingyuan or Forest Park gates and are connected by shuttle buses. Below are the top 10 attractions with practical details. Prices and hours change seasonally, so confirm before travel.

  1. Zhangjiajie National Forest Park (Yuanjiajie Area)

    The core of the park, featuring the iconic sandstone pillars and the famous "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain" viewpoint. Yuanjiajie is the most visited area, with the Natural Bridge and the First Bridge Under Heaven.

    Ticket: Park entrance ticket (4-day pass) approximately ¥228 (includes shuttle buses; cable cars and elevators extra).

    Hours: Roughly 7:00–18:00 (varies by season).

    Tips: Arrive early to avoid crowds; take the Bailong Elevator up and hike down. Bring water and wear sturdy shoes.

  2. Bailong Elevator (Hundred Dragons Elevator)

    A glass elevator built into a cliff face, rising about 326 meters to Yuanjiajie. It offers dramatic views and saves a long climb.

    Ticket: Approximately ¥72 one way (single ride).

    Hours: Roughly 7:00–18:00 (seasonal).

    Tips: Queues can be long at peak times; go early or late. Not recommended for those with a fear of heights.

  3. Tianzi Mountain (Tianzishan)

    A scenic area with panoramic views of pillar forests, often shrouded in mist. Highlights include the Imperial Writing Brush Peaks and the West Sea Stone Forest.

    Ticket: Included in the main park ticket; cable car approximately ¥67 one way.

    Hours: Roughly 7:00–18:00.

    Tips: Best visited in the morning for clearer views. The cable car is useful if you want to save energy.

  4. Golden Whip Stream (Jinbian Xi)

    A flat, scenic valley trail about 7.5 km long, running between towering peaks. It is one of the easiest and most beautiful hikes in the park.

    Ticket: Included in the main park ticket.

    Hours: Daylight hours, roughly 7:00–18:00.

    Tips: Watch for monkeys—do not feed them or carry visible food. The trail is mostly shaded and suitable for most fitness levels.

  5. Tianmen Mountain (Tianmenshan)

    Located near Zhangjiajie city, this mountain features a 7.5 km cable car, the "Heaven's Gate" natural arch, and glass skywalks along the cliff.

    Ticket: Approximately ¥258–278 (includes cable car and shuttle; glass walk extra).

    Hours: Roughly 8:00–18:00 (seasonal; last entry earlier).

    Tips: Book tickets in advance during holidays. The cable car is the main route up; the 999-step stairway to Heaven's Gate is optional but steep.

  6. Glass Bridge (Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon)

    A 430-meter-long glass-bottomed bridge spanning a deep canyon. It is a separate attraction from the national forest park.

    Ticket: Approximately ¥138 for the bridge (combined canyon tickets available).

    Hours: Roughly 8:00–17:00.

    Tips: Advance booking is required; daily visitor numbers are limited. Bags and some items may need to be stored, and shoe covers are provided.

  7. Baofeng Lake (Baofeng Hu)

    A calm lake surrounded by peaks, explored by boat with local Tujia folk songs. A relaxing contrast to the mountain hikes.

    Ticket: Approximately ¥96 (includes boat ride).

    Hours: Roughly 8:00–17:30.

    Tips: Combine with a visit to nearby Huanglong Cave. The boat ride lasts about 30–40 minutes.

  8. Huanglong Cave (Yellow Dragon Cave)

    A large karst cave system with stalactites, underground rivers, and lit chambers. One of the region's major non-mountain attractions.

    Ticket: Approximately ¥100.

    Hours: Roughly 8:00–17:30.

    Tips: The cave is cool and humid; bring a light layer. Paths can be slippery, so wear grippy shoes.

  9. Wulingyuan Scenic Area (Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon & Surroundings)

    The broader scenic zone encompassing the forest park, streams, and canyon areas. It is the gateway for many of the region's trails and viewpoints.

    Ticket: Main park pass covers core areas; separate tickets for canyon and cave attractions.

    Hours: Roughly 7:00–18:00.

    Tips: Use the Wulingyuan gate for access to Baofeng Lake and the canyon. Shuttle buses connect major trailheads.

  10. Zhangjiajie City & Tujia Folk Customs Park

    The city itself offers access to Tianmen Mountain and local Tujia culture. The Tujia Folk Customs Park showcases traditional architecture and performances.

    Ticket: Park entry approximately ¥60–80 (varies by show).

    Hours: Roughly 8:00–17:30.

    Tips: A good option for an arrival or departure day. Check performance schedules in advance.

Practical Tips

  • Best time to visit: April–October for greenery and clearer skies; winter offers fewer crowds but colder, mistier conditions.
  • Tickets: The main park pass is valid for 4 days. Buy tickets online where possible, especially for the Glass Bridge and Tianmen Mountain, which have daily limits.
  • Getting around: Shuttle buses inside the park are included in the main ticket. Cable cars, elevators, and the glass bridge cost extra.
  • Weather: Fog and rain are common; carry a rain jacket and check forecasts. Views can disappear in heavy mist, so plan flexible days.
  • Footwear: Expect many stairs and uneven paths; wear comfortable, grippy shoes.
  • Wildlife: Monkeys along Golden Whip Stream can be aggressive—keep food hidden and do not feed them.
  • Language: English signage is limited in some areas; a translation app or offline map helps.
  • Etiquette: Follow posted rules at glass walkways and bridges; storage of bags may be required.
