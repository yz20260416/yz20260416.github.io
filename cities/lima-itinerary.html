Lima 3-Day Itinerary — Travel Guide

Lima is often treated as a quick layover on the way to Cusco or Machu Picchu, but Peru's capital deserves far more than a rushed afternoon. As one of South America's great culinary capitals, a UNESCO-recognized historic center, and a city perched dramatically on cliffs above the Pacific, Lima rewards travelers who give it three full days. This itinerary balances the essential sights — the colonial Plaza Mayor, the bohemian streets of Barranco, and the pre-Incan pyramids of Huaca Pucllana — with the food, coffee, and ocean views that make Lima unforgettable. It's designed for a comfortable pace: mornings for sightseeing, afternoons for neighborhoods and museums, and evenings for long, memorable dinners.

Day 1 — Historic Center & Miraflores

Start where Lima began: the Plaza Mayor, flanked by the Cathedral, the Government Palace, and the Archbishop's Palace. Arrive early (around 9 a.m.) to beat tour groups. Don't miss the Casa de Aliaga, a colonial mansion still owned by descendants of Jerónimo de Aliaga, one of Pizarro's companions — visits are by guided tour only, so reserve in advance. For coffee, duck into a café under the arcades or head to the recently restored Bar Cordano, a Lima institution since 1905.

For lunch, seek out a huarique — a humble, family-run eatery — near the center; ask your hotel for a recommendation, as the best ones change frequently. In the afternoon, take a taxi (or the Metropolitano bus) to Pueblo Libre for the Museo Larco, home to an extraordinary collection of pre-Columbian ceramics and gold, set in a bougainvillea-draped mansion. The on-site café is a pleasant spot for a break.

End the day in Miraflores: walk the Malecón to the Parque del Amor for sunset over the Pacific, then choose between a classic cevichería (go for lunch-style hours if you can) or one of Lima's acclaimed nikkei restaurants blending Japanese and Peruvian technique. Book dinner reservations at least a few days ahead for the most popular spots.

Day 2 — Barranco & the Coast

Barranco is Lima's bohemian heart, and it's best explored on foot in the morning light. Cross the Puente de los Suspiros ("Bridge of Sighs"), wander the colorful streets, and pop into small galleries and street-art corridors. Brunch options abound — look for a café with a courtyard, and try a lomo saltado sandwich or fresh fruit juices.

In the afternoon, choose a museum: the MAC (Museo de Arte Contemporáneo) for modern Latin American art, or MATE, the Mario Testino museum, for photography. Then walk (or ride) north along the Malecón cliffside boardwalk toward Miraflores — it's one of the city's great urban promenades, with paragliders overhead and surfers below.

For the evening, start with a pisco sour at a Barranco rooftop bar, then dine at a criollo restaurant serving Peruvian comfort classics like ají de gallina or tacu tacu. Barranco's restaurant scene is more relaxed than Miraflores' fine dining, which makes it a good contrast to Day 1.

Day 3 — Markets, Pyramids & Farewell

Begin at a local market — the Surquillo Market (near Miraflores) is a favorite for food tours, where you can taste unusual fruits, fresh cheeses, and anticuchos (grilled skewers). A guided market visit is worth the cost for context and translation. Alternatively, the Mercado de Magdalena is a more local, less touristy option.

After lunch, head to Huaca Pucllana, a striking adobe pyramid complex right in the middle of Miraflores, built by the Lima culture around 500 CE. Guided tours run throughout the day (book a slot online). Then use the late afternoon for shopping along Avenida Larco or a final pisco tasting at a bar in Miraflores.
