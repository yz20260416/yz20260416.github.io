Jerusalem Travel Cost Guide

Jerusalem is a city of extraordinary contrasts — ancient and modern, sacred and secular, bustling and contemplative. As one of the world's most visited destinations, it offers options for every budget, from dormitory hostels in the Old City to luxury hotels overlooking the Mount of Olives. This guide breaks down typical costs across accommodation, food, transport, and activities so you can plan a trip that fits your travel style. All prices are listed in Israeli New Shekels (₪) with approximate US dollar equivalents (1 USD ≈ 3.7 ₪, as of early 2025). Prices fluctuate with exchange rates and season, so treat these figures as planning estimates.

Budget Breakdown by Travel Style

Costs in Jerusalem vary widely depending on season (spring and autumn are peak), location (inside vs. outside the Old City), and whether you observe Shabbat (Friday evening to Saturday evening), when many businesses close and some transport halts. Accommodation in Detail

  • Hostels: The Abraham Hostel is a well-known budget hub with dorms from ₪90–130. Old City hostels are cheaper but basic; expect shared bathrooms and no-frills rooms.
  • Mid-range hotels: Concentrated in West Jerusalem (Rehavia, German Colony, city center). Prices spike during Jewish and Christian holidays — book months ahead.
  • Luxury: The King David, Mamilla Hotel, and Waldorf Astoria anchor the high end. Expect Shabbat rates and minimum-stay requirements during peak periods.
  • Alternative: Airbnb apartments in neighborhoods like Nachlaot or Katamon can be good value for stays of 3+ nights, especially for families.

Food and Drink

  • Street food: Falafel (₪15–25), shawarma (₪30–50), and hummus plates (₪25–40) are the budget traveler's best friends. Machane Yehuda Market is a paradise for cheap eats.
  • Casual restaurants: A sit-down lunch runs ₪50–90 per person; dinner ₪80–150. Look for business lunch specials (עסקית) on weekdays.
  • Fine dining: Top restaurants like Machneyuda or Eucalyptus charge ₪150–300 per main; tasting menus can exceed ₪400 per person.
  • Kosher considerations: Kosher restaurants close on Shabbat; many in the Old City and East Jerusalem are halal and open. Non-kosher options exist in West Jerusalem but are less common.
  • Water and coffee: Tap water is safe and free; a café espresso costs ₪10–15, a cappuccino ₪14–20.
