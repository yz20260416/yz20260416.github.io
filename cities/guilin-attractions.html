Guilin: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Guilin, in the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region of southern China, is famous for its dramatic karst limestone peaks, winding rivers, and misty landscapes that have inspired Chinese painters and poets for centuries. The city works well as a base for exploring the Li River, ancient towns, terraced rice fields, and caves. Below are ten of the most rewarding attractions, with practical details to help you plan. Prices and hours change periodically, so always confirm with official sources before visiting.

  1. Li River Cruise (Lijiang)

    The classic Guilin experience: a scenic boat journey from Zhujiang Pier (near Guilin) to Yangshuo, passing the celebrated karst scenery, including the spot pictured on the 20-yuan banknote near Xingping. Most cruises take about four hours.

    • Tickets: Roughly CNY 210–450 per person depending on boat class (standard to deluxe), usually including a simple lunch; children and seniors may receive discounts.
    • Hours: Boats typically depart between 8:00 and 10:30 a.m.; book ahead, especially in peak season.
    • Tips: Sit on the upper deck or the right side for the best views; bring sun protection and cash for tips and snacks; consider the shorter Xingping–Yangshuo or Yangshuo–Xingping segment if you want a cheaper, less crowded option.

  2. Reed Flute Cave (Ludi Yan)

    A large limestone cave filled with stalactites, stalagmites, and colorful lighting, one of Guilin's most popular indoor attractions. The visit is a self-guided or guided walk of about 500 meters through several chambers.

    • Tickets: Approximately CNY 90 for adults; discounts for children and seniors.
    • Hours: Generally 8:00 a.m.–5:30 p.m. (last entry around 5:00 p.m.); hours can vary seasonally.
    • Tips: The cave is cool and damp—bring a light layer; paths can be slippery, so wear shoes with grip; visit early or late to avoid tour-group crowds.

  3. Elephant Trunk Hill (Xiangbi Shan)

    Guilin's emblem, a hill shaped like an elephant drinking from the Li River, located right in the city. The park offers riverside walks, pavilions, and the famous "water moon" arch formed by the trunk and its reflection.

    • Tickets: Around CNY 55–75 for the scenic area; some viewpoints along the river are free.
    • Hours: Typically 6:30 a.m.–7:00 p.m. (may extend in summer).
    • Tips: Go at dusk for the best photos and cooler weather; combine with a riverside walk along the Two Rivers and Four Lakes area.

  4. Longji Rice Terraces (Longsheng)

    Terraced rice fields carved into steep mountains about two hours from Guilin, home to Zhuang and Yao villages. The two main areas are Ping'an and Dazhai (Jinkeng), with spectacular views in spring (water-filled terraces) and autumn (golden harvest).

    • Tickets: About CNY 80–100 for the scenic area entrance; cable car at Dazhai costs extra (roughly CNY 100–120 round trip).
    • Hours: Scenic area generally open 8:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m.; villages accessible outside these hours if staying overnight.
    • Tips: Stay overnight in a village guesthouse to catch sunrise; bring sturdy shoes for steep paths; expect cooler temperatures at altitude; respect local Yao customs when photographing residents.

  5. Yangshuo West Street (Xi Jie)

    The lively pedestrian heart of Yangshuo, packed with cafes, restaurants, bars, and souvenir shops in a mix of traditional and modern buildings. It's the social hub for travelers exploring the surrounding countryside.

    • Tickets: Free to walk.
    • Hours: Open all day; liveliest in the evening.
    • Tips: Prices are higher here than elsewhere in Yangshuo—bargain for souvenirs; explore side streets for quieter local eateries; use it as a base for cycling and rafting trips.

  6. Xingping Ancient Town and Laozhai Hill

    A quieter, older town on the Li River about 25 km from Yangshuo, famous as the setting of the 20-yuan note view. A short climb up Laozhai Hill (also called Xianggong Hill area viewpoints) rewards with panoramic river-and-peak vistas.

    • Tickets: Town entry generally free; small fees (CNY 10–30) for some viewpoint trails or Laozhai Hill access.
    • Hours: Town open all day; viewpoints best at sunrise and sunset.
    • Tips: Combine with a Li River bamboo raft trip; bring water and wear grippy shoes for the climb; weekdays are far less crowded than weekends.

  7. Two Rivers and Four Lakes (Liangjiang Sihu)

    A scenic water system winding through central Guilin, linking the Li and Taohua rivers with four lakes. Evening boat cruises pass illuminated pagodas, bridges, and performance sites—a relaxed way to see the city.

    • Tickets: Day cruises roughly CNY 80–120; night cruises about CNY 200–260 (varies by route and season).
    • Hours: Cruises typically run from late afternoon into the evening (roughly 2:00–10:00 p.m. departure windows).
    • Tips: The night cruise is the highlight—book a day ahead in peak season; the lakeside promenades are free to stroll and great for photos.

  8. Solitary Beauty Peak and Jingjiang Prince's Palace (Duxiu Feng)

    A historic royal palace complex dating to the Ming dynasty, with the dramatic Solitary Beauty Peak rising behind it. Climb the 300-plus steps for a 360-degree view over Guilin's rooftops and karst hills.

    • Tickets: Around CNY 100–120 for the combined palace and peak area.
    • Hours: Generally 8:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m.
    • Tips: The climb is steep but short—allow 30–45 minutes round trip; go in the morning for clearer light; the palace grounds include exhibits on Guilin's history.

  9. Fubo Hill (Wave-Subduing Hill)

    A riverside hill with caves, Buddhist carvings, and a summit pavilion offering views over the Li River and city. It's a compact, easy stop near Elephant Trunk Hill.

    • Tickets: Approximately CNY 25–30.
    • Hours: Typically 6:30 a.m.–6:30 p.m.
    • Tips: Combine with Elephant Trunk Hill in one morning; the climb is short and manageable for most fitness levels; look for the ancient inscriptions inside the cave.

  10. Seven Star Park (Qixing Gongyuan)

    Guilin's largest urban park, featuring seven karst peaks arranged like the Big Dipper, plus caves, a zoo area, and the famous Camel Hill. It's a pleasant half-day escape with plenty of shade.

    • Tickets: Park entry
