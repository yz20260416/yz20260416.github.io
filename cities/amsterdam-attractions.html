Top Attractions in Amsterdam

Amsterdam packs an extraordinary amount into a compact, walkable centre. Its 17th-century canal ring is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lined with gabled houses and crossed by some 1,500 bridges. Beyond the waterways, visitors find historic courtyards, landmark towers, a bustling brewery, and even a haunted attraction. This guide highlights ten of the city's most compelling sights for international travellers.

  1. Canals of Amsterdam
    The city has more than 100 kilometres of grachten (canals), about 90 islands and 1,500 bridges, with the three main canals—Herengracht, Prinsengracht and Keizersgracht—forming the concentric Grachtengordel dug during the Dutch Golden Age. This 17th-century canal ring, including the Jordaan, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site lined with 1,550 monumental buildings.
  2. Begijnhof
    One of Amsterdam's oldest hofjes, this tranquil courtyard is surrounded by historic buildings, mostly private dwellings, and was originally a béguinage. Today it also contains two churches: the Catholic Miracle Church and the English Reformed Church.
  3. Magere Brug (Skinny Bridge)
    This white-painted wooden bascule bridge crosses the Amstel at Kerkstraat between the Keizersgracht and Prinsengracht. The first bridge here dates to 1691, and the present version was built in 1934, earning its name because the original was famously narrow.
  4. Blauwbrug (Blue Bridge)
    This historic bridge over the Amstel connects the Rembrandtplein area with Waterlooplein, just south of the Stopera. It takes its name from a blue-painted wooden bridge that stood here from around 1600, and the current 1883 span is inspired by grand European bridge architecture.
  5. Montelbaanstoren
    Standing on the Oudeschans canal, this tower was originally built in 1516 as part of Amsterdam's defensive walls. Its decorative top half, designed by Hendrick de Keyser, was added in 1606, bringing the tower to its current 48-metre height.
  6. Munttoren (Mint Tower)
    This tower stands on the busy Muntplein square where the Amstel river meets the Singel canal, near the flower market and the eastern end of the Kalverstraat shopping street. It is one of the city centre's most recognisable landmarks.
  7. Brouwerij 't IJ
    This small brewery occupies a former bath house named Funen, right next to the De Gooyer windmill, and takes its name from the IJ waterfront. Opened by former musician Kaspar Peterson in October 1985, it was among the first of the Netherlands' modern small breweries.
  8. Royal Coster Diamonds
    Founded in 1840, this diamond polishing factory is the oldest in the world and has handled historic masterpieces including the re-polishing of the Koh-i-Noor and the Dresden Green Diamond. Visitors can see the craft of diamond polishing up close.
  9. Amsterdam Dungeon
    This haunted attraction in central Amsterdam depicts history through an interactive adventure using live actors, a ride, shows and special effects to simulate dark and bleak historical times. Opened in September 2005, it is operated by Merlin Entertainments, the company behind the London, York, Berlin and Hamburg Dungeons.
  10. De Kleine Komedie
    Dating from 1788, this is today the oldest theatre in Amsterdam, situated on the Amstel near the Halvemaansteeg. Its 503-seat venue stages both upcoming and established Dutch talent.

Practical tips

  • Walk or cycle the canal ring early in the morning for quieter streets and the best light on the gabled houses.
  • The Begijnhof is a quiet residential courtyard—keep noise down and check opening hours before visiting.
  • Brouwerij 't IJ is next to the De Gooyer windmill, making it an easy pairing; expect crowds on sunny weekend afternoons.
  • Book Amsterdam Dungeon tickets online in advance to skip queues, especially during school holidays.
  • Many sights cluster around the Amstel and Muntplein, so group the Blauwbrug, Magere Brug, Munttoren and De Kleine Komedie into a single walking route.
