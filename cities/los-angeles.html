Best Time to Visit Los Angeles

Los Angeles is the second-most populous city in the United States and the commercial, financial, and cultural center of Southern California, drawing over 3.6 million visitors in 2023. Visit in spring or early fall for mild temperatures and lighter crowds, or time a trip around its Olympic legacy as the city prepares to host the Summer Games for a third time in 2028.

Local Character

Los Angeles was founded on September 4, 1781, by Spanish governor Felipe de Neve on the village of Yaanga, a site originally inhabited by the indigenous Tongva people — a founding date that gives the city its Spanish name and its long layering of indigenous, Spanish, Mexican, and American influences. A visitor walking the city's historic core can trace that 1781 origin directly, which makes the downtown area more than a business district. The city's growth was shaped less by gold than by water and oil: the 1890s oil discovery brought rapid expansion, and the 1913 completion of the Los Angeles Aqueduct, which delivers water from Eastern California, made large-scale settlement possible. Understanding this engineered water supply explains why a semi-arid basin became a metropolis of 3.87 million people. Downtown Los Angeles is also evolving as a cultural center with the world's largest showcase of architecture designed by Frank Gehry, a draw for design-minded travelers that a generic beach-and-studio itinerary would miss. The city proper stretches about 469 square miles across the Los Angeles Basin, the Santa Monica Mountains, and the San Fernando Valley, so locals treat distance as a fact of daily life — plan your days by neighborhood rather than by landmark.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Downtown Los Angeles Architecture Walk — See the world's largest showcase of Frank Gehry-designed architecture while exploring the city's evolving cultural core.
  2. Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum — The historic stadium that hosted the 1932 and 1984 Summer Olympics and anchors the city's Olympic legacy ahead of 2028.
  3. Los Angeles Aqueduct Landmarks — Trace the 1913 water system that delivers water from Eastern California and made the modern city possible.
  4. Yaanga and Founding Site Area — Visit the historic core where the city was founded on September 4, 1781, atop the Tongva village of Yaanga.
  5. San Fernando Valley — Explore the northern stretch of the city beyond the Santa Monica Mountains, a vast area often skipped by beach-focused visitors.
