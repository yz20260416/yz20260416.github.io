Chongqing Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Chongqing, a sprawling megacity in southwest China where the Yangtze and Jialing rivers meet, is famous for its dramatic mountain-and-river skyline, spicy hotpot, and steep stairways that connect neighborhoods stacked up hillsides. As one of China's four municipalities, it blends historic wartime heritage with modern landmarks and a famously vibrant night scene. Below are ten top attractions with practical details to help you plan your visit. Note that prices and hours can change—always confirm locally or on official channels before you go.

  1. Hongya Cave (Hongyadong)

    A multi-level stilt-house complex modeled on traditional Ba architecture, glowing with lanterns at night and offering river views, shops, and snack stalls. It is one of Chongqing's most photographed landmarks.

    • Tickets: Free to enter the complex; individual venues charge separately.
    • Hours: Public areas generally open all day; shops and restaurants typically 10:00–22:00 (later on weekends).
    • Tips: Visit after dusk for the illuminated views, but arrive early to avoid crowds. The best photo spots are from Qiansimen Bridge and the riverside promenade.

  2. Ciqikou Ancient Town

    A preserved old town on the Jialing River known for stone-paved lanes, teahouses, temples, and local snacks such as ciba (glutinous rice cakes) and spicy noodles. It offers a glimpse of Chongqing's traditional river-town life.

    • Tickets: Free; some temples and museums charge small fees (roughly ¥10–30).
    • Hours: Open all day; shops typically 09:00–21:00.
    • Tips: Go on weekday mornings for quieter lanes. Wear comfortable shoes—the streets are uneven and hilly.

  3. Three Gorges Museum (Chongqing China Three Gorges Museum)

    A major museum covering the history of the Three Gorges, Ba-Yu culture, and wartime Chongqing. It sits beside the Great Hall of the People and is excellent for understanding the region before or after a Yangtze cruise.

    • Tickets: Free (reservation with ID/passport required).
    • Hours: Typically 09:00–17:00, closed Mondays (except public holidays).
    • Tips: Book ahead via the official WeChat account. Allow 2–3 hours; English signage is limited but audio guides are often available.

  4. Great Hall of the People (Chongqing People's Auditorium)

    A striking 1950s landmark with a distinctive domed roof inspired by traditional architecture, located on the edge of the city center. The surrounding plaza is popular for photos and evening strolls.

    • Tickets: Exterior and plaza free; interior visits usually require a small fee (around ¥10–20) or event tickets.
    • Hours: Plaza open all day; interior access varies by events—typically 09:00–17:00.
    • Tips: Combine with the adjacent Three Gorges Museum. Evening lighting makes for great photos.

  5. Liziba Light Rail Station (Monorail Through a Building)

    A viral photo spot where Line 2 of the monorail appears to pass through a residential building. It is a quick, free stop that showcases Chongqing's famously vertical urban design.

    • Tickets: Free to view; standard metro fare applies if riding.
    • Hours: Viewing area accessible all day; metro runs roughly 06:30–22:30.
    • Tips: Use the designated viewing platform across the street. Avoid peak commuting hours for better photos and less crowding.

  6. Raffles City Chongqing & Crystal Sky Bridge

    A modern riverside complex featuring a horizontal skyscraper and an observation deck with sweeping views of the confluence of the Yangtze and Jialing rivers. Ideal for skyline photography.

    • Tickets: Observation deck roughly ¥100–180 depending on package; mall entry free.
    • Hours: Observation deck typically 10:00–22:00; mall 10:00–22:00.
    • Tips: Go near sunset for day-to-night views. Check weather—haze can reduce visibility.

  7. Jiefangbei Pedestrian Street

    The commercial heart of Chongqing, anchored by the People's Liberation Monument. Surrounding streets are packed with shopping malls, hotpot restaurants, and street food, making it a lively base for exploring downtown.

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Hours: Open all day; shops generally 10:00–22:00.
    • Tips: Visit in the evening for the neon atmosphere. Use it as a starting point for walks to Hongya Cave and the riverside.

  8. Yangtze River Cableway

    A historic cable car crossing the Yangtze, offering aerial views of the river, bridges, and dense hillside neighborhoods. A short but memorable ride that captures the city's geography.

    • Tickets: Roughly ¥20–30 one way (prices vary by season and route).
    • Hours: Typically 07:30–22:00; confirm current schedule.
    • Tips: Ride around sunset for dramatic light. Queues can be long on holidays—buy tickets early or go off-peak.

  9. Eling Park

    A hilltop park with gardens, pavilions, and panoramic views over the rivers and city. It is a quieter green escape and a good spot to watch the skyline, especially in the evening.

    • Tickets: Free (some small attractions may charge).
    • Hours: Generally 06:00–22:00.
    • Tips: Bring water and wear sturdy shoes—paths are steep. The viewing platforms are best at dusk.

  10. Dazu Rock Carvings

    A UNESCO World Heritage Site featuring thousands of Buddhist, Taoist, and Confucian carvings dating from the 7th to 13th centuries. It is a day trip from central Chongqing and a highlight for history and art lovers.

    • Tickets: Roughly ¥115–135 for the main Baodingshan site (combination tickets may cost more).
    • Hours: Typically 08:30–18:00 (last entry around 17:00).
    • Tips: Allow a full day including travel (about 1.5–2 hours each way by car/bus). Go early, bring sun protection, and hire a guide or audio guide for context.

Practical Tips for Visiting Chongqing

  • Getting around: The metro is fast and affordable; use a transportation card or mobile payment. Taxis and ride-hailing are common but traffic can be heavy.
  • Weather: Summers are hot and humid; winters are cool and damp. Bring layers and an umbrella—rain is frequent.
  • Terrain: Expect steep stairs and hills. Comfortable walking shoes are essential.
