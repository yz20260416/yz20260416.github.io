San Francisco Top 10 Attractions

San Francisco packs an extraordinary amount of scenery, history, and culture into 49 square miles. From the iconic Golden Gate Bridge to the historic cells of Alcatraz, the city rewards travelers who plan ahead — many top attractions sell out days or weeks in advance, and the famous fog can change your itinerary in an afternoon. This guide covers the ten essential stops, what tickets cost, and how to make the most of each visit. Prices are typical adult admission as of recent seasons and change frequently, so always confirm on official websites before booking.

Detailed Descriptions

1. Golden Gate Bridge

The world's most photographed bridge and San Francisco's defining landmark. Walk or bike the 1.7-mile span for sweeping views of the bay, Alcatraz, and the Marin Headlands. The welcome center on the San Francisco side offers exhibits on the bridge's 1937 engineering triumph.

Tickets: Free to walk or bike. Drivers pay a toll southbound (electronic only; roughly $8–9).

Tips: Go early morning or late afternoon for the best light and fewer crowds. Bring a windproof layer — it's consistently 10°F colder and breezier on the bridge than in the city. For the classic postcard view, head to Battery Spencer or Vista Point on the Marin side.

2. Alcatraz Island

The notorious former federal penitentiary on a rocky island in the bay now operates as a national park site. The ferry ride, cellhouse audio tour (narrated by former guards and inmates), and harbor views make this one of the city's most memorable experiences.

Tickets: Approximately $45–55 for adults, including ferry and audio tour. Night tours cost more.

Tips: Book two to four weeks ahead — summer and holiday dates sell out fast. Only one authorized operator (Alcatraz City Cruises) sells tickets; avoid third-party resellers. Dress in layers, and allow about 3 hours round-trip.

3. Fisherman's Wharf & Pier 39

A lively waterfront district famous for its colony of lounging sea lions, clam chowder in sourdough bowls, street performers, and views toward Alcatraz. It's touristy but genuinely fun, especially for families.

Tickets: Free to wander. Nearby attractions (Aquarium of the Bay, Madame Tussauds, bay cruises) charge separately, typically $25–40 each.

Tips: Visit the sea lions mid-morning when they're most active. Eat at the working fishing piers (like Pier 45) for fresher, better-value seafood than the main promenade. Combine with a walk to Ghirardelli Square for chocolate and views.

4. Golden Gate Park

A 1,017-acre urban park larger than Central Park, stretching from the city center to the Pacific. Highlights include the Japanese Tea Garden, the de Young Museum, the Conservatory of Flowers, Stow Lake, and the bison paddock.

Tickets: Park entry free. Japanese Tea Garden ~$10–16; de Young ~$15–20; Conservatory of Flowers ~$10–14. Some museums offer free admission days.

Tips: Rent a bike or use the park's shuttle on weekends. Sunday brings road closures for pedestrians and cyclists (JFK Drive). Pair the park with a walk on Ocean Beach at the western end.

5. California Academy of Sciences

A combined aquarium, planetarium, natural history museum, and four-story rainforest dome under one living roof. It's one of the city's best rainy-day and family attractions.

Tickets: Approximately $40–45 for adults; discounts for children, seniors, and students. Thursday nights (21+) are often cheaper.

Tips: Reserve planetarium show times at entry — they fill up. Arrive at opening to beat school groups. The living roof offers great views of Golden Gate Park.

6. Lombard Street

The famous "crookedest street," an eight-switchback block on Russian Hill lined with hydrangeas and brick paving. It's a five-minute stop, but an essential San Francisco photo.

Tickets: Free.
