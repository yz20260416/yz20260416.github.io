Top Attractions in San Francisco

San Francisco packs an extraordinary range of experiences into a compact, hilly peninsula: historic neighborhoods, waterfront landmarks, sprawling parks, and one of the world's most recognizable bridges. Whether you ride a vintage streetcar along the bay, wander the oldest Chinatown in North America, or climb a mosaic-covered staircase for sweeping views, the city rewards exploration on foot and by transit. Here are ten attractions that should top any international visitor's itinerary.

  1. Fisherman's Wharf
    This northern waterfront neighborhood is one of the city's best-known tourist districts, historically a working fishing port and now a lively hub of seafood stands, piers, and bay views. The Aquatic Park Historic District is often considered part of the area.
    Tip: Arrive in the morning before the crowds build, and combine it with a walk to nearby Pier 39 and the aquarium.
  2. Chinatown
    Centered on Grant Avenue and Stockton Street, this is the oldest Chinatown in North America and one of the largest Chinese enclaves outside Asia, established in the early 1850s. It has retained its own customs, language, and character while shaping Chinese-American history.
    Tip: Enter through the Dragon Gate on Grant Avenue and explore the side alleys, where you'll find small shops and food producers.
  3. Golden Gate Fortune Cookie Company
    Opened in 1962 and tucked off Ross Alley in Chinatown, this small factory makes traditional fortune cookies plus chocolate, almond, and other sweets. Visitors can watch workers fold cookies by hand on motorized circular griddles.
    Tip: Go on a weekday morning for a quieter look at the production line; small purchases are expected if you observe the workers.
  4. Castro District
    Commonly called "the Castro," this Eureka Valley neighborhood was one of the first gay neighborhoods in the United States and remains one of the world's most prominent symbols of LGBTQ+ activism and events.
    Tip: Visit the historic Castro Theatre area and the rainbow crosswalks; weekends are liveliest, but weekdays are calmer for walking.
  5. Conservatory of Flowers
    Completed in 1879, this greenhouse and botanical garden in Golden Gate Park is the park's oldest building and the oldest remaining municipal wooden conservatory in the United States. It houses a collection of rare and exotic plants.
    Tip: Combine it with a stroll through Golden Gate Park; check current opening days and admission prices before you go.
  6. 16th Avenue Tiled Steps
    This 163-step stairway in the Golden Gate Heights neighborhood stretches 90 feet high and is covered in over 2,000 unique tiles made from more than 75,000 glass fragments. Fodor's calls it "possibly the world's largest mosaic staircase," and it leads up to Grandview Park.
    Tip: Go early in the morning for soft light and fewer visitors, then continue to the top of Grandview Park for panoramic city and ocean views.
  7. Fort Point National Historic Site
    This masonry seacoast fortification stands on the southern side of the Golden Gate at the entrance to San Francisco Bay, originally fortified by the Spanish in 1794. The current fort sits directly beneath the Golden Gate Bridge's southern approach.
    Tip: Check National Park Service hours before visiting, and bring a jacket; the wind at the bridge's base can be strong year-round.
  8. Fort Mason
    This former U.S. Army post in the Marina District served for more than a century, first as a coastal defense site and later as a military port that was the principal Pacific campaign port during World War II. Today it hosts cultural venues and open waterfront space.
    Tip: Walk the waterfront paths toward Aquatic Park and Ghirardelli Square for an easy, scenic half-day route.
  9. Aquarium of the Bay
    Located at The Embarcadero and Beach Street at the edge of Pier 39, this public aquarium focuses on local aquatic animals from San Francisco Bay and neighboring watersheds as far as the Sierra Mountains. Since 2005 its mission has centered on ocean conservation and climate action.
    Tip: Buy tickets online in advance to skip lines, and visit on a weekday morning when school groups are fewer.
  10. F Market & Wharves
    This heritage streetcar line runs through the city using historic equipment from San Francisco's retired fleet and from cities around the world, operated by Muni with support from the nonprofit Market Street Railway. It's a scenic and practical way to travel between the Castro, Market Street, and the waterfront.
    Tip: Board at the Castro or along Market Street and ride to Fisherman's Wharf; a standard Muni fare covers the trip, and vintage cars vary day to day.

Practical tips

  • San Francisco's weather varies by neighborhood and time of day; layer clothing and carry a windproof jacket, especially near the Golden Gate and the waterfront.
  • Use Muni streetcars, buses, and cable cars for sightseeing; the F Market & Wharves line doubles as transport and an attraction.
  • Visit popular spots like Fisherman's Wharf and Chinatown early in the morning to avoid peak crowds.
  • Check official websites for current opening hours and ticket prices, since schedules for sites such as the Conservatory of Flowers and Fort Point can change seasonally.
  • Wear comfortable walking shoes: many of the city's highlights, from the Tiled Steps to the Castro, are best explored on foot over hilly terrain.
