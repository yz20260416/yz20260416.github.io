Buenos Aires 3-Day Itinerary

Buenos Aires is a sprawling, passionate capital where grand European-style boulevards meet colorful working-class barrios, and where dinner rarely starts before 9 p.m. Three days is enough to sample its essential flavors: the historic plaza where Argentina's independence took shape, the painted houses of La Boca, the elegant cafés and bookstores of Recoleta, and — above all — the tango, the steak, and the malbec. This itinerary balances headline sights with neighborhood wandering, and it builds in the late starts and long lunches that define porteño life. Use it as a flexible framework: book the tango show and any museum visits in advance, and leave room for spontaneous detours, because Buenos Aires rewards slow exploration.

Day 1 — Historic Center, San Telmo & the Birth of Tango

TimeActivityNotes & Food
9:00 a.m. Plaza de Mayo walking loop: Casa Rosada, Cabildo, and the Metropolitan Cathedral. Free to wander; the cathedral (Pope Francis's former parish) is open to visitors. Watch for the Mothers of Plaza de Mayo on Thursday afternoons.
11:00 a.m. Museo Histórico Nacional or the Manzana de las Luces (block of enlightenment). Pick one — both are compact and give context to the city's colonial and independence history.
1:00 p.m. Lunch in San Telmo at El Federal (Carlos Calvo 599). Classic corner bar with wood-paneled charm; order the milanesa or a picada board. Budget roughly US$15–20 per person.
3:00 p.m. San Telmo stroll: Plaza Dorrego, Defensa Street antique shops, and the Mercado de San Telmo. The famous Sunday antiques fair is the highlight — if your trip includes a Sunday, swap days accordingly. Otherwise the market hall is still worth a graze.
5:30 p.m. La Boca: El Caminito's painted conventillos and the Boca Juniors stadium exterior. Go with daylight and stick to the main tourist streets; take a taxi or rideshare rather than wandering far off El Caminito. ~20 min from San Telmo.
8:30 p.m. Dinner and tango at El Viejo Almacén (Independencia 299) or Señor Tango in San Telmo. Book ahead. Dinner-and-show packages run roughly US$60–100 per person including wine. Arrive early for the best tables.

Day 2 — Recoleta, Palermo & Modern Buenos Aires

TimeActivityNotes & Food
9:00 a.m. Breakfast at Café La Biela (Av. Quintana 596), a Recoleta institution facing the cemetery. Order medialunas and a café con leche. Expect a tourist markup but unbeatable people-watching.
10:00 a.m. Recoleta Cemetery guided walk, then the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes (free entry). The cemetery is a labyrinth of mausoleums including Eva Perón's tomb; a guide (or audio tour) is worth it. The fine arts museum has an excellent Argentine collection.
12:30 p.m. Walk or taxi to Palermo; browse the MALBA (Museo de Arte Latinoamericano de Buenos Aires). MALBA's collection of Latin American modern art is a highlight; check opening hours (closed Tuesdays).
2:00 p.m. Lunch in Palermo Soho at Don Julio (Guatemala 4691) — book well in advance — or La Cabrera (José Antonio Cabrera 5099). Both are parrilla landmarks. Expect US$25–40 per person with wine. If fully booked, try Las Cabras nearby for a more casual grill.
4:30 p.m. Palermo shopping and park time: Serrano and Thames streets for boutiques, then the Rose Garden (El Rosedal) in Parque Tres de Febrero. Rent a rowboat on the lake or simply enjoy the gardens; the Japanese Garden is a short walk away.
8:00 p.m. Sunset drinks at a Palermo rooftop (e.g., Trading Post or Sky Bar), then dinner at Oviedo (Beruti 2602) for refined Spanish-Argentine seafood. Porteños eat late — a 9:30 p.m. reservation is normal. Oviedo is a splurge (~US$50+ pp) but consistently excellent.
