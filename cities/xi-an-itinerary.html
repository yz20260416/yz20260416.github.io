Xi'an, the capital of Shaanxi Province and one of China's Four Great Ancient Capitals, served as the imperial seat for 13 dynasties over more than 1,100 years. Today it blends the Terracotta Army, intact Ming city walls, and the Muslim Quarter's living food culture with a fast-growing modern skyline. This three-day plan is paced for first-time visitors: it covers the essential sights, spreads the major attractions across manageable distances, and leaves room for Xi'an's excellent food. All timings assume you start each day around 8:00–8:30 a.m.

Day 1: Terracotta Army, City Wall, and the Muslim Quarter

TimePlanNotes
Morning Terracotta Army (Bingmayong), about 40 km east of the city Leave by 8:00 a.m. to arrive near opening (8:30 a.m.). Visit pits in order 1 → 3 → 2, then the Bronze Chariot Hall. Allow 3–4 hours including travel. Book tickets online in advance (passport required); the site is often sold out on holidays.
Afternoon Return to the city; lunch near the Bell Tower; Xi'an City Wall (South Gate, Yongningmen) Rent a bike on the wall (about 45 CNY for 90 minutes) or walk the 13.7 km loop. The South Gate is the most impressive entry point. Late afternoon light is best for photos.
Evening Muslim Quarter (Huimin Jie) food walk; Bell and Drum Towers illuminated Start at the Drum Tower and wander the side alleys (Beiyuanmen and Dapiyuan) rather than the main tourist strip. The towers are lit after dark and can be viewed from the square without a ticket.

Restaurant picks: Lunch at De Fa Chang (dumpling banquet near the Bell Tower) or a quick biangbiang noodle shop; dinner graze the Muslim Quarter — roujiamo (Chinese "burger"), yangrou paomo (mutton soup with torn bread), and liangpi cold noodles. For a sit-down dinner, Xi'an Restaurant (Zhonglou branch) serves classic Shaanxi dishes.

Day 2: Shaanxi History Museum, Big Wild Goose Pagoda, and Tang-Style Nightlife

TimePlanNotes
Morning Shaanxi History Museum Free entry but timed tickets must be reserved online days ahead (passport needed); the paid Treasures Gallery is worth it. Allow 2–3 hours. Closed Mondays.
Afternoon Big Wild Goose Pagoda (Dayan Ta) and Da Ci'en Temple; optional Shaanxi Grand Opera teahouse The pagoda dates to the Tang dynasty and is linked to the monk Xuanzang. Climb for city views or tour the temple grounds. The North Square fountains run on a schedule in the afternoon and evening.
Evening Tang Paradise (Datang Furong Yuan) or the Grand Tang Mall (Datang Buyecheng) The Grand Tang Mall is a free, pedestrian boulevard lined with Tang-style architecture, street performers in period costume, and light shows — a relaxed evening stroll. Tang Paradise is a paid garden-and-show complex nearby.

Restaurant picks: Lunch near the museum at Wei Jia Liangpi (casual, famous cold noodles) or First Noodle Under the Sun (Yijia Mian) for hand-pulled noodles; dinner at Zui Chang'an for modern Shaanxi cuisine near the pagoda, or the food court at the Grand Tang Mall.

Day 3: Bell Tower, Great Mosque, and the Old City Core

TimePlanNotes
Morning Bell Tower and Drum Tower; Great Mosque (Huajue Xiang) The two towers sit a short walk apart; a combined ticket saves money. The Great Mosque, founded in the Tang era, blends Islamic and Chinese architecture and is an active place of worship — dress modestly and keep noise down.
Afternoon Stele Forest Museum (Beilin) or Daxingshan Temple; wander the Shuyuanmen calligraphy street The Stele Forest holds thousands of engraved stone tablets spanning 2,000 years — a quiet counterpoint to the crowds. Shuyuanmen, just outside, is a pleasant street for calligraphy brushes, ink, and souvenirs.
Evening Farewell dinner; optional Chang Hen Ge dance show at Huaqing Palace (if staying an extra night) or a Tang-dynasty music-and-dance performance in the city Book show tickets a day ahead. Otherwise, end with a slow dinner in the old city and a final walk along the illuminated South Gate.

Restaurant picks: Lunch at Fan Ji Lazhi Roujiamo (a Xi'an institution for pork sandwiches) or Lao Sun Jia for yangrou paomo; dinner at Haidilao for hotpot or a local kaorou (barbecue) spot in the Muslim Quarter's side streets.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Metro Line 2 connects the Bell Tower, South Gate, and the airport line interchange; Line 9 serves the Terracotta Army area (with a short bus/taxi link). Taxis and ride-hailing apps are cheap but traffic is heavy — prefer the metro for long hops.
  • Tickets: Major sites (Terracotta Army, Shaanxi History Museum, City Wall) require online reservations with your passport number, often days in advance. Avoid Chinese public holidays (especially the first week of October and Lunar New Year) when crowds are extreme.
  • Best seasons: Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) are mildest. Summers are hot and humid; winters are cold and dry but quieter.
  • Money and connectivity: Mobile payment (Alipay/WeChat Pay) is near-universal; set up an international card link before arrival. Carry some cash for small vendors. A local SIM or eSIM helps with maps and translation apps.
  • Etiquette and safety: Dress modestly at the Great Mosque and active temples. Bargain politely in markets. Xi'an is generally very safe; watch for pickpockets in the crowded Muslim Quarter at night.
  • Language: English signage is common at major sights but limited elsewhere — save Chinese names of destinations and dishes on your phone.
  • Pacing: The Terracotta Army day involves the most travel; keep the evening light. If you have a fourth day, add Huaqing Palace and Mount Li, or a day trip to the Qianling Mausoleum.
