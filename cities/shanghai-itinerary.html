Shanghai 3-Day Itinerary

Shanghai is a city of dazzling contrasts, where 1920s art-deco facades stand in the shadow of futuristic skyscrapers and traditional longtang alleys hum with modern life. This three-day itinerary is designed to help you experience the best of both worlds—the historic Bund and French Concession, the cutting-edge Pudong skyline, and the city's celebrated food scene—without feeling rushed. Each day is organized around a logical geographic cluster so you spend less time in transit and more time exploring. Whether it's your first visit or a return trip, these schedules balance must-see landmarks with authentic local experiences and restaurant recommendations for every meal.

Day 1 — Classic Shanghai & the Bund

Morning: People's Square & Shanghai Museum

Start at People's Square (Metro Lines 1, 2, 8), the civic heart of the city. Visit the Shanghai Museum (free admission, closed Mondays) to see superb collections of ancient bronzes, ceramics, and calligraphy—allow about 1.5–2 hours. Stroll through the adjacent People's Park to watch locals practicing tai chi.

Midday: Nanjing Road & Lunch

Walk east along Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street, one of the world's busiest shopping avenues, toward the Bund.

Lunch recommendation: Jia Jia Tang Bao (near People's Square) for famous soup dumplings (xiaolongbao), or Yang's Fry-Dumpling for a quick, budget-friendly Shanghai classic.

Afternoon: The Bund & Yu Garden

Arrive at the Bund (Waitan) and walk the 1.5 km promenade lined with historic colonial-era buildings facing the Pudong skyline. Continue to the Old City and explore Yu Garden (Yuyuan), a classical Ming-dynasty garden with rockeries, pavilions, and ponds. The surrounding bazaar is lively but touristy—great for souvenirs and snacks.

Evening: Bund Night Views & Dinner

Return to the Bund for sunset and the illuminated skyline—arguably Shanghai's signature moment. For a splurge, book a river cruise (about 45–60 minutes) from the Shiliupu or Jinling Road pier.

Dinner recommendation: Lost Heaven (Yunnan folk cuisine, Bund location) for a memorable meal with skyline views, or Din Tai Fung (Xintiandi) for reliable dumplings and Taiwanese dishes.

Day 2 — Modern Pudong & Local Culture

Morning: Lujiazui Skyline

Cross to Pudong (Metro Line 2 to Lujiazui) and head up one of the iconic towers. The Shanghai Tower observation deck (118th floor) offers the highest panoramic view in China; the Oriental Pearl Tower and SWFC (100th floor) are excellent alternatives. Book tickets online in advance to skip queues.

Midday: Riverside Walk & Lunch

Explore the Lujiazui waterfront and the elevated pedestrian bridges, then visit the Shanghai Ocean Aquarium if traveling with family.

Lunch recommendation: Fu He Hui (vegetarian fine dining, Michelin- starred) for a special occasion, or the food halls at IFC Mall for convenient mid-range options.

Afternoon: Xintiandi & Shikumen Culture

Return to Puxi and wander Xintiandi, a restored shikumen (stone-gate) neighborhood now filled with boutiques, galleries, and cafés. Visit the Site of the First National Congress of the CPC nearby for historical context, and relax in the leafy lanes.
