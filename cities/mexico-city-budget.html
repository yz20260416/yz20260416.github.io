Mexico City Travel Cost Guide

Mexico City is one of the most rewarding destinations in the Americas — a sprawling capital of ancient pyramids, world-class museums, colonial architecture, and arguably the best street food on the continent. The good news for travelers is that it remains remarkably affordable compared to other major global cities, though costs vary widely depending on your travel style. Budget backpackers can get by on roughly $35–50 USD per day, mid-range travelers should plan for $80–150 USD per day, and those seeking luxury hotels, fine dining, and private tours can easily spend $250+ USD daily. This guide breaks down realistic costs across accommodation, food, transport, and activities so you can plan a trip that fits your budget. All figures are in US dollars (converted from Mexican pesos at roughly 17–18 MXN per USD) and reflect 2024–2025 prices.

Accommodation in Detail

  • Hostels: Dorm beds run $12–25 per night in popular areas like Roma Norte, Condesa, and the Historic Center. Private hostel rooms cost $35–60. Quality is generally high, with many hostels offering rooftop bars, free breakfast, and coworking spaces.
  • Budget hotels: Simple hotels (often called hoteles) in the Centro Histórico start around $30–45 per night for a double room.
  • Mid-range: Boutique hotels and well-located Airbnbs in Roma, Condesa, and Juárez run $60–120 per night. Book 2–4 weeks ahead for the best rates, especially during festivals.
  • Luxury: Polanco and Paseo de la Reforma are home to five-star properties (Four Seasons, St. Regis, Sofitel) starting around $200 and climbing well past $500 per night.

Food Costs

  • Street food & markets: Tacos al pastor ($0.70–1.50 each), quesadillas, tlacoyos, and tortas keep daily food costs under $15. Mercado de San Juan and Mercado de Medellín are excellent for cheap, high-quality eats.
  • Casual restaurants: Comida corrida (set lunch) at fondas costs $5–8 and includes soup, main, and agua fresca. Mid-range restaurants charge $10–20 per main.
  • Fine dining: Mexico City is a global gastronomic capital. Tasting menus at Pujol or Quintonil run $150–250+ per person without drinks; a nice dinner at a trendy Roma restaurant is $40–70 per person.
  • Coffee & drinks: Specialty coffee $3–4; craft beer $4–6; cocktails at upscale bars $10–15.

Transportation

  • Metro: At roughly $0.30 per ride, it's one of the world's cheapest and most extensive subway systems. Avoid rush hours (7–9 AM, 6–8 PM) and be mindful of pickpockets.
  • Metrobús & RTP buses: Similarly cheap and useful for routes the Metro doesn't cover.
  • Uber & DiDi: Widely available and inexpensive — most cross-city trips cost $4–10. Use them at night for safety and convenience.
  • Airport transfer: Authorized airport taxis cost $12–18; Uber from the airport $8–15; the Metrobús Line 4 is under $1 if you're traveling light.
  • Bike share: Ecobici requires registration; day passes are around $5 and great for Roma/Condesa exploration.
