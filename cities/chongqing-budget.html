Chongqing is one of China's most affordable major cities, despite its reputation as a megacity of 32 million people. Prices here sit noticeably below Beijing and Shanghai, which makes it a favorite for budget travelers exploring southwestern China. The city's famous hotpot, dense public transport network, and many free or low-cost attractions mean you can travel comfortably on almost any budget. The figures below are daily estimates in Chinese yuan (CNY), with approximate US dollar equivalents at a rate of roughly 7.2 CNY to 1 USD. Costs are per person and assume shared costs where relevant.

Accommodation Details

  • Backpacker: Hostels cluster around Jiefangbei and Ciqikou, with dorm beds typically 60–120 CNY. Private hostel rooms run 150–250 CNY.
  • Mid-Range: Business hotels and 3–4 star chains (e.g., Atour, Holiday Inn Express) charge 300–600 CNY for doubles, often including breakfast. Locations near metro lines in Yuzhong and Jiangbei districts offer the best value.
  • Luxury: Five-star properties with Yangtze or Jialing river views (e.g., near Jiefangbei or Nan'an) start around 1,200 CNY and climb well past 3,000 CNY for suites. High-rise "view rooms" are the signature splurge in Chongqing.

Food Costs

  • Backpacker: Street noodles (xiaomian) cost 8–15 CNY, dumplings and snacks 5–15 CNY, and a basic single-person hotpot 40–70 CNY. Convenience-store meals keep daily food under 90 CNY.
  • Mid-Range: A proper Chongqing hotpot meal for two runs 150–300 CNY total; local restaurants serve full meals for 40–80 CNY per person. Coffee and Western food cost more (30–50 CNY).
  • Luxury: Premium hotpot chains and hotel restaurants charge 300–800 CNY per person, and fine dining with river views can exceed 1,000 CNY. Hotel breakfast buffets are often included in room rates.

Transport Costs

  • Metro: Fares run 2–10 CNY depending on distance; a stored-value card or mobile QR payment is convenient. The famous Liziba station (monorail through a building) is on Line 2.
  • Taxis/Didi: Flag fall is about 10 CNY; cross-town trips typically 20–50 CNY. Didi (ride-hailing) is widely used and often cheaper.
  • Airport: Metro Line 10 connects Jiangbei Airport to the city for under 10 CNY; taxis cost 80–120 CNY. The high-speed rail station (Chongqing North/West) links to the metro network.
  • River ferries and cable car: The Yangtze River Cable Car costs 20–30 CNY one way; cross-river ferries are similarly cheap.

Activity Costs

  • Free: Hongya Cave (exterior and terraces), Ciqikou Old Town, Liziba monorail viewpoint, Eling Park, and many riverside walks cost nothing.
  • Low-cost: Yangtze River Cable Car (20–30 CNY), Two Rivers night cruise (80–150 CNY), Three Gorges Museum (free entry), and Raffles City observation deck (around 100 CNY).
  • Day trips: Dazu Rock Carvings (transport plus entry roughly 150–250 CNY), Wulong Karst (organized tours 300–500 CNY).
  • Luxury: Private guides (400–800 CNY/day), helicopter or premium night cruises, and spa packages push activity spending higher.

Practical Tips

  • Cash vs. mobile payment: WeChat Pay and Alipay are near-universal; set these up before arrival. Carry some cash (200–500 CNY) for small vendors and older shops.
  • Book hotpot wisely: Popular hotpot restaurants have long queues; arrive before 6pm or use queue apps. Ask for "micro-spicy" (微辣) if you are not used to heat.
  • Stay near a metro line: Chongqing's hills and heat make walking between districts tiring; metro-adjacent hotels save both time and taxi money.
  • Summer heat: July–August temperatures exceed 35°C; budget for taxis or indoor activities, and plan outdoor sightseeing for mornings and evenings.
  • Language: English is limited outside major hotels; translation apps and screenshots of addresses help enormously.
  • Tipping: Not customary in China; service charges at luxury hotels are usually already included.
