Top Attractions in Ho Chi Minh City

Ho Chi Minh City blends French colonial landmarks, historic wartime sites, Chinese heritage and family-friendly parks into one energetic metropolis. From the grand opera house and city hall to the vast Chinatown of Chợ Lớn and the legendary Củ Chi tunnels, the city offers a compact but varied set of highlights for international visitors. This guide covers ten of the most rewarding stops, each with a practical tip to help you plan your day.

  1. Independence Palace (Reunification Hall)
    This historic landmark was designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ and served as the presidential palace, home and workplace of the president of South Vietnam, and it was the site of the Fall of Saigon. Today it stands as one of the city's most important historic sites.
    Tip: Visit in the morning to avoid the midday heat, and allow at least an hour to explore the grounds and preserved rooms.
  2. Củ Chi Tunnels
    This immense network of connecting tunnels in Củ Chi District was the location of several military campaigns during the Vietnam War and served as the Viet Cong's base of operations for the Tết Offensive in 1968. Soldiers used the tunnels as hiding spots during combat.
    Tip: The site is outside the city center, so book a half-day tour or arrange transport in advance and wear comfortable shoes.
  3. Saigon Central Post Office
    This handsome post office sits in Paris Commune Square in Saigon ward, right next to the Saigon Notre-Dame Basilica, the city's cathedral. Its colonial architecture makes it one of downtown's most photographed landmarks.
    Tip: Combine your visit with the neighboring cathedral and visit early in the day for cooler weather and fewer crowds.
  4. Saigon Opera House
    This municipal opera house in downtown Ho Chi Minh City was designed by French architects and completed in 1900 as the Opéra de Saïgon. The 500-seat building is a fine example of French colonial architecture in Vietnam.
    Tip: Check the performance schedule in advance if you want to see a show, otherwise visit the exterior in the late afternoon when the light is best.
  5. Ho Chi Minh City Hall
    Officially the Ho Chi Minh City People's Council and People's Committee Head Office, this city hall is the seat of the city government. It faces Nguyễn Huệ Boulevard and houses the office of the city's Chairman.
    Tip: The building is not generally open for public tours, so view the illuminated facade from Nguyễn Huệ pedestrian boulevard in the evening.
  6. Chợ Lớn (Cholon)
    This unofficial quarter on the west bank of the Saigon River was historically a separate city before its urbanization in the 1930s–1950s and has long been inhabited by Chinese people. It is considered the largest Chinatown in the world by area, spanning former Districts 5 and 6.
    Tip: Explore the temples and markets on foot in the morning, and bring small cash for snacks and temple donations.
  7. Mariamman Temple
    This temple is dedicated to the Hindu goddess Mariamman and was built in the late 19th century by the Nagarathar, a trading community from Tamil Nadu, India. It remains an active and colorful place of worship in the city.
    Tip: Remove your shoes before entering and dress modestly; mornings are quieter and better for photography.
  8. Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens
    Commissioned in 1864 and opened to the public in 1869, this is Vietnam's oldest zoo and botanical garden and one of the world's oldest continuously operating zoos. The botanical gardens were founded in 1864.
    Tip: Go early in the morning when animals are most active and the gardens are coolest; it is a good option for families.
  9. Suối Tiên Theme Park
    This amusement park in Tăng Nhơn Phú features several entertainment areas whose landscaping and attractions illustrate Vietnam's history and legends, including the story of Âu Cơ and Lạc Long Quân. It also has an artificial seawater pool, a dinosaur garden and a roller coaster.
    Tip: Set aside a full day and bring swimwear and sun protection if you plan to use the seawater pool.
  10. Bình Quới Tourist Village
    This tourist attraction in Bình Thạnh District comprises two parts of the Bình Quới Tourist Area, established by the Vietnamese government in 1975–1976 and expanded with a second area built from 1979 to 1980. It sits on the Thanh Da peninsula on the Saigon River.
    Tip: Come in the late afternoon for river breezes and sunset views, and try the riverside dining typical of the area.

Practical tips

  • Start major landmarks such as Independence Palace and the Central Post Office early in the morning to beat both the heat and the tour groups.
  • Dress modestly and remove shoes when visiting temples such as the Mariamman Temple.
  • For outlying sites like the Củ Chi Tunnels and Suối Tiên Theme Park, arrange transport or a guided tour in advance, as they are well outside the downtown core.
  • Many government buildings, including City Hall, are best appreciated from the outside; check opening details before planning an interior visit.
  • Carry small cash for entrance fees, temple donations and snacks, and keep hydrated during the hotter midday hours.
