Ho Chi Minh City: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Ho Chi Minh City, still widely known as Saigon, is Vietnam's largest metropolis and its economic heart. The city blends French colonial architecture, wartime history, and a fast-moving modern street life. Below are ten attractions that offer a well-rounded introduction to the city, with practical details to help you plan your visit. Prices and hours can change, so confirm current information on official websites or at your hotel before setting out.

  1. Independence Palace (Reunification Palace)

    The former presidential palace of South Vietnam is famous as the site where a North Vietnamese tank crashed through the gates on April 30, 1975, marking the end of the Vietnam War. The preserved interiors, war rooms, and rooftop helicopter pad make it one of the city's most historically significant stops.

    • Ticket price: Approximately 65,000 VND for adults; discounts for children and students.
    • Opening hours: Daily, roughly 8:00–11:30 and 13:00–16:00 (closed for lunch).
    • Visiting tips: Arrive early to avoid tour groups; allow 1–1.5 hours. The on-site exhibits are largely self-guided, so a guidebook or audio guide helps.

  2. War Remnants Museum

    This museum documents the Vietnam War through photographs, military equipment, and personal accounts. Exhibits on Agent Orange and civilian casualties are powerful and emotionally heavy. Expect a sobering but important experience.

    • Ticket price: Approximately 40,000 VND for adults.
    • Opening hours: Daily, about 7:30–17:30 (last entry around 17:00).
    • Visiting tips: Set aside 1.5–2 hours. Some exhibits are graphic; consider whether they suit younger children. Visit in the morning when it is cooler and less crowded.

  3. Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

    Built in the late 19th century with materials imported from France, this red-brick cathedral is one of the city's most recognizable landmarks. It sits at the heart of District 1, facing the historic post office.

    • Ticket price: Free to view from outside; interior access is limited and depends on mass schedules and restoration work.
    • Opening hours: Exterior viewable at any time; mass times are typically in the early morning and evening.
    • Visiting tips: The cathedral has undergone long-term restoration, so check whether the interior is open. Dress modestly if attending mass, and avoid photographing worshippers during services.

  4. Saigon Central Post Office

    Designed in the French colonial style and completed in the late 19th century, this working post office is a charming architectural relic with vaulted ceilings, old maps, and wooden phone booths. It is directly across from the cathedral, making the two easy to combine.

    • Ticket price: Free.
    • Opening hours: Daily, approximately 7:00–19:00.
    • Visiting tips: It is an active post office, so be mindful of customers. You can buy postcards and stamps here and mail them on site for a memorable souvenir.

  5. Ben Thanh Market

    The city's most famous market is a landmark in District 1, selling clothing, souvenirs, textiles, coffee, and street food. It is busy, colorful, and a good introduction to local commerce.

    • Ticket price: Free to enter.
    • Opening hours: Roughly 6:00–18:00 for the main market; a night market operates nearby in the evening.
    • Visiting tips: Bargain politely—start at about half the quoted price. Watch your belongings in crowds, and agree on prices before buying food.

  6. Bitexco Financial Tower Skydeck

    This 68-story tower offers panoramic views over the city from its observation deck. The helipad-inspired design is a modern counterpoint to the city's colonial architecture. Sunset is a popular time to visit.

    • Ticket price: Approximately 200,000 VND for adults; reduced rates for children and seniors.
    • Opening hours: Daily, roughly 9:30–21:30.
    • Visiting tips: Go shortly before sunset for day and night views. Check for online discounts, and note that drinks at the skydeck bar are extra.

  7. Jade Emperor Pagoda

    Built in the early 20th century by the Cantonese community, this Taoist temple is dedicated to the Jade Emperor. It is known for its intricate carvings, incense smoke, and turtle pond, and remains an active place of worship.

    • Ticket price: Free (donations welcome).
    • Opening hours: Daily, approximately 7:00–18:00.
    • Visiting tips: Dress respectfully (cover shoulders and knees). Avoid flash photography, keep quiet, and do not disturb worshippers. Mornings are quieter.

  8. Cholon (Chinatown) and Binh Tay Market

    The Cholon district is the city's historic Chinatown, home to Chinese temples, herbal medicine shops, and bustling markets. Binh Tay Market is the area's wholesale hub and a lively place to explore local trade and food.

    • Ticket price: Free to explore; temple donations optional.
    • Opening hours: Binh Tay Market roughly 6:00–18:00; temples generally daytime hours.
    • Visiting tips: Go in the morning when the market is most active. Combine with a visit to Thien Hau Temple. Use ride-hailing apps or a metered taxi to get there.

  9. Cu Chi Tunnels (Day Trip)

    About 1.5–2 hours northwest of the city, the Cu Chi Tunnels are a network of underground passages used during the Vietnam War. Visitors can explore sections of the tunnels and learn about wartime life. It is technically outside the city but is one of the region's most popular excursions.

    • Ticket price: Approximately 35,000–50,000 VND for the site; organized tours typically cost more and include transport.
    • Opening hours: Daily, roughly 7:00–17:00.
    • Visiting tips: Book a half-day tour or arrange transport in advance. Wear comfortable shoes and light clothing; the tunnels are narrow and not suitable for everyone. Bring water and sun protection.

  10. Nguyen Hue Walking Street and the Saigon Riverfront

    This pedestrian boulevard runs from the city center toward the Saigon River and is a pleasant place to stroll in the evening. It is lined with cafes, street performers, and views of the Bitexco Tower, and connects to the riverfront promenade.

    • Ticket price: Free.
    • Opening hours: Open 24 hours; liveliest in the evening.
    • Visiting tips: Visit after dark for cooler temperatures and street life. Keep an eye on your belongings, and use the area as a starting point for an evening walk along the river.

Practical Tips for Visiting Ho Chi Minh City

  • Best time to visit: The dry season from December to April is generally the most comfortable. The rainy season (May–November) brings short, heavy afternoon downpours.
  • Getting around: Ride-hailing apps (Grab, Be) are convenient and often cheaper than street taxis. Use metered taxis from reputable companies, and agree on fares for traditional cyclos.
  • Money: The currency is the Vietnamese dong (VND). Carry small notes for markets and street food; many places accept cards, but cash is still widely used.
  • Dress code: Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples and religious sites.
  • Heat and safety: Stay hydrated, use sun protection, and watch for traffic when crossing streets—move at a steady pace and let motorbikes flow around you.
  • Etiquette: Ask before photographing people, especially at markets and temples. Bargaining is acceptable in markets but not in shops with fixed prices.
  • Verify details: Ticket prices and opening hours change; check official sources or ask your accommodation before visiting.
