Zanzibar: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Zanzibar, an archipelago off the coast of Tanzania, blends Swahili, Arab, Indian, and European influences into a distinctive cultural landscape. From the labyrinthine alleys of Stone Town to the spice farms of the interior and the turquoise waters of the northern beaches, the islands offer a compact but varied itinerary. Below are ten attractions worth prioritizing, with practical details on tickets, hours, and how to make the most of each visit. Prices are given in Tanzanian shillings (TZS) with approximate US dollar equivalents, and hours can shift seasonally or after Ramadan, so confirm locally before setting out.

  1. Stone Town (Mji Mkongwe)

    The historic core of Zanzibar City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the archipelago's cultural heart. Its narrow lanes reveal carved wooden doors, coral-stone merchant houses, mosques, churches, and bustling markets. Walking the old town is free; individual sights such as the Old Fort, the House of Wonders (currently closed for restoration), and the former slave market site at the Anglican Cathedral charge small fees or ask for donations.

    Ticket priceFree to walk; individual sites TZS 5,000–10,000 (approx. US$2–4)
    Opening hoursPublic streets open 24 hours; sights generally 8:00–18:00
    Visiting tipsDress modestly (shoulders and knees covered), especially near mosques. Hire a licensed guide (TZS 30,000–50,000 for a half-day) to navigate the alleys and hear the history. Visit early morning or late afternoon to avoid heat and crowds.

  2. Jozani Chwaka Bay National Park

    Zanzibar's only national park protects a mangrove and groundwater forest that is home to the endemic Zanzibar red colobus monkey, along with Sykes' monkeys, duikers, and diverse birdlife. A boardwalk crosses the mangrove swamp, and guided forest walks are included with entry.

    Ticket priceApprox. TZS 25,000–35,000 (US$10–14) for non-resident adults; children reduced
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 7:30–17:30
    Visiting tipsGo early for the best wildlife sightings. Keep a respectful distance from the colobus monkeys and never feed them. Wear closed shoes and bring insect repellent. The park is about 35 km southeast of Stone Town and easily combined with a spice farm visit.

  3. Spice Farms (Kizimbani and Kidichi)

    Zanzibar was once the world's leading producer of cloves, and spice tours remain a signature experience. Guides lead visitors through plantations of cloves, cinnamon, nutmeg, vanilla, cardamom, and black pepper, explaining their cultivation and traditional medicinal uses, often ending with a tasting and the chance to buy fresh spices.

    Ticket priceApprox. TZS 20,000–30,000 (US$8–12) per person, including guide
    Opening hoursDaily, generally 8:00–17:00
    Visiting tipsBook through a reputable operator or your hotel. Bring cash for spice purchases and a hat for sun. Tours last one to two hours; combine with Jozani or a rural village visit for a full-day excursion.

  4. Prison Island (Changuu Island)

    A short boat ride from Stone Town, Changuu was built as a prison for rebellious slaves and later used as a quarantine station. Today it is best known for its colony of Aldabra giant tortoises, some over a century old, and for its beaches and snorkeling offshore.

    Ticket priceIsland entry approx. TZS 10,000–15,000 (US$4–6); boat transfer TZS 30,000–50,000 return, often arranged with a guide
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 8:30–17:00; boats run from Stone Town waterfront
    Visiting tipsAgree the boat price and return time before departure. Do not ride or crowd the tortoises. Bring snorkeling gear and water; facilities are limited. Combine with a Stone Town walking tour in the morning.

  5. Nungwi Beach

    On the northern tip of Unguja, Nungwi is one of Zanzibar's most popular beaches thanks to its swimmable waters at all tides, white sand, and lively village atmosphere. It is a hub for dhow-building, sunset cruises, diving, and snorkeling, with a wide range of restaurants and bars.

    Ticket priceFree public beach; activities priced separately (sunset dhow cruise approx. TZS 50,000–80,000)
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; activity operators generally 8:00–18:00
    Visiting tipsSwim within marked areas and be aware of currents. Respect local dress when walking through the village away from the beach. Book diving and snorkeling with established operators and check equipment before departure.

  6. Kendwa Beach

    Neighboring Nungwi, Kendwa is known for its wide, calm bay and tidal conditions that allow swimming throughout the day. It hosts the monthly Full Moon Party, a large beach event, and offers a quieter, resort-oriented alternative to Nungwi's busier strip.

    Ticket priceFree public beach; Full Moon Party entry varies (often TZS 20,000–50,000)
    Opening hoursOpen 24 hours; beach bars and restaurants typically 10:00 until late
    Visiting tipsBook accommodation well ahead around full moon. The beach is calmer for families than some east-coast spots. Arrange transport back to Stone Town in advance, as night driving is best avoided.

  7. Mnemba Atoll Marine Conservation Area

    Off the northeast coast near Matemwe, Mnemba Atoll is Zanzibar's premier snorkeling and diving destination, with coral gardens, turtles, reef fish, and seasonal dolphins. The atoll surrounds a private island; day visitors use boats from Matemwe and nearby villages.

    Ticket priceMarine conservation fee approx. TZS 10,000–20,000 (US$4–8) plus boat and guide, typically TZS 60,000–120,000 per person for a half-day trip
    Opening hoursDay trips generally depart 8:00–9:00 and return by 13:00–15:00; no access to the private island
    Visiting tipsBook with a licensed operator who provides life jackets and reef-safe practices. Do not touch coral or chase marine life. Sea conditions can be rough; trips may be cancelled in bad weather. Bring sunscreen, a rash guard, and water.

  8. The Old Fort (Ngome Kongwe), Stone Town

    Built by Omani Arabs around 1700, the Old Fort is Stone Town's oldest surviving building. Its thick coral-stone walls enclose a courtyard that hosts an amphitheater, craft stalls, and cultural performances, and it is a convenient orientation point beside the House of Wonders and Forodhani Gardens.

    Ticket priceFree entry; performances and some events ticketed
    Opening hoursDaily, roughly 8:00–18:00
    Visiting tipsVisit in the late afternoon and continue to Forodhani Gardens for the evening food stalls. Bargain politely at craft stalls. The fort is a good starting point for a self-guided Stone Town walk.

  9. Forodhani Gardens Night Market

    This waterfront park in Stone Town transforms each evening into a food market where vendors grill skewers of seafood and meat, press fresh sugarcane juice, and sell Zanzibar pizza and urojo soup. It is as much a social ritual as a meal, popular with locals and visitors alike.

    Ticket priceFree entry; food typically TZS 3,000–15,000 (US$1–6) per item
    Opening hoursDaily from around 17:30 until roughly 22:00, busiest after dark
    Visiting tipsChoose stalls with high turnover and watch food being cooked to order. Carry small notes and coins. It is busiest around 19:00–21:00; go earlier for a calmer experience. Keep valuables secure in crowds.

  10. Jambiani and the Southeast Coast

    The southeast coast, including Jambiani and Paje, offers long white-sand beaches, dramatic tides, and a strong seaweed-farming culture. Paje is a center for kitesurfing thanks to steady winds and shallow lagoons, while Jambiani is quieter and well suited to village walks and relaxing.

    Ticket priceFree public beaches; kitesurfing lessons typically TZS 150,000–250,000 (US$60–100) per session
