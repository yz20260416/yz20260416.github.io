Dali Travel Guide: The Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Dali, set in Yunnan Province between the Cangshan Mountains and Erhai Lake, blends Bai minority culture, historic old towns, and dramatic scenery. This 3-day itinerary balances the ancient streets of Dali Old Town, lakeside villages, and mountain viewpoints, with morning, afternoon, and evening schedules plus restaurant suggestions and practical tips for a smooth trip.

Day 1: Dali Old Town and the Three Pagodas

TimePlanDetails
Morning Explore Dali Old Town Walk the main gates (South Gate and Wuhua Tower), wander Renmin Road and Fuxing Road, and visit the small museums and temples inside the old town. Start early to avoid tour groups.
Afternoon Three Pagodas and Chongsheng Temple About 1.5 km north of the old town. The three pagodas date to the Tang dynasty; the temple complex behind them is a reconstruction with good mountain views. Allow 2–3 hours.
Evening Old Town at night Dinner in the old town, then stroll the illuminated streets. The area around Foreigner Street (Yangren Jie) has cafes and bars.

Restaurant Recommendations (Day 1)

  • Bai-style lunch: Try local restaurants serving rubing (grilled cheese), fish dishes, and wild vegetables in the old town lanes.
  • Dinner: Choose a courtyard restaurant near Renmin Road for Yunnan dishes such as steam-pot chicken and mushroom hotpot.
  • Cafes: Foreigner Street has long-standing coffee shops popular with travelers.

Day 2: Erhai Lake Villages and Cycling

TimePlanDetails
Morning Xizhou Village About 18 km north of the old town. See well-preserved Bai courtyard houses, the morning market, and try Xizhou baba (a local flatbread). Allow 2 hours.
Afternoon Cycle or drive the lake's west shore Head south along the lakeside road toward Shuanglang or the closer villages of Caicun and Longkan. Rent a bike or e-bike in the old town or take a taxi between villages. Stop at lakeside viewpoints.
Evening Sunset by the lake Watch the sunset over Erhai from a west-shore village, then return to Dali Old Town or stay overnight near the lake.

Restaurant Recommendations (Day 2)

  • Xizhou: Snack on freshly made Xizhou baba and local dairy products; small eateries near the market serve simple Bai meals.
  • Lakeside lunch: Village restaurants along the west shore serve Erhai fish and freshwater shrimp.
  • Dinner: Back in the old town, try a mushroom hotpot restaurant (seasonal wild mushrooms in summer and autumn).

Day 3: Cangshan Mountain and Shaxi or the Old Town's Edges

TimePlanDetails
Morning Cangshan Mountain cable car Take the Zhonghe cable car or the Gantong cable car up Cangshan for cloud-forest trails and views over Erhai. Walk part of the paved mountain path between stations. Bring a jacket; it is cooler at altitude.
Afternoon Option A: Shaxi Ancient Town day trip, or Option B: relax in Dali Option A: Shaxi is about 2 hours' drive north; a long but rewarding half-day for its preserved market square and Bai architecture. Option B: Visit the Dali Museum or the Tianlongbabu film studio area, or simply rest in a courtyard cafe.
Evening Final dinner and shopping Buy local crafts such as tie-dye textiles and silverwork, then enjoy a farewell dinner in the old town.

Restaurant Recommendations (Day 3)

  • Post-hike lunch: Noodle shops in the old town serve rice noodles and crossing-the-bridge noodles.
  • Shaxi (if chosen): Small guesthouse restaurants around the old market square serve local farm-style dishes.
  • Farewell dinner: A Bai cuisine restaurant with set menus is a good way to sample multiple local dishes.

Practical Tips

  • Best time to visit: March to May and September to November offer mild, clear weather. Summer is the rainy season; winters are sunny but cold at night.
  • Altitude: Dali sits at about 2,000 m. Take it easy on arrival and stay hydrated.
  • Getting there: Dali has an airport and a railway station with high-speed trains from Kunming (about 2 hours). The old town is roughly 30–40 minutes by taxi from either.
  • Getting around: Taxis and ride-hailing apps work well; e-bikes and bicycles are ideal around Erhai Lake. Agree on fares or use the meter.
  • Money: Mobile payment (WeChat Pay, Alipay) is widely used; carry some cash for small vendors and rural areas.
  • Etiquette: Dress modestly at temples, ask before photographing locals, and avoid single-use plastics where possible.
  • Sun protection: UV levels are high at this altitude—bring sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses.
  • Language: English is limited outside tourist areas; a translation app and saved Chinese place names help.
