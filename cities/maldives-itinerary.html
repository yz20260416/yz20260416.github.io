Maldives 3-Day Itinerary

The Maldives is a dream destination of over 1,000 coral islands scattered across the Indian Ocean, famous for turquoise lagoons, white-sand beaches, and world-class snorkeling and diving. While many visitors spend their entire trip at a single resort, a well-planned 3-day itinerary lets you experience the best of both worlds: the vibrant local culture of Malé and the serene beauty of a resort island. This guide assumes you arrive at Velana International Airport (MLE) in the morning and combine a day of local exploration with two days of island luxury. Note that many resorts require a speedboat or seaplane transfer, so book these in advance through your accommodation.

At-a-Glance Itinerary

Day Morning Afternoon Evening
Day 1
Malé & Local Life		 Arrive at MLE; transfer to a hotel in Malé or Hulhumalé. Visit the Old Friday Mosque (Hukuru Miskiy) and the Maldives National Museum. Explore the bustling Malé Fish Market and Local Market; stroll along the Artificial Beach and the waterfront promenade. Sunset dinner at a waterfront restaurant; try mas huni and fresh tuna at a local café.
Day 2
Resort Island & Water Activities		 Speedboat or seaplane transfer to your resort island. Settle in and enjoy a welcome drink; morning snorkeling on the house reef. Lunch at the resort; afternoon dolphin-watching cruise or a guided dive/snorkel safari. Sunset cocktails on the beach; dinner at the resort's overwater restaurant.
Day 3
Relaxation & Departure		 Sunrise yoga or a morning swim; leisurely breakfast; optional spa treatment. Last snorkel or paddleboarding session; check out and transfer back to MLE. Departure from Velana International Airport.

Day 1 — Malé & Local Life

  • Old Friday Mosque (Hukuru Miskiy): A 17th-century coral-stone mosque with intricate carvings — a UNESCO-listed cultural site (modest dress required).
  • Maldives National Museum: Housed in the Sultan Park complex, showcasing royal artifacts and Islamic heritage.
  • Fish Market & Local Market: Watch fishermen unload the day's catch and browse dried fish, fruit, and handicrafts.
  • Restaurant picks: Seagull Café (rooftop, international and Maldivian dishes), Shell Beans (casual local eats), or The Sea House (seafood with lagoon views).

Day 2 — Resort Island & Water Activities

  • Transfers: Speedboats serve islands within roughly 90 minutes of Malé; seaplanes reach farther atolls (daylight flights only).
  • Snorkeling & diving: The Maldives sits on coral reefs teeming with reef sharks, rays, and tropical fish — most resorts offer guided house-reef snorkeling and PADI dive courses.
  • Dolphin cruise: A popular late-afternoon excursion to spot spinner dolphins in the channels.
  • Restaurant picks: Most meals are at the resort — look for overwater seafood grills, beachfront buffets, and Maldivian-themed nights featuring garudhiya (fish soup) and rihaakuru (fish paste).

Day 3 — Relaxation & Departure

  • Morning: Sunrise yoga, a final swim, or a spa treatment using local coconut and frangipani oils.
  • Afternoon: Paddleboarding, kayaking, or a short beach walk before checkout.
  • Departure: Allow buffer time for transfers — seaplane and speedboat schedules can be affected by weather.

Practical Tips

  • Best time to visit: November to April (dry northeast monsoon); May to October is wetter but offers lower rates.
  • Currency: Maldivian rufiyaa (MVR); US dollars are widely accepted at resorts. Carry small bills for tips and local purchases.
  • Dress code: Modest clothing is required in Malé and local islands (shoulders and knees covered); swimwear is fine on resort beaches and designated "bikini beaches."
  • Transfers: Book speedboat/seaplane transfers through your resort in advance and confirm timings against your flight arrival.
  • Connectivity: Wi-Fi is common at resorts; buy a local SIM (Dhiraagu or Ooredoo) at the airport for data on local islands.
  • Environment: Use reef-safe sunscreen, avoid touching coral, and respect marine-life viewing guidelines.
  • Budget: Resorts are expensive; local guesthouses on islands like Maafushi offer a more affordable alternative for budget travelers.
