Top Attractions in Bangkok

Bangkok rewards visitors who look beyond the obvious: a riverside mall built on historic docks, a monumental swing that once featured in Brahmin ceremonies, and communities where bronze casting and puppet theatre still thrive. This guide picks ten diverse attractions — from soaring observation towers and royal palaces to shrines, cathedrals and canal-side neighbourhoods — that together capture the city's sacred, commercial and creative sides.

  1. Baiyoke Tower II — An 88-storey, 309 m skyscraper hotel on Ratchaprarop Road, home to Baiyoke Sky Hotel, the tallest hotel in Southeast Asia. Its observation and revolving decks offer sweeping views over the city skyline. Tip: Visit around sunset for the best light and thinner crowds.
  2. Dusit Palace — A compound of royal residences built north of Rattanakosin Island between 1897 and 1901 by King Chulalongkorn, later the primary residence of Thai kings. Tip: Combine with nearby Vimanmek Mansion and allow a half-day; dress modestly as several buildings are royal property.
  3. Democracy Monument — A public monument occupying a traffic circle on Ratchadamnoen Avenue, roughly halfway between Sanam Luang and the Golden Mount. It is a landmark of modern Thai political history. Tip: Visit in the cooler early morning or evening; it is an easy walk from Khaosan Road.
  4. Giant Swing (Sao Chingcha) — A 21.15 m swing-shaped religious structure standing in front of Wat Suthat, formerly used in a traditional Brahmin ceremony and now one of Bangkok's notable landmarks. Tip: Pair the visit with Wat Suthat next door and the nearby Devasathan shrine.
  5. Erawan Shrine — Formally the Thao Maha Phrom Shrine, it houses a statue of Phra Phrom, the Thai representation of Brahma, and is popularly worshipped as a guardian spirit. Tip: Come early morning or after dark to avoid peak crowds; it sits at a busy central intersection near Chit Lom.
  6. Assumption Cathedral — The principal Catholic church of Thailand and main church of the Archdiocese of Bangkok, located on Oriental Avenue in Bang Rak. It hosted papal visits by Pope John Paul II in 1984 and Pope Francis in 2019. Tip: Combine with a stroll along Charoen Krung Road and the riverside nearby.
  7. Asiatique The Riverfront — A large open-air mall occupying the former docks of the East Asiatic Company, facing the Chao Phraya River and Charoen Krung Road; it opened in 2012 after extensive renovation. Tip: Arrive by riverboat in the late afternoon and stay for dinner and the riverside atmosphere.
  8. Khaosan Road — A 410 m street in the Bang Lamphu area of Phra Nakhon District, constructed in 1892 during the reign of Rama V and about 1 km north of the Grand Palace. Tip: It is liveliest at night; visit in the morning if you prefer a quieter walk en route to the royal quarter.
  9. Ban Bu Community — A traditional community on Bangkok's Thonburi side, regarded as the last source of bronzework handicraft makers in the city and now a cultural tourism destination. Tip: Go on a weekday to see craftsmen at work; reach it by crossing the river to Bangkok Noi.
  10. Khlong Bang Luang Artist House (Baan Sinlapin) — An art gallery and puppet theatre in Phasi Charoen, housed in a former family residence that was renovated into a gallery. Tip: Check puppet show times in advance and combine with a long-tail boat trip along the Thonburi canals.

Practical tips

  • Start early: most temples, shrines and palaces open in the morning and are far more pleasant before the midday heat.
  • Dress modestly for religious sites — cover shoulders and knees at temples, shrines and the cathedral.
  • Use the Chao Phraya riverboats to reach riverside spots like Asiatique and Bang Rak, avoiding road traffic.
  • Thonburi-side attractions such as Ban Bu and the Artist House are best paired on one canal-and-craft day.
  • Carry small change for entrance fees and offerings, and confirm opening hours locally as they can change.
