Zanzibar 3-Day Itinerary

Zanzibar, the "Spice Island" off the coast of Tanzania, blends Swahili, Arab, Indian, and European influences into one of the Indian Ocean's most captivating destinations. With turquoise waters, powder-white beaches, a UNESCO-listed Stone Town, and spice farms that perfume the air, three days is enough to sample the island's highlights—if you plan wisely. This itinerary balances culture, nature, and relaxation, with realistic travel times between the island's main areas. It assumes you base yourself in Stone Town on Day 1 and move to the beaches (Nungwi or Kendwa in the north) for Days 2–3, which minimizes backtracking.

Day 1: Stone Town — Culture, History & Spice

Time Activity Notes
8:00–9:00 Breakfast at your hotel or Lukmaan Restaurant Try urojo (Zanzibari soup) or a Swahili breakfast of chapati and tea.
9:00–11:30 Guided walking tour of Stone Town Book a licensed guide (~$25–40). Highlights: Forodhani Gardens, Old Fort, House of Wonders (exterior; still under restoration), Old Slave Market & Anglican Cathedral, and the tangled alleys of the historic quarter.
11:30–12:30 Visit Darajani Market Zanzibar's bustling central market—spices, produce, fish, and local life. Go with your guide or dress modestly and ask before photographing vendors.
12:30–14:00 Lunch at Zanzibar Coffee House or Lukmaan Great rooftop views and Swahili-Indian dishes; the coffee house roasts its own beans.
14:00–16:30 Spice farm tour (Kizimbani or Kidichi) ~30–40 min drive from town. Smell and taste cloves, cinnamon, nutmeg, vanilla, and cardamom; ends with a spice-infused lunch or tea. Book through your hotel (~$30–50 incl. transport).
17:00–18:30 Sunset at Forodhani Gardens night market Arrive before dusk for the famous Zanzibar pizza, grilled seafood skewers, sugarcane juice, and mishkaki.
19:30–21:30 Dinner at The Rock (Michamvi) or Emerson on Hurumzi rooftop The Rock is a scenic 45-min drive (book ahead); Emerson's rooftop offers traditional Swahili taarab music and set menus—reserve in advance.

Day 2: North Beaches — Nungwi & Kendwa

  • 7:30–8:30: Breakfast, then check out and transfer north to Nungwi (~1–1.5 hrs by private car; arrange through your hotel, ~$40–60).
  • 10:00–12:30: Settle in and swim at Nungwi Beach—one of the few spots on the island with swimmable tides all day.
  • 12:30–14:00: Lunch at Mama Mia (Italian, beachfront) or Baraka Beach Restaurant for fresh seafood.
  • 14:30–17:00: Dhow sunset cruise from Nungwi or Kendwa (~$25–40 pp, 2 hrs). Sail past fishing villages; snorkeling stops are often included.
  • 17:30–18:30: Sunset drinks at Kendwa Rocks—famous for its full-moon parties and relaxed beach bar.
  • 19:30–21:30: Dinner at The Beach House (Nungwi) or Essque Zalu for a splurge. Seafood platters and Swahili curries are standouts.

Day 3: Marine Life & Relaxation

  • 7:00–11:30: Snorkeling or diving trip to Mnemba Atoll (from Nungwi/Kendwa, ~$50–80 pp including gear and lunch). Expect turtles, reef fish, and—seasonally—dolphins. Book with a reputable operator (e.g., Spanish Dancer Divers or One Ocean).
  • 12:00–13:30: Lunch back at the beach—Cholo's Bar or Langi Langi for casual grilled fish and chips.
  • 14:00–16:00: Free time: kayaking, paddleboarding, a spa treatment, or simply beach lounging.
  • 16:00–17:30: Optional visit to the Nungwi turtle aquarium (community-run; small entry fee) or a local dhow-building yard.
  • 18:00–19:00: Final sunset on the beach.
  • 19:30–21:30: Farewell dinner at Kendwa Rocks or Z Hotel rooftop. If departing the next morning, pre-book your airport transfer (~1.5 hrs from Nungwi).

Practical Tips

  • Best time to visit: June–October (dry, cooler) and December–February (hot, dry). Avoid the long rains (April–May) and short rains (November).
  • Getting around: Private drivers (~$40–70/day) are the most reliable option. Dala-dalas (shared minibuses) are cheap but slow; taxis are negotiable—agree on the fare first.
  • Currency & payments: Tanzanian shilling (TZS) is standard; USD is widely accepted but bring small, clean bills. ATMs exist in Stone Town and Nungwi but can be unreliable—carry cash.
  • Dress code: Modest dress is expected in Stone Town (shoulders and knees covered). Beachwear is fine on the coast.
  • Health: Drink bottled or filtered water; use mosquito repellent, especially at dusk. Check current visa and yellow fever requirements before travel.
  • Tipping: 10% at restaurants is customary; $1–2 per bag for porters; $5–10/day for guides.
  • Book ahead: Popular restaurants (The Rock, Emerson on Hurumzi) and dive operators fill up in high season.
  • Respect local culture: Ask before photographing people; during Ramadan, be mindful of eating/drinking in public during daylight hours.
