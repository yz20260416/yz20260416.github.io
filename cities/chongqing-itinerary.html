Chongqing in 3 Days: A Complete Itinerary

Chongqing is a sprawling megacity in southwest China, built across steep hills at the confluence of the Yangtze and Jialing rivers. Known for its dramatic skyline, spicy hotpot, foggy skies, and maze-like streets where the "ground floor" can be the 10th story, it rewards travelers who plan around its geography. This 3-day itinerary balances the city's iconic sights with its food, neighborhoods, and river views, with morning, afternoon, and evening schedules for each day.

Day 1: Downtown Chongqing — Jiefangbei, Hongya Cave, and the Rivers

Time Activity Details
Morning Jiefangbei (Liberation Monument) and Central Business District Start at the Jiefangbei pedestrian area, the city's commercial heart. Wander the surrounding streets, visit the monument, and explore nearby shopping arcades. This is a good orientation point for the city.
Afternoon Raffles City Chongqing and Chaotianmen Walk to Raffles City, home to the horizontal "Crystal" skybridge (a viewing deck with river views). Nearby Chaotianmen is where the Yangtze and Jialing meet — a classic photo spot. Book skybridge tickets in advance during peak season.
Evening Hongya Cave (Hongyadong) Visit after dark when the stilted complex is lit up. It's crowded but iconic. Explore the multi-level shops and eateries, then walk to the Qiansimen Bridge for a classic skyline view.

Day 2: Culture, History, and Local Life

Time Activity Details
Morning Three Gorges Museum and People's Great Hall The Three Gorges Museum (free entry, closed Mondays) covers the region's history and the dam project. Across the plaza, the People's Great Hall is a striking landmark worth a photo stop.
Afternoon Ciqikou Ancient Town A preserved old town with teahouses, snack stalls, and handicraft shops. Try local snacks like ciba (glutinous rice cakes) and mapo tofu. It's touristy but atmospheric; go early afternoon to avoid the worst crowds.
Evening Yangtze River Night Cruise A river cruise (roughly 1–1.5 hours) offers the best views of the illuminated skyline and bridges. Book tickets through your hotel or at Chaotianmen docks; choose a mid-range boat for a good balance of comfort and price.

Day 3: Mountains, Art, and Hotpot

Time Activity Details
Morning Eling Park or Nanshan For city views, head to Eling Park (central, easy access) or the Nanshan area for a slower pace. Nanshan also has the Chongqing Planning Exhibition Gallery and pleasant walking trails.
Afternoon Huangjueping Graffiti Street A hillside neighborhood covered in street art, near the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute. Good for photography and a relaxed wander; combine with a stop at a local teahouse.
Evening Hotpot dinner End the trip with Chongqing's signature meal. Choose a reputable hotpot restaurant and order a mild (weí) broth if you're not used to the heat. See restaurant suggestions below.

Restaurant Recommendations

  • Hotpot: Look for established chains and local favorites around Jiefangbei and Nan'an. Popular options include well-known hotpot brands with English menus; ask your hotel for a current recommendation, as restaurants change.
  • Xiaomian (spicy noodles): Street stalls and small shops serve Chongqing's beloved breakfast noodles — cheap, fast, and everywhere. Try them near your hotel in the morning.
  • Sichuan cuisine: Restaurants in the Jiefangbei and Guanyinqiao areas serve classics like mapo tofu, kung pao chicken, and shuizhu fish. Guanyinqiao is also a major food and nightlife district.
  • Snacks: Ciqikou and Hongya Cave have stalls for ciba, skewers, and sweet treats — good for grazing rather than full meals.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: The metro is fast and cheap; lines 1, 2, 3, and 6 cover most sights. Line 2's elevated section along the Jialing River is a scenic ride. Taxis and ride-hailing apps work well but traffic can be heavy.
  • Geography: Chongqing is extremely hilly. Expect stairs and steep walks; wear comfortable shoes. Addresses can be confusing because of multiple street levels — use map apps with GPS.
  • Weather: Summers are hot and humid; winters are cool and foggy. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons. Bring an umbrella year-round.
  • Food: Chongqing food is spicy and numbing (málà). Ask for "bú là" (not spicy) or "wēi là" (mild) if needed. Hotpot is often eaten with a dipping sauce of sesame oil and garlic to cool the heat.
  • Language: English is limited outside major hotels and tourist sites. Have hotel names and destinations written in Chinese, and use translation apps.
  • Money and connectivity: Mobile payments (WeChat Pay, Alipay) are standard; set them up before arrival if possible. Carry some cash as backup. Buy a local SIM or eSIM for data.
  • Crowds: Hongya Cave, Ciqikou, and river cruises are busiest on weekends and holidays. Visit early or on weekday evenings when possible.
  • Etiquette: Queue politely, keep voices moderate in museums, and avoid photographing people without permission in residential areas.
