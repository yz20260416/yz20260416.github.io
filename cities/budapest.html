Best Time to Visit Budapest

Budapest, Hungary's capital, straddles the Danube with hilly Buda on one bank and lively Pest on the other, famed for its thermal baths, grand coffeehouses, and dramatic Parliament skyline. Visit in spring or autumn for mild weather and thinner crowds, or in December for Christmas markets and steam rising off the thermal pools.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Hungarian Parliament Building — A neo-Gothic riverside landmark offering guided tours of its ornate chambers and the Holy Crown.
  2. Buda Castle and Fisherman's Bastion — A hilltop palace complex with panoramic terraces and fairy-tale towers overlooking the Danube.
  3. Széchenyi Thermal Bath — One of Europe's largest thermal bath complexes, with steaming outdoor pools open year-round.
  4. Dohány Street Synagogue — The largest synagogue in Europe and a striking Moorish-style monument with a moving memorial garden.
  5. Ruin Bars of the Jewish Quarter — Converted courtyards and abandoned buildings turned into eclectic nightlife spots in District VII.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Hungarian Forint (HUF, Ft)
  • Language: Hungarian (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: CET (UTC+1), CEST (UTC+2) in summer
  • Visa: EU/EEA and many Western nationals can enter visa-free for up to 90 days, while other visitors should check Schengen visa requirements before travelling.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Budapest?
Three to four days covers the main sights, a thermal bath visit, and time for the ruin bars and coffeehouses.
Is Budapest expensive?
It is generally more affordable than Western European capitals, though prices in the tourist centre have risen in recent years.
How do I get around Budapest?
The metro, trams, and buses are efficient and inexpensive, and the city centre is easily walkable.
