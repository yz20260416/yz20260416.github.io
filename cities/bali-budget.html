Bali Travel Cost Guide

Bali has a reputation as one of Southeast Asia's most affordable destinations, but costs vary dramatically depending on how you travel. A backpacker can survive on roughly $30–45 per day, while a mid-range traveler enjoying boutique hotels and restaurant meals should budget $80–150 per day. Those seeking luxury villas, private drivers, and fine dining can easily spend $250–500+ per day. The figures below are per-person daily estimates (in USD) based on 2024–2025 prices, assuming two people sharing accommodation where relevant. Prices are higher in tourist hubs like Seminyak, Canggu, and Ubud, and noticeably lower in Amed, Sidemen, and West Bali.

Currency tip: Bali uses the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). At the time of writing, roughly 1 USD ≈ 15,500–16,000 IDR. Always check the current rate before you travel, and withdraw cash from reputable bank ATMs (BCA, Mandiri, BNI) to avoid poor exchange rates and card-skimming risks.

What Things Actually Cost: Sample Prices

  • Nasi campur at a local warung: $1.50–3.00
  • Western-style brunch in Canggu: $8–15
  • Bintang beer (large bottle, shop): $2.50–3.50; at a bar: $4–6
  • Fresh coconut on the beach: $1.50–2.50
  • Scooter rental (daily, long-term discount available): $5–8
  • Gojek/Grab ride across town (5–10 km): $2–4
  • Private driver for a full day (8–10 hours): $50–70
  • Budget guesthouse double room: $15–25
  • Mid-range hotel with pool: $45–90
  • Balinese massage (1 hour, local spa): $10–18; resort spa: $40–80
  • Snorkeling trip to Nusa Penida or Amed: $25–45
  • Mount Batur sunrise trek (guided): $35–55
  • Surf lesson (2 hours, including board): $25–40
  • Tourist SIM card (data package, 30 days): $8–15
  • Temple entry (e.g., Tanah Lot, Uluwatu): $3–6
