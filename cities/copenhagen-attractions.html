Top Attractions in Copenhagen

Copenhagen blends royal history, striking architecture, and lively neighbourhoods into a compact, walkable city. From the copper-green spires and canals of Christianshavn to the grand palaces and squares at its heart, the Danish capital rewards visitors who explore on foot or by bike. Here are ten attractions most likely to appeal to international travellers.

  1. Amalienborg
    The official residence of the Danish monarch and royal family, this complex of four identical Rococo palaces surrounds an octagonal courtyard centred on the equestrian statue of Frederick V. It remains one of the finest examples of Classical palace architecture in the city.
    Tip: Time your visit to catch the changing of the Royal Life Guards, which marches through the square daily around noon.
  2. Christiansborg Palace
    Sitting on the islet of Slotsholmen, this palace houses the Danish Parliament, the Prime Minister's Office, and the Supreme Court, while several parts are still used by the monarch. It is a working centre of Danish government as well as a historic landmark.
    Tip: The Royal Reception Rooms and palace chapel are open to visitors; check opening days before you go, as government use can affect access.
  3. Børsen
    This 17th-century commodity bourse, built under Christian IV in 1619–1640, is considered a leading example of Dutch Renaissance style in Denmark. It stands next to Christiansborg Palace and is one of the city's most photographed historic buildings.
    Tip: Combine it with a walk around Slotsholmen; the building is best viewed from the surrounding streets and canal-side paths.
  4. Church of Our Saviour
    This Baroque church is famous for its external spiral staircase winding up to the spire, offering sweeping views over central Copenhagen. Its carillon plays melodies every hour from 8 am to midnight.
    Tip: Climb the tower on a clear day for the best views, and pause in Christianshavn's canal streets afterwards.
  5. Freetown Christiania
    This intentional community and former army barracks in Christianshavn began in 1971 as a squatted military base and remains a distinctive, self-governing neighbourhood. Its atmosphere and street art draw curious visitors from around the world.
    Tip: Respect local rules and photography restrictions, and explore the surrounding Christianshavn canals afterwards.
  6. Church of Our Lady
    Copenhagen's Lutheran cathedral, situated on Frue Plads next to the University of Copenhagen, was designed by Christian Frederik Hansen in the Neoclassical style and completed in 1829. Its clean lines and central location make it an easy cultural stop.
    Tip: It sits within easy walking distance of Strøget and the Round Tower area, so slot it into a central walking route.
  7. Charlottenborg Palace
    This large town mansion at the corner of Kongens Nytorv and Nyhavn has housed the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts since 1754. Today it also contains Kunsthal Charlottenborg, a contemporary art venue, and the Royal Art Library.
    Tip: Pair a gallery visit with a stroll along Nyhavn's colourful harbourfront just steps away.
  8. City Hall Square (Rådhuspladsen) and Copenhagen City Hall
    This central square in front of the National Romantic city hall, designed by Martin Nyrop and built between 1892 and 1905, is a hub for events and demonstrations. It also marks the southwestern end of the Strøget pedestrian street.
    Tip: Use the square as a central meeting point and orientation landmark before exploring Strøget and Tivoli nearby.
  9. Caritas Well
    Built in 1608 by Christian IV, this is the oldest fountain in Copenhagen and sits on Gammeltorv within the Strøget pedestrian zone. It is known for its golden apples that "jump" on the Queen's birthday.
    Tip: It is a quick, free stop along Strøget; visit on a weekday to avoid the busiest shopping crowds.
  10. Bellevue Beach
    This 700-metre sandy beach with adjoining lawns at Klampenborg, on the northern outskirts of Copenhagen, attracts up to 500,000 visitors a year. Its blue-striped lifeguard towers and nearby Bauhaus architecture make it a distinctive coastal escape.
    Tip: Reach it easily by S-train to Klampenborg; go on a warm summer day and combine it with a look at Arne Jacobsen's nearby Bellavista estate.

Practical tips

  • Many central sights—Amalienborg, Christiansborg, Børsen, Caritas Well, and City Hall Square—are within walking distance, so plan a single day on foot.
  • Use the S-train for outlying spots like Bellevue Beach at Klampenborg; a rechargeable travel card covers trains, metro, and buses.
  • Check opening hours for palace interiors and churches in advance, as government and religious use can limit access.
  • Visit popular outdoor sights such as Church of Our Saviour's tower and Nyhavn early in the morning to avoid crowds.
  • Respect local rules in Freetown Christiania, including photography restrictions, and keep to marked paths.
