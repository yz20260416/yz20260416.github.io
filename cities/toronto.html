Local Character

Toronto's character is shaped by more than 10,000 years of Indigenous habitation and by waves of immigration that have made it one of the most cosmopolitan cities on earth, with over 200 ethnic origins and more than 160 languages spoken among its residents. A visitor should care because this diversity is not abstract—it shows up in everyday neighbourhood life, food, and festivals. One specific historical quirk worth knowing is the broadly disputed Toronto Purchase, in which the Mississaugas surrendered the area to the British Crown; the British then established the town of York in 1793 and made it the capital of Upper Canada. Visitors interested in the city's origins should look beyond the modern skyline to this founding moment. Another detail a generic guide would miss is the Battle of York during the War of 1812, when American troops occupied the town for two weeks and caused heavy damage; York was later renamed and incorporated as the City of Toronto in 1834. Knowing this helps a traveller understand why Toronto's early identity differs from that of older Canadian cities. The city proper expanded through annexation and amalgamation to its current 631 square kilometres in 1998, which explains why today's Toronto feels like a patchwork of former towns and neighbourhoods rather than a single uniform downtown.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Toronto Harbour — The city's waterfront on the northwestern shore of Lake Ontario, offering ferry access to the Toronto Islands and skyline views.
  2. Toronto Stock Exchange — A symbol of Canada's largest financial centre and the de facto operational headquarters of the country's five largest banks.
  3. Ravine and urban forest network — Deep ravines and urban forest thread through the city's sloping plateau, giving Toronto unusually green walking routes for a major metropolis.
  4. Indigenous heritage sites — The Toronto area has been inhabited by Indigenous peoples for more than 10,000 years, and sites across the city reflect this deep history.
  5. Toronto City Council chamber — Home of the 25-ward unicameral council and the mayor, a stop for visitors curious about how Canada's largest city is governed.

FAQ

How many people live in Toronto?
The city proper had a census population of 2,794,356 in 2021, while the Greater Toronto Area has 6,712,341 residents.
What is the best time of year to visit Toronto?
June and September offer the best balance of pleasant temperatures and manageable crowds, though July and August are warmest.
How large is the city of Toronto?
The city proper covers 631 square kilometres, having expanded through annexation and amalgamation to its current area in 1998.
