Best Time to Visit Kyoto

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, from 794 until 1869, and remains the country's cultural anchor, home to the Agency for Cultural Affairs and numerous UNESCO-listed temples and shrines. Visit in spring for cherry blossoms or autumn for fiery foliage, when the city's gardens and palace grounds are at their most striking.

Local Character

Kyoto's original grid, laid out as Heian-kyō in 794, followed traditional Chinese feng shui and the model of Chang'an — a detail visitors can still trace in the city's straight north–south avenues and the naming of its streets. The city's identity is bound up with its thousand-year role as the imperial seat, which is why it is sometimes called the 'thousand-year capital' (sennen no miyako), and why so many of its temples, shrines, and palaces retain courtly associations rather than merely religious ones. Kyoto was spared large-scale destruction during World War II, so its prewar cultural heritage survives largely intact — meaning the wooden temples and machiya townhouses you walk past are often originals, not reconstructions. Because the emperors ruled from here for eleven centuries, the city also carries the memory of pivotal events such as the Ōnin War and the Honnō-ji Incident, which shaped the nation's history. For a visitor, this layered past is best felt not in a single monument but in the everyday fabric of the city, where ancient street plans and preserved heritage sit alongside modern life.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Kiyomizu-dera — A hillside Buddhist temple whose wooden stage offers sweeping views over the city and is especially famous in autumn.
  2. Kinkaku-ji — The Golden Pavilion, a Zen temple whose upper floors are covered in gold leaf and reflected in a surrounding pond.
  3. Ginkaku-ji — The Silver Pavilion, a tranquil Zen temple with moss gardens and a raked-sand cone that contrast with its gilded counterpart.
  4. Kyoto Imperial Palace — The former seat of Japan's imperial court, set within a large walled park that visitors can stroll freely.
  5. Kyoto Tower — A landmark observation tower near the main station, offering panoramic views of the city's temple-dotted skyline.
