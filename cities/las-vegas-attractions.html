Las Vegas: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Las Vegas is far more than casinos and neon. From the Bellagio's dancing fountains to the engineering marvel of Hoover Dam, the city and its surroundings offer a wide range of experiences for every type of traveler. Below are ten attractions worth building your itinerary around, with current pricing, hours, and practical tips to help you plan. Prices and hours change frequently, so always confirm details on official websites before you go.

  1. Bellagio Fountains & Conservatory

    The choreographed fountain show on the eight-acre lake in front of the Bellagio is one of the Strip's most iconic free attractions, with water jets reaching up to 460 feet. Indoors, the Bellagio Conservatory & Botanical Garden rotates elaborate seasonal displays five times a year.

    • Ticket price: Free
    • Hours: Fountain shows run roughly every 30 minutes from mid-afternoon until around midnight on weekdays, and every 15 minutes on weekends and holidays (weather permitting). The Conservatory is open 24 hours.
    • Tips: Arrive 10–15 minutes early for a front-row spot; the elevated view from the Strip sidewalk near Paris Las Vegas is excellent. Visit the Conservatory early morning to avoid crowds.

  2. Fremont Street Experience

    The five-block pedestrian mall in downtown Las Vegas is covered by a 1,500-foot LED canopy that hosts free light-and-sound shows nightly. It's also home to SlotZilla, a zip line that flies riders beneath the canopy, plus live music stages and classic casinos like the Golden Nugget.

    • Ticket price: Free to enter; SlotZilla zip line from about $45 (Zipline) to $60+ (Zoomline) depending on day and time
    • Hours: The mall is open 24 hours; light shows typically run hourly from dusk until around 1 a.m.
    • Tips: Evenings are liveliest but busiest. Downtown is a 15–20 minute rideshare from the Strip; combine with a visit to the Neon Museum nearby.

  3. High Roller Observation Wheel

    At 550 feet, the High Roller at The LINQ is one of the tallest observation wheels in the world. A full rotation takes about 30 minutes in glass-enclosed cabins, offering sweeping views of the Strip and the valley, especially at sunset.

    • Ticket price: Daytime tickets typically around $25–$30 for adults; evening and happy-hour cabins cost more (roughly $40–$60). Discounts available online.
    • Hours: Generally open daily from around noon to midnight, with hours varying by season.
    • Tips: Book online for lower prices and skip the ticket line. The "Happy Half Hour" cabin includes an open bar. Go 30–45 minutes before sunset for the best light.

  4. The Strip Walking Tour (Self-Guided)

    Walking the 4.2-mile Las Vegas Strip lets you experience themed resorts, street performers, and free attractions such as the Mirage's volcano (check current status), the Venetian's canals, and the Flamingo's wildlife habitat. It's the best way to get a feel for the city's scale and spectacle.

    • Ticket price: Free (self-guided)
    • Hours: Anytime; evenings are cooler and more atmospheric
    • Tips: Wear comfortable shoes and carry water—summer heat is extreme. Pedestrian bridges keep you off busy intersections. Plan a one-way route and take a rideshare or the Las Vegas Monorail back.

  5. Hoover Dam

    About 45 minutes from the Strip, this Depression-era engineering landmark straddles the Nevada–Arizona border. The visitor center and guided powerplant tours explain how the dam tamed the Colorado River and supplies power and water to the Southwest.

    • Ticket price: Visitor center admission around $10; guided powerplant tour around $15; combined tours roughly $30 (children and seniors discounted)
    • Hours: Visitor center generally open 9 a.m.–5 p.m. daily; tours run throughout the day
    • Tips: Book tours in advance during peak season. Go early to beat heat and crowds. Bring ID—security checks are in place. Combine with a stop at the Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Memorial Bridge for dramatic views.

  6. Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area

    A 30-minute drive west of the Strip, Red Rock Canyon offers a 13-mile scenic drive, hiking trails, and striking sandstone formations. It's a favorite for hikers, climbers, and photographers seeking a break from the casinos.

    • Ticket price: Day-use vehicle pass around $20 (timed-entry reservations required during peak periods); America the Beautiful annual pass accepted
    • Hours: Scenic drive generally open 6 a.m.–dusk, with seasonal variations; visitor center typically 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m.
    • Tips: Reserve a timed-entry slot online in advance—they sell out on weekends and holidays. Bring sun protection and plenty of water; cell service is spotty on trails.

  7. The Mob Museum

    Housed in a restored 1933 federal courthouse in downtown Las Vegas, this Smithsonian-affiliated museum tells the story of organized crime and law enforcement in America, with interactive exhibits, artifacts, and a working distillery in the basement.

    • Ticket price: General admission around $30 for adults; discounts for seniors, students, and locals; combo tickets with the Neon Museum available
    • Hours: Typically 9 a.m.–9 p.m. daily, with reduced hours on some holidays
    • Tips: Allow 2–3 hours. The crime-lab and firearms-training experiences cost extra but are popular—book ahead. Pair with Fremont Street, a short walk away.

  8. The Neon Museum (Neon Boneyard)

    This outdoor "boneyard" preserves and displays iconic neon signs from Las Vegas's past, from the Stardust to the Moulin Rouge. Guided tours explain the history of the signs and the city's mid-century casino culture.

    • Ticket price: General admission around $20–$25; guided tours roughly $30–$35; evening "Brilliant!" shows cost extra
    • Hours: Generally open late morning to evening, with hours varying by season; closed some major holidays
    • Tips: Book timed tickets online—walk-ups often sell out. Evening visits combine the signs with the "Brilliant!" light projection show. It's about 10 minutes from Fremont Street.

  9. Venetian & Palazzo Gondola Rides

    Glide along the Grand Canal inside or outside The Venetian on a gondola steered by a singing gondolier. The resort's replica canals, St. Mark's Square, and frescoed ceilings make it one of the Strip's most elaborate themed environments.

    • Ticket price: Indoor gondola rides around $35–$40 per person; outdoor rides around $40–$45; private gondolas available at higher rates
    • Hours: Typically 10 a.m.–10 p.m., with variations by season
    • Tips: Indoor rides are cooler in summer; outdoor rides offer better photo opportunities. Book online to secure a time slot, especially on weekends.

  10. Seven Magic Mountains

    This large-scale public art installation by Ugo Rondinone features seven stacked towers of brightly painted boulders rising from the desert floor, about 25 minutes south of the Strip near Jean, Nevada. It's a striking and free photo stop.

    • Ticket price: Free
    • Hours: Open daily, generally dawn to dusk; no admission gate
    • Tips: Go at sunrise or sunset for the best light and smaller crowds. There is no shade—bring water and sun protection. The site is on a dirt shoulder off I-15; parking is limited, so avoid peak weekend afternoons.

Practical Tips for Visiting Las Vegas

  • Best time to visit: Spring (March–May) and fall (September–November) offer mild temperatures. Summer highs routinely exceed 100°F, and winter evenings can be chilly.
  • Getting around: The Strip is walkable but long—distances are deceiving. Use the Las Vegas Monorail, rideshares, or the Deuce bus for longer hops. Rental cars are useful for day trips like Hoover Dam and Red Rock Canyon.
  • Resort fees: Most Strip hotels charge nightly resort fees (often $30–$50) not included in advertised rates. Confirm the total cost before booking.
  • Book ahead: Popular tours, shows, and timed-entry attractions (Red Rock Canyon, Neon Museum, Hoover Dam tours) sell out during peak periods.
  • Budget for extras: Many attractions are free, but parking, drinks, and add-on experiences add up. Look for online discounts and combo tickets.
  • Stay hydrated and use sun protection: Desert heat is serious, especially in summer. Carry water on walks and hikes.
  • Verify details: Prices and hours in this guide are approximate and change seasonally—check official attraction websites before your visit.
