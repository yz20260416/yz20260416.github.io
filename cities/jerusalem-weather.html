Jerusalem Weather by Month

Jerusalem sits at roughly 800 meters (2,600 feet) above sea level in the Judean hills, which gives it a noticeably more temperate and drier climate than Israel's coastal cities like Tel Aviv. The city experiences a Mediterranean climate with hot, rain-free summers and cool, wet winters—and on rare occasions, even snow. Rainfall is almost entirely concentrated between November and March, while the long summer stretches from May to September with virtually no precipitation. Because of the elevation, evenings cool down appreciably year-round, so packing layers is wise in every season. The table below summarizes typical conditions month by month to help you plan your visit.

Note: Figures are long-term averages and can vary considerably year to year. Jerusalem's elevation means temperatures run a few degrees cooler than the coast, and winter nights can dip close to freezing.

Best Months to Visit

  • April–May and October–November — The sweet spots. Mild temperatures, low rainfall, and comfortable conditions for exploring on foot. Spring brings wildflowers; autumn offers clear skies and warm days.
  • March and September — Shoulder seasons with mostly good weather, fewer crowds, and often lower prices. March can still see showers; September can still be quite hot.
  • June–August — Reliable sunshine and no rain, but hot and crowded. Best for early risers and travelers who don't mind the heat.
  • December–February — Coolest and wettest, with a small chance of snow. Quieter and atmospheric, but pack for rain and cold, especially for early-morning visits to holy sites.

Practical Tips

  • Dress in layers year-round. Jerusalem's elevation means warm days often turn into cool evenings—a light jacket is useful even in summer.
  • Summer sun is intense. Between June and September, use high-SPF sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses, and carry water. Plan outdoor sightseeing for morning or late afternoon.
  • Winter needs real rain gear. A waterproof jacket and sturdy, non-slip shoes are essential from November to March, when stone streets and steps in the Old City become slippery.
  • Snow is rare but possible. Jerusalem occasionally sees snowfall (typically January–February), which can briefly disrupt transport—check forecasts if traveling then.
  • Respect religious sites. Modest dress (covered shoulders and knees) is expected at many holy sites regardless of the weather; carry a light scarf or wrap.
  • Check the calendar. Major Jewish, Christian, and Muslim holidays can bring large crowds, altered opening hours, and higher accommodation prices—worth checking before you book, whatever the season.
  • Rainfall varies year to year. Use the table as a planning guide, but confirm the forecast shortly before your trip.
