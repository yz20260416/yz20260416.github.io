Berlin in 3 Days: A Complete Itinerary

Berlin is a sprawling, history-dense capital where imperial palaces, Cold War relics, world-class museums, and a famously creative nightlife scene sit side by side. Three days is enough to cover the essentials if you plan by district: Mitte for the headline sights, Kreuzberg and Friedrichshain for street culture and nightlife, and Charlottenburg or Prenzlauer Berg for a slower pace. This itinerary balances major landmarks with neighborhood exploration and includes restaurant picks and practical tips to help you move efficiently through the city.

Day 1: Historic Mitte and the Wall

Time Activity Notes
Morning Brandenburg Gate, Reichstag, Holocaust Memorial Book Reichstag dome visit online in advance (free). Walk 5 minutes between sights.
Afternoon Museum Island (Pergamon or Neues Museum) and Berlin Cathedral Buy a Museum Island day pass; reserve timed entry for Pergamon.
Evening East Side Gallery and sunset at the Spree riverbank Walk or take the S-Bahn to Ostbahnhof; the gallery is open-air and free.

Restaurant recommendations: Lunch at Zur letzten Instanz (traditional German, near Alexanderplatz) or grab a currywurst at Curry 36. Dinner at Nobelhart & Schmutzig (Michelin-starred, book ahead) or the more casual Katz Orange in Mitte.

Day 2: Cold War History and Kreuzberg

Time Activity Notes
Morning Checkpoint Charlie, Topography of Terror, and a section of the Berlin Wall Topography of Terror is free; allow 60–90 minutes.
Afternoon Jewish Museum Berlin, then wander Kreuzberg's Bergmannkiez Museum entry requires a timed ticket; the neighborhood is best on foot.
Evening Dinner and drinks along the Landwehrkanal or in Friedrichshain Book popular spots; Berlin dining peaks after 8 pm.

Restaurant recommendations: Lunch at Markthalle Neun (street food, Thursday "Street Food Thursday") or Monsieur Vuong (Vietnamese, no reservations). Dinner at Lokal (seasonal German) or Ora (in a former church).

Day 3: Palaces, Parks, and Neighborhood Charm

Time Activity Notes
Morning Charlottenburg Palace and gardens Take the U-Bahn or S-Bahn; combine with a walk along the Ku'damm.
Afternoon Tiergarten, Victory Column, and Potsdamer Platz Rent a bike for the park; the Sony Center is worth a quick look.
Evening Prenzlauer Berg: Mauerpark (Sunday flea market) and dinner on Kastanienallee Mauerpark is liveliest on Sundays; otherwise explore the side streets.

Restaurant recommendations: Lunch at Schwarzes Café (Charlottenburg, open late) or Zenkichi (Japanese, book ahead). Dinner at Prater Garten (Berlin's oldest beer garden) or Bandol sur Mer (Provençal, Prenzlauer Berg).

Practical Tips

  • Transport: Buy a Berlin WelcomeCard or a 72-hour AB ticket for unlimited U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram, and bus travel. Validate tickets before boarding; inspectors fine on the spot.
  • Museum planning: Reserve Reichstag, Pergamon, and Jewish Museum tickets online days ahead, especially in summer.
  • Cash: Many small restaurants and kiosks are cash-only; carry euros.
  • Sunday closures: Most shops close on Sundays; museums and markets stay open.
  • Nightlife: Clubs often have strict door policies and long queues; go early or with a local. Carry ID.
  • Safety: Berlin is generally safe; watch for pickpockets around Alexanderplatz and on busy transit lines.
  • Weather: Pack layers year-round; winters are cold and gray, summers mild with occasional rain.
  • Language: English is widely spoken, but a few German phrases are appreciated.
