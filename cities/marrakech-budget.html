```html Marrakech Travel Cost Guide

Marrakech is one of North Africa's most captivating destinations, where medieval medina alleys, ornate palaces, and bustling souks meet chic rooftop cafés and luxury riads. The good news for travelers is that the city remains remarkably affordable by Western standards—but costs vary dramatically depending on whether you sleep in a hostel dorm in the medina or a five-star resort in the Palmeraie. This guide breaks down realistic daily budgets across three travel styles—budget, mid-range, and luxury—covering accommodation, food, transport, and activities, so you can plan with confidence. All figures are per person, per day, in Moroccan dirhams (MAD) with approximate USD equivalents (1 USD ≈ 10 MAD; check current rates before you go).

Accommodation in Detail

  • Budget: Hostel dorms in the medina run 80–150 MAD per night; private rooms in simple guesthouses (often family-run) start around 200–350 MAD. Shared bathrooms are common at this level.
  • Mid-range: Traditional riads—courtyard houses with plunge pools and rooftop terraces—are Marrakech's signature stay. A comfortable riad room typically costs 400–900 MAD, including breakfast.
  • Luxury: Top-tier riads and five-star resorts (e.g., in the Hivernage or Palmeraie districts) range from 1,500 MAD to well over 5,000 MAD per night, with spa facilities, pools, and fine dining on site.
  • Tip: Book riads directly by phone or email when possible—owners often offer better rates than booking platforms, and you can confirm exactly which room you're getting.

Food & Drink in Detail

  • Budget: Street stalls and hole-in-the-wall eateries serve tagines, msemen (flatbread), and grilled skewers for 20–50 MAD per dish. A hearty meal at Jemaa el-Fna's food stalls costs 50–80 MAD.
  • Mid-range: Sit-down restaurants in the medina and Gueliz serve three-course meals for 150–300 MAD. A mint tea at a café runs 10–20 MAD; fresh orange juice on the square is about 10 MAD.
  • Luxury: Marrakech has a growing fine-dining scene—expect 400–800+ MAD per person for tasting menus at top restaurants, plus wine (imported alcohol is pricey, often 80–150 MAD per glass).
  • Tip: Lunch is often better value than dinner at the same restaurants. Many riads include breakfast, which can save 40–60 MAD daily.

Transport in Detail

  • Walking: The medina is best explored on foot—free, but expect to get lost (part of the fun).
  • Petit taxis: The small beige taxis are the workhorse of local transport. A short ride within the city center costs 20–40 MAD; agree on the price or insist on the meter before departing.
  • Buses: Local buses cost around 4–7 MAD per ride—cheap but crowded and slow.
  • Airport transfer: A petit taxi from Menara Airport to the medina runs 100–150 MAD (negotiate); pre-booked private transfers cost 200–350 MAD.
  • Car hire: Around 300–500 MAD per day for a small car, useful for day trips to the Atlas Mountains or Essaouira.

Activities & Attractions

  • Free: Jemaa el-Fna square (evenings are liveliest), wandering the souks, the tanneries (small tip expected), and people-watching from café terraces.
