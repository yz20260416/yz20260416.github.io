Top Attractions in Nairobi

Nairobi is one of the few capitals where you can watch lions at dawn and visit a literary icon's home by afternoon. Kenya's capital blends world-class wildlife, cultural heritage, and colonial-era history into a compact, accessible city. From the only national park bordering a major city to museums celebrating independence and traditional homesteads, these are the attractions most likely to reward an international visitor's time.

  1. Nairobi National Park — Established in 1946 just 7 km south of the city, this is a rare national park fenced on three sides with an open southern boundary that lets migrating wildlife move to the Kitengela plains. Herbivores gather here during the dry season, making game viewing especially rewarding.
  2. Giraffe Centre — Located in Lang'ata about 20 km from central Nairobi, this wildlife conservation facility was established to protect the vulnerable Rothschild's giraffe found only in East African grasslands. It began in 1979 as a nature sanctuary and offers hands-on wildlife education for visitors.
  3. Karen Blixen Museum — Set "at the foot of the Ngong Hills" about 10 km outside Nairobi, this is the former African home of Danish author Karen Blixen, famous for her 1937 book Out of Africa. The estate offers a vivid window into colonial-era life in Kenya.
  4. National Museums of Kenya (Nairobi National Museum) — The headquarters of Kenya's state heritage corporation sits on Museum Hill between the CBD and Westlands. Its collections and research span palaeontology, archaeology, ethnography, and biodiversity, making it the country's premier museum stop.
  5. Bomas of Kenya — This tourist village in Langata, about 10 km from the CBD, displays traditional homesteads belonging to several Kenyan tribes. It is also home to one of the largest auditoriums in Africa and hosts cultural performances.
  6. African Heritage House — Gazetted as a national monument in 2016, this house designed by Alan Donovan preserves forgotten African architectural designs, drawing inspiration from structures such as the Great Mosque of Djenne in Mali. It faces Nairobi National Park and is open for tours, meals, events, and overnight stays.
  7. Uhuru Gardens National Monument and Museum — This commemorative park and museum celebrates Kenya's independence from the British Empire on 12 December 1963; "uhuru" is Swahili for freedom. It features a Mũgumo tree said to mark the exact spot where the colonial flag was lowered and the Kenyan flag raised.
  8. Nairobi Railway Museum — Adjacent to Nairobi railway station, this museum opened in 1971 and displays exhibits from the defunct East African Railways. It retains a working rail connection and houses three operational steam locomotives kept securely under cover.

Practical tips

  • Visit Nairobi National Park early in the morning during the dry season for the best herbivore sightings, and note that the open southern boundary allows wildlife to migrate.
  • The Giraffe Centre and Karen Blixen Museum are both in the Lang'ata/Karen area — pair them in a single half-day trip to save on travel time.
  • Book African Heritage House tours, lunches, or overnight stays in advance, as visits are arranged rather than walk-in.
  • Check opening hours for the National Museum and Railway Museum before setting out; both are best visited on weekday mornings when quieter.
  • Bomas of Kenya is about 10 km from the CBD — allow extra time for traffic and confirm performance schedules ahead of your visit.
