Nairobi Weather by Month

Nairobi sits at roughly 1,795 meters (5,889 feet) above sea level, giving Kenya's capital a surprisingly mild, spring-like climate year-round despite its location just south of the equator. Daytime highs typically hover between 21°C and 27°C (70–81°F), while nights can dip to 10–14°C (50–57°F) — cool enough that a jacket is welcome after sunset. Rather than four seasons, Nairobi experiences two rainy periods: the "long rains" from March to May and the "short rains" from October to December, with dry, sunny spells in between. Use the guide below to plan around rainfall, temperatures, and crowds.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: January–February and June–September. These are the driest, sunniest windows, ideal for Nairobi National Park, day trips to the Ngong Hills, and onward safaris to the Maasai Mara or Amboseli.
  • Best for budget travelers: April–May and November. Rain keeps crowds and prices down, though expect muddy roads and afternoon storms.
  • Best for wildlife: July–October. The dry season draws animals to water sources and coincides with the Great Migration in the Mara (typically July–October).
  • Best for greenery and photography: late March–May and October–November. The city and surrounding highlands turn vividly green.

Practical Tips

  • Pack layers, not just summer clothes. Mornings and evenings can feel chilly (10–13°C / 50–55°F), especially June–August. A light jacket or fleece is essential.
  • Carry a compact rain jacket or umbrella if visiting March–May or October–December. Rain often arrives as intense but short afternoon showers.
  • Use high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses. Even on overcast days, the equatorial sun at this altitude is strong.
  • Plan outdoor activities for mornings during rainy seasons — showers typically build in the afternoon.
  • Expect cool, misty mornings in July–August. This is normal "Nairobi winter" weather; it usually clears by midday.
  • Book ahead for December–February and July–August, when holidaymakers and safari-goers peak.
  • Bring sturdy shoes for unpaved roads and park trails during the wet months; mud is common.
  • Check altitude effects: at ~1,800 m, some visitors feel mildly breathless on arrival — stay hydrated and take the first day easy.
