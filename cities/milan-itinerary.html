Milan is Italy's financial and fashion capital, but beneath its businesslike surface lies a city rich in art, history, and cuisine. From Leonardo da Vinci's The Last Supper to the soaring spires of the Duomo, from aperitivo culture to world-class shopping, Milan rewards visitors who plan ahead. This three-day itinerary balances iconic landmarks with neighborhoods, food, and practical logistics. Note that many major sights require advance booking — especially The Last Supper and the Duomo terraces — so reserve tickets well before you travel.

Day 1: The Historic Center

TimeActivityNotes
Morning Duomo di Milano and rooftop terraces Book a combined cathedral + terrace ticket online. Elevator access to the terraces is available; stairs are cheaper but steep. Allow 2–3 hours.
Late morning Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II Adjacent to the Duomo. Admire the glass-vaulted arcade and mosaics; a coffee at a historic café here is pricey but atmospheric.
Afternoon Teatro alla Scala and Museo Teatrale The theater museum offers glimpses into the auditorium when rehearsals permit. Then walk to Piazza dei Mercanti and Castello Sforzesco.
Evening Aperitivo in Brera Brera's cobbled streets are ideal for an evening stroll. Many bars offer a drink with a buffet of small bites (apericena).

Restaurant suggestions: For lunch near the Duomo, try a panzerotto or slice at Luini (a Milanese institution for fried stuffed dough). For dinner in Brera, book a traditional trattoria such as Osteria del Binari or a modern spot like Ratanà near Porta Nuova.

Day 2: Art, Churches, and Canals

TimeActivityNotes
Morning The Last Supper (Santa Maria delle Grazie) Tickets sell out weeks or months ahead; book on the official site. Entry is timed and visits are brief (about 15 minutes with the painting). Combine with a visit to the church.
Late morning Basilica di Sant'Ambrogio One of Milan's oldest churches, with Romanesque architecture and the tomb of St. Ambrose.
Afternoon Navigli district Walk the canals, browse boutiques and vintage shops, and enjoy a relaxed lunch. On the last Sunday of each month, the Navigli hosts a large antiques market.
Evening Dinner along the canals Navigli is lively at night with aperitivo bars and restaurants; reservations are wise on weekends.

Restaurant suggestions: Lunch at Ristorante Milano or a canal-side trattoria; for dinner, Al Pont de Ferr (traditional Milanese) or Taglio for casual, high-quality pizza and salads. Try risotto alla milanese and ossobuco at least once.

Day 3: Fashion, Design, and Modern Milan

TimeActivityNotes
Morning Quadrilatero della Moda and Corso Como Window-shop the luxury boutiques of Via Montenapoleone and Via della Spiga, then head to Corso Como for concept stores and galleries.
Afternoon Porta Nuova and Bosco Verticale Milan's modern skyline, including the vertical forest towers and Piazza Gae Aulenti. Optionally visit the Fondazione Prada or HangarBicocca for contemporary art (check opening days).
Evening Final dinner and passeggiata Return to the center for a farewell dinner; consider a rooftop bar for views of the Duomo at night.

Restaurant suggestions: Lunch at Eataly Smeraldo or a bakery for a quick panino; for a special final dinner, book Cracco (upscale) or Trattoria Milanese (classic, family-run).

Practical Tips

  • Transport: Milan's metro, trams, and buses are efficient. Buy a daily or multi-day ATM ticket; validate paper tickets. The city center is walkable, but the metro saves time between distant sights.
  • Booking: Reserve The Last Supper and Duomo terraces in advance. Check museum closing days (many close Mondays).
  • Dining customs: Lunch is typically 12:30–14:30 and dinner from 19:30. A coperto (cover charge) is common; tipping is modest. Many restaurants close in August.
  • Money and safety: Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for small cafés and markets. Watch for pickpockets around the Duomo and on crowded trams.
  • Dress code: Churches require covered shoulders and knees. Comfortable shoes are essential given the amount of walking.
  • Day trips: If you have extra time, consider a train to Lake Como (about 1 hour) or Bergamo.
  • Language: Italian is the local language; English is common in tourist areas, but a few phrases are appreciated.
