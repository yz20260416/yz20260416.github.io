Local Character

Singapore's identity begins with its geography and trade history: it was a maritime emporium known as Temasek before Stamford Raffles established it in 1819 as a British entrepôt trading post. A visitor should care because this trading-post origin explains the layered Malay, Chinese, Indian and colonial influences visible in street names, shophouse architecture and food culture today. The island's territory includes the main island plus over 60 satellite islands and islets, a detail most visitors miss; it means ferry trips to places like Pulau Ubin offer a glimpse of kampong life and unspoiled shoreline within a short ride of the skyscrapers. Singapore's four official languages — English, Malay, Mandarin and Tamil — reflect the multicultural population, and English serves as the common language in public services, making navigation easy. Multi-racialism is enshrined in the constitution and shapes daily life, from public holidays to the mix of hawker stalls. Despite having the second-highest population density of any country, urban planning has preserved numerous green and recreational spaces, so a walk between districts often passes through parks and gardens. The country's rapid development into one of the Four Asian Tigers, despite lacking natural resources and a hinterland, is a source of local pride and context for its orderly, forward-looking streetscape.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Singapore Strait waterfront — The southern shoreline facing the Singapore Strait and the Riau Islands offers skyline views and ferry connections to the southern islets.
  2. Straits of Johor causeway — The northern crossing to Malaysia's State of Johor is a busy land link and a vantage point on the country's only land border.
  3. Pulau Ubin and the satellite islands — A short boat ride reaches one of Singapore's 60-plus islets, where older kampong-style life and quiet shores contrast with the mainland.
  4. Civic District colonial landmarks — Buildings from the 1819 Raffles trading-post era and the 1867 Straits Settlements period anchor the historic core.
  5. Equator-adjacent green spaces — Despite extreme population density, planned parks and gardens give the island its reputation as a green city.

FAQ

Where exactly is Singapore?
It is an island city-state about one degree of latitude (137 km) north of the equator, off the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula, bordering the Strait of Malacca to the west, the Singapore Strait to the south, the South China Sea to the east and the Straits of Johor to the north.
What languages will I hear and use?
Singapore has four official languages — English, Malay, Mandarin and Tamil — and English is the common language used exclusively in many public services, so most visitors can get by in English alone.
When is the best time to visit?
The island is hot and humid year-round, but February to April is generally drier and a comfortable window for sightseeing, while October and November tend to be the wettest months.
