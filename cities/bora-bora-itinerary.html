Bora Bora 3-Day Itinerary

Bora Bora is the crown jewel of French Polynesia — a volcanic island ringed by a turquoise lagoon and a necklace of motu (islets) that seem almost too perfect to be real. With only three days, the goal is simple: spend as much time as possible on and in the water, save one morning for the island's dramatic interior, and leave room for unhurried sunsets. This itinerary balances the lagoon's greatest hits — snorkeling with rays and sharks, a full lagoon circuit, and a Mount Pahia hike — with the island's best dining, from casual roulottes to overwater fine dining. It assumes you're staying on the main island or a nearby motu and can arrange transfers through your hotel.

Day 1 — Arrival, Matira Beach & First Sunset

  • Morning: Arrive at Bora Bora Airport (BOB) on Motu Mute. Take the hotel shuttle boat to your accommodation and check in. If you arrive early, drop bags and head straight for the water.
  • Late morning: Settle onto Matira Beach — the island's only truly public white-sand beach and the best swimming spot on the main island. The water is shallow, warm, and impossibly clear.
  • Afternoon: Snorkel the Coral Garden near Motu Toopua or book a short trip to the Lagoonarium, a natural aquarium where you can swim among tropical fish, rays, and small sharks.
  • Sunset: Walk to Matira Point for the classic view back toward Mount Otemanu. Arrive 30–45 minutes before sunset to claim a spot.
  • Dinner: For an authentic, budget-friendly meal, head to the roulottes (food trucks) in Vaitape — try poisson cru (raw fish in coconut milk and lime) and grilled mahi-mahi. For a splurge with atmosphere, book Bloody Mary's, a legendary beachside restaurant with fresh seafood and a sand floor.

Day 2 — Full-Day Lagoon Tour

  • Morning (8:00–9:00 AM): Depart on a full-day lagoon excursion (book ahead; most hotels can arrange it). A typical tour circles the island and stops at the stingray and shark feeding sites inside the lagoon — you'll wade in waist-deep water as rays glide past and blacktip reef sharks cruise nearby.
  • Midday: Stop at a motu for a picnic lunch — usually grilled fish, coconut bread, fresh fruit, and poisson cru, often prepared on-site. Many tours include snorkeling stops at coral gardens and a visit to a pearl farm.
  • Afternoon: Continue to the outer reef for deeper snorkeling, or relax on a private motu. Some tours include a stop at the famous sandbar for photos with Mount Otemanu in the background.
  • Evening: Return to your hotel around 4:00–5:00 PM. Freshen up and head to dinner.
  • Dinner: Book Lagoon by Jean-Georges (at the St. Regis) for refined French-Polynesian cuisine over the water, or La Villa Mahana — a tiny, romantic spot run by a French chef, widely considered the island's best table. Reserve both well in advance.

Day 3 — Island Interior & Departure

  • Early morning: Hike Mount Pahia (about 2–3 hours round trip with a guide) for panoramic views over the lagoon and Mount Otemanu. The trail is steep and muddy in places — go with a guide and start early to beat the heat.
  • Alternative: If hiking isn't for you, take a 4x4 safari up to the WWII coastal defense cannons and the lookout points on the island's interior ridges — history plus some of the best views on the island.
  • Late morning: Wander Vaitape, the main town, for souvenirs: black pearls, vanilla, monoi oil, and pareos. Visit the small market and the waterfront.
  • Lunch: Grab a casual bite in Vaitape — a café serving fresh fish sandwiches or a roulotte plate — before your transfer.
  • Afternoon: Depending on your flight time, enjoy one last swim at Matira Beach or your hotel's pool, then take the shuttle boat to the airport. Note that inter-island flights to Papeete are short (about 45 minutes) but check-in and boat transfers take time — allow 2.5–3 hours before departure.

Restaurant Recommendations at a Glance

  • Bloody Mary's — iconic beachfront seafood, sand floor, celebrity history. Book ahead; mid-to-high prices.
  • La Villa Mahana — intimate, chef-driven French-Polynesian tasting menus. Reserve weeks in advance.
  • Lagoon by Jean-Georges — overwater fine dining at the St. Regis; stunning setting and polished service.
  • Roulottes in Vaitape — food trucks serving poisson cru, grilled fish, and casual local plates. Best value on the island.
  • Matira Beach snacks — casual beachside spots for sandwiches, smoothies, and cold drinks near the island's best beach.

Practical Tips

  • Currency & payments: The local currency is the CFP franc (XPF). Cards are accepted at hotels and upscale restaurants, but bring cash for roulottes, markets, and small vendors.
  • Book tours early: Lagoon tours, the Mount Pahia hike, and top restaurants fill up — especially in peak season (May–October). Reserve before you arrive.
  • Transfers: The airport is on its own motu; you'll need a boat transfer to your hotel. Confirm arrangements with your accommodation in advance.
  • Sun & reef safety: Use reef-safe sunscreen, wear water shoes on coral, and never stand on coral. Respect tour guidelines around rays and sharks.
