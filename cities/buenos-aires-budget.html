Buenos Aires Travel Cost Guide

Buenos Aires is one of South America's most affordable major cities for international travelers, offering a compelling mix of European-style architecture, world-class steak and wine, vibrant tango culture, and bustling neighborhoods — all at prices that can suit backpackers and luxury travelers alike. That said, Argentina's persistent inflation and complex currency situation mean costs can shift quickly, and the gap between "tourist prices" and "local prices" is wider here than in most destinations. This guide breaks down typical daily costs across three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — so you can plan realistically. Figures are given in US dollars for stability, with rough Argentine peso equivalents where useful, and reflect 2024–2025 conditions.

Accommodation

  • Hostels: Dorm beds run $12–25/night in Palermo, San Telmo, and Recoleta; private rooms in hostels $35–60. Quality is generally high, with many hostels offering breakfast and social activities.
  • Mid-range hotels & apart-hotels: $60–120/night for well-located three-star properties or Airbnb apartments. Palermo Soho and Recoleta command premiums; Congreso and Abasto offer better value.
  • Luxury: $200–500+ for five-star hotels (Alvear, Faena, Four Seasons) and design boutique hotels in Palermo. High-season (December–February, plus major events) can push rates up 30–50%.
  • Long-stay tip: Weekly and monthly apartment rentals are common and significantly cheaper per night; negotiate directly or via local agencies.

Food & Drink

  • Budget: Empanadas ($1–2 each), choripán from street stalls ($3–5), supermarket meals, and "menú del día" lunches at neighborhood restaurants ($8–12). A liter of local beer or a coffee costs $2–4.
  • Mid-range: A parrilla dinner with steak, sides, and wine runs $25–45 per person; café breakfasts $8–12; craft beer bars $5–8 per pint.
  • Luxury: Top-tier tasting menus and celebrated parrillas (Don Julio, Tegui) run $80–150+ per person with wine. Buenos Aires remains exceptional value at this tier compared to Europe or the US.
  • Wine: Excellent Malbec from a wine shop costs $5–10; restaurant markups are moderate. The "cubierto" (cover charge) of $2–5 per person is standard at sit-down restaurants.

Transport

  • SUBE card: Required for SUBTE (metro) and colectivos (buses). A single ride is roughly $0.30–0.50 — among the cheapest urban transit in the world. Buy/reload at kiosks.
  • Taxis & remises: Metered taxis and app-based services (Uber, Cabify, DiDi) are plentiful. Cross-neighborhood trips typically $5–10; airport trips $15–30 (Ezeiza) or $8–15 (Aeroparque).
  • Bike & walking: The city has an extensive bike-lane network and a free public bike program (registration required). Central neighborhoods are highly walkable.

Activities & Attractions

  • Free/cheap: Free walking tours (tip-based), Recoleta Cemetery ($5–10), Parque Tres de Febrero, Puerto Madero strolls, Sunday San Telmo antiques market, and La Boca's Caminito (free to wander).
  • Museums: MALBA (~$8), Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes (free), Museo de la Pasión Boquense (~$10). Many museums offer free admission on weekday afternoons.
  • Tango: Milongas (dance halls) cost $5–10 entry; group classes $10–15; professional dinner-and-show packages $50–150 (budget option: La Catedral club; premium: Rojo Tango).
  • Day trips: Tigre Delta ($10–20 by train + boat), Colonia del Sacramento, Uruguay (ferry $50–90 round trip), estancia day tours ($80–150).
  • Football: Boca Juniors or River Plate match tickets $30–100 depending on fixture and seating.

Practical Tips

  • Currency is the big variable: Argentina has multiple exchange rates. Official rates, the "blue" (parallel) market, and card-based preferential rates (MEP/tarjeta) can differ substantially. As of 2024–2025, using cards and official digital channels has become more competitive, but always check current conditions — rates and rules change frequently. Carry some cash (pesos) for small vendors and transit.
  • Budget in USD, spend in pesos: Because of inflation, peso prices rise constantly. Anchor your planning to dollar amounts and expect to re-check prices on arrival.
  • Book accommodation early in high season: December–February and major events (e.g., tango festivals) sell out; prices spike.
  • Eat on local schedules: Lunch is the value meal (menú del día); dinner starts late (9–10 pm). "Happy hour" and "promo" culture is widespread.
  • Tipping: 10% is customary at restaurants; small tips for taxis and hotel staff are appreciated but not obligatory.
  • Safety & scams: Petty theft (phone snatching) occurs in tourist areas and on transit — keep valuables discreet. Be wary of unofficial money changers ("arbolitos") on Florida Street.
  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely accepted in hotels and mid-range+ restaurants, but many small shops, taxis, and markets prefer cash. Keep a mix.
  • Airport choice: Ezeiza (international) is far from the center (~45–60 min); Aeroparque (domestic/regional) is close to Palermo and Recoleta — factor transfer costs into your budget.
