London 3-Day Itinerary

London is one of the world's great cities — a sprawling patchwork of royal palaces, world-class museums, historic pubs, and cutting-edge food. With only three days, the key is to cluster sights by neighborhood so you spend your time exploring rather than sitting on the Tube. This itinerary balances the essential "bucket list" landmarks with quieter local corners, and every day ends with a restaurant recommendation worth planning around. Book major attractions (Tower of London, Westminster Abbey, West End shows) online in advance to skip ticket queues, and wear comfortable shoes — you'll walk more than you expect.

Day 1: Westminster & the South Bank — Icons and River Views

TimeActivityDetails
8:30 AM Westminster Abbey Arrive at opening to beat the crowds. Allow 1.5–2 hours for the coronation church, Poets' Corner, and royal tombs. Book timed entry online.
10:45 AM Big Ben & Houses of Parliament Walk past the Elizabeth Tower and Parliament Square. (Note: Big Ben is the bell; the tower is the Elizabeth Tower.)
11:30 AM Churchill War Rooms Fascinating underground WWII bunker and museum near St James's Park. Allow ~1.5 hours.
1:15 PM Lunch near St James's Park Grab a picnic from a deli and eat in the park, or try The Red Lion (a classic Westminster pub) for fish and chips.
2:30 PM Walk the South Bank Cross Westminster Bridge and stroll east past the London Eye, street performers, and the Royal Festival Hall toward Tate Modern.
3:30 PM Tate Modern Free entry to Britain's national gallery of modern art. Don't miss the Turbine Hall installation and the viewing level.
5:30 PM Borough Market (if open) or Shakespeare's Globe exterior Borough Market runs Wed–Sat; otherwise admire the Globe and continue along the river.
7:00 PM Dinner Dinner recommendation: Padella (Borough Market) for hand-rolled pasta — arrive early or expect a queue — or Anchor Bankside for pub fare with river views.

Day 2: The City, Tower & East London — History Meets Edge

TimeActivityDetails
9:00 AM Tower of London Book the first entry slot. See the Crown Jewels early before queues build, then explore the White Tower and hear a Yeoman Warder tour. Allow 3 hours.
12:15 PM Tower Bridge Walk across the bridge and, optionally, visit the Tower Bridge Exhibition for the glass-floor walkway.
1:00 PM Lunch Recommendation: Borough Market street food (if it's Wed–Sat) or St John Bread and Wine near Spitalfields for British classics.
2:30 PM St Paul's Cathedral Climb the dome (528 steps) for panoramic views, or stay on the floor to admire the mosaics and Whispering Gallery.
4:30 PM Sky Garden or The Monument The Sky Garden offers free 360° views (book online in advance); The Monument commemorates the Great Fire of 1666.
6:00 PM Shoreditch & Brick Lane Explore street art, vintage shops, and the Spitalfields area. Sunday brings the famous Brick Lane Market.
7:30 PM Dinner Dinner recommendation: Dishoom Shoreditch for Bombay-style café food (book ahead), or a curry on Brick Lane at Aladin.

Day 3: West End, Museums & Royal London — Culture and Grandeur

TimeActivityDetails
9:00 AM Buckingham Palace & Changing of the Guard Check the schedule (typically 11:00 AM on certain days; confirm in advance). Arrive early for a good spot, or book a State Room tour in summer.
10:30 AM St James's Park & Horse Guards Parade Walk through the park to Horse Guards, then down Whitehall past Downing Street.
11:30 AM British Museum Free entry. Focus on the Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies, and the Parthenon sculptures. Allow 2–2.5 hours.
2:00 PM Lunch in Bloomsbury or Covent Garden Recommendation: The Lamb (a historic Bloomsbury pub) or Dishoom Covent Garden for a sit-down meal.
3:30 PM National Gallery (Trafalgar Square) Free entry; see Van Gogh's Sunflowers, Turner, and the Impressionists. A 1.5-hour highlights visit works
