Xi'an Travel Budget Guide: Costs for Backpackers, Mid-Range, and Luxury Travelers

Xi'an is one of China's most rewarding destinations, home to the Terracotta Army, a remarkably intact ancient city wall, and the buzzing Muslim Quarter. Compared with Beijing or Shanghai, prices here are moderate, but costs still vary widely depending on your travel style. This guide breaks down typical daily and one-time expenses across three budgets so you can plan with confidence. Figures are in Chinese yuan (CNY) with approximate US dollar equivalents, based on 2024–2025 prices. Per-person daily totals assume shared costs where relevant.

Sample 3-Day Budgets (Per Person)

  • Backpacker: roughly ¥600–1,150 (~$84–160) for three days, staying in dorms, eating street food, and using the metro.
  • Mid-range: roughly ¥1,900–3,800 (~$265–535), including a comfortable hotel, sit-down meals, Terracotta Army tickets, and a show.
  • Luxury: roughly ¥5,500–14,000+ (~$770–1,960+), with a five-star hotel, private guides, fine dining, and premium transport.

Practical Tips

  • Book major sites online: The Terracotta Army and Shaanxi History Museum require advance reservations, especially in peak season (April–October and Chinese holidays).
  • Use mobile payment: Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted almost everywhere; set them up with an international card before arrival. Carry a little cash for small stalls.
  • Metro is excellent: Xi'an's metro reaches the airport, the Bell Tower, the Big Wild Goose Pagoda, and the Terracotta Army area (with a short bus connection).
  • Avoid holiday surcharges: Prices for hotels and transport spike during Golden Week (early October) and Spring Festival. Book early or travel off-peak.
  • Stay inside the City Wall for walkable access to the Muslim Quarter, Bell Tower, and Drum Tower; Qujiang suits luxury travelers near the pagoda.
  • Negotiate guides and drivers: Private tours are rarely fixed-price; compare quotes and confirm inclusions in writing.
  • Eat where locals eat: Biangbiang noodles, roujiamo, and yangrou paomo are cheap, filling, and best found in the Muslim Quarter and side streets rather than tourist strips.
