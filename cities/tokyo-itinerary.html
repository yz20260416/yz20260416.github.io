Tokyo 3-Day Itinerary

Tokyo is a city of dazzling contrasts — centuries-old temples sit beside neon-lit skyscrapers, and tranquil gardens exist minutes from the world's busiest crossing. With only three days, the key is to group neighborhoods by area and alternate between iconic sights, local food, and moments of calm. This itinerary balances Tokyo's must-see highlights with authentic experiences, organized so you spend less time on trains and more time exploring. It assumes you're staying near a major station (Shinjuku, Shibuya, or Tokyo Station work well) and using public transit.

Day 1 — East Tokyo: Tradition & Old Town

Theme: Start with Tokyo's historic heart — temples, markets, and the charming old districts of Asakusa and Ueno.

TimeActivityDetails
7:30–9:00 Breakfast near your hotel Grab a quick onigiri or tamago sando (egg sandwich) from a konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) — a genuine Tokyo morning ritual.
9:00–11:30 Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori (Asakusa) Tokyo's oldest temple (founded 628 AD). Arrive early to beat crowds. Walk the Nakamise shopping street for souvenirs and snacks like ningyo-yaki and melon pan.
11:30–12:30 Lunch in Asakusa Try tempura at a local spot (e.g., Daikokuya) or unagi (grilled eel) at Asakusa Unatetsu.
13:00–15:00 Tokyo Skytree (Sumida) Short walk or one train stop away. Japan's tallest structure (634 m) with observation decks. Book tickets online to skip lines.
15:30–17:30 Ueno Park & Ameyoko Market Stroll Ueno's museums and shrines, then browse Ameyoko — a bustling open-air market for street food, seafood, and bargains.
18:30–20:30 Dinner in Ueno or Asakusa Try izakaya dining (small plates + drinks) — e.g., Torikizoku (yakitori chain) or a local tonkatsu shop.

Day 2 — West Tokyo: Pop Culture & Modern Energy

Theme: Dive into Tokyo's contemporary side — Shibuya, Harajuku, and Shinjuku's neon nights.

TimeActivityDetails
8:30–9:30 Breakfast Café breakfast in Shibuya — try a kissaten (old-school coffee shop) for thick toast and pour-over coffee.
9:30–11:00 Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko Statue Experience the world's busiest pedestrian crossing. For a view, head to Shibuya Sky (book ahead) or the Starbucks overlooking the crossing.
11:00–13:00 Harajuku: Takeshita Street & Meiji Jingu Browse quirky fashion on Takeshita-dori, then walk to the serene Meiji Shrine — a forested oasis in the city.
13:00–14:00 Lunch in Harajuku Try crepes on Takeshita Street, or a proper meal at Afuri (yuzu ramen) or a gyoza spot.
14:30–16:30 Omotesando & Aoyama Architectural stroll down Omotesando avenue (flagship stores, cafés, Omotesando Hills). Optional: Nezu Museum garden.
17:00–19:00 Shinjuku Gyoen or Golden Gai Relax in Shinjuku Gyoen garden (closes 6 pm), then explore the tiny bars of Golden Gai or the neon of Kabukicho.
19:00–21:00 Dinner in Shinjuku Options: ramen at Ichiran, yakitori in Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane"), or sushi at a conveyor-belt spot like Uobei.
