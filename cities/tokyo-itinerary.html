Tokyo in Three Days: A Complete Itinerary Guide

Tokyo rewards both first-time visitors and seasoned travelers with an unmatched blend of tradition, technology, and gastronomy. This three-day itinerary balances iconic landmarks with neighborhood exploration, structured so each day covers a distinct area of the city to minimize transit time. Mornings are reserved for major sights before crowds peak, afternoons for cultural immersion and shopping, and evenings for dining and nightlife. All recommendations assume a base near central Tokyo (Shinjuku, Shibuya, or Tokyo Station), with transit via the efficient rail network.

Day 1: Eastern Tokyo — Asakusa, Ueno, and Akihabara

Time Activity Details
Morning Senso-ji Temple & Asakusa Arrive by 8:00 AM to beat crowds at Tokyo's oldest temple. Enter via Kaminarimon Gate, stroll Nakamise-dori for souvenirs, and explore the temple grounds. Optional: a short Sumida River walk or river cruise.
Late Morning Ueno Park & Museums Take the Ginza Line to Ueno. Choose one museum: Tokyo National Museum (best for Japanese art and artifacts) or the National Museum of Nature and Science. Stroll through Ueno Park and Shinobazu Pond.
Afternoon Ameyoko Market & Akihabara Lunch and browsing at Ameyoko's open-air market, then walk or ride to Akihabara for electronics, anime culture, and retro arcades.
Evening Dinner in Asakusa or Kanda Return toward Asakusa for traditional dining, or stop in Kanda for izakaya-style food near the station.

Restaurant recommendations:

  • Daikokuya (Asakusa) — historic tempura restaurant, casual and affordable.
  • Kamiya Bar (Asakusa) — one of Tokyo's oldest Western-style bars, known for its "Denki Bran" cocktail.
  • Ameyoko street stalls — grilled seafood, fruit, and snacks for a quick lunch.

Day 2: Central Tokyo — Imperial Palace, Ginza, and Shibuya

Time Activity Details
Morning Imperial Palace East Gardens Free entry; gardens open at 9:00 AM (closed Mondays and Fridays). Walk the grounds and view the palace exterior. Alternatively, join a guided palace tour (advance reservation required).
Late Morning Ginza Walk or ride to Ginza for upscale shopping, the Kabukiza Theatre exterior, and department store food halls (depachika) — excellent for lunch.
Afternoon Shibuya & Harajuku Head to Shibuya Crossing, the Hachiko statue, and Shibuya Sky observation deck (book timed tickets in advance). Continue to Harajuku for Takeshita Street and Omotesando's architecture and boutiques.
Evening Dinner & Shibuya nightlife Dine in Shibuya or nearby Ebisu. Optional: a rooftop drink or a stroll through Nonbei Yokocho's tiny bars.

Restaurant recommendations:

  • Sushi Tokami (Ginza) — acclaimed sushi counter; reserve well ahead.
  • Ginza Kagari — famous for rich chicken-based ramen.
  • Afuri (Ebisu/Shibuya) — lighter yuzu-shio ramen, good for a quick dinner.

Day 3: Western Tokyo — Shinjuku, Meiji Shrine, and Day Trip Option

Time Activity Details
Morning Meiji Jingu Shrine Arrive early for a peaceful forest walk to the shrine. Combine with a visit to Yoyogi Park and, if interested, the Nezu Museum or teamLab Borderless (tickets required).
Late Morning Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden One of Tokyo's finest gardens; small entry fee. Excellent for seasonal flowers and a quiet break.
Afternoon Shinjuku exploration Browse the observatories at the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (free), shop in Shinjuku, and wander Kabukicho and Omoide Yokocho.
Evening Dinner in Omoide Yokocho or Golden Gai Yakitori and small-plate dining in atmospheric alleyways; Golden Gai offers tiny, characterful bars.

Restaurant recommendations:

  • Omoide Yokocho stalls (Shinjuku) — yakitori and grilled skewers in narrow lanes.
  • Ichiran (Shinjuku) — solo-booth tonkotsu ramen, reliable and quick.
  • Tsunahachi (Shinjuku) — long-established tempura restaurant.

Day trip alternative: Replace Day 3's afternoon with a half-day trip to Nikko, Kamakura, or Yokohama using a JR Pass or regional rail ticket; depart early and return by evening.

Practical Tips

  • Transit: Buy a Suica or PASMO IC card for trains, buses, and convenience stores. The rail network is the fastest way around; taxis are expensive.
  • Timing: Major sights (Senso-ji, Meiji Jingu, Shibuya Sky) are best before 9:00 AM or after 4:00 PM to avoid peak crowds.
  • Reservations: Book popular restaurants and observation decks (Shibuya Sky, teamLab) days or weeks in advance, especially on weekends.
  • Cash: Many small restaurants and market stalls are cash-only; carry yen despite widespread card acceptance.
  • Etiquette: No eating while walking, quiet phone use on trains, and queue politely. Tipping is not customary.
  • Connectivity: Rent a pocket Wi-Fi or buy a prepaid SIM/eSIM at the airport for maps and translation.
  • Weather: Check forecasts; summers are hot and humid, winters mild but chilly. Carry a compact umbrella.
  • Luggage: Use coin lockers at major stations or luggage-forwarding services (takkyubin) between hotels.
