Nairobi 3-Day Itinerary

Nairobi is one of the few capital cities in the world with a national park on its doorstep, making it a surprisingly rewarding stop for travelers — whether you're using it as a gateway to a safari or exploring it in its own right. This 3-day itinerary balances wildlife, culture, food, and history, with realistic travel times to help you get the most out of Kenya's vibrant capital. It's designed for travelers based in a central area (Westlands, Kilimani, or the CBD), and assumes you have private transport or are comfortable using ride-hailing apps like Bolt or Uber, which are affordable and widely used.

Day 1 — Wildlife & City Orientation

Time Activity Notes
7:00 – 7:30 AM Breakfast at your hotel or a local café (e.g., Java House or Artcaffé) Grab a hearty breakfast — you'll be on your feet most of the morning.
7:30 – 11:30 AM Nairobi National Park — game drive Enter via the main gate early to catch animals before the heat. Expect to see lions, giraffes, zebras, rhinos, and buffalo against a skyline backdrop. Book a guided drive or self-drive with a 4x4.
12:00 – 1:30 PM Lunch at Carnivore Restaurant Famous for its nyama choma (grilled meat) and game meat tasting menu. A short drive from the park's Langata gate.
2:00 – 3:30 PM Giraffe Centre (Langata) Feed endangered Rothschild's giraffes from a raised platform. Great for photos and a quick, memorable stop.
4:00 – 5:30 PM Karen Blixen Museum Tour the former home of the Out of Africa author, set in beautiful gardens at the foot of the Ngong Hills.
7:00 PM onward Dinner in Westlands Try Mama Oliech Restaurant for authentic Kenyan fish and ugali, or About Thyme for a relaxed bistro dinner.

Day 2 — Culture, History & Local Life

Time Activity Notes
8:00 – 9:00 AM Breakfast and transfer to the city center Allow extra time for morning traffic into the CBD.
9:00 – 10:30 AM Nairobi National Museum Excellent exhibits on Kenyan prehistory, ethnography, and wildlife. The adjacent Snake Park is worth a quick visit.
11:00 AM – 12:30 PM Kibera walking tour (with a reputable community-based operator) One of Africa's largest informal settlements — a guided tour offers real insight into local entrepreneurship and community projects. Book in advance and go with an established guide.
1:00 – 2:00 PM Lunch at K'osewe Restaurant or a local eatery in Kilimani Try traditional dishes like sukuma wiki, chapati, and pilau.
2:30 – 4:00 PM Maasai Market (location rotates by day — check ahead) Browse and bargain for crafts, beadwork, and textiles. Bring small cash notes and negotiate politely.
4:30 – 5:30 PM Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) helipad view Panoramic views over the city — a great spot for sunset photos.
7:00 PM onward Dinner at Talisman (Karen) or Nyama Mama (Westlands) Talisman offers a fusion menu in a garden setting; Nyama Mama serves modern Kenyan comfort food.

Day 3 — Nature, Markets & Farewell

Time Activity Notes
8:00 – 9:00 AM Breakfast and transfer to Limuru/Karen area Plan for a 45–60 minute drive depending on traffic.
9:00 AM – 12:00 PM Karura Forest — walking or cycling Lush urban forest with marked trails, waterfalls, and a café. Rent bikes at the gate or join a guided walk.
12:30 – 1:30 PM Lunch at River Café (Karura Forest) Relaxed outdoor café right inside the forest — perfect after a morning of walking.
2:00 – 3:30 PM Bomas of Kenya (optional) or Village Market shopping Bomas showcases traditional homesteads and cultural dance performances; Village Market is great for last-minute gifts and crafts.
4:00 – 5:30 PM Relax at your hotel or visit Ngong Road Forest Sanctuary A quieter nature option if you want a slower finish to the trip.
7:00 PM onward Farewell dinner at Hero (Westlands) or INTI (Kilimani) Both offer excellent contemporary cuisine and lively atmospheres for a final night out.

Practical Tips

  • Transport: Use Bolt or Uber for reliable, metered rides. Agree on fares for taxis and avoid unmarked cabs. Traffic is heavy between 7–9 AM and 5–7 PM — plan around it.
  • Safety: Nairobi is generally safe for tourists in central and upscale areas, but avoid walking alone at night, keep valuables out of sight, and don't carry large amounts of cash. Use hotel safes.
  • Money: Kenyan shillings (KES) are preferred for markets and small vendors; cards are widely accepted at restaurants and malls. M-Pesa mobile money is ubiquitous — many businesses accept it.
  • Health: Check visa and vaccination requirements (yellow fever certificate may be required depending on your origin). Drink bottled or filtered water. Nairobi sits at ~1,795 m (5,889 ft), so stay hydrated.
