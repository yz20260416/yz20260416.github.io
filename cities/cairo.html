Local Character

Cairo's name comes from the Arabic al-Qāhirah, meaning 'the Vanquisher,' given by the Fatimid Caliph al-Mu'izz when the city was founded in 969 and made the Fatimid capital in 973; tradition holds that the planet Mars, called an-Najm al-Qāhir or 'the Conquering Star,' was rising at the moment of founding, so the city's identity is literally written in the sky above it. A visitor walking the old streets can look up at the same stars and grasp why Cairenes have long treated their city as destined rather than merely built. The city is titled 'the city of a thousand minarets' for its preponderance of Islamic architecture, a nickname earned across the Ayyubid and Mamluk periods (12th–16th centuries) when Cairo superseded the older settlement of Fustat as the main urban center; this matters to travelers because the medieval core, not the modern skyline, holds the city's architectural soul. Cairo also hosts Al-Azhar University, Egypt's oldest university and one of the oldest in the world, keeping the city a living center of scholarship rather than a museum piece. Egyptian daily life here is inseparable from the Nile Delta setting and the layered remains of Memphis, Heliopolis, and the Giza pyramid complex, all now within the city's reach.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Giza Pyramid Complex — The ancient pyramids and Sphinx now stand within Cairo's modern boundaries, a short drive from the city center.
  2. Historic Cairo (Islamic Cairo) — The medieval core, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979, holds the dense minarets and monuments that earned Cairo its 'city of a thousand minarets' title.
  3. Al-Azhar University and Mosque — Egypt's oldest university and one of the world's oldest, still active as a center of Islamic scholarship in the old city.
  4. Cairo Opera House — Home of the Cairo Symphony Orchestra, anchoring the city's modern performing-arts scene on Gezira Island.
  5. Fustat — The predecessor settlement to Cairo, whose remains sit south of the historic center and trace the city's origins before the Fatimid founding of 969.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Cairo?
October through April offers the mildest weather, with spring and autumn especially comfortable for sightseeing.
How do I get around Cairo?
The Cairo Metro, opened in 1987, is Africa's oldest metro system and among the world's fifteen busiest, with over a billion annual rides; taxis and ride-hailing fill in the rest.
Why is Cairo called the city of a thousand minarets?
The nickname reflects its preponderance of Islamic architecture, built up especially during the Ayyubid and Mamluk periods from the 12th to 16th centuries.
