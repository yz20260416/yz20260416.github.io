Buenos Aires Weather by Month

Buenos Aires enjoys a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, meaning there's genuinely something to recommend every month of the year. Summers (December–February) are hot and humid, winters (June–August) are mild and occasionally chilly, and the shoulder seasons of spring and autumn bring some of the most pleasant weather on the continent. Because the city sits in the Southern Hemisphere, seasons are reversed from North America and Europe—so if you're escaping a northern winter, you'll arrive in the middle of porteño summer. Rainfall is fairly evenly distributed throughout the year, though late summer and early autumn tend to be the wettest. Below is a month-by-month breakdown of average high temperatures, average low temperatures, and typical rainfall to help you plan your trip.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: March–May and September–November. These shoulder seasons offer mild temperatures, manageable crowds, and comfortable sightseeing conditions.
  • Best for warm-weather lovers: December–February. Expect heat and humidity, but also long evenings, outdoor dining, and a buzzing summer atmosphere.
  • Best for budget travelers: June–August. Winter is low season, so you'll find lower hotel rates and fewer queues, though you'll need a warm coat.
  • Best for photographers: November. The city's jacaranda trees erupt in purple blossoms, making it one of the most photogenic months.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for variability. Buenos Aires weather can shift quickly, especially in spring and autumn. Layering is your best strategy year-round.
  • Summer heat and humidity. January and February can feel oppressive. Plan outdoor activities for the morning, carry water, and use sunscreen—the Argentine sun is strong.
  • Winter is milder than you might expect. Snow is essentially unheard of in the city. A medium-weight coat, scarf, and layers will see you through, but buildings can be chilly indoors, so bring a sweater.
  • Rain is spread throughout the year. A compact umbrella or light rain jacket is worth packing regardless of season. Storms can be sudden and intense, particularly in summer.
  • Watch the wind. The city can be breezy, and the "pampero" wind occasionally brings sharp temperature drops after warm spells.
  • Book around holidays. January, Easter week, and July winter holidays are peak domestic travel periods—reserve accommodation and restaurant tables in advance.
  • Check daily forecasts. Local weather apps are reliable for short-term planning, and porteños plan their outdoor time around the day's conditions.
