Bali Weather by Month

Bali sits just 8 degrees south of the equator, which gives the island a tropical monsoon climate with two distinct seasons: the dry season (roughly April–October) and the wet season (roughly November–March). Temperatures are remarkably stable year-round, hovering between 26°C and 31°C (79°F–88°F) in the lowlands, while the cooler hill towns of Ubud and Bedugul run a few degrees lower. What changes dramatically is rainfall: the wet season can bring short, heavy afternoon downpours, while the dry season delivers long stretches of sunshine. The tables and guidance below will help you match your trip to the weather you want.

Figures are approximate long-term averages for the southern lowlands (Denpasar/Kuta area). Rainfall in Ubud and the central hills is typically 20–40% higher, and the northern coast around Lovina is drier than the south during the wet season.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall weather: April–October — the dry season brings sunshine, lower humidity, and reliable conditions for beaches, hiking, and diving. May, June, and September are the sweet spot: dry and sunny but less crowded and cheaper than peak season.
  • Peak season (July–August): driest and breeziest weather, but expect the highest prices, booked-out villas, and traffic. Book well in advance.
  • Best value: March–April and October–November — shoulder months with mostly good weather, fewer crowds, and lower accommodation rates. Expect occasional showers, especially in the afternoons.
  • Wet season (November–March): not a dealbreaker — rain usually comes in intense bursts rather than all-day drizzle, mornings are often clear, and prices are at their lowest. It's also the greenest, most lush time to visit rice terraces and waterfalls.

Practical Tips

  • Plan around the rain, not against it: In the wet season, schedule outdoor activities (treks, temple visits, boat trips) for the morning and keep afternoons flexible for spa time, cafés, or museum visits.
  • Pack for humidity: Lightweight, quick-drying clothing works best year-round. Bring a compact rain jacket or umbrella if travelling November–March, and reef-safe sunscreen regardless of season.
  • Mind the microclimates: Ubud and the central highlands are cooler and wetter than the coast — a light layer is useful for early mornings and evenings. The north coast (Lovina, Amed) is drier in the wet season and offers calmer seas for diving.
  • Watch the surf and currents: The dry season (May–September) brings bigger swell to the west coast (Kuta, Uluwatu) — great for surfers, but swimmers should heed flags and choose patrolled beaches. The east coast is calmer then.
  • Book ahead in peak season: July–August and the Christmas/New Year period see the highest demand. Reserve accommodation and popular activities (Mt. Batur sunrise trek, Nusa Penida day trips) early.
  • Check tide tables for beach days: Some southern beaches (e.g., Seminyak, Canggu) have strong currents and shifting sand at certain tides; tide apps help you pick the best swimming windows.
  • Stay hydrated and sun-smart: The equatorial sun is intense even on cloudy days — UV levels are high year-round, so reapply sunscreen and carry water on hikes.
