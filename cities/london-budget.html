London has a reputation as one of the world's most expensive cities, but the reality is far more nuanced. With careful planning, a traveler can experience the city's world-class museums, markets, parks, and neighborhoods on a surprisingly modest budget — while those seeking luxury will find no shortage of five-star options. This guide breaks down realistic daily and weekly costs across three travel styles: budget, mid-range, and luxury. All figures are in British pounds (GBP) and reflect 2024–2025 prices; expect London to run roughly 20–40% more expensive than most other UK cities.

Accommodation in Detail

  • Budget: Hostel dorms in Zones 1–2 run £25–£45 per night; private hostel rooms £60–£90. Budget hotel chains (Premier Inn, Travelodge) can hit £50–£80 if booked well in advance, especially outside central zones.
  • Mid-range: Expect £120–£220 for a decent 3–4★ double room. Booking 2–3 months ahead and staying in Zones 2–3 (e.g., Clapham, Stratford, Greenwich) can cut costs by 25–40%.
  • Luxury: Iconic hotels (The Savoy, Claridge's, The Langham) start around £400–£600 per night and climb steeply. Boutique luxury in Mayfair or Kensington typically runs £350–£500.
  • Money-saver: Accommodation is London's biggest cost variable — a well-located hostel or Zone 2 hotel saves more than any other trimming. Avoid major event weekends (e.g., marathon, Wimbledon) when prices spike.

Food and Drink

  • Budget (£20–£35/day): Supermarket meal deals (£4–£5), street food markets (Borough, Camden, Brick Lane: £6–£10 per dish), and self-catering. A pint runs £5–£7; a coffee £3–£4.
  • Mid-range (£50–£90/day): Café breakfasts (£8–£12), pub lunches (£12–£18), and one restaurant dinner (£20–£35 per main). Pre-theatre menus offer excellent value at £20–£30 for two courses.
  • Luxury (£150–£400+/day): Fine dining tasting menus start around £80–£150 per person (before wine); afternoon tea at a top hotel is £60–£90; Michelin-starred dinners can exceed £200 per head.
  • Money-saver: Eat your main meal at lunch (set menus are far cheaper), use markets for quality cheap eats, and note that many restaurants add a 12.5% service charge — check before tipping extra.

Transport

  • Contactless is king: Tap in and out with a contactless card or phone — fares are automatically capped (around £8.90/day in Zones 1–2, roughly £42–£45/week).
  • Budget: Walk wherever possible (central London is compact), use buses (£1.75 per journey, hopper fare lets you take two buses within an hour), and avoid peak hours.
  • Mid-range: A 7-Day Travelcard or contactless weekly cap covers unlimited Tube and bus travel in Zones 1–2.
  • Luxury: Black cabs (£15–£40 for typical central journeys), private airport transfers (£60–£120), and chauffeur services.
  • Airport note: Heathrow Express is fast but pricey (£25+); the Piccadilly line (£5–£6) or Elizabeth line are far cheaper. Gatwick and Stansted express trains run £15–£25 if booked ahead.

Activities and Attractions

  • Free highlights: British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, Natural History Museum, V&A, Science Museum, Hyde Park, and the Changing of the Guard — all free. Free walking tours (tip-based, £5–£10 tip) are excellent value.
  • Paid attractions (budget £10–£35 each): Tower of London (~£35), Westminster Abbey (~£29), London Eye (~£35), St Paul's Cathedral (~£25). Book online in advance for discounts.
  • Theatre: West End tickets range from £20 (restricted view, day seats) to £100+; TKTS booth in Leicester Square offers same-day discounts of up to 50%.
  • Luxury extras: Private guided tours (£150–£400), premium theatre seats (£100–£250), spa days, and helicopter tours (£200+).
  • Money-saver: The London Pass (£80–£120 for 2–3 days) only pays off if you visit 3+ major paid attractions daily — otherwise skip it and prioritize free museums.

Sample Weekly Budgets (per person)

  • Budget traveler: £400–£800 per week (hostel, self-catering, free attractions, contactless transport).
  • Mid-range traveler: £1,400–£2,700 per week (3–4★ hotel, mixed dining, several paid attractions and a show).
  • Luxury traveler: £4,000–£10,000+ per week (5★ hotel, fine dining, private tours and transfers).

Practical Money-Saving Tips

  • Book early: Accommodation and advance train tickets are dramatically cheaper 2–3 months out. Avoid school holidays and major events.
  • Stay in Zone 2–3: Hotels are cheaper and well-connected; the savings often exceed the extra transport cost.
  • Use contactless fare caps: Never buy single paper tickets — contactless is always cheaper and automatically capped daily and weekly.
