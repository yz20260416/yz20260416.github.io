Hanoi Weather by Month

Hanoi sits in northern Vietnam's subtropical climate zone, which means it experiences four distinct seasons — a rarity in Southeast Asia. Travelers can encounter everything from humid 35°C (95°F) summer days to cool, drizzly 15°C (59°F) winter afternoons within the same year. Understanding the monthly weather patterns is essential for packing correctly, choosing the right wardrobe, and timing your trip to match your travel style — whether that's avoiding the crowds, chasing clear skies for photography, or simply staying dry.

Seasonal Breakdown

  • Winter (December–February): Cool and often grey, with temperatures rarely dropping below 10°C (50°F) but feeling chillier due to damp air. The "nồm" drizzle of late winter makes streets slick and laundry slow to dry. Pack layers and a light waterproof jacket.
  • Spring (March–April): A transition period — warming temperatures and blooming flowers, but March can still be misty and humid. April is one of the year's most comfortable months.
  • Summer (May–September): Hot, sticky, and wet. Rain typically arrives in intense afternoon bursts rather than all-day soaks, so mornings often stay usable. July and August are the peak of both heat and rainfall. This is also typhoon season along the coast, which can affect travel plans to Ha Long Bay and beyond.
  • Autumn (October–November): Widely regarded as Hanoi's finest stretch — lower humidity, clearer skies, and comfortable temperatures. November in particular is crisp and dry.

Best Months to Visit

  • Overall best: October and November — dry, comfortable, and photogenic, with the city at its most livable.
  • Runner-up: March and April — mild and green, though March can be drizzly.
  • Budget and crowd-avoidance: June–August — expect heat and rain, but fewer tourists and lower hotel rates.
  • Avoid if rain bothers you: July–August, the wettest stretch of the year.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for the season, not the stereotype: Winter visitors often underestimate the damp chill — bring a warm layer and rain shell. Summer visitors should prioritize breathable fabrics and a compact umbrella.
  • Plan outdoor sightseeing for mornings: In summer, thunderstorms tend to build in the afternoon; front-load temple visits, walking tours, and lake strolls before noon.
  • Watch the air quality: Hanoi occasionally experiences poor air quality in winter (December–February) due to temperature inversions and agricultural burning. Sensitive travelers may want a mask or to limit strenuous outdoor activity on hazy days.
  • Check typhoon forecasts: If your itinerary includes Ha Long Bay or coastal areas from July to October, monitor weather warnings and keep flexibility in your plans.
  • Dress in layers for winter: Buildings are often unheated, so indoor spaces can feel as cool as outdoors — a light fleece or sweater helps.
  • Book autumn early: October–November is peak season; accommodations and popular tours fill up weeks in advance.
