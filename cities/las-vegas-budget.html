Las Vegas Travel Budget Guide: Backpacker, Mid-Range, and Luxury Costs

Las Vegas is famous for its casinos, shows, and nightlife, but the city can be surprisingly affordable—or extravagantly expensive—depending on how you travel. The biggest cost variables are accommodation, gambling, and dining, since hotel prices spike on weekends, during conventions, and around major events. This guide breaks down estimated daily costs for three traveler types, using per-person figures based on double occupancy for accommodation unless noted otherwise. All amounts are in U.S. dollars and reflect typical midweek pricing; expect hotel rates to rise 50–200% on weekends and holidays.

Accommodation Details

  • Backpacker: Hostels such as those near Fremont Street offer dorm beds from about $25–$50. Budget motels and older off-Strip properties can run $40–$70 midweek. Note that resort fees (often $20–$45 per night) apply at most hotels, even budget ones.
  • Mid-Range: Standard rooms at mid-tier Strip resorts (Excalibur, Luxor, Harrah's, Flamingo) typically range $80–$180 midweek, higher on weekends. Downtown options like the Golden Nugget can be cheaper.
  • Luxury: Suites and premium rooms at top resorts start around $300–$500 midweek and climb steeply for events. Five-star properties add resort fees, valet, and dining minimums.

Food Details

  • Backpacker: Casinos historically offered cheap eats, but true bargains are now mostly off-Strip. Grocery stores, taco shops, and fast-food value menus keep costs near $20–$35 daily.
  • Mid-Range: Casual Strip restaurants, food halls, and happy hours fit a $50–$90 daily budget. Buffets range roughly $25–$55 per person.
  • Luxury: Celebrity chef restaurants, steakhouses, and omakase counters push daily food costs to $150–$400 or more, especially with wine pairings.

Transport Details

  • Walking is free but distances on the Strip are long; the Deuce bus runs 24/7 along the Strip and Downtown for a few dollars per ride or a day pass.
  • The Las Vegas Monorail serves the east side of the Strip; day passes are around $13–$15.
  • Rideshares and taxis from the airport to the Strip typically cost $20–$30; taxis from the airport add a surcharge.
  • Rental cars run $40–$80+ per day plus parking fees at resorts (often $15–$30 nightly).

Activity and Entertainment Costs

  • Free: Bellagio fountains, Fremont Street Experience, conservatory displays, people-watching, and nearby hiking (Red Rock Canyon charges a small entry fee).
  • Paid attractions: Shows range from about $40 (discount seats) to $250+ for headliners; observation decks, gondola rides, and thrill rides typically cost $25–$40 each.
  • Gambling: Set a strict daily budget—treat it as entertainment spending, not an investment.

Practical Tips

  • Book midweek: Sunday–Thursday rates are far lower than Friday–Saturday, and convention weeks (check the Las Vegas Convention Center calendar) inflate prices dramatically.
  • Budget for resort fees: Most hotels charge mandatory nightly resort fees that are not included in advertised room rates—confirm the total before booking.
  • Use player's cards and promo codes: Signing up for casino loyalty programs can unlock discounted rooms and dining credits.
  • Drink water and pace yourself: Desert heat is intense; free water is available in casinos, and carrying a bottle saves money.
  • Look for discount tickets: Same-day show tickets at Tix4Tonight-style booths and online deals can cut entertainment costs significantly.
  • Stay off-Strip for savings: Downtown and off-Strip hotels offer lower rates and often free parking, at the cost of extra transport time.
  • Set a gambling limit: Decide your maximum loss in advance and leave cards at the hotel if needed.
