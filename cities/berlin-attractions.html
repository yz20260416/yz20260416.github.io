Berlin Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Berlin is a city where history, art, and modern life collide. From the remnants of the Berlin Wall to world-class museums and grand public squares, the German capital offers an enormous range of experiences for every type of traveler. This guide covers the ten essential attractions, with practical details on tickets, opening hours, and tips to help you plan an efficient and rewarding visit. Note that prices and hours can change, so always confirm on official websites before you go.

  1. Brandenburg Gate (Brandenburger Tor)

    The iconic 18th-century neoclassical gate is Berlin's most famous landmark and a symbol of German reunification. It marks the eastern end of the Tiergarten and the start of Unter den Linden boulevard.

    • Tickets: Free to view from the outside; no admission required.
    • Opening hours: Accessible 24/7 as an outdoor monument.
    • Visiting tips: Visit early morning for fewer crowds and better photos. Combine with a stroll through the nearby Reichstag area and Tiergarten. The gate is especially atmospheric at night when illuminated.

  2. Reichstag Building

    The seat of the German parliament features a striking glass dome designed by Norman Foster, offering panoramic views over the city and a fascinating look at the Bundestag below.

    • Tickets: Free, but advance registration is required for the dome and rooftop terrace.
    • Opening hours: Generally daily around 8:00–24:00 (last admission around 22:00); closed on some holidays. Confirm current hours online.
    • Visiting tips: Book your free slot well in advance, especially in summer. Bring photo ID. The rooftop restaurant is a good option for a break with a view.

  3. Museum Island (Museumsinsel)

    A UNESCO World Heritage site on the Spree River, home to five world-class museums including the Pergamon Museum, Neues Museum (with the bust of Nefertiti), and Alte Nationalgalerie.

    • Tickets: Individual museum tickets vary (roughly €10–€14); a Museum Island day pass or the Berlin Museum Pass offers better value. The Pergamon Museum has been undergoing phased renovation, so check which sections are open.
    • Opening hours: Typically Tuesday–Sunday around 10:00–18:00, with some museums open later on Thursdays; most close on Mondays. Verify per museum.
    • Visiting tips: Reserve timed tickets online in peak season. Allow at least half a day, ideally a full day. The Lustgarten and Berlin Cathedral nearby are worth a stop.

  4. Berlin Wall Memorial (Gedenkstätte Berliner Mauer)

    Located on Bernauer Straße, this memorial preserves a stretch of the original wall, the death strip, and a documentation center explaining the division of Berlin.

    • Tickets: Free admission to the outdoor site and documentation center.
    • Opening hours: Outdoor grounds generally accessible daily; the documentation center and exhibition have set hours (often Tuesday–Sunday), so check ahead.
    • Visiting tips: Start at the visitor center for context, then walk the preserved section. The viewing tower offers a clear view of the former death strip layout.

  5. East Side Gallery

    The longest remaining section of the Berlin Wall, transformed into an open-air gallery of murals by artists from around the world, including the famous "Fraternal Kiss."

    • Tickets: Free.
    • Opening hours: Open-air and accessible 24/7.
    • Visiting tips: Go early to avoid crowds and tour groups. The gallery stretches about 1.3 km along the Spree near Ostbahnhof—wear comfortable shoes. Note that some murals have suffered weathering and graffiti.

  6. Checkpoint Charlie and the Wall Museum

    The famous Cold War crossing point between East and West Berlin, now a busy tourist area with the Mauermuseum (Haus am Checkpoint Charlie) documenting escape attempts.

    • Tickets: The checkpoint itself is free; the Mauermuseum charges an admission fee (roughly €15–€18; check current rates).
    • Opening hours: The street checkpoint is always accessible; the museum is generally open daily around 9:00–22:00.
    • Visiting tips: The area is crowded and commercial—treat it as a quick photo stop. The museum is dense and popular; go early. For deeper history, pair with the Berlin Wall Memorial.

  7. Alexanderplatz and the TV Tower (Fernsehturm)

    Berlin's central square and the 368-meter TV Tower, offering an observation deck and revolving restaurant with sweeping city views.

    • Tickets: Observation deck tickets typically around €25–€30 for adults; discounts for children and reduced rates available. Book online for timed entry.
    • Opening hours: Generally daily from around 9:00 or 10:00 until late evening (often 23:00–24:00); hours vary seasonally.
    • Visiting tips: Book a timed slot in advance to avoid long queues. Sunset slots are popular and sell out—reserve early. The square itself is free and well connected by U-Bahn and S-Bahn.

  8. Charlottenburg Palace (Schloss Charlottenburg)

    Berlin's largest palace, a Baroque residence with ornate state rooms, gardens, and a collection of porcelain and historic artifacts.

    • Tickets: Various options (palace only, palace plus special exhibitions, gardens); adult palace tickets roughly €12–€19 depending on the combination. Garden access is generally free.
    • Opening hours: Typically Tuesday–Sunday around 10:00–17:00 (longer in summer); closed Mondays. Confirm seasonal hours.
    • Visiting tips: Allow 2–3 hours for the palace and gardens. The surrounding park is ideal for a picnic in good weather. Combine with a walk along the nearby Spree.

  9. Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe (Holocaust Memorial)

    A powerful memorial near the Brandenburg Gate consisting of 2,711 concrete stelae, with an underground information center documenting the Holocaust.

    • Tickets: Free admission to the field of stelae and the information center.
    • Opening hours: The stelae field is open 24/7; the underground information center has set hours (often Tuesday–Sunday, around 10:00–19:00 in summer, shorter in winter).
    • Visiting tips: Maintain a respectful, quiet atmosphere. The information center is emotionally intense—allow time afterward. Avoid climbing on the stelae.

  10. Berlin Cathedral (Berliner Dom)

    A grand Protestant cathedral on Museum Island with a richly decorated interior, a crypt, and a dome gallery offering excellent views over central Berlin.

    • Tickets: Adult admission roughly €10–€12; reduced rates for students and children. Dome access is included in most tickets.
    • Opening hours: Generally Monday–Saturday around 9:00–19:00 and Sunday from around 12:00 (services may restrict access); hours vary seasonally.
    • Visiting tips: Climb the dome for one of the best central views—there are many stairs, so wear comfortable shoes. Check service times if you want to attend or avoid them. Pair with Museum Island.

Practical Tips for Visiting Berlin

  • Transport: Buy a Berlin WelcomeCard or day passes for the U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram, and bus network; validate tickets before travel.
  • Museum Pass: The 3-day Museum Pass covers many state museums and can save money if you plan to visit several.
  • Book ahead: Reserve timed tickets for the Reichstag dome, TV Tower, and major museums, especially in summer.
  • Mondays: Many museums close on Mondays—plan outdoor sights (Wall Memorial, East Side Gallery, Brandenburg Gate) for that day.
  • Currency and cards: Germany uses the euro; card acceptance is common but some small venues are cash-only, so carry some cash.
  • Walking: Central attractions cluster near Mitte—wear comfortable shoes and combine nearby sights into walking routes.
  • Respectful visiting: Memorials are solemn sites; keep noise low and follow site rules.
  • Verify details: Prices and opening hours change seasonally and due to renovations—always confirm on official websites before your visit.
