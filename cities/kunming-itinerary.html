Kunming Travel Guide: The Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Kunming, the capital of Yunnan Province, is known as the "Spring City" for its mild year-round climate. Sitting at roughly 1,890 meters (6,200 feet) above sea level, it serves as a gateway to Yunnan's ethnic minority regions and offers a mix of scenic lakes, karst landscapes, temple gardens, and one of China's most famous food markets. This 3-day itinerary balances the city's highlights with day trips, and includes restaurant suggestions and practical advice for a smooth visit.

Day 1: City Highlights and Local Culture

Time Activity Details
Morning Green Lake Park (Cuihu) Start early (7:00–9:00) to watch locals practice tai chi, dance, and play traditional instruments. The park is free and especially lively in the morning. Nearby is Yunnan University's old campus, worth a short stroll.
Late Morning Yunnan Provincial Museum Free entry (closed Mondays). Excellent exhibits on Dian Kingdom bronze artifacts and Yunnan's ethnic cultures. Allow 1.5–2 hours.
Afternoon Yuantong Temple Kunming's largest and oldest Buddhist temple (over 1,200 years old), with a sunken main hall and ponds. Entry is inexpensive (around ¥6). Combine with a walk through nearby Yuantong Hill Park to see the animals or gardens.
Evening Nanping Street & Jinmabiji Square Pedestrian shopping area with street food and modern malls. The Golden Horse and Jade Rooster archways (Jinmabiji) are a city landmark and photogenic at night.

Where to eat on Day 1:

  • Breakfast: Try guoqiao mixian (crossing-the-bridge rice noodles) at any local noodle shop near Green Lake — Jianxinyuan is a well-known chain.
  • Lunch: Yunnan Renjia (multiple locations) serves classic Yunnan dishes such as steam-pot chicken (qiguo ji) and wild mushroom stir-fries.
  • Dinner: Lao Dian Guo or a shiguo yu (stone-pot fish) restaurant near Nanping Street for a lively local dining experience.

Day 2: Stone Forest Day Trip

Time Activity Details
Early Morning Depart for Shilin (Stone Forest) About 85 km (53 mi) southeast of Kunming. Take a high-speed train from Kunming South Station to Shilin West (about 20–30 minutes), then a shuttle bus, or join a day tour. Leave by 7:30–8:00 to beat crowds.
Morning–Midday Stone Forest Scenic Area A UNESCO World Heritage karst landscape of limestone pillars. Entry around ¥130–175 depending on season. Wear sturdy shoes; paths are uneven. Allow 3–4 hours to explore the main and minor stone forests.
Afternoon Return to Kunming; rest or explore Return by mid-afternoon. Optional: visit the Yunnan Ethnic Village by Dianchi Lake, or simply rest and adjust to the altitude.
Evening Dianchi Lake & Haigeng Dam Sunset views over the lake with the Western Hills in the background. In winter (Nov–Mar), thousands of Siberian black-headed gulls gather here — a favorite local spectacle.

Where to eat on Day 2:

  • Lunch: Eat near the Stone Forest entrance — local restaurants serve rushi (Yunnan cheese) and wild vegetable dishes.
  • Dinner: Fuzhaolou near Dianchi for freshwater fish and Yunnan specialties, or the night market at Yuanxi Road for grilled skewers and snacks.

Day 3: Western Hills, Temples, and Markets

Time Activity Details
Morning Western Hills & Dragon Gate (Longmen) Take a cable car or hike up for panoramic views over Dianchi Lake. The Dragon Gate grottoes were carved into the cliff face by hand over centuries. Allow 3 hours including transport. Entry plus cable car roughly ¥70–100.
Afternoon Huating Temple & Nie'er Tomb On the way down, visit Huating Temple, one of the region's most important Buddhist temples, and the tomb of composer Nie'er (author of China's national anthem).
Late Afternoon Zhuanxin Market (Cuihua Street) Kunming's famous wet market — a sensory highlight with wild mushrooms (seasonal), cured meats, flowers, and exotic produce. Great for photos and souvenirs.
Evening Farewell dinner & Guandu Old Town (optional) Guandu Old Town, south of the city, has a restored temple complex and snack streets. Alternatively, enjoy a relaxed dinner downtown.

Where to eat on Day 3:

  • Breakfast: Erkuai (rice cake) stalls — grilled or in soup — are a Kunming staple.
  • Lunch: Dianchi Road area restaurants serving guoqiao mixian and Yunnan ham dishes.
  • Dinner: Yunnan Wild Mushroom hotpot — try Yexianggu or similar mushroom-hotpot restaurants (book ahead in summer mushroom season, June–September).

Practical Tips

  • Altitude: At ~1,890 m, most visitors are fine, but take it easy on Day 1, stay hydrated, and avoid heavy alcohol initially.
  • Climate: Mild year-round, but UV is strong — bring sunscreen and a hat. Winters are dry and sunny; summers bring afternoon showers. Layer clothing for cool mornings and evenings.
  • Transport: The metro is clean, cheap, and covers most sights (Lines 1–6). Use ride-hailing apps (DiDi) or taxis; agree on meter use. For Stone Forest, the high-speed train is fastest.
  • Language: English is limited outside hotels and major attractions. Have a translation app and your destinations written in Chinese characters.
  • Money: Mobile payments (WeChat Pay/Alipay) dominate; set these up in advance if possible. Carry some cash for small vendors and markets.
  • Mushroom season: Yunnan's wild mushrooms (June–September)
