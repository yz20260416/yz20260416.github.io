Phuket: A Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Phuket is Thailand's largest island, blending powder-white west-coast beaches, dramatic limestone scenery, a UNESCO-listed old town, and some of the country's best seafood. Three days is enough to sample the highlights if you plan around the island's geography: the busy west coast (Patong, Kata, Karon), the quieter north (Bang Tao, Nai Yang), and the historic east-coast capital, Phuket Town. This itinerary balances beach time, culture, and a boat trip, with practical logistics for each day.

Day 1 — West Coast Beaches and Patong

TimePlan
Morning Start at Kata Beach or Karon Beach for a swim and breakfast at a beachfront café. Kata Noi, just south of Kata, is smaller and calmer. If you want a viewpoint, drive to Karon Viewpoint (also called Kata Viewpoint) for a panorama of three bays.
Afternoon Move to Patong Beach. Swim, then walk Bangla Road (quiet by day) and the beachfront promenade. Optional: a Thai massage or a beginner surf lesson at Patong or Kata (seasonal, roughly May–October).
Evening Sunset at Promthep Cape (about 20–30 minutes south of Kata) — arrive 45 minutes before sunset for a good spot. Dinner back in Kata or Patong.

Restaurant recommendations (Day 1):

  • The Boathouse (Kata) — seafood and Thai classics with sea views; mid-to-upper price.
  • Mom Tri's Kitchen (Kata Noi) — refined Thai and international dishes, ocean-facing terrace.
  • Joe's Downstairs (Kalim, near Patong) — sunset cocktails and modern seafood.
  • Street food on Thanon Ratuthit (Patong) — cheap pad thai, satay, and mango sticky rice.

Day 2 — Island Hopping (Phi Phi or Phang Nga Bay)

TimePlan
Morning Early pickup (around 7:00–8:00) for a speedboat or longtail tour. Choose Phi Phi Islands for beaches and snorkeling (Maya Bay, Bamboo Island) or Phang Nga Bay for limestone karsts, James Bond Island, and sea caves (kayaking). Book through a reputable operator and confirm national park fees are included.
Afternoon Continue the tour with snorkeling stops and lunch (usually included). Most tours return to the pier between 16:00 and 17:30.
Evening Rest and recover. Dinner near your hotel or explore Phuket Town's night market if you're staying east.

Restaurant recommendations (Day 2):

  • Blue Elephant (Phuket Town) — Thai fine dining in a restored colonial mansion; book ahead.
  • Raya Restaurant (Phuket Town) — beloved local spot for southern Thai dishes like crab curry.
  • One Chun (Phuket Town) — casual, well-regarded southern Thai cuisine.
  • Mor Mu Dong (east coast) — rustic seafood in a mangrove setting; a local favorite.

Day 3 — Phuket Old Town and the North

TimePlan
Morning Explore Phuket Old Town: Sino-Portuguese shophouses along Thalang Road, Soi Romanee, and Dibuk Road. Visit Wat Chalong (the island's most important temple) and, optionally, the Big Buddha on Nakkerd Hill for views (check opening status, as access has been periodically restricted).
Afternoon Drive north to Bang Tao or Nai Yang for a quieter beach afternoon. Nai Yang borders Sirinat National Park. Grab lunch at a local spot along the way.
Evening Final dinner — either a beachfront seafood meal in the north or a return to Phuket Town for the Sunday Walking Street market on Thalang Road (Sundays only).

Restaurant recommendations (Day 3):

  • Kopitiam by Wilai (Phuket Town) — Hainanese-style coffee shop dishes and local classics.
  • Tu Kab Khao (Phuket Town) — southern Thai in a heritage building.
  • The Beach Restaurant (Nai Yang) — relaxed seafood near the national park.
  • Bampot Kitchen & Bar (Bang Tao) — modern Thai and international plates.

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Phuket has no rail; options are Grab, metered taxis (agree fare first), songthaews, or a rental car/scooter. Distances are longer than they look — Patong to Phuket Town is roughly 45 minutes in traffic.
  • Best time to visit: November to April is the dry season; May to October is monsoon season with cheaper rates, occasional rough seas, and some tour cancellations. Always check sea conditions before boat trips.
  • Boat tours: Book with licensed operators, confirm national park fees, and expect early starts. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a dry bag, and seasickness tablets if prone.
  • Money: Carry cash for markets, songthaews, and small restaurants; ATMs charge fees. Major hotels and restaurants accept cards.
  • Etiquette: Dress modestly at temples (cover shoulders and knees), remove shoes before entering buildings, and never touch someone's head or point feet at people or Buddha images.
  • Safety: Swim only in flagged areas and heed rip-current warnings (the west coast can be dangerous in the monsoon season). Wear a helmet if riding a scooter and ensure your travel insurance covers it.
  • Health: Drink bottled or filtered water, use mosquito repellent at dusk, and keep hydrated — Phuket is hot and humid year-round.
  • Packing: Light clothing, a rain jacket (especially May–October), sandals, and a power adapter (Thailand uses 220V, type A/B/C outlets).
