Dublin Weather by Month

Dublin enjoys a temperate maritime climate, meaning winters are mild rather than freezing and summers are pleasantly warm without ever becoming truly hot. The city sits on Ireland's east coast, sheltered from the worst Atlantic storms, but it still sees its fair share of rain — the locals will tell you that a "soft day" (light drizzle) is simply part of life here. Rainfall is fairly evenly distributed across the year, with no true dry season, and temperatures rarely dip below freezing or climb above 20°C (68°F). This makes Dublin a year-round destination, though the experience varies considerably depending on when you visit. Seasonal Overview

  • Spring (March–May): Gradually warming, with longer days and blooming parks. March can still feel chilly and blustery, but by May the city is lively and outdoor cafés start to fill up. Rainfall stays moderate.
  • Summer (June–August): The warmest and driest months, though "warm" here means highs around 19°C (66°F). Long daylight hours (up to 17 hours in June and July) make this ideal for sightseeing and day trips. August is the wettest summer month and coincides with peak tourist season.
  • Autumn (September–November): September is often a sweet spot — still mild, fewer crowds, and comfortable temperatures. October and November turn wetter and cooler, with falling leaves adding charm to the city's parks.
  • Winter (December–February): Mild but dark, with only about 8 hours of daylight around the solstice. Rain is frequent but snow is rare and rarely accumulates. Christmas markets and cozy pubs give the city a festive atmosphere.

Best Months to Visit

May, June, and September are generally considered the best months to visit Dublin. May and June offer the longest days, mild temperatures, and manageable crowds before the peak summer rush. September combines pleasant weather with fewer tourists and lower accommodation prices. July and August are the warmest and most festive months (with events like the Dublin Horse Show and various summer festivals), but they're also the busiest and priciest. October through February is best for budget travelers and those seeking a quieter, more atmospheric Dublin — just pack for rain and short days.

Practical Tips for Dublin Weather

  • Always carry a rain jacket or umbrella. Rain can appear and disappear within minutes, and locals rarely bother with umbrellas in light drizzle. A waterproof layer with a hood is more practical than an umbrella on windy days.
  • Dress in layers. Temperatures can shift throughout the day, and the wind off the Liffey can feel sharper than the thermometer suggests. A light sweater plus a waterproof outer layer works year-round.
  • Don't trust the forecast too far ahead. Irish weather is notoriously changeable; a five-day forecast is about as reliable as it gets. Check conditions each morning.
  • Pack comfortable, waterproof shoes. Cobblestones and occasional puddles make sturdy footwear essential, especially if you plan to walk a lot (which you should — Dublin is very walkable).
  • Book summer accommodation early. July and August see the highest demand and prices; reserving months in advance can save significantly.
  • Embrace indoor attractions on rainy days. Museums like the National Museum of Ireland, the Guinness Storehouse, and Trinity College's Book of Kells are perfect rainy-day options — and many are free.
  • Watch for wind. Coastal Dublin can be breezy, particularly along the bay in Clontarf and Howth. A windproof layer makes waterfront walks far more enjoyable.
  • Sunset times vary dramatically. In June, it stays light until nearly 11 p.m., while December sunsets arrive before 4:30 p.m. Plan outdoor activities accordingly.
