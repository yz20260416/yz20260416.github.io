Top Attractions in Vancouver

Vancouver blends dramatic Pacific Northwest scenery with vibrant urban neighbourhoods, and its best attractions showcase both. From the sail-shaped roofs of Canada Place to the classical gardens of Chinatown and the rainforest trails of Stanley Park, the city rewards visitors who explore on foot and by water. This guide highlights ten standout sights drawn from Vancouver's rich cultural and natural landscape, ideal for an international visitor's itinerary.

  1. Stanley Park — One of the city's most popular attractions for tourists and locals alike, drawing an estimated eight million visitors every year, with a wealth of statues, monuments, and landmarks including the famous Hollow Tree western red cedar stump. Tip: Rent a bike to ride the seawall loop; go early morning on weekdays to avoid crowds.
  2. Canada Place — This Burrard Inlet landmark, with fabric roofs resembling sails, houses the Vancouver Convention Centre East Building, the Pan Pacific Hotel, and Flyover Vancouver, and serves as the main cruise terminal where Alaska cruises originate. Tip: Walk the promenade at sunset for harbour and mountain views; it's free and open to the public.
  3. Granville Island — A peninsula and shopping district in the Fairview neighbourhood across False Creek from Downtown, once an industrial manufacturing area and now home to a public market, restaurants, a marina, and a hotel. Tip: Arrive by the tiny Aquabus ferry from downtown; the public market is best on weekday mornings.
  4. Gastown — The original settlement that became the core of Vancouver, now a national historic site and neighbourhood in the northwest section of the Downtown Eastside, adjacent to Downtown Vancouver. Tip: Visit in the afternoon for the steam clock and cobblestone streets; pair with nearby Chinatown.
  5. Chinatown — Canada's largest Chinatown, centred around Pender Street and surrounded by Gastown, the downtown business districts, and Strathcona, reflecting Vancouver's large ethnic Chinese community. Tip: Go daytime for markets and heritage buildings; the Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Garden is a short walk away.
  6. Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Classical Chinese Garden — Located in Chinatown at 578 Carrall Street, this was the first Chinese garden built outside of Asia, comprising a free public park and a ticketed garden whose mandate is to bridge Chinese and western cultures. Tip: Book a guided tour for the Ming-style architecture; the free park side is open without admission.
  7. Bloedel Conservatory — A domed conservatory and aviary at the top of Queen Elizabeth Park, opened in 1969, whose triodetic dome houses around 500 plant species and more than 200 birds. Tip: Combine with the Queen Elizabeth Park viewpoint; small admission fee applies.
  8. Harbour Centre (The Lookout) — This 1977 downtown skyscraper's "Lookout" tower offers a 360-degree viewing deck and the Top of Vancouver revolving restaurant, giving an unobstructed view of the city. Tip: Go near dusk to see both daylight and city lights; tickets available at the base.
  9. False Creek — A short narrow inlet in the heart of Vancouver separating Downtown and the West End from the rest of the city, with Granville Island located within it. Tip: Ride the Aquabus or walk the seawall between Yaletown and Olympic Village for waterfront views.
  10. Digital Orca — A 2009 powder-coated aluminium sculpture of a killer whale by Douglas Coupland, installed next to the Vancouver Convention Centre and owned by Pavco. Tip: A quick photo stop near Canada Place; combine with a harbourfront walk.

Practical tips

  • Many waterfront sights (Canada Place, False Creek seawall, Stanley Park) are free; budget for ticketed spots like the Bloedel Conservatory, Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Garden, and Harbour Centre Lookout.
  • Use the Aquabus or False Creek Ferries to link Granville Island, Yaletown, and Olympic Village—faster and more scenic than backtracking on foot.
  • Vancouver's weather is changeable: carry a light rain layer even in summer, and save indoor attractions like the conservatory for wet afternoons.
  • Cluster your sightseeing: Gastown, Chinatown, and the Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Garden are walkable from each other, as are Canada Place and Digital Orca.
  • Visit popular spots early in the day on weekdays; Stanley Park and Granville Island get busiest on sunny weekends.
