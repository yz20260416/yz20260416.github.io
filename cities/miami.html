Local Character

Miami takes its name from the Miami River, derived from Mayaimi, the historic name of Lake Okeechobee and the Native Americans who lived around it, and locals still call the city 'The 305' after its original area code — a shorthand that signals belonging. The Tequesta tribe occupied the area for roughly 2,000 years, with a village of hundreds dating to 500–600 BCE at the mouth of the Miami River; a visitor standing where that settlement stood gains a sense of the city's deep roots beneath the skyline. Downtown holds one of the largest concentrations of international banks in the U.S., and PortMiami is the world's busiest cruise port by passenger traffic and cruise lines, so harbor activity is part of daily life. With 70.2 percent of residents identifying as Hispanic or Latino, the city is often called the 'Gateway to Latin America,' a character felt in its food, media, and street life.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. PortMiami — The world's busiest cruise port by passenger traffic and cruise lines, offering departures and harbor views.
  2. Downtown Miami — Home to one of the largest concentrations of international banks in the U.S. and over 300 high-rises.
  3. Health District — The medical hub housing Jackson Memorial Hospital, the nation's largest hospital with 1,547 beds.
  4. Miami River mouth — Site of a Tequesta village dating to 500–600 BCE, grounding the city in thousands of years of habitation.
  5. University of Miami campus area — Anchored by the Miller School of Medicine, the university's academic medical center and teaching hospital.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Miami?
January through March offers dry, mild weather and is rated Excellent, though crowds are high.
Why is Miami called the Gateway to Latin America?
Its strong commercial and cultural ties to Latin America and a 70.2 percent Hispanic and Latino population earned it that nickname.
How many high-rises does Miami have?
Miami has the third-largest skyline in the U.S., with over 300 high-rises, 70 of which exceed 492 ft (150 m).
