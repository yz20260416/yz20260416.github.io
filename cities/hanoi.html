Best Time to Visit Hanoi

Hanoi, Vietnam's thousand-year-old capital, blends French colonial boulevards, ancient temples and a buzzing Old Quarter street-food scene. Visit in autumn (October–November) or spring (March–April) for mild temperatures and clear skies, avoiding the hot, humid summer and chilly, damp winter.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple — A serene lake at the heart of the Old Quarter, linked to a small island temple by a red wooden bridge.
  2. Old Quarter — A maze of narrow streets organised by traditional trades, packed with street food, markets and cafés.
  3. Temple of Literature — Vietnam's first university, a tranquil Confucian complex of courtyards, pavilions and stone stelae.
  4. Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum and Ba Dinh Square — The imposing granite mausoleum where the nation's founder lies in state, surrounded by formal gardens.
  5. Imperial Citadel of Thang Long — A UNESCO-listed complex of archaeological remains and gates revealing Hanoi's royal history.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Vietnamese Dong (VND, ₫)
  • Language: Vietnamese (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: ICT (UTC+7)
  • Visa: Many nationalities can enter Vietnam visa-free for short stays, while others need an e-visa or visa obtained in advance.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Hanoi?
Three to four days covers the main sights and street-food scene, with extra time for day trips to Halong Bay or Ninh Binh.
How do I get around Hanoi?
Ride-hailing apps like Grab, taxis and cyclos are convenient, and the compact Old Quarter is best explored on foot.
Is Hanoi safe for travellers?
Hanoi is generally safe, though petty theft and traffic require care, especially when crossing streets and in crowded markets.
