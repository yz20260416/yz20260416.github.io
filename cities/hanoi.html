Best Time to Visit Hanoi

Hanoi is Vietnam's capital, a 1,000-year-old city on the Red River whose layered past is visible in its imperial citadel, French colonial boulevards, and tube-house alleys. Visit in autumn (October–November) or spring (March–April) for the most comfortable weather.

Local Character

Hanoi has carried many names, and each one reveals a piece of its character. It was first called Long Biên ('dragons interweaving') and later Long Đỗ ('dragon belly') — names tied to the Red River's curved formation around the city, which was symbolized as a dragon; the colonial-era Long Biên Bridge still carries that ancient name today, so a walk across it connects you to the city's earliest identity. In 1010, Emperor Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital here and named it Thăng Long ('ascending dragon'), the name that endured for centuries and still appears on the city's ceremonial gates and museum signage. The city's built landscape reflects its layered history: pre-colonial Vietnamese architecture, French colonial buildings, brutalist apartment blocks, and the narrow tube houses that line its disorganized alleys, all stemming from 20th-century growth — wandering these streets is the best way to read Hanoi's history without a guidebook. When the Nguyễn dynasty moved the capital to Huế in 1802, Hanoi was renamed in 1831, and the city later served as the capital of French Indochina from 1902 to 1945, which explains the wide, tree-lined boulevards and grand villas of the French Quarter. The city's many names — Long Biên, Đại La, Thăng Long, Đông Kinh, and finally Hanoi — are a reminder that this is a place shaped by successive dynasties rather than a single founding moment.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long — A UNESCO-listed complex of royal palaces and gates that served as the seat of Vietnamese emperors for centuries.
  2. Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum — A solemn granite monument where the preserved body of Ho Chi Minh lies in state, set beside the historic Ba Đình Square.
  3. Hoàn Kiếm Lake — The city's central lake, linked to the legend of the returned sword, with the red Húc Bridge leading to Ngọc Sơn Temple.
  4. West Lake — Hanoi's largest lake, ringed by temples, cafés, and the historic Trấn Quốc Pagoda.
  5. Long Biên Bridge — A French-colonial-era steel cantilever bridge over the Red River that carries the name of Hanoi's earliest recorded identity.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Hanoi?

What is Hanoi's old name and why does it matter?
Hanoi was known as Thăng Long ('ascending dragon') from 1010, and the name survives in landmarks like the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long.
