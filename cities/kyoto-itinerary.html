Kyoto in 3 Days: A Complete Itinerary

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, and its 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines, and preserved wooden machiya townhouses make it one of the world's great historic cities. This three-day itinerary balances headline sights with quieter neighborhoods, organized by morning, afternoon, and evening so you can move efficiently across the city. Kyoto's sights are spread across distinct districts, so each day clusters attractions by area to minimize transit. Book popular temples and experiences in advance, and expect crowds at the famous spots—early mornings are your best ally.

Day 1: Eastern Kyoto — Southern Higashiyama and Gion

TimePlanNotes
Morning Kiyomizu-dera Temple → Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lanes → Yasaka Shrine Arrive at Kiyomizu-dera by 7:00–8:00 a.m. to beat tour groups. The temple opens at 6:00 a.m. Walk downhill through the preserved stone-paved lanes, stopping for matcha and sweets.
Afternoon Kodai-ji Temple → Kennin-ji Temple → walk through Gion Kodai-ji's bamboo grove and raked gardens are serene; Kennin-ji, founded in 1202, is Kyoto's oldest Zen temple. Lunch near Gion or on Hanamikoji's side streets.
Evening Gion evening stroll → dinner in Pontocho Alley or Gion Spot geiko and maiko heading to appointments around 5:30–6:30 p.m. Do not photograph them without permission—it is prohibited on many private lanes.

Restaurant recommendations: Lunch at Omen (udon, near Kodai-ji) or Hisago (oyako-don, near Nanzen-ji side). Dinner at Gion Karyo (kaiseki, reserve ahead) or a casual yakitori spot in Pontocho. For budget travelers, Nishiki Market stalls near Shijo-Kawaramachi work well for a late lunch.

Day 2: Western Kyoto — Arashiyama and Kinkaku-ji

TimePlanNotes
Morning Arashiyama Bamboo Grove → Tenryu-ji Temple → Okochi Sanso Villa Take the JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama (about 15 minutes from Kyoto Station). The bamboo grove is best before 8:00 a.m. Tenryu-ji's Sogenchi Garden is a UNESCO World Heritage site; Okochi Sanso includes matcha with admission.
Afternoon Togetsukyo Bridge → optional Sagano Romantic Train or boat ride → Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion) Bus or taxi from Arashiyama to Kinkaku-ji takes 30–40 minutes. Kinkaku-ji is open 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.; late afternoon light is beautiful but crowds remain heavy.
Evening Dinner near Kyoto Station or in the Kiyamachi area Rest after a full day; Kyoto Station's Ramen Koji (10th floor, Isetan side) offers a fun casual dinner.

Restaurant recommendations: Lunch at Shigetsu (Buddhist vegetarian shojin ryori inside Tenryu-ji; reserve) or Arashiyama Yoshimura (soba with river views). Dinner at Ippodo tea room for a lighter option, or Kichi Kichi Omurice near Pontocho (reservations essential).

Day 3: Central and Northern Kyoto — Fushimi Inari, Nijo Castle, and Kinkaku alternatives

TimePlanNotes
Morning Fushimi Inari Taisha (early) → Tofuku-ji Temple Fushimi Inari is open 24 hours and free. Start by 7:00 a.m. to hike the torii tunnels in relative quiet; the full loop to the summit takes 2–3 hours. Tofuku-ji is two stops away and famous for its autumn maples and Zen gardens.
Afternoon Nijo Castle → Nishiki Market → Kyoto Imperial Palace (optional) Nijo Castle (UNESCO) showcases the shogun's palace with "nightingale floors." Nishiki Market is a 400-year-old covered food street—go hungry and try tamagoyaki, tsukemono, and yuba.
Evening Kiyamachi and Pontocho riverside walk → farewell dinner If energy permits, swap in a tea ceremony experience (many studios near Gion offer 45–60 minute sessions).

Restaurant recommendations: Breakfast or brunch near Fushimi at Vermillion Cafe (near the shrine entrance). Lunch at Nishiki Market stalls or Nishiki Warai (okonomiyaki). Farewell dinner at Kikunoi Roan (accessible kaiseki) or Menbaka Fire Ramen for a memorable, theatrical bowl.

Practical Tips

  • Transport: Kyoto's buses reach most sights but are slow and crowded. Combine the subway, JR lines, and taxis for efficiency. An IC card (ICOCA or Suica) works on buses, subways, and trains. A one-day bus pass (¥700) pays off only if you take three or more bus rides.
  • Timing: Major temples open between 8:00 and 9:00 a.m. and close between 4:30 and 5:00 p.m. (some later in peak season). Plan temple visits for mornings and evenings for dining and neighborhoods.
  • Reservations: Kaiseki restaurants and popular spots like Kichi Kichi require advance booking, often weeks ahead. Ryokan and machiya stays book out months in advance during cherry blossom (late March–early April) and autumn foliage (mid-November–early December) seasons.
  • Etiquette: Speak quietly at temples, remove shoes where indicated, and never photograph geiko/maiko without permission. Photography is restricted in some temple halls.
  • Money: Many small restaurants and market stalls are cash-only. Carry yen; 7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs accept foreign cards.
  • Weather: Summers are hot and humid—carry water and start early. Winters are cold but quiet, with fewer crowds and occasional snow on temple roofs.
  • Luggage: Use coin lockers at Kyoto Station or luggage-forwarding services (takkyubin) between hotels to travel light.
  • Day trips: Nara (45 minutes by train) and Osaka (30 minutes by shinkansen or rapid train) are easy half-day extensions if you have a fourth day.
