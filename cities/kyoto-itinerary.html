Kyoto 3-Day Itinerary

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, and today it remains the country's cultural heart—home to more than 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines, traditional wooden machiya townhouses, and some of the finest cuisine in Japan. Three days is enough to cover the essentials if you plan carefully, grouping sights by district to minimize travel time and starting early to beat the crowds at headline attractions. This itinerary balances marquee sights (Fushimi Inari, Kinkaku-ji, Arashiyama Bamboo Grove) with quieter neighborhoods, gardens, and food experiences that reveal the city's deeper character. It assumes you're staying centrally—downtown Kawaramachi or near Kyoto Station—and using buses, trains, and taxis rather than a rental car.

Day 1: Eastern Kyoto — Southern Higashiyama & Gion

Time Activity Notes
7:00–9:00 Fushimi Inari Taisha Arrive before 8:00 to walk the torii gate tunnels in near-silence. Hike at least to the Yotsutsuji intersection (about 30–40 minutes up) for city views. Free entry; open 24 hours.
9:30–11:00 Kiyomizu-dera Take the Keihan line to Kiyomizu-Gojo, then walk up the preserved Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lanes. The temple's wooden stage juts over the hillside; the Otowa Waterfall below offers its three-stream "wish" ritual. Entry ¥500.
11:15–12:00 Kodai-ji & Yasaka Shrine Kodai-ji's Zen gardens are a tranquil counterpoint to Kiyomizu's crowds. Continue north through Maruyama Park to Yasaka Shrine, Gion's guardian shrine.
12:15–13:30 Lunch: Gion Karyo (or Omen near Kodai-ji) Karyo serves refined kaiseki-style set lunches in Gion from around ¥3,000–5,000. For a budget option, Omen's udon with sesame dipping sauce is a Kyoto institution (¥1,500–2,500).
13:45–15:00 Nanzen-ji & the Philosopher's Path Nanzen-ji's massive Sanmon gate and the brick Meiji-era aqueduct are highlights. Walk north along the canal-side Philosopher's Path toward Ginkaku-ji.
15:00–16:00 Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion) Despite the name, the pavilion was never silvered—its beauty lies in the moss garden and raked-sand cone. Entry ¥500. Closes at 17:00.
16:30–18:00 Gion walk & Hanamikoji Street Stroll the lantern-lit lanes of Gion. Respect signage—many alleys are private. Maiko sightings are most likely around 17:30–18:30.
18:30–20:30 Dinner: Giro Giro Hitoshina (or Kikunoi Roan) Giro Giro offers inventive, affordable kaiseki courses (~¥6,000) in a lively setting near Kawaramachi. Kikunoi Roan is a more formal counter option (~¥12,000+). Book ahead either way.

Day 2: Western Kyoto — Arashiyama & Kinkaku-ji

Time Activity Notes
7:30–9:00 Arashiyama Bamboo Grove & Tenryu-ji Take the JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama. The bamboo path is magical before 8:30. Tenryu-ji's Sogenchi Garden (a UNESCO site) opens at 8:30; entry ¥500.
9:15–10:30 Okochi Sanso Villa A lesser-known hillside estate with panoramic views and a matcha included in the ¥1,000 admission. A peaceful alternative to the crowded grove.
10:45–12:00 Togetsukyo Bridge & riverside Walk the bridge and the Katsura River banks. Optional: the Sagano Romantic Train (book ahead) or a short boat ride.
12:00–13:15 Lunch: Shigetsu (inside Tenryu-ji) or Arashiyama Yoshimura Shigetsu serves shojin ryori (Buddhist vegetarian) in a temple setting (¥4,000–7,000). Yoshimura offers soba with river views (~¥1,500–2,500).
13:45–15:00 Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion) Take a taxi or the Randen tram + bus. The gold-leaf pavilion reflected in the mirror pond is Kyoto's most photographed sight. Entry ¥500; the one-way route takes 30–45 minutes.
15:15–16:15 Ryoan-ji A short bus ride from Kinkaku-ji. The famous rock garden rewards slow, quiet contemplation. Entry ¥600.
16:30–17:30 Nishijin textile district or return to center Optional stop at the Nishijin Textile Center for kimono shows, or head back to rest before dinner.
18:30–20:30 Dinner: Izakaya in Pontocho Alley Pontocho's narrow lane is lined with restaurants. Try Yakitori Ichidai or Kyoto Gogyo (famous burnt-miso ramen, ~¥1,200). Reservations recommended for sit-down spots.

Day 3: Central Kyoto — Castles, Markets & Craft

Time Activity Notes
8:30–10:00 Nijo Castle The shogun's Kyoto residence, with "nightingale floors" that chirp underfoot and stunning screen paintings. Entry ¥1,300 (incl. Ninomaru Palace). Allow 90 minutes
