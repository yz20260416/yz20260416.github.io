Best Time to Visit Petra

Petra is the ancient Nabataean capital carved into rose-red sandstone cliffs in southern Jordan, reached through the iconic Siq gorge to the Treasury and beyond to tombs, temples and a Roman-style theatre. Visit in spring or autumn for mild walking weather, and arrive at opening time to experience the site before tour groups arrive.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. The Siq — A narrow, winding sandstone gorge that serves as the dramatic main entrance to the ancient city.
  2. Al-Khazneh (The Treasury) — Petra's most famous monument, a towering Hellenistic-style facade carved directly into the pink cliff face.
  3. The Monastery (Ad Deir) — A vast carved facade reached by a climb of roughly 800 steps, rewarding visitors with sweeping valley views.
  4. The Royal Tombs — A row of monumental Nabataean tomb facades, including the Urn Tomb, cut high into the eastern cliffs.
  5. Petra by Night — A candlelit evening walk through the Siq to the Treasury, accompanied by Bedouin music and storytelling.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Jordanian Dinar (JOD)
  • Language: Arabic (English is widely spoken in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: EET (UTC+2), with daylight saving in summer
  • Visa: Many nationalities can obtain a Jordan Pass, which waives the visa fee and includes Petra entry, while other visitors can get a visa on arrival or apply in advance.

FAQ

How many days do I need to see Petra?
Two full days are ideal: one for the main trail to the Treasury and Monastery, and a second for the Royal Tombs, High Place of Sacrifice and lesser-visited areas.
What is the best time of day to visit?
Arrive at opening around 6am to beat the crowds and heat, and stay for late-afternoon light on the Treasury facades.
How do I get to Petra?
Most visitors fly into Amman or Aqaba and travel by car or bus to Wadi Musa, the town adjoining the archaeological site, roughly three hours from Amman.
