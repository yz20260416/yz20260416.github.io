Local Character

Petra's inhabitants originally called it Raqmu, a name that reflects its Nabataean roots and reminds visitors that today's iconic Greek-derived name is a later overlay. The Nabataeans, a nomadic Arab people, settled here in the 4th century BC and turned the city into a thriving trading hub by leveraging its position near incense routes. Their genius is evident in the elaborate rainwater collection and desert farming systems that sustained a population of up to 20,000 at its peak. A visit to the site's water channels and cisterns offers a glimpse into how this civilization mastered an arid environment. Another detail often missed is that by 7000 BC, some of the earliest recorded farmers had already settled at Beidha, just north of Petra, making the area one of the earliest agricultural settlements in the region. This deep history adds layers to Petra beyond its famous facades, showing a continuity of human ingenuity in the desert.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Al-Khazneh (The Treasury) — A magnificent rock-cut facade, possibly the mausoleum of Nabataean king Aretas IV, carved into the rose-red sandstone.
  2. The Siq — A narrow gorge that serves as the dramatic main entrance to Petra, leading visitors between towering rock walls.
  3. Umm el-Biyara — A mountain top with an Edomite site established no earlier than the seventh century BC, offering panoramic views.
  4. Beidha — A Pre-Pottery Neolithic settlement just north of Petra, where some of the earliest recorded farmers settled by 7000 BC.
  5. Petra's Water Conduit System — An ancient network of channels and cisterns that allowed the Nabataeans to thrive in the desert.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Petra?
Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) offer pleasant temperatures and moderate crowds, making them ideal for exploring the site.
How much time should I allocate for visiting Petra?
At least two full days are recommended to cover the main attractions, including the Siq, Al-Khazneh, and the monastery, as well as hikes to lesser-known sites.
Is Petra accessible for people with mobility issues?
The main trail through the Siq involves uneven terrain and some stairs, but options like horse-drawn carriages and guided tours can help visitors with limited mobility; however, many areas remain challenging.
