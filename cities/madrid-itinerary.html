Madrid in Three Days: A Complete Itinerary

Madrid rewards travelers who pace themselves. The Spanish capital combines world-class art museums, royal palaces, lively plazas, and a dining culture built around long lunches and late dinners. This three-day itinerary balances the essential sights with neighborhood exploration, structured as morning, afternoon, and evening blocks. Distances in the historic center are walkable, and the city's metro system connects everything else efficiently.

Day 1: Historic Madrid and the Art Triangle

Time Activity Notes
Morning Puerta del Sol, Plaza Mayor, and the Royal Palace Start at Puerta del Sol (Kilometer Zero), walk to Plaza Mayor, then tour the Royal Palace (book tickets online; closed during official ceremonies).
Afternoon Lunch near Plaza de Oriente, then the Prado Museum Reserve a Prado timed entry. Focus on Velázquez, Goya, and El Greco; the museum is vast, so pick highlights.
Evening Tapas crawl in La Latina Cava Baja and Cava Alta streets are lined with bars. Eat standing at the bar like locals; dinner typically starts after 9 pm.

Restaurant picks: Casa Lucio (La Latina, famous for huevos estrellados), Botín (claimed as the world's oldest restaurant, roast suckling pig), and Mercado de San Miguel for a standing lunch of tapas and vermouth.

Day 2: Art, Parks, and Retiro

Time Activity Notes
Morning Reina Sofía Museum and Atocha area See Picasso's Guernica and works by Dalí and Miró. The museum opens at 10 am; arrive early to avoid queues.
Afternoon Lunch in Huertas, then Retiro Park and the Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum Stroll the Retiro's Crystal Palace and row on the lake. The Thyssen complements the Prado with European masters.
Evening Sunset at the Temple of Debod, dinner in Chamberí or Malasaña The Egyptian temple offers free entry and west-facing views. Malasaña has a young, lively dining scene.

Restaurant picks: Taberna El Sur (Huertas, Spanish classics), Juana la Loca (La Latina, creative tapas), and Calle Ponzano in Chamberí for a modern tapas-bar strip.

Day 3: Neighborhoods and Local Life

Time Activity Notes
Morning El Rastro flea market (Sundays) or Malasaña and Chueca walking tour El Rastro runs Sunday mornings along Calle Ribera de Curtidores. On other days, explore Malasaña's Plaza del Dos de Mayo and Chueca's streets.
Afternoon Lunch, then the Sorolla Museum or a day trip option The Sorolla Museum (former home of painter Joaquín Sorolla) is intimate and often overlooked. Alternatively, take a half-day trip to Toledo or Segovia by train.
Evening Flamenco show and late dinner Book flamenco at Corral de la Morería or Cardamomo in advance. Dinner afterward is normal; kitchens stay open late.

Restaurant picks: Mercado de San Antón (Chueca, rooftop terrace), La Musa (Malasaña), and Sobrino de Botín or Casa Lucio for a final traditional meal.

Practical Tips

  • Timing: Spaniards eat lunch around 2–3 pm and dinner after 9 pm. Many shops close for midday siesta, especially smaller ones.
  • Museum tickets: Book Prado, Reina Sofía, and Royal Palace tickets online to skip queues. Free-entry windows exist (e.g., Prado evenings), but expect crowds.
  • Transport: The metro is fast and cheap; buy a Multi Card (tarjeta multi) for reloadable fares. Central Madrid is best explored on foot.
  • Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas like Puerta del Sol, the metro, and El Rastro.
  • Language: English is common in tourist areas, but basic Spanish phrases are appreciated.
  • Weather: Summers are hot (July–August often exceeds 35°C/95°F); spring and autumn are ideal. Winters are cool but mild.
  • Money: Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for small bars and markets.
