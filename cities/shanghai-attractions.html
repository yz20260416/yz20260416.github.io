Top Attractions in Shanghai

Shanghai blends historic riverfront architecture, ancient temples, futuristic skyscrapers, and vibrant art districts into one of Asia's most dynamic cities. From the colonial-era Bund to the soaring Jin Mao Tower in Pudong, the city offers something for every kind of traveler. This guide highlights ten standout attractions likely to appeal to international visitors, each drawn from verified sources and paired with practical advice for planning your visit.

  1. The Bund and Chen Yi Square — The Bund is Shanghai's iconic waterfront promenade, and Chen Yi Square along it features the only bronze sculpture of Chen Yi, the city's first communist mayor. The area is the classic spot for views of the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River.
    Tip: Visit around sunset or after dark when the skyline lights up; arrive early on weekends to avoid crowds.
  2. Jin Mao Tower — This 420.5-meter, 88-story landmark skyscraper in Lujiazui, Pudong, contains a shopping mall, offices, and the Grand Hyatt Shanghai hotel starting on the 53rd floor. At completion it was among the tallest buildings in the world.
    Tip: Head to the observation deck on a clear day for sweeping views; combine with a stroll through Lujiazui's riverside parks.
  3. City God Temple of Shanghai — This Taoist and folk religion temple in the old city commemorates Shanghai's elevation to municipal status and honors three figures venerated as the city's gods. Locals often call it the "Old City God Temple."
    Tip: Pair your visit with the surrounding Yuyuan Garden bazaar area; go early morning for a quieter experience.
  4. Confucian Temple of Shanghai — A folk Confucian temple in the old city, modeled on the original temple in Qufu and the Beijing temple but on a smaller scale, located on Wenmiao Road in Huangpu District. The surrounding Wenmiao Market hosts a temple fair every Sunday morning.
    Tip: Time your visit for a Sunday to browse the market; mornings are coolest and least crowded.
  5. 50 Moganshan Road (M50) — This contemporary art district houses a community of more than a hundred artists whose studios are open to the public, often compared to New York's SoHo and Beijing's 798 Art Zone. Time named it among the top ten things to do in Shanghai.
    Tip: Go on a weekday afternoon when galleries are open and quieter; many studios close on Mondays.
  6. Bund Sightseeing Tunnel — This tunnel connects the Shanghai Bund with Pudong and houses a cable-hauled passenger rail line, offering a novel way to cross the Huangpu River. It's a quirky, family-friendly alternative to the ferry or metro.
    Tip: Ride it one way and return via the Bund waterfront walk for the best of both experiences.
  7. Duolun Road — This historic street in Hongkou District, formerly Darroch Road, is lined with heritage architecture and cultural sites. It's a pleasant area for exploring Shanghai's literary and republican-era history.
    Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby Hongkou area; weekday mornings are calmest for photography.
  8. Huaihai Road — One of central Shanghai's two major shopping streets (alongside Nanjing Road), Huaihai Road is more upscale and favored by local residents, with its main section in the former French Concession. It's ideal for fashion, dining, and people-watching.
    Tip: Explore the leafy side streets of the former French Concession nearby for cafés and boutiques.
  9. Disneytown at Shanghai Disney Resort — This shopping, dining, and entertainment complex at the Shanghai Disney Resort in Pudong consists of five districts, including Broadway Plaza and Lakeshore, and features a 3,000-square-meter World of Disney Store. It's the Shanghai equivalent of Downtown Disney.
    Tip: Visit in the evening for dining and entertainment; it's free to enter, unlike the theme park itself.
  10. Holy Trinity Church — This Protestant church in Huangpu District, consecrated in 1869, was designed in Gothic Revival style by British architect Sir George Gilbert Scott. After 1875 it was commonly referred to as a cathedral.
    Tip: Check service times before visiting; the exterior is especially photogenic in late-afternoon light.

Practical tips

  • Best time to visit: Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer the most comfortable weather for walking the Bund and exploring historic districts.
  • Getting around: The metro is fast and inexpensive; use it to link the Bund, Lujiazui, and the former French Concession, and walk between nearby sights.
  • Temple etiquette: Dress modestly and keep voices low at City God Temple and the Confucian Temple; some areas may restrict photography.
  • Timing sights: Visit the Bund at dusk for skyline views, M50 on weekdays, and the Confucian Temple market on Sunday mornings.
  • Tickets: Skyscraper observation decks and the Bund Sightseeing Tunnel charge admission; many temples and streets are free or low-cost, so carry small change and a mobile payment app.
