Shanghai Top 10 Attractions

Shanghai is China's most cosmopolitan city — a dazzling blend of 1920s Art Deco glamour, futuristic skylines, ancient water towns, and vibrant street life. Whether you have two days or two weeks, these ten attractions capture the city's essence, from the neon-lit Bund to tranquil classical gardens. Below you'll find descriptions, current ticket prices (in Chinese yuan, RMB), and insider tips to help you plan a smooth, memorable visit.

1. The Bund (外滩 — Waitan)

Shanghai's iconic waterfront promenade stretches 1.5 km along the Huangpu River, lined with 26 historic colonial-era buildings on one side and a sweeping view of the futuristic Pudong skyline — including the Oriental Pearl Tower and Shanghai Tower — on the other. It's the city's most photographed spot and especially magical at night when both banks light up.

Tickets: Free (public promenade) Hours: Open 24 hours Best time: Sunset to 9 PM
Tip: Arrive before 6 PM to secure a railing spot for the 7 PM light show. Avoid weekends and Chinese public holidays — it gets extremely crowded. The Bund Sightseeing Tunnel (¥50) is a fun but kitschy optional extra.

2. Yu Garden (豫园 — Yuyuan)

A classical Ming Dynasty garden dating to 1559, Yu Garden is a compact masterpiece of rockeries, koi ponds, pavilions, and zigzag bridges. The surrounding Yuyuan Bazaar is a lively (if touristy) maze of souvenir shops and snack stalls — perfect for sampling xiaolongbao and other local treats.

Tickets: ¥40 (garden only; bazaar free) Hours: 8:45 AM – 4:45 PM (last entry 4:15 PM) Nearest metro: Line 10, Yuyuan Garden Station
Tip: Visit on a weekday morning right at opening to beat the tour groups. Combine with a stroll through the bazaar afterward, but eat at nearby local restaurants rather than the bazaar's overpriced stalls.

3. Shanghai Tower Observation Deck (上海中心大厦)

At 632 meters, Shanghai Tower is China's tallest building and the world's second-tallest. Its 118th-floor observation deck offers jaw-dropping 360° views over the entire city — on clear days you can see all the way to the Yangtze River delta. The high-speed elevators whisk you up in under a minute.

Tickets: ¥180 (adult); ¥120 (children/seniors) Hours: 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM (last entry 8:30 PM) Nearest metro: Line 2, Lujiazui Station
Tip: Check the weather forecast before buying — haze can ruin the view. Go about an hour before sunset to see the city in daylight, at dusk, and lit up at night. Book online to skip ticket queues.

4. Shanghai Museum (上海博物馆)

One of China's finest museums, housing over 120,000 artifacts across bronze, ceramics, calligraphy, painting, jade, and furniture galleries. The collection spans 5,000 years of Chinese history, and the building itself — shaped like an ancient bronze ding — is an architectural landmark on People's Square.

Tickets: Free (reservation required) Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM, Tue–Sun (closed Mondays) Nearest metro: Lines 1/2/8, People's Square Station
Tip: Reserve your free timed entry slot on the museum's official WeChat account or website up to 7 days in advance. Allow at least 2–3 hours. Audio guides (¥40) are excellent and available in English.
