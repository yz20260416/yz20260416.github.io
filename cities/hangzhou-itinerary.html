Hangzhou Travel Guide: A Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province, is one of China's most celebrated destinations, famed for West Lake (Xī Hú), its tea culture, and a history stretching back over a thousand years as a former imperial capital of the Southern Song Dynasty. Marco Polo once described it as one of the finest cities in the world. Today it blends serene classical landscapes with the energy of a modern tech hub. This guide lays out a balanced three-day itinerary with morning, afternoon, and evening schedules, restaurant suggestions, and practical tips to help you make the most of your visit.

Day 1: West Lake and the Classic Hangzhou

Time Activity Details
Morning West Lake loop Start early at Broken Bridge (Duàn Qiáo) and walk the Su Causeway (Sū Dī), a 2.8 km tree-lined path built in the 11th century by poet-official Su Dongpo. Continue to the Quyuan Fenghe (Lotus Pond) area.
Afternoon Boat ride and island temples Take a public ferry to Xiaoyingzhou (Little Paradise Island) to see the Three Pools Mirroring the Moon, a scene featured on the Chinese 1-yuan note. Visit the Leifeng Pagoda for panoramic lake views.
Evening Impression West Lake show Book tickets in advance for the outdoor "Impression West Lake" performance (staged on the water near Yue Fei Temple) or take a relaxed evening stroll along the lakeside promenade.

Where to eat: For lunch, try Louwailou (30 Gushan Road), a historic restaurant on the lake known for West Lake vinegar fish and Longjing shrimp. For dinner, Zhiweiguan (multiple branches) offers affordable, authentic Hangzhou dishes such as Dongpo pork and beggar's chicken.

Day 2: Tea Culture, Temples, and Old Streets

Time Activity Details
Morning Longjing tea villages Visit Meijiawu or Longjing Village to tour tea plantations, learn about Dragon Well tea processing, and enjoy a tea tasting. Spring (March–April) is harvest season and the most atmospheric time to visit.
Afternoon Lingyin Temple and Feilai Peak Explore one of China's most important Buddhist temples, founded in 328 AD, and the surrounding Feilai Feng (Peak Flown from Afar) with its hundreds of ancient cliff carvings.
Evening Hefang Street and Qinghefang Wander the restored Qing-dynasty shopping street for souvenirs, traditional snacks, and the Hu Qing Yu Tang Chinese Medicine Museum.

Where to eat: Lunch at a tea-house restaurant in Meijiawu (try tea-smoked duck and dishes cooked with Longjing leaves). For dinner, Green Tea Restaurant (Longjing Road branch) serves popular Hangzhou fare in a garden setting; arrive early or expect a wait.

Day 3: Grand Canal, Museums, and Modern Hangzhou

Time Activity Details
Morning Grand Canal district Walk or cruise along the Hangzhou section of the UNESCO-listed Grand Canal. Visit the Gongchen Bridge area and the Canal Museum, then explore the historic Xiaohe Street and Qiaoxi historic blocks.
Afternoon Museums or Xixi Wetland Choose the Zhejiang Provincial Museum or China National Tea Museum for culture, or head to Xixi National Wetland Park for boat rides through reed-lined waterways.
Evening Qianjiang New City See the modern skyline, the light show along the Qiantang River (check schedules), and dine in the riverside malls. The Hangzhou Grand Theatre area is especially lively after dark.

Where to eat: Lunch near the canal at a local noodle shop (try Pian'erchuan noodles, a Hangzhou specialty). For a final dinner, Jin Sha at the Four Seasons Hotel offers refined Hangzhou cuisine, while Grandma's Home is a budget-friendly local favorite (expect queues).

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Hangzhou has an efficient metro system, extensive bike-sharing, and taxis. The metro is the easiest way to reach West Lake and major sights; buy a transit card or use mobile payment.
  • Best time to visit: Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer mild weather. Summers are hot and humid; winters are cool but quieter.
  • West Lake crowds: Visit early morning or on weekdays. The lake is free to enter; individual attractions and boat rides charge fees.
  • Mobile payment: Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely used; set up an account before arrival or carry cash as backup.
  • Language: English is limited outside major hotels and attractions. Download a translation app and save Chinese names of destinations.
  • Tickets: Book popular shows and museum slots online in advance, especially on weekends and holidays.
  • Etiquette: Dress modestly at temples, keep voices low in tea houses, and avoid touching the cliff carvings at Feilai Peak.
  • Day trips: If time allows, consider a side trip to Wuzhen water town (about 1.5 hours by bus) or the Moganshan mountain area.
