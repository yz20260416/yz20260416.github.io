Istanbul Top 10 Attractions

Straddling two continents and shaped by three great empires, Istanbul is a city where Byzantine domes, Ottoman palaces, and bustling bazaars sit side by side with modern cafés and galleries. With so much to see, first-time visitors should focus on the historic peninsula and the Bosphorus shoreline. Below are the ten attractions that best capture the city's layered soul, along with current ticket guidance and practical tips to help you plan your days efficiently.

Attraction Description Ticket Price (approx.) Visiting Tips
1. Hagia Sophia Must-see A 6th-century architectural marvel built by Emperor Justinian, later an Ottoman mosque, a museum, and now a working mosque again. Its massive dome, golden mosaics, and soaring interior make it one of the world's great buildings. Free for worshippers and visitors (donations welcome). Visit early morning or after 4 PM to avoid crowds. Modest dress required; headscarves available at the entrance. Closed to tourists during prayer times.
2. Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) An early 17th-century Ottoman masterpiece famous for its six minarets and 20,000 hand-painted Iznik tiles. Still an active mosque, it faces Hagia Sophia across Sultanahmet Square. Free (donations welcome). Dress modestly and remove shoes. Avoid Friday midday prayers. Best photographed from Sultanahmet Square or the Hippodrome at sunset.
3. Topkapı Palace The opulent residence of Ottoman sultans for nearly 400 years. Explore courtyards, the harem, the treasury, and the Sacred Relics chamber, with sweeping views over the Bosphorus. ~₺1,700 (approx. €45) for the full ticket incl. harem. Allow 3–4 hours. Buy tickets online to skip queues. The harem requires a separate ticket — get the combined pass. Closed Tuesdays.
4. Basilica Cistern A vast underground water reservoir built in 532 CE, supported by 336 marble columns. Atmospheric lighting and the famous Medusa-head column bases make it unforgettable. ~₺800 (approx. €22). Book timed-entry tickets online. Cool and damp inside — bring a light layer. Visit on a hot afternoon for a refreshing break.
5. Grand Bazaar One of the world's oldest and largest covered markets, with over 4,000 shops across 60+ streets. A maze of carpets, jewelry, ceramics, spices, and leather goods. Free to enter. Go on weekday mornings for calmer browsing. Bargain politely — start around 50% of the asking price. Closed Sundays.
6. Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı) A fragrant, colorful market dating to the 1660s, packed with spices, Turkish delight, teas, dried fruits, and souvenirs. Smaller and easier to navigate than the Grand Bazaar. Free to enter. Visit in the morning for the freshest produce and fewer crowds. Sample before buying; prices are fairly fixed but light haggling is fine.
7. Süleymaniye Mosque The grandest of Istanbul's imperial mosques, designed by master architect Mimar Sinan in the 1550s. Its hilltop location offers some of the best free views of the Golden Horn and city skyline. Free (donations welcome). Less crowded than the Blue Mosque — ideal for a peaceful visit. Combine with a walk through the surrounding Süleymaniye neighborhood's cafés and bookshops.
8. Galata Tower A medieval stone tower rebuilt by the Genoese in 1348, offering a 360° panorama of the city from its observation deck. A symbol of the Beyoğlu district. ~₺650 (approx. €18). Go at opening time (9 AM) or near sunset for the best light and shorter lines. The surrounding Galata streets are perfect for a post-visit stroll.
