Top Attractions in Istanbul

Straddling Europe and Asia along the Bosphorus, Istanbul layers nearly two millennia of Byzantine and Ottoman history beneath a bustling modern city. From subterranean cisterns and Roman aqueducts to hilltop panoramas and labyrinthine bazaars, the city rewards curious travelers at every turn. Here are ten standout attractions that capture Istanbul's extraordinary mix of ancient monuments, living neighborhoods, and sweeping waterfront views.

  1. Basilica Cistern — The largest of several hundred ancient cisterns beneath Istanbul, this atmospheric subterranean chamber was built in the 6th century during the reign of Byzantine emperor Justinian I and lies just southwest of the Hagia Sophia. Today it is kept with little water, allowing visitors to walk among its forest of columns.
    Tip: Visit early in the morning to avoid long queues, and allow time to see the famous Medusa-head column bases.
  2. Galata Tower — Built as a watchtower within the Walls of Galata, this landmark structure in Beyoğlu has served many purposes over the centuries and has operated as an exhibition venue and museum since 2020. It remains one of the defining symbols of both Beyoğlu and Istanbul.
    Tip: Come near sunset for panoramic city views, then explore the surrounding Galata streets and cafés.
  3. Grand Bazaar — One of the largest and oldest covered markets in the world, the Grand Bazaar contains 61 covered streets and over 4,000 shops across roughly 30,700 m², drawing a quarter to half a million visitors daily. In 2014 it ranked No. 1 among the world's most-visited tourist attractions.
    Tip: Go on a weekday morning for calmer browsing, and be prepared to bargain politely.
  4. Maiden's Tower — Also known as Leander's Tower since the Byzantine period, this tower sits on a small islet at the southern entrance of the Bosphorus, about 200 m from the coast of Üsküdar. It even appeared on the reverse of the Turkish 10 lira banknote from 1966 to 1981.
    Tip: Combine a visit with a ferry ride on the Bosphorus; the tower is especially photogenic from the Üsküdar waterfront at dusk.
  5. Aqueduct of Valens — This Roman aqueduct system was built in the late 4th century AD to supply Constantinople, with construction begun under Constantius II and completed in 373 by Emperor Valens. It remained in use for many centuries and its arches still span a busy modern boulevard.
    Tip: View it from Atatürk Boulevard; it's a quick, free photo stop between the Grand Bazaar area and Fatih.
  6. İstiklal Avenue — This 1.4 km pedestrian street in the historic Beyoğlu (Pera) district is one of Istanbul's most famous avenues, running from Tünel Square toward Taksim. It took its modern name, meaning "Independence," after the Republic was declared in 1923.
    Tip: Ride the historic nostalgic tram, and wander the side passages such as Çiçek Pasajı for cafés and meyhanes.
  7. Çamlıca Hill — At 288 m above sea level on the Asian side in Üsküdar, this hill offers a panoramic view of the southern Bosphorus and the mouth of the Golden Horn. It is a popular visitor attraction with Ottoman-themed tea gardens.
    Tip: Arrive in the late afternoon for tea with a view, and stay for sunset over the strait.
  8. Çamlıca Tower — This 369 m telecommunications tower on Little Çamlıca Hill in Üsküdar features observation decks and restaurants. Its reinforced concrete structure rises 221 m, topped by a 168 m steel antenna.
    Tip: Book observation-deck tickets in advance and visit on a clear day for views stretching across both continents.
  9. Dolmabahçe Clock Tower — Ordered by Sultan Abdülhamid II and designed by court architect Sarkis Balyan between 1890 and 1895, this Ottoman neo-baroque clock tower stands outside Dolmabahçe Palace by its Treasury Gate. It sits along the European waterfront of the Bosphorus next to Dolmabahçe Mosque.
    Tip: Pair the visit with a tour of Dolmabahçe Palace; the waterfront square is free to stroll.
  10. Belgrad Forest — This mixed deciduous forest lies adjacent to Istanbul and is named after the village settled by Serbs deported from Belgrade in 1521 when it fell to the Ottomans. Several historical reservoirs lie within the forest, which stretches across the Sarıyer and Eyüp districts.
    Tip: Go on a weekday morning for quieter walking trails, and bring water — facilities are limited inside the forest.

Practical tips

  • Many major sites (Basilica Cistern, Galata Tower, Çamlıca Tower) draw crowds — book tickets online and start early.
  • Use Istanbulkart for ferries, trams, and metro; a Bosphorus ferry is the best way to see the Maiden's Tower and waterfront landmarks.
  • Dress modestly and carry a light scarf when visiting mosques and shrines.
  • Wear comfortable shoes — the historic peninsula and Beyoğlu involve plenty of walking over uneven cobblestones and hills.
  • Check opening hours during Ramadan and public holidays, when some sites operate on reduced schedules.
