Cape Town is one of the most affordable major cities in the world for international visitors, yet costs vary dramatically depending on your travel style. A backpacker can survive on roughly R600–R900 (US$33–50) per day, while a mid-range traveler should budget around R1,800–R2,800 (US$100–155) per day, and luxury travelers can easily spend R6,000+ (US$330+) daily. The figures below are per-person daily estimates in South African rand (ZAR), with approximate US dollar equivalents at roughly R18 to the dollar — always check current exchange rates, as the rand is volatile. Peak season (December–January and the February–March school holidays) pushes accommodation prices up 30–60%, while May–August (winter) offers the best value outside of whale-watching and wildflower season.

Key Cost Details & Sample Prices

  • Table Mountain Aerial Cableway: ~R420 (US$23) return for adults; book online to skip queues. Hiking up is free.
  • Robben Island ferry & tour: ~R600 (US$33) — book well in advance, especially in summer.
  • Kirstenbosch Botanical Garden: ~R230 (US$13); free entry on select days for South African citizens.
  • Wine tasting in Stellenbosch/Franschhoek: R60–R150 (US$3–8) per estate; full-day group tours R900–R1,500.
  • Boulders Beach (penguins): ~R190 (US$11) conservation fee.
  • Cape Point: ~R400 (US$22) entry; the drive itself via Chapman's Peak (toll ~R60) is the highlight.
  • Uber airport transfer: R350–R500 (US$19–28) to the City Bowl; MyCiTi bus is far cheaper but slower.
  • Restaurant tipping: 10–15% is standard; check whether a service charge is already included.
  • SIM/data: Tourist SIMs from R100–R200 with data packages; free Wi-Fi is common in cafés and hotels.

Practical Money-Saving Tips

  • Travel in shoulder season (April–May, September–November): You get near-summer weather with accommodation 20–40% cheaper than December.
  • Use ride-hailing apps instead of street taxis: Uber and Bolt are reliable, metered, and often cheaper than airport transfers or rental cars for short trips.
  • Skip the rental car for city days: Parking is limited and traffic is heavy; rent only for Winelands or Garden Route trips and split costs.
  • Eat where locals eat: Bo-Kaap, Salt River, and Observatory offer excellent value; the Neighbourgoods Market at the Old Biscuit Mill (Saturdays) is a great budget-friendly feast.
  • Buy the MyCiTi "myconnect" card (R35) for cheap, safe bus travel along the Atlantic Seaboard and to the Waterfront.
  • Book major attractions online: Cableway, Robben Island, and popular tours sell out in peak season and online prices are sometimes discounted.
  • Carry some cash: Most places take cards, but minibus taxis, tips, and township tours may prefer cash. Use ATMs inside malls or banks for safety.
  • Stay alert on costs of "free" tours: Township tours and walking tours often expect tips of R100–R200 per person.

Safety & Budget Notes

Cape Town is generally safe for tourists in main areas, but petty crime (phone snatching, car break-ins) is a real risk. Budget a little extra for secure parking (R50–R100/day) and use registered taxis at night. Travel insurance is strongly recommended — medical costs are modest by Western standards but evacuation coverage matters for hikes and adventure activities. Finally, keep a 10–15% buffer in your budget: the rand's exchange rate swings can change your effective costs significantly between booking and travel.
