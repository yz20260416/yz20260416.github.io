Athens 3-Day Itinerary

Athens is one of the world's oldest cities, a place where 2,500-year-old monuments sit beside bustling modern neighborhoods, rooftop bars, and a vibrant street-food scene. Three days is enough to see the essential sights, explore a couple of neighborhoods, and eat very well — but only if you plan around the heat, the crowds, and Athens' famously spread-out archaeology. This itinerary balances the "must-sees" (the Acropolis, the Ancient Agora, the Acropolis Museum) with local favorites (Anafiotika, Exarchia, the Central Market) and structured meal stops so you're never wandering hungry. It assumes you're staying in the Plaka, Monastiraki, or Koukaki area, which puts most sights within walking distance or a short metro ride.

Day 1 — Ancient Athens & the Acropolis

Time Activity Notes & Food
8:00–8:30 Breakfast near your hotel Grab a koulouri (sesame bread ring) and Greek coffee; skip a heavy meal before the climb.
8:30–11:00 Acropolis & Parthenon Book a timed-entry ticket online in advance (€20 summer / €10 winter; combined €30 ticket covers multiple sites). Arrive at opening to beat heat and crowds. Wear grippy shoes — the marble is slippery.
11:00–11:30 Walk down via the Theatre of Dionysus slope Included in the Acropolis ticket; a quiet, underrated stop.
11:30–13:00 Acropolis Museum Air-conditioned, excellent, and best done before lunch. €15 (€10 winter). Don't miss the top-floor Parthenon Gallery and the glass floor over the excavation below.
13:00–14:30 Lunch in Koukaki Try Mani Mani (Maniot cuisine) or To Psaraki for seafood; for a quick bite, souvlaki at Kalamaki Bar.
15:00–17:00 Plaka & Anafiotika stroll Wander the whitewashed lanes of Anafiotika (a Cycladic village tucked under the Acropolis) and the Roman Agora.
17:00–18:30 Rest / hotel break Athens afternoons are hot; a siesta is a local habit for good reason.
19:00–21:00 Dinner in Plaka Taverna Platanos (historic, garden seating) or Spondi (modern Greek, book ahead).
21:00–22:30 Rooftop drink with Acropolis view A for Athens (Monastiraki) or 360 Cocktail Bar.

Day 2 — Agora, Monastiraki & Local Life

  • 9:00 — Breakfast: Falafellas (Monastiraki) for falafel wraps or a bakery bougatsa (custard pastry).
  • 9:30–11:30 — Ancient Agora & Temple of Hephaestus: Use your combined ticket. The Temple of Hephaestus is the best-preserved ancient Greek temple in Greece.
  • 11:30–12:30 — Roman Agora & Hadrian's Library: Small, quick, and included in the combined ticket.
  • 12:30–14:00 — Central Market (Varvakeios): A working market with fish, meat, and spice stalls. Have lunch at a market taverna — Diporto (no sign, downstairs, legendary) or Karamanlidika for cured meats and meze.
  • 14:00–16:00 — Monastiraki flea market & Ermou Street: Browse antiques, vinyl, and souvenirs; coffee stop at Tailor Made or Mokka (one of Athens' oldest coffee roasters).
  • 16:00–18:00 — Rest or optional: National Archaeological Museum (€12) — world-class collection if you have energy; otherwise, a rooftop pool break.
  • 19:30–21:30 — Dinner in Psyrri or Gazi: Karamanlidika (if you didn't do lunch there), Oineas (wine-focused taverna), or Funky Gourmet for a splurge tasting menu (reserve well ahead).
  • 21:30 onward — Drinks: Gazi for clubs and bars; Psyrri for low-key wine bars.

Day 3 — Neighborhoods, Views & Farewell

  • 8:30 — Breakfast: Krinos (since 1923) for loukoumades (honey doughnuts) or a proper sit-down at Mam in Exarchia.
  • 9:30–11:00 — Mount Lycabettus: Take the funicular (€10 round trip) or walk up for panoramic city views. Go early — the summit gets crowded and hot.
  • 11:00–13:00 — Exarchia & Kolonaki: Exarchia for street art and bookshops; Kolonaki for boutiques and the Benaki Museum (€12, excellent Greek art and history).
  • 13:00–14:30 — Lunch in Kolonaki: Filippou (traditional, family-run since 1923) or Nice n Easy for healthy options.
  • 14:30–16:00 — Optional: Panathenaic Stadium (€5) or the National Garden for a shaded walk.
  • 16:00–17:30 — Final souvenir run: Monastiraki or the Athens Central Market for olive oil, honey, and herbs (better prices than tourist shops).
  • 18:00–19:30 — Sunset at Filopappou Hill: Free, quieter than Lycabettus, with a direct view of the Parthenon. Bring water and a snack.
  • 20:00 — Farewell dinner: Strofi (rooftop, Acropolis view) or To Steki tou Ilia in Thissio for grilled lamb chops.

Practical Tips

  • Tickets: Buy Acropolis and museum tickets online in advance. The €30 combined ticket (valid 5 days) covers the Acropolis, Ancient Agora, Roman Agora
