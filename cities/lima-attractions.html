Top Attractions in Lima

Lima, Peru's capital, blends a UNESCO-listed colonial core with bohemian seaside neighborhoods and bold modern architecture. From the grand Plaza Mayor and its cathedral to cliff-top shopping and surf-friendly beaches, the city rewards travelers who explore both its historic streets and its coastal districts. Here are ten standout attractions for a typical international visitor.

  1. Historic Centre of Lima — The city's colonial heart spans the districts of Lima and Rímac, with a Monumental Zone and a UNESCO World Heritage Site whose marked buildings date to the Viceroyalty era. It is the essential starting point for understanding Lima's layered past.
  2. Metropolitan Cathedral of Lima — Begun by Francisco Pizarro in 1535 and completed in 1797, this cathedral on the Plaza Mayor is the seat of the Archdiocese of Lima. Its long construction history and position on the main square make it a landmark not to miss.
  3. Archbishop's Palace, Lima — Located at the Plaza Mayor in the Historic Centre, this is the residence of the Archbishop of Lima and the administrative headquarters of the Archdiocese. Its ornate colonial façade adds to the square's architectural ensemble.
  4. Balconies of Lima — These carved wooden balconies, mostly built in the late 17th and 18th centuries during the Viceroyalty, are scattered throughout the Historic Centre and were key to UNESCO's World Heritage declaration. Wandering the streets to spot them is a highlight of the old town.
  5. Basilica and Convent of Nuestra Señora de la Merced — Built in 1535 under Friar Miguel de Orenes, this church is designed in the ornate Churrigueresque Baroque style. It venerates the Virgin of Mercy, patroness of the Peruvian Armed Forces.
  6. Casa de Osambela — Also known as Casa de Oquendo, this late colonial mansion stands on the site of a Dominican novitiate destroyed by the 1746 earthquake. It is one of the largest mansions in central Lima, notable for its wide façade and fine balconies.
  7. Palacio de Torre Tagle — This colonial-era mansion, two blocks southeast of the Plaza Mayor, now houses offices of Peru's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Its built area of 1,699 m² uses materials brought from Spain, Panama, and other Central American countries.
  8. Alameda Chabuca Granda — This pedestrian promenade in the historic centre sits beside the Rímac River and partially incorporates the single-block Santa Street west of the Government Palace. It hosts annual art biennials and gastronomy festivals, making it a lively stop.
  9. Barranco District — This seaside district is Lima's bohemian and artistic centre, home to many of Peru's leading artists, musicians, and photographers. Its beaches are popular with surfers, and a marina completed in 2008 serves the yacht club.
  10. Larcomar — This shopping centre in Alfredo Salazar Park sits at José Larco Avenue and the Malecón de la Reserva in Miraflores, across from the JW Marriott Hotel Lima. Its cliff-top setting and ocean views make it a favorite with tourists and locals alike.

Practical tips

  • Visit the Historic Centre and Plaza Mayor in the morning to see the Cathedral and Archbishop's Palace before the midday heat and crowds.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for spotting the Balconies of Lima and the colonial mansions—many are best seen on foot along the old streets.
  • Check local listings for festivals and gastronomy events at Alameda Chabuca Granda, which hosts annual art biennials and food fairs.
  • Head to Barranco and Larcomar in the late afternoon for sunset views over the Pacific and a relaxed seaside atmosphere.
  • Note that the Palacio de Torre Tagle houses a government ministry, so view it from the exterior rather than expecting interior tours.
