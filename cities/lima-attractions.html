Lima Top 10 Attractions

Lima, the “City of Kings,” blends colonial grandeur, pre-Columbian history, Pacific coastlines, and one of the world’s most celebrated culinary scenes. From the UNESCO-listed historic center to bohemian seaside districts, these ten attractions capture the best of Peru’s capital. Prices below are approximate in Peruvian soles (S/) and US dollars (USD), with adult general-admission rates noted; always confirm current prices and opening hours before visiting, as they can change seasonally.

Top 10 Attractions at a Glance

Attraction Description Ticket Price (approx.) Visiting Tips
1. Plaza Mayor & Historic Center The heart of colonial Lima and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The grand square is flanked by the Government Palace, the Cathedral, the Archbishop’s Palace, and the Municipal Palace. Wander the surrounding streets for balconies, churches, and museums. Free to walk
(individual sites charge separately)		 Visit early morning or late afternoon for softer light and fewer crowds. Tip: The changing of the guard at the Government Palace is a colorful spectacle (check current schedule; typically around midday on select days).
2. Lima Cathedral (Catedral de Lima) A stunning colonial cathedral on the Plaza Mayor, built in the 16th century and rebuilt after earthquakes. It houses the tomb of conquistador Francisco Pizarro, ornate chapels, and a religious art museum. S/ 10–15
(~USD 3–4)		 Dress respectfully (shoulders/knees covered). Tip: Combine with the Plaza Mayor walk; guided tours inside are often available for a small extra fee.
3. Museo Larco Housed in an 18th-century mansion built over a pre-Columbian pyramid, this museum holds an exceptional collection of ceramic, gold, and textile artifacts spanning 5,000 years of Peruvian history. Famous for its erotic pottery gallery and beautifully organized storage rooms. S/ 35–40
(~USD 10–11)		 Allow 2–3 hours. Tip: The on-site café and garden are lovely; go on a weekday morning for a quieter experience. English signage is excellent.
4. Huaca Pucllana A massive adobe pyramid and ceremonial complex built by the Lima culture (circa 500 CE), located right in the Miraflores district. Excavations are ongoing, and the site includes a small museum and an upscale restaurant. S/ 15–20
(~USD 4–6)		 Visits are by guided tour only (included in ticket; Spanish/English schedules vary). Tip: Evening tours under floodlights are magical—book ahead. Wear comfortable shoes; the terrain is uneven.
5. Miraflores & Larcomar Lima’s modern seaside district, famous for parks, cafés, and the clifftop shopping/entertainment complex Larcomar, with sweeping Pacific views. The Malecón (seafront promenade) is ideal for walking or cycling, and Parque Kennedy is a lively hub. Free
(Larcomar shops/restaurants vary)		 Great for sunset. Tip: The malecón stretches for miles—rent a bike or stroll from Parque del Amor to the lighthouse. Watch for paragliders overhead.
