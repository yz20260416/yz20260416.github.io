Local Character

Las Vegas began not as a gambling town but as a water stop: in 1829, Mexican trader Antonio Armijo's party of 60 men and 100 mules paused at the Las Vegas Springs, whose streams fed a meadow ecosystem that gave the city its name ("las vegas" means "the meadows"). Visitors interested in the city beneath the neon can seek out the springs' legacy, since it explains why a settlement exists in this desert at all. The valley was winter home to the Southern Paiute people, who used its resources before moving to higher elevations each summer—a reminder that the area's history long predates the Strip. Paleo-Indians passed through 10,000 years ago, leaving petroglyphs, and Rafael Rivera is credited as the first non-Native American to encounter the valley in 1829. The city itself was settled in 1905 and incorporated in 1911, making it the most populous North American city founded in the 20th century—a fact that lends its downtown an unusually young, boom-town character. Note that the famous Strip lies outside city limits in unincorporated Paradise and Winchester, so the city proper and its resort corridor are distinct jurisdictions. The nickname "Sin City" reflects a long-standing local tolerance for adult entertainment that shaped the city's identity and its role as a film and television setting.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. The Las Vegas Strip — The legendary boulevard of casino-hotels and entertainment venues, technically located outside city limits in Paradise and Winchester.
  2. Downtown Las Vegas — The historic city core, home to a concentration of casinos, bars, and entertainment distinct from the Strip.
  3. Las Vegas Springs — The natural water source that named the city and supported its meadow ecosystem, central to the valley's early history.
  4. Petroglyph Sites — Rock art left by Paleo-Indians who traveled through the Las Vegas area roughly 10,000 years ago.
  5. Southern Paiute Heritage Areas — Territory used as winter residence by the Southern Paiute people, who utilized the valley's resources seasonally.

FAQ

Is the Las Vegas Strip inside the city of Las Vegas?
No—the Strip lies outside city limits in the unincorporated towns of Paradise and Winchester, though it is commonly associated with Las Vegas.
When is the best time to visit Las Vegas?
Spring (March–May) and fall (September–November) offer the most comfortable desert temperatures, while summer is extremely hot and winter is mild but cooler.
Why is Las Vegas called "Sin City"?
The nickname reflects the city's long-standing tolerance for numerous forms of adult entertainment, which shaped its identity and its popularity as a film and television setting.
