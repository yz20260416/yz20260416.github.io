Moscow 3-Day Itinerary

Moscow is a city of dramatic contrasts — onion-domed churches beside glass skyscrapers, imperial palaces next to Soviet-era monuments, and a metro system that doubles as an underground art museum. With three days you can cover the essential sights of the historic center, dive into Russian art and history, and still leave room for borscht, blini, and a stroll along the Moskva River. This itinerary is designed to be walkable where possible, with metro rides (the stations themselves are attractions) connecting the major clusters. It assumes you start each day around 9:00 and pace yourself — Moscow rewards slow looking as much as fast sightseeing.

Day 1 — Red Square & the Kremlin

Morning

  • 9:00 — Red Square. Arrive early to beat the crowds and the midday tour buses. Walk the full length from the State Historical Museum to St. Basil's Cathedral. Admire the Kremlin wall, Lenin's Mausoleum (exterior; entry is limited and often closed), and the Resurrection Gate.
  • 10:00 — St. Basil's Cathedral. Step inside the iconic 16th-century church — the interior is a maze of narrow corridors and painted chapels. Allow 45–60 minutes.
  • 11:15 — GUM department store. Cross the square for a coffee or ice cream under the glass-roofed arcades of this 19th-century shopping hall. It's as much an architectural sight as a retail one.

Afternoon

  • 12:30 — Lunch. Try Stolovaya No. 57 in GUM for affordable Soviet-style cafeteria food, or Bosco Café for a sit-down meal with a view of the square.
  • 14:00 — The Kremlin. Enter via the Alexander Garden ticket office (book online in advance — Armoury tickets sell out). Prioritize the Armoury Chamber (imperial regalia, Fabergé eggs, carriages), Cathedral Square with its three cathedrals, the Tsar Bell, and the Tsar Cannon. Budget 3 hours; the Armoury alone deserves an hour.

Evening

  • 17:30 — Zaryadye Park. A short walk from the Kremlin, this modern park has a floating bridge with panoramic views over the Moskva River and St. Basil's. Great for sunset photos.
  • 19:30 — Dinner. Café Pushkin (Tverskoy Blvd) for classic Russian cuisine in a charming library-like setting, or Grand Cafe Dr. Zhivago near Red Square for modern Russian dishes and people-watching.

Day 2 — Art, History & the Metro

Morning

  • 9:30 — Tretyakov Gallery. The world's finest collection of Russian art, from medieval icons (including Rublev's Trinity) to 19th-century masters like Repin and Levitan. Start with the icon hall, then work chronologically. Allow 2.5–3 hours.
  • 12:30 — Lunch. Try a nearby Georgian spot (look for khachapuri and khinkali) or a café on Lavrushinsky Lane.

Afternoon

  • 14:00 — Metro tour. Moscow's metro is a sight in itself. Ride the circle line (Line 5) and stop at Komsomolskaya, Novoslobodskaya, Belorusskaya, and Kievskaya for chandeliers, mosaics, and marble. Then head to Ploshchad Revolyutsii (Revolution Square) for its bronze sculptures. A single ticket covers the ride; allow 1.5–2 hours with stops.
  • 16:00 — Christ the Savior Cathedral. Rebuilt in the 1990s after its Soviet demolition, this massive white-and-gold cathedral offers a viewing platform with river views. Dress modestly.
  • 17:15 — Old Arbat Street. Stroll this pedestrian street for street performers, souvenir shops, and the historic Spaso House (US ambassador's residence).

Evening

  • 19:30 — Dinner. White Rabbit (Smolenskaya) for inventive modern Russian cuisine with skyline views, or LavkaLavka for farm-to-table Russian dishes.
