Local Character

Jiuzhaigou literally means 'Nine Settlement Valley,' named for the nine Tibetan villages that have existed along the valley for centuries. These villages, such as Zechawa and Rize, are still home to Tibetan and Qiang communities whose traditional customs and architecture offer a glimpse into the region's heritage. A visitor should care because these settlements provide a living cultural dimension to the natural landscape. In 1975, forestry scientist Wu Zhonglun surveyed the area and was so moved by its beauty that he urged the government to protect it, leading to the cessation of logging and the establishment of the nature reserve in 1980. This historical quirk highlights the foresight that preserved Jiuzhaigou for future generations.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Five Flower Lake — A shallow lake with crystal-clear water revealing submerged tree trunks and vibrant colors.
  2. Nuorilang Waterfall — A wide, multi-tiered waterfall that is one of the most iconic sights in Jiuzhaigou.
  3. Long Lake — The largest and deepest lake in Jiuzhaigou, surrounded by snow-capped mountains.
  4. Zechawa Valley — A scenic valley home to Tibetan villages and stunning alpine scenery.
  5. Rize Valley — A valley known for its colorful lakes and dense forests, ideal for hiking.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Jiuzhaigou?
Autumn (September to October) offers spectacular foliage and pleasant weather, while spring (April to May) has fewer crowds.
Are there any restrictions on visitor numbers?
Yes, during high season a daily quota of 12,000 visitors may be enforced, so it's advisable to book tickets in advance.
What should I pack for a trip to Jiuzhaigou?
Bring layers, as temperatures can vary greatly between day and night, and comfortable walking shoes for exploring the trails.
