Bangkok 3-Day Itinerary

Bangkok is a city of dazzling contrasts — glittering temples sit beside soaring skyscrapers, street-food stalls hum with activity near Michelin-starred restaurants, and serene canals wind through a metropolis that never truly sleeps. With three days, you can experience the best of the Thai capital: the historic glory of the Grand Palace and Wat Pho, the vibrant markets of Chatuchak and Chinatown, the riverside charm of the Chao Phraya, and the buzzing nightlife of Silom and Sukhumvit. This itinerary balances must-see landmarks with authentic local food, cultural immersion, and enough breathing room to enjoy the city at your own pace.

Best time to visit: November–February (cool, dry season). Getting around: Use the BTS Skytrain and MRT subway for speed, river boats for scenic travel, and Grab (ride-hailing) for late-night convenience.

Day 1 – Royal History & Riverside Culture (Old Town / Rattanakosin)

Time Activity Restaurant / Food Recommendation
8:00 AM Arrive early at the Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew (Emerald Buddha). Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered). Allow 2–3 hours. Breakfast: Krua Apsorn (Dinso Road) – famous for crab omelette and Thai curries. Or grab khao tom (rice soup) at a local stall near the palace.
11:30 AM Walk 10 minutes to Wat Pho — home of the 46-meter Reclining Buddha and Thailand's original massage school. Consider a 30-minute traditional massage. Lunch: The Deck by the River (Arun Residence) – riverside Thai cuisine with views of Wat Arun. Or Err (Michelin Bib Gourmand) for rustic Thai dishes.
2:00 PM Take the ferry across the Chao Phraya to Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn). Climb the central prang for panoramic river views. Snack: Try roti sai mai (candy floss roti) or fresh coconut ice cream from a street vendor near the pier.
4:00 PM Explore Museum Siam (interactive Thai history) or wander the Pak Khlong Talat flower market — best in late afternoon. Coffee break: Hong Sieng Kong – a stunning riverside café in a 200-year-old house.
6:30 PM Sunset drinks at a rooftop bar, then dinner in Chinatown (Yaowarat). Walk along Yaowarat Road for street-food heaven. Dinner: Nai Mong Hoi Tod (crispy oyster omelette), Guay Jub Ouan Pochana (peppery rolled noodles), and Yaowarat Toast for dessert. For a sit-down option: T&K Seafood.

Day 2 – Markets, Modern Bangkok & Nightlife (Sukhumvit / Chatuchak)

Time Activity Restaurant / Food Recommendation
