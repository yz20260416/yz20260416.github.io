Huangshan Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Huangshan (Yellow Mountain) in Anhui Province is one of China's most celebrated mountain landscapes, famous for its granite peaks, twisted pines, sea of clouds, hot springs, and winter snow. The scenic area is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a favorite among hikers, photographers, and nature lovers. Most attractions sit within or near the Huangshan Scenic Area, with cable cars and stone-step trails connecting the main viewpoints. Below are the top 10 must-see attractions, with practical details to help you plan your visit. Note that ticket prices and hours can change seasonally, so confirm before traveling.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions

  1. Lotus Peak (Lianhua Feng)

    Huangshan's highest peak at 1,864 meters, rising dramatically above the sea of clouds. The climb involves steep, narrow stone steps and ladders, rewarding hikers with panoramic views. It is often closed in winter or bad weather for safety.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket (approx. CNY 190 peak season, CNY 150 low season).

    Hours: Typically 6:30–16:30 (seasonal variations); closed in winter or during maintenance.

    Tips: Start early to avoid crowds, carry water, and check closure status before planning the climb.

  2. Bright Summit (Guangming Ding)

    One of Huangshan's three main peaks at 1,860 meters, known as the best spot for sunrise and sunset. The summit is relatively accessible and offers sweeping views over the surrounding peaks and cloud seas.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Open with the scenic area, generally 6:30–17:00 (seasonal).

    Tips: Arrive 30–45 minutes before sunrise; bring warm layers, as summit temperatures are much lower than at the base.

  3. Guest-Greeting Pine (Yingke Song)

    Huangshan's most iconic pine, with a branch extending like a welcoming arm. Located near the Yuping Cable Car station, it is one of the most photographed sights in the park.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Accessible during scenic area hours, typically 6:30–17:00.

    Tips: Visit early morning or late afternoon to avoid peak tour-group times; the area can be very crowded midday.

  4. West Sea Grand Canyon (Xihai Daxiagu)

    A spectacular gorge with vertiginous cliff paths, stone steps, and viewing platforms. The full loop is strenuous; a funicular (ground cable car) helps shorten the route.

    Ticket: Main scenic area ticket covers entry; the West Sea funicular costs extra (approx. CNY 100 one way).

    Hours: Typically 7:00–16:30; may close in winter or poor weather.

    Tips: Allow 3–4 hours for the loop; wear sturdy shoes and avoid the canyon in icy conditions.

  5. Beginning-to-Believe Peak (Shixin Feng)

    A popular peak in the eastern part of the scenic area, famous for its dense pine forest and classic Huangshan rock formations. The short climb offers excellent views and is a good alternative when major peaks are crowded.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Generally 6:30–17:00 (seasonal).

    Tips: Combine with nearby Cloud-Dispelling Pavilion for a half-day eastern circuit.

  6. Cloud-Dispelling Pavilion (Paiyun Ting)

    A classic viewpoint overlooking the West Sea, especially dramatic when clouds roll through the valleys. It is a short walk from the Baiyun Hotel area and a favorite sunset spot.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Open during scenic area hours, typically 6:30–17:00.

    Tips: Check the weather forecast; the pavilion is most spectacular after rain or in early morning cloud conditions.

  7. Flying-Over Stone (Feilai Shi)

    A balancing rock formation perched precariously on a peak, one of Huangshan's most recognizable natural sculptures. It sits near the route between Bright Summit and the West Sea area.

    Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Accessible during scenic area hours, typically 6:30–17:00.

    Tips: Best photographed in morning light; the viewpoint is a short detour from the main trail.

  8. Yuping Cable Car and Yuping Scenic Area

    The Yuping (Jade Screen) area is the gateway to the Guest-Greeting Pine and several major peaks. The cable car saves a long, steep climb and offers aerial views of the granite spires.

    Ticket: Cable car approx. CNY 90 one way (peak season); scenic area ticket required separately.

    Hours: Cable car typically 6:30–17:30 (seasonal; last rides earlier in low season).

    Tips: Buy tickets in advance during holidays; queues can be long, so ride up early and hike down if time allows.

  9. Yungu Cable Car and Eastern Steps

    The Yungu Cable Car connects the eastern base to the White Goose Ridge area, a convenient route to the Beginning-to-Believe Peak and central trails. The eastern steps below offer a quieter hiking alternative.

    Ticket: Cable car approx. CNY 80 one way; scenic area ticket required separately.

    Hours: Typically 6:30–17:30 (seasonal).

    Tips: Hiking up the eastern steps takes about 2–3 hours; the cable car is a good option if you are short on time or carrying luggage.

  10. Huangshan Hot Springs (Zui Wenquan)

    A historic hot spring area at the foot of the mountain, traditionally one of Huangshan's "four wonders." It is a relaxing stop after hiking, with bathing facilities and nearby lodging.

    Ticket: Hot spring bathing approx. CNY 100–200 depending on the facility; separate from the scenic area ticket.

    Hours: Generally 10:00–22:00 (varies by facility).

    Tips: Book in advance on weekends; bring your own swimwear if you prefer, and allow time to rest before onward travel.

Practical Tips for Visiting Huangshan

  • Best seasons: Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) offer mild weather and clear views; winter brings snow and fewer crowds but some trails close. Summer is rainy and busy.
  • Main ticket: The scenic area entrance ticket is valid for multiple days (commonly three days) but requires re-registration; keep your ticket and ID handy.
  • Getting there: High-speed trains serve Huangshan North Station; buses and taxis connect to the scenic area transfer centers at Tangkou and the hot spring area. Private cars are not allowed on the mountain roads.
  • Shuttle buses: Mandatory scenic-area shuttle buses run from the transfer centers to the cable car stations (approx. CNY 19–23 each way).
  • Accommodation: Staying overnight on the mountain (e.g., near Bright Summit or Baiyun Hotel) is the best way to catch sunrise and sunset, but rooms are limited and expensive—book well ahead.
  • Packing: Bring layered clothing, rain gear, sturdy walking shoes, snacks, and cash or a mobile payment app; summit prices for food and water are high.
  • Crowds: Avoid Chinese public holidays (especially the first week of October and Lunar New Year) when trails and cable cars are extremely busy.
  • Safety: Stone steps can be slippery in rain or ice; use handrails, avoid closed routes, and check weather forecasts before hiking exposed peaks.
