Huangshan Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Huangshan (Yellow Mountain) in Anhui Province is one of China's most celebrated mountain landscapes, famous for its granite peaks, twisted pines, sea of clouds, hot springs, and winter snow. The scenic area is large, with cable cars, stone-step trails, and several distinct zones, so planning which attractions to prioritize matters. Below are the top 10 must-see attractions with descriptions, ticket information, opening hours, and practical visiting tips. Prices and hours can change by season and policy, so confirm before travel.

  1. Yuping Peak (Jade Screen Peak) and the Welcoming-Guest Pine

    The iconic heart of Huangshan, home to the famous Welcoming-Guest Pine (Yingke Pine) and dramatic granite spires. Trails here connect to the Lotus Peak and the western side of the mountain, and the area offers some of the classic viewpoints that define Huangshan postcards.

    • Ticket: Included in the main Huangshan Scenic Area ticket (approximately CNY 190 peak season, CNY 150 low season; cable cars extra).
    • Opening hours: Scenic area typically 6:30–16:30 (varies by season and gate).
    • Tips: Arrive early to avoid crowds; the pine is a short walk from the Yuping Cable Car upper station. Wear sturdy shoes for steep stone steps.

  2. Lotus Peak (Lianhua Feng)

    One of Huangshan's highest and most dramatic peaks, reached by steep stone stairways with chains for support. The climb rewards with panoramic views, though it is physically demanding and may close in winter or bad weather.

    • Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.
    • Opening hours: Same as scenic area; seasonal closures apply.
    • Tips: Check closures before planning; not suitable for those with vertigo or limited mobility. Allow 1.5–2.5 hours round trip.

  3. Bright Summit Peak (Guangming Ding)

    The second-highest peak and one of the best places to watch sunrise and sunset. Its relatively accessible summit makes it a favorite for photographers and overnight visitors staying in the mountain hotels.

    • Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.
    • Opening hours: Open with the scenic area; sunrise viewing requires pre-dawn hiking.
    • Tips: Bring warm layers even in summer; sunrise spots fill early. A headlamp is useful for pre-dawn trails.

  4. West Sea Grand Canyon (Xihai Grand Canyon)

    A spectacular gorge with cliffside walkways, suspension bridges, and vertiginous viewpoints. The loop trails are among the most scenic and adventurous walks in Huangshan, with the "Sky-Walking Path" section offering thrilling exposure.

    • Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket; the ground cable car (if operating) is extra.
    • Opening hours: Typically 7:00–16:00; may close in winter or severe weather.
    • Tips: The full loop takes 3–5 hours; assess fitness and weather. Sections may close seasonally—check locally.

  5. Beginning-to-Believe Peak (Shixin Feng)

    A classic viewpoint known for its dense, sculptural pine trees and layered mountain views. It is a popular stop on the eastern circuit and offers some of the best pine-and-peak compositions.

    • Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.
    • Opening hours: Same as scenic area.
    • Tips: Combine with nearby Lion Peak and Paiyun Pavilion for a rewarding half-day route.

  6. Sea of Clouds Viewing Points (Yungu and Beihai Areas)

    Huangshan's famous "sea of clouds" is best seen from elevated viewpoints such as those around Beihai and the Yungu area, especially after rain or in early morning. These zones also offer gentler boardwalk paths and access to several hotels.

    • Ticket: Included in the main scenic area ticket.
    • Opening hours: Same as scenic area.
    • Tips: Clouds are most likely after rain and at dawn; stay overnight on the mountain to maximize chances.

  7. Hongcun Ancient Village

    A UNESCO-listed Ming- and Qing-era village at the foot of Huangshan, famous for its crescent-shaped pond, whitewashed houses, and cinematic scenery. It is an easy add-on for travelers interested in Huizhou architecture and culture.

    • Ticket: Approximately CNY 104 (multi-day entry typically available).
    • Opening hours: Generally 7:30–17:30 (varies by season).
    • Tips: Visit early morning or late afternoon for softer light and fewer tour groups; about 40–60 minutes by road from the Huangshan scenic area.

  8. Xidi Ancient Village

    Another UNESCO-listed Huizhou village, quieter than Hongcun, with ornate ancestral halls, stone lanes, and well-preserved residences. It offers a more relaxed cultural counterpoint to the mountain.

    • Ticket: Approximately CNY 104.
    • Opening hours: Generally 8:00–17:00 (varies by season).
    • Tips: Pair with Hongcun for a full day of village exploring; hiring a local guide adds context to the architecture.

  9. Huangshan Hot Springs (Piaoxue and Surroundings)

    Natural hot springs at the mountain's base, traditionally one of Huangshan's "four wonders." A soak is a popular way to recover after steep hiking, and the area includes resort facilities.

    • Ticket: Hot spring venues charge separately, typically CNY 100–200 depending on facility.
    • Opening hours: Commonly 10:00–22:00; confirm with the specific resort.
    • Tips: Book ahead in peak season; bring swimwear and avoid soaking immediately after intense exertion.

  10. Tunxi Old Street (Huangshan City)

    A historic Song-dynasty commercial street in Huangshan City (Tunxi), lined with traditional shops, Hui-style architecture, and local snacks. It is a convenient cultural stop before or after the mountain and a good place to buy tea and crafts.

    • Ticket: Free to walk; individual museums or attractions may charge small fees.
    • Opening hours: Street open all day; shops typically 9:00–21:00.
    • Tips: Try local Huangshan maofeng tea and Huizhou snacks; it is close to the airport and railway connections.

Practical Tips

  • Tickets: The main Huangshan Scenic Area ticket covers most peaks and trails but not cable cars, which typically cost CNY 80–100 per ride. Buy tickets online in advance during holidays.
  • Best seasons: Spring and autumn offer clear views and comfortable temperatures; winter brings snow and fewer crowds but some trail closures; summer is busy and rainy.
