Los Angeles Top 10 Attractions

Los Angeles is a sprawling metropolis where sun-soaked beaches, world-famous film studios, cutting-edge museums, and iconic neighborhoods collide. Whether you're a first-time visitor chasing Hollywood dreams or a repeat traveler diving into LA's diverse food and art scenes, these ten attractions represent the best of what the City of Angels has to offer. Below you'll find descriptions, current ticket prices (as of 2025), and practical tips to help you plan a smooth, memorable visit.

Attraction Description Ticket Price (approx.) Visiting Tips
1. Universal Studios Hollywood A working film studio and full-scale theme park in one. Ride the famous Studio Tour through real backlots, then experience immersive attractions like Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, Jurassic World, and Super Nintendo World. $109–$149 (1-day general admission; varies by date) Buy tickets online in advance for savings. Arrive at opening to beat crowds. Consider the Express Pass on weekends and holidays. Parking is $30–$50.
2. Hollywood Walk of Fame & TCL Chinese Theatre Stroll 1.3 miles of sidewalk stars honoring celebrities, then visit the historic TCL Chinese Theatre (formerly Grauman's) with its famous handprints and footprints in the forecourt. The Hollywood Sign is visible from nearby vantage points. Free (walk); theatre interior tours ~$25 Go early morning for fewer crowds and better photos. Avoid costumed characters demanding tips. Combine with the Hollywood & Highland Center for views of the sign.
3. Griffith Observatory Perched on Mount Hollywood, this Art Deco observatory offers stunning panoramic views of LA, the Hollywood Sign, and the Pacific. Inside, explore space and science exhibits and peer through telescopes (free on clear nights). Free (planetarium shows ~$10) Parking is limited and fills fast—arrive before 10 a.m. or take the DASH Observatory shuttle from the Metro. Best visited at sunset for spectacular city views.
4. The Getty Center A hilltop campus designed by Richard Meier housing European paintings, sculptures, illuminated manuscripts, and decorative arts. The Central Garden and city-view terraces are attractions in themselves. Free (parking $25) Reserve a free timed-entry ticket online in advance. Take the tram from the parking garage. Closed Mondays. Allow at least 3–4 hours to explore.
5. Santa Monica Pier & Beach An iconic end-of-route-66 pier featuring Pacific Park amusement rides, an aquarium, street performers, and seafood shacks. The wide beach below is perfect for sunbathing, biking, and watching sunsets over the Pacific. Free (pier entry); ride wristbands ~
