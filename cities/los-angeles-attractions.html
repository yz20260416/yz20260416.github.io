Top Attractions in Los Angeles

Los Angeles rewards visitors who look beyond the postcard image. From a hilltop observatory with sweeping basin views to a historic funicular climbing Bunker Hill, the city blends entertainment landmarks with cultural and architectural surprises. This guide highlights ten attractions most likely to appeal to international travelers, drawn from real sites across Hollywood, Downtown, Venice, and beyond.

  1. Griffith Observatory — Perched on the south-facing slope of Mount Hollywood in Griffith Park, this observatory commands views of the Los Angeles Basin, Downtown, Hollywood, and the Pacific Ocean, with a close-up look at the Hollywood Sign. It offers an extensive array of space and science-related displays, and admission has historically been free.
    Tip: Visit around sunset for the best light and city views; check current planetarium show times and parking fees in advance.
  2. Hollywood Walk of Fame — This landmark features more than 2,800 five-pointed terrazzo-and-brass stars embedded in the sidewalks along fifteen blocks of Hollywood Boulevard and three blocks of Vine Street. The stars honor achievement in entertainment, from actors and filmmakers to musicians and fictional characters.
    Tip: Go early in the morning to avoid crowds and heat; pair it with a stroll past nearby Hollywood Boulevard landmarks.
  3. Angels Flight — This historic 2 ft 6 in narrow-gauge funicular railway in the Bunker Hill district of Downtown Los Angeles has two cars, Olivet and Sinai, running in opposite directions on a shared cable. The tracks cover 298 feet over a vertical gain of 96 feet.
    Tip: Ride it as a quick, scenic shortcut between Downtown levels; fares are modest, so have small change or a card ready.
  4. The Last Bookstore — Located at 453 S Spring Street in Downtown Los Angeles, this independent bookstore has been called California's largest new and used bookstore by Condé Nast Traveler. Its labyrinthine shelves and art installations make it a destination in itself.
    Tip: Combine it with a Downtown walking day; weekday mornings are quieter for browsing and photos.
  5. L.A. Live — This entertainment complex in the South Park District of Downtown Los Angeles sits adjacent to Crypto.com Arena and the Los Angeles Convention Center. Costing approximately US$2.5 billion to build, it anchors a lively downtown district.
    Tip: Check the events calendar before visiting; evenings are liveliest when arena events are scheduled.
  6. Crossroads of the World — This open-air mall on Sunset Boulevard and Las Palmas features a central building designed to resemble an ocean liner, surrounded by cottage-style bungalows. Designed by Robert V. Derrah and built in 1936, it has been called America's first outdoor shopping mall.
    Tip: It is now primarily private offices, so admire the architecture from the street rather than expecting shopping.
  7. Beyond Baroque Literary Arts Center — Founded in 1968 and housed in Venice's old City Hall building (built in 1906) at 681 Venice Boulevard, this center offers public readings, workshops, a bookstore, and a chapbook/small press archive. It sits near the beach in a historic setting.
    Tip: Check the events schedule for readings; combine your visit with a walk along nearby Venice Beach.
  8. The Brewery Art Colony — Also known as the Brewery Arts Complex, this 16-acre compound of twenty-one former warehouses has been called the largest live-and-work artists colony in the world. It includes a former Edison power plant chimney dating to 1903, work studios, living lofts, restaurants, and galleries.
    Tip: Visits are best timed with open-studio events; otherwise, appreciate the industrial architecture from the surrounding streets.
  9. Avenue of the Athletes — This walk of fame in the Echo Park neighborhood honors athletes, many of them Olympians, most with some connection to Los Angeles. It is a low-key, locally loved tribute to sporting achievement.
    Tip: Pair it with a visit to Echo Park Lake for a relaxed afternoon of walking and people-watching.
  10. Crumbs and Whiskers — This cat café in Los Angeles fosters rescue cats while offering café amenities, partnering with rescues that save cats at risk of euthanasia and homelessness. It is a feel-good stop for animal lovers.
    Tip: Reserve a time slot online in advance, as seating is limited and popular sessions fill up.

Practical tips

  • Los Angeles is spread out, so group attractions by neighborhood—Downtown (Angels Flight, The Last Bookstore, L.A. Live) and Hollywood (Walk of Fame) work well on separate days.
  • Start outdoor sights like Griffith Observatory and the Walk of Fame early to beat crowds and midday heat.
  • Several attractions are free or low-cost, but parking and planetarium shows may carry fees—check ahead.
  • Some listed sites, such as Crossroads of the World and The Brewery Art Colony, are primarily working spaces; respect private areas and view from public vantage points.
  • Book timed experiences like cat café sessions in advance, especially on weekends.
