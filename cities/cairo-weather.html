Cairo Weather by Month

Cairo sits at the southern edge of the Nile Delta and experiences a hot desert climate (BWh), characterized by long, dry, sun-drenched summers and mild, occasionally cool winters. Rainfall is minimal year-round, arriving in brief, sparse showers mostly between November and March. Understanding Cairo's seasonal rhythm is key to planning a comfortable trip: spring and autumn offer the most pleasant conditions for sightseeing, summer demands early starts and shade, and winter brings crisp mornings alongside the city's (slightly) wettest months.

Best Months to Visit

  • Top picks: October–November and March–April. Warm days (23–29°C / 73–84°F), cool evenings, and minimal rain make these the most comfortable months for exploring Islamic Cairo, the Pyramids of Giza, and Coptic Cairo on foot.
  • Runner-up: February. Mild daytime temperatures and low tourist volumes suit budget-conscious travelers willing to layer up.
  • Budget and shoulder season: June–August. Hotel rates drop and attractions are quieter, but daytime heat (35°C / 95°F) is intense. If you visit then, sightsee at dawn, rest midday, and re-emerge in the evening.
  • Avoid if heat-sensitive: July and August, when combined heat and humidity can be draining for families with young children or older travelers.

Practical Tips

  • Dress in layers: Cairo's desert climate swings sharply between day and night, especially from November to March. A light jacket or sweater is useful even in spring evenings.
  • Hydrate and protect: Carry bottled water year-round and use high-SPF sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses—UV levels are strong even in winter.
  • Time your sightseeing: In summer, visit outdoor sites like the Pyramids and Saqqara at opening time (around 8 a.m.) and save museums for the hottest hours.
  • Watch for khamsin winds: Between March and May, occasional dust-laden khamsin storms can reduce visibility and irritate eyes and airways—carry a scarf and stay indoors if one hits.
  • Rain is rare but real: The few winter showers can leave streets slick and drainage overwhelmed; a compact umbrella is worth packing from November to February.
  • Ramadan timing: The Islamic holy month shifts each year; if your visit overlaps, expect adjusted opening hours and lively evening streets—plan meals and site visits accordingly.
  • Check air quality: Cairo can experience poor air quality, particularly in autumn (rice-straw burning season) and during dust events; sensitive travelers should monitor forecasts.
