Petra Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Petra, the ancient Nabataean city carved into rose-red sandstone cliffs in southern Jordan, is one of the world's most remarkable archaeological sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985. Visitors enter through the Siq, a narrow gorge that opens dramatically onto the Treasury, then explore tombs, temples, a Roman-style theatre, and a monastery spread across a vast landscape. This guide covers the ten essential sites within the archaeological park, with current ticket information, opening hours, and practical advice for making the most of your visit.

Opening hours: The site is generally open daily from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM in summer (April–October) and 6:00 AM to 4:00 PM in winter (November–March). Last entry is roughly one hour before closing. Petra by Night runs from approximately 8:30 PM to 10:30 PM on scheduled evenings. Hours can shift during Ramadan and public holidays, so confirm with the visitor center on arrival.

Top 10 Must-See Attractions

  1. The Siq
    The main entrance to Petra is a spectacular 1.2-kilometer (0.75-mile) narrow gorge flanked by cliffs up to 80 meters high. Ancient water channels, carved niches, and rock-cut reliefs line the walls. Walking through the Siq takes 20–30 minutes and builds anticipation before the Treasury suddenly appears through a narrow opening. Go early to avoid crowds and enjoy the cool shade.
  2. Al-Khazneh (The Treasury)
    Petra's iconic facade, 39 meters tall, is carved directly into the sandstone and was likely a royal tomb. The best light is in the morning, when the rock glows pink and orange. The plaza in front fills quickly after 9:00 AM, so arrive at opening time. Local guides offer context on the Hellenistic influences in the carvings.
  3. The Street of Facades
    Just past the Treasury, this wide avenue is lined with more than 40 rock-cut tombs with elaborate doorways and pediments. It served as the main ceremonial route into the city center. Allow 15–20 minutes to walk and examine the tomb facades, which show a range of Nabataean and classical architectural styles.
  4. The Theatre
    Carved into the hillside by the Nabataeans and later expanded by the Romans, this amphitheatre held around 8,500 spectators. Much of it was cut directly from the rock, with only the stage area built up. It sits near the Street of Facades and is easy to combine with a visit to the Royal Tombs.
  5. The Royal Tombs (Urn, Silk, Corinthian, Palace)
    A cluster of large, ornate tomb facades on the eastern hillside, including the Urn Tomb (later converted into a Byzantine church) and the Palace Tomb. The climb to the Urn Tomb rewards visitors with panoramic views over the valley. Budget 45–60 minutes for the loop.
  6. The Colonnaded Street and Great Temple
    The heart of the Roman-era city features a paved colonnade, the remains of the Great Temple complex, and the Temenos Gate. Excavations have revealed a sophisticated Nabataean temple with a large lower precinct. Interpretive signs explain the site's history; allow 30–45 minutes.
  7. Qasr al-Bint
    One of Petra's few freestanding structures rather than a rock-cut monument, this temple dates to around the 1st century BC and was likely dedicated to the Nabataean god Dushara. It stands at the western end of the Colonnaded Street and marks the transition toward the Monastery trail.
  8. Ad-Deir (The Monastery)
    Petra's largest monument, a 50-meter-wide facade reached by climbing roughly 800 rock-cut steps. The hike takes 45–60 minutes each way from the Colonnaded Street and is steep and exposed in the afternoon sun. Start early, carry water, and consider hiring a donkey only if you are comfortable with animal welfare standards. The reward is a quieter site and sweeping views from the ridge.
  9. The High Place of Sacrifice
    A Nabataean altar site atop Jebel Madbah, reached by a steep trail of about 45 minutes from the Street of Facades. The summit offers 360-degree views and passes rock-cut obelisks and ancient cisterns along the way. Descend via the Wadi Farasa trail to see the Roman Soldier Tomb and the Garden Temple.
  10. The Petra Museum
    Located near the visitor center at the site entrance, this modern museum displays artifacts excavated from Petra and the surrounding region, including sculpture, ceramics, and coins. It provides useful context before or after your walk. Entry is included with the main Petra ticket.

Practical Tips for Visiting Petra

  • Start at opening time. Gates open at 6:00 AM; arriving early means cooler temperatures, better light at the Treasury, and fewer crowds on the Monastery trail.
  • Buy the Jordan Pass. If you are visiting Jordan for several days, the Jordan Pass covers the Petra entry fee and waives the visa fee, often saving money overall. Purchase it online before arrival.
  • Plan for two days. The site covers roughly 10 square kilometers and involves significant walking and elevation change. A two-day ticket lets you cover the main monuments on day one and the Monastery and High Place on day two.
  • Wear sturdy shoes. Paths are rocky, uneven, and often steep. Sandals and smooth-soled shoes are a poor choice for the Monastery steps or the High Place trail.
  • Carry water and sun protection. There is little shade on the main routes, and temperatures regularly exceed 35°C (95°F) in summer. Refill stations are limited; buy water at the visitor center or small stalls near the Treasury.
  • Budget for extras. Golf-cart shuttles, donkey rides, and guide services cost extra (typically 10–40 JOD depending on distance and negotiation). Agree on prices before setting off.
  • Petra by Night is separate. The candlelit walk to the Treasury is atmospheric but does not include a full site tour. Book tickets in advance during peak season.
  • Respect local vendors and animals. Petra is within a Bedouin community area; bargaining is normal, but be mindful of animal welfare when considering rides.
  • Check seasonal hours. Winter closing is 4:00 PM, which limits afternoon exploration. Confirm current hours and any Ramadan adjustments at the visitor center.
