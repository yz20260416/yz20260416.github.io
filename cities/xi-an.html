Best Time to Visit Xi'an

Xi'an, the capital of Shaanxi province in northwestern China, is one of the country's oldest cities and served as the imperial capital of dynasties including the Western Zhou, Qin, Han, Sui and Tang under the name Chang'an. It is now China's second-most popular tourist destination, best known for the Terracotta Army and its Silk Road heritage. Visit in spring or autumn for the mildest weather and the best conditions for exploring its archaeological sites.

Local Character

Xi'an's character is inseparable from its long role as Chang'an, the capital of the Western Zhou, Qin, Western Han, Sui and Tang dynasties, and the city's identity still centers on that imperial legacy rather than on any single monument. A visitor should care because this continuity explains why major sights such as the Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor are treated as national touchstones rather than isolated attractions. The city's place on the Silk Roads is equally specific: five component sites of the UNESCO-listed Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor network lie within Xi'an, so the city is not merely a stop on the route but a designated part of the heritage. Travelers interested in trade, religion and cross-cultural exchange should seek these out, as they reveal the city's historic role as a gateway between China's interior and Central Asia. Even the name carries a quirk worth knowing: 'Xi'an' means 'Western Peace' and was adopted in 1369 under the early Ming dynasty, while earlier Jesuit missionaries recorded it as Si-ngan-fou, a form still echoed in the Latin name of the local Catholic archdiocese. Locals and scholars alike distinguish the written apostrophe in Xi'an, which separates the two syllables and prevents the name being read as a single syllable, a small detail that signals the city's care for its own nomenclature.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor — The burial complex of China's first emperor, famed for its excavated Terracotta Army of life-size clay soldiers.
  2. Silk Roads component sites of Xi'an — Five sites within the city that form part of the UNESCO-listed Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor network.
  3. Fenghao site — The twin capitals of Feng and Hao of the Western Zhou, located on opposite banks of the Feng River in the western suburbs.
  4. Xianyang Qin capital area — The site of the Qin capital erected north of the Wei River during the Warring States period, predecessor to later imperial capitals.
  5. Xi'an city museums and university district — Home to institutions such as Xi'an Jiaotong University and Northwest University, reflecting the city's role as a research and education hub.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit Xi'an?
April, May and October offer the most comfortable temperatures, though these are also the busiest months; late spring and early autumn balance weather and crowds best.
Why is the city called Xi'an?
Xi'an is the pinyin romanization of the Chinese name meaning 'Western Peace,' adopted in 1369 under the early Ming dynasty; the apostrophe distinguishes its two syllables from a single-syllable reading.
What makes Xi'an significant beyond the Terracotta Army?
Xi'an was Chang'an, capital under dynasties including the Western Zhou, Qin, Western Han, Sui and Tang, and it contains five component sites of the UNESCO-listed Silk Roads network.
