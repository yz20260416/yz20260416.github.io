Toronto Travel Weather Guide

Toronto's weather swings widely across the year, and the numbers make the best and worst windows clear. July is the warmest month (26.2°C average high, 17.5°C low) but also one of the wettest by frequency, with 11.8 rain days — expect hot, humid afternoons and occasional thunderstorms. January is the harshest (highs of -0.5°C, lows of -7.0°C), so winter visitors need serious cold-weather gear and should plan for snow and ice. The sweet spot is late spring and early autumn: May (18.1°C highs, 9.4 rain days) and September (22.1°C highs, just 8.2 rain days) offer comfortable temperatures with less rain, making them the most practical months for sightseeing on foot.

Practical tips

  • Layer up year-round: Even summer evenings can cool to the mid-teens, and winter days demand thermal base layers, a warm coat, hat, and gloves.
  • Always carry rain protection: Toronto sees 8–13 rain days in every month, so a compact umbrella or waterproof shell is worth packing regardless of season.
  • Target May or September for the best balance: These months pair comfortable temperatures with relatively fewer rain days and lighter crowds than July and August.
  • Book indoor backups in summer: July and August combine heat, humidity, and frequent showers — keep museums, galleries, and the PATH network on your list.
  • Winter travel needs footwear: With January lows near -7°C and snow and ice common, insulated, waterproof boots are essential for getting around safely.
