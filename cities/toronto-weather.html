Toronto Weather by Month

Toronto has a humid continental climate, which means travellers can expect four distinct seasons: warm, often humid summers; crisp, colourful autumns; cold, snowy winters; and fresh, unpredictable springs. Temperatures are quoted in Celsius (with Fahrenheit in brackets), and rainfall figures are long-term monthly averages — treat them as a guide rather than a guarantee, since Toronto's weather can swing widely from year to year. Summer afternoons can feel much hotter than the thermometer suggests once humidity is factored in, while winter days are often sharpened by wind chill coming off Lake Ontario.

Best months to visit:

  • June to early September — warmest weather, longest days and the busiest festival season; book accommodation early.
  • Late September to mid-October — arguably the sweet spot: comfortable temperatures, lower humidity, autumn foliage and thinner crowds.
  • May and early June — mild, green and quiet before peak season, with good value on hotels.
  • December — magical for holiday markets and lights, provided you're prepared for the cold.
  • Avoid if weather-sensitive: January and February are the harshest months, though indoor attractions remain fully open.

Practical Tips

  • Pack in layers year-round. Toronto temperatures can shift 10 °C or more within a day, especially in spring and autumn.
  • Summer humidity: July and August often feel several degrees hotter than the stated high. Light, breathable clothing and water bottles are essential; carry a compact umbrella for afternoon thunderstorms.
  • Winter gear is non-negotiable: a proper coat, insulated boots with grip, gloves, a hat and a scarf. Wind chill near the lake can make −5 °C feel like −15 °C.
  • Rainfall is spread fairly evenly across the year, so a small umbrella or waterproof shell is worth packing in any season.
  • Use the PATH: Toronto's 30 km underground pedestrian network connects Union Station, major attractions and shopping — a lifesaver on rainy or bitterly cold days.
  • Check the forecast daily. Lake-effect weather and fast-moving systems mean conditions can change quickly; local apps are more reliable than long-range predictions.
  • Shoulder seasons offer value: May and late September–October combine decent weather with lower prices and smaller crowds than midsummer.
