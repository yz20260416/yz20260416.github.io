Ho Chi Minh City: A Perfect 3-Day Itinerary

Ho Chi Minh City (still widely called Saigon) blends French colonial architecture, war history, street-food culture, and relentless energy. Three days is enough to cover the historic center, the war-era landmarks, the Mekong Delta fringe, and the city's excellent food scene — if you plan around the heat and traffic. This itinerary assumes you stay in District 1, the most convenient base for first-time visitors.

Day 1: Historic Center and Colonial Saigon

TimeActivityNotes
Morning Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon (exterior), Central Post Office, and a walk down Dong Khoi Street Start early (7:30–8:00) to beat the heat. The cathedral has been under restoration for years, so view it from outside. The Post Office is free to enter and still operating.
Afternoon Reunification Palace (Independence Palace), then the War Remnants Museum Both are within walking distance of each other. The War Remnants Museum is heavy but essential; allow 1.5–2 hours. Many museums close for lunch around 11:30–13:30, so check hours.
Evening Dinner in District 1, then a rooftop drink or a stroll through Nguyen Hue Walking Street Nguyen Hue is pedestrianized in the evening and lively with locals. Rooftop bars cluster around the street and nearby hotels.

Restaurant ideas for Day 1: Pho Le (District 5, for a classic bowl of pho — a short Grab ride away), Propaganda Vietnamese Bistro (District 1, near the palace), or Secret Garden (rooftop Vietnamese home cooking, District 1).

Day 2: Markets, Temples, and Local Life

TimeActivityNotes
Morning Ben Thanh Market for souvenirs and breakfast, then Jade Emperor Pagoda (Chua Ngoc Hoang) Ben Thanh is busiest and best early. The Jade Emperor Pagoda is a short taxi ride away in District 1 and is one of the city's most atmospheric temples — go before 10:00 to avoid crowds.
Afternoon Cho Lon (Chinatown) in District 5: Binh Tay Market, Thien Hau Temple, and the lanes around Hai Thuong Lan Ong Street This is the city's Chinese quarter and feels distinctly different from District 1. Allow 2–3 hours. A guided food walk here is worthwhile.
Evening Street-food dinner in District 4 or a Vietnamese cooking class District 4 (especially around Vinh Khanh Street) is famous for seafood and grilled skewers. Cooking classes typically run half-day and can be booked for the afternoon instead.

Restaurant ideas for Day 2: Banh Mi Huynh Hoa (District 1, arguably the city's most famous banh mi — expect a queue), Quan Bui (District 1, sit-down Vietnamese), or Oc Dao or a Vinh Khanh Street seafood stall (District 4).

Day 3: Day Trip and Farewell Evening

TimeActivityNotes
Morning Half-day trip to the Cu Chi Tunnels (or, for a lighter option, the Mekong Delta at My Tho/Ben Tre) Cu Chi is about 1.5 hours by road; most tours leave around 8:00 and return by early afternoon. Book ahead. Wear closed shoes and light clothing.
Afternoon Return to the city, rest, then visit the Fine Arts Museum or take a cyclo ride through District 3 The Fine Arts Museum (a colonial-era mansion) is a calm, uncrowded stop. District 3 has leafy streets and local cafes.
Evening Farewell dinner and a river cruise or rooftop finale Saigon River dinner cruises depart from the Bach Dang wharf area. Alternatively, end at a rooftop bar with skyline views.

Restaurant ideas for Day 3: Cuc Gach Quan (District 1, rustic Vietnamese in a converted house — reserve ahead), Nha Hang Ngon (garden setting, wide menu of regional dishes), or Hum Vegetarian (if you want a meat-free finale).

Practical Tips

  • Getting around: Use Grab (the regional ride-hailing app) for cars and motorbike taxis; it is cheap and avoids haggling. Metered Vinasun and Mai Linh taxis are reliable — avoid unmarked cabs.
  • Traffic and crossings: Cross streets slowly and steadily; motorbikes flow around pedestrians. Do not make sudden movements.
  • Heat and rain: The dry season runs roughly December to April; the rainy season (May–November) brings short, heavy afternoon downpours. Carry water, sunscreen, and a compact rain jacket. Plan indoor activities for midday.
  • Money: The currency is the Vietnamese dong (VND). Carry small notes for markets and street food; many places are cash-only. ATMs are widely available in District 1.
  • Dress code: Cover shoulders and knees at temples and pagodas.
  • Safety: Petty theft (phone snatching from motorbikes) occurs — keep phones out of sight near roads and hold bags securely.
  • Language: English is common in tourist areas but limited elsewhere; a translation app helps. Learn a few phrases such as "xin chao" (hello) and "cam on" (thank you).
  • Booking: Reserve popular restaurants and Cu Chi/Mekong tours a day or two ahead, especially on weekends.
