London Weather by Month

London has a temperate maritime climate, which means it rarely gets extremely hot or cold—but it does mean the weather can be famously changeable. A sunny morning can turn into a drizzly afternoon and back again before dinner. Rainfall is spread fairly evenly across the year, so there's no true "dry season," though spring and early summer tend to be the sunniest and most pleasant. Temperatures are moderate year-round: winters hover just above freezing, while summers are warm but rarely oppressive. The guide below breaks down typical conditions month by month to help you plan your trip, pack wisely, and choose the best time to visit.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: May, June, and September. These months offer the sweet spot of mild-to-warm temperatures, longer daylight hours, lower rainfall than autumn, and manageable crowds compared with July and August.
  • Best for sunshine and festivals: June–August. Expect the warmest weather, outdoor events, and the longest days—but also peak prices and the largest crowds at major attractions.
  • Best for budget travelers: January–February and November. Hotel rates drop, attractions are quieter, and you can often find deals—just pack for cold, damp, and short days.
  • Best for festive atmosphere: late November–December. Christmas markets, lights, and seasonal events make up for the cold and early sunsets.
  • Avoid if you dislike crowds and heat: mid-July to late August, when popular sites and transport are at their busiest.

Practical Tips

  • Pack layers, not just a coat. London weather shifts quickly; a waterproof shell, a light sweater, and an umbrella will serve you better than one heavy garment.
  • Always carry a compact umbrella or rain jacket. Rain is frequent and often brief—showers pass quickly, so don't cancel plans over a grey sky.
  • Wear comfortable, waterproof shoes. You'll walk a lot, and cobblestones and pavements can be slick after rain.
  • Check the forecast daily, not weekly. London forecasts beyond a few days are unreliable; use a weather app each morning to plan outdoor activities.
  • Book peak-season trips early. If visiting June–August or around Christmas, reserve accommodation and major attractions (e.g., the Tower of London, Warner Bros. Studio Tour) well in advance.
  • Embrace indoor options. Museums, galleries, theatres, and pubs are perfect rainy-day backups—many major museums offer free entry.
  • Mind the daylight. In winter, sunset can be before 4 p.m., so plan outdoor sightseeing for the morning; in summer, daylight lasts past 9 p.m., giving you long evenings.
  • Use public transport in bad weather. The Tube and buses keep you dry and are often faster than walking in the rain.
