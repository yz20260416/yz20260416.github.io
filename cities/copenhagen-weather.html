Copenhagen Weather by Month

Copenhagen sits on the eastern coast of Denmark at roughly 55° north latitude, giving it a temperate maritime climate shaped by the North Atlantic Drift and the Baltic Sea. That means relatively mild winters for its latitude, cool and comfortable summers, and weather that can shift quickly in any season. Rainfall is spread fairly evenly across the year—there's no true dry season—though late summer and autumn tend to be the wettest. Daylight varies dramatically, from nearly 18 hours of light around the summer solstice to just 7 hours in late December, which significantly affects how much sightseeing you can pack into a day. The table below summarizes typical conditions month by month.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall (weather + atmosphere): May, June, and September. Mild temperatures, long daylight, and manageable crowds. June offers the magic of near-solstice light and lively Midsummer festivities.
  • Best for warm weather and events: July and August, with highs around 22°C (72°F). Expect peak prices and the most tourists, plus August's higher rainfall.
  • Best value: April–May and September–October bring lower prices, fewer visitors, and generally decent conditions—just pack rain gear.
  • Best for budget travelers who don't mind cold: November–February, though daylight is limited and many attractions run shorter hours.
  • Best for festive cheer: December, for Tivoli Gardens' Christmas season and the city's Christmas markets.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for all seasons in one day. Danish weather is famously changeable; a waterproof shell, layers, and sturdy walking shoes are wise year-round.
  • Always carry rain protection. Rain is spread evenly across the year, so even a sunny July forecast can turn showery by afternoon. A compact umbrella or rain jacket beats waiting it out.
  • Use daylight to your advantage. In winter, plan indoor activities (museums, design shops, cafés) and schedule outdoor sightseeing for the 10 a.m.–2 p.m. window when light is best. In summer, take advantage of 17+ hours of daylight for evening walks and harbour swims.
  • Book summer accommodation early. July and August are peak season; hotels and popular restaurants fill up weeks in advance.
  • Dress for wind. Copenhagen is breezy, especially near the harbour and on bridges. A windproof layer makes a real difference, particularly in autumn and winter.
  • Winter isn't extreme, but it's dark. Temperatures rarely drop far below freezing thanks to the maritime influence, but limited daylight (about 7 hours in December) can affect your itinerary—factor in cosy indoor stops like Tivoli, Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek, and the city's famous cafés.
  • Check the forecast daily. Danish meteorology is reliable for short-range forecasts; use apps like DMI (the Danish Meteorological Institute) for accurate local predictions.
