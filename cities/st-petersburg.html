Local Character

St. Petersburg's character is inseparable from water and light: it sits on the Neva delta, and the drawbridges over the Neva are raised each night to let ships pass, a spectacle visitors should plan an evening around since it strands late wanderers on whichever bank they're on. The city's name day traces to 29 June, the feast of apostles Peter and Paul, whose small wooden church was consecrated alongside the original citadel, making them the city's heavenly patrons; travelers here in late June will find the date woven into local observance. The city was the imperial capital from 1712 to 1918 (briefly yielding to Moscow 1728–1730) before the government moved back to Moscow after the October Revolution, which explains why so much of Russia's court architecture and the Hermitage's collections sit here rather than in the current capital. Its UNESCO-listed historic centre and the nickname 'Northern Capital' reflect that lingering imperial role.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. The Hermitage — One of the largest art museums in the world, housed in the imperial Winter Palace complex.
  2. Historic Centre of Saint Petersburg — A UNESCO World Heritage Site encompassing the city's imperial-era palaces, canals and monuments.
  3. Lakhta Center — The tallest skyscraper in Europe, standing on the Gulf of Finland shore.
  4. Neva River drawbridges — The city's nightly bridge openings let ships through and are a signature St. Petersburg sight.
  5. National Library of Russia — One of the country's major federal institutions and a landmark of the city's scholarly tradition.

FAQ

When are the White Nights?
The city barely darkens from late May through early July, with the June solstice period the brightest and busiest.
Why are the bridges raised at night?
The drawbridges over the Neva open to let ships pass, so plan your evening route — you may be stuck on one bank until morning.
What is the city's name day?
29 June, the feast of apostles Peter and Paul, the city's heavenly patrons, marked by local observance.
