Top Attractions in Hangzhou

Hangzhou, capital of Zhejiang Province, blends serene West Lake scenery with deep Buddhist heritage and well-preserved historic quarters. Visitors can explore ancient temples tucked into forested hills, wander Qing-era mansions and medicine halls, or cross the mighty Qiantang River on a landmark bridge. This guide highlights ten attractions most likely to appeal to international travelers, from celebrated monasteries to gardens and archaeological sites.

  1. Lingyin Temple
    This prominent Chan Buddhist temple near Hangzhou is renowned for its many pagodas and grottos, and is the largest of several temples in the Wulin Mountains with their famous religious rock carvings. Its name is often translated as "Temple of the Soul's Retreat."
    Tip: Arrive early in the morning to avoid crowds, and allow extra time to explore the surrounding grottos and carvings.
  2. Hupao Spring (Dreaming of Tiger Spring)
    This spring and park in southwestern Hangzhou has water that seeps from quartzite and has been prized for centuries as among the finest in China. It is especially popular for brewing local Longjing tea and is counted among the Ten Scenes of West Lake.
    Tip: Visit in spring or autumn and combine your trip with a tea-tasting session nearby.
  3. Former Residence of Hu Xueyan
    This nationally protected traditional Chinese mansion and Jiangnan-style garden sits at No. 18 Yuanbao Street in Shangcheng District, just southeast of West Lake. It forms part of the city's Qinghefang Historic District.
    Tip: Pair your visit with a stroll through the adjacent Qinghefang Historic District for shops and snacks.
  4. Hu Qing Yu Tang
    Once an important center of traditional Chinese medicine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, its site at 95 Dajing Lane is now a TCM museum and drugstore with a reputation rivaling Beijing's Tongrentang.
    Tip: Located in the lively Hefang Street area; go on a weekday to browse the museum and dispensary more comfortably.
  5. Qiantang River Bridge
    This combined road and railway bridge spans the Qiantang River at Hangzhou in Zhejiang Province. It is a striking piece of engineering and a recognizable city landmark.
    Tip: Best viewed at sunset from the riverbanks; note that it is an active transport bridge rather than a pedestrian attraction.
  6. Temple of the Qian Kings
    This temple honors the Qian family, who ruled as kings of Wuyue in the 10th century between the Tang and Song dynasties, and its grounds include the tomb of Qian Liu. Though often associated with Qian Liu specifically, it officially commemorates the entire dynasty.
    Tip: Combine with a walk along West Lake's southern shore, where the temple is conveniently located.
  7. Hangzhou Botanical Garden
    This large public botanical garden is located in Hangzhou City, Zhejiang Province. It offers a peaceful green escape with diverse plant collections.
    Tip: Spring is the best season for blossoms; the garden is a short ride from West Lake and makes a relaxed half-day outing.
  8. Hangzhou Zoo
    Hangzhou Zoo is located in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province. It is a family-friendly attraction within easy reach of the city's scenic western areas.
    Tip: Visit on weekday mornings when animals are most active and crowds are lighter.
  9. Haichao Temple
    This Buddhist temple in Shangcheng District was, alongside Lingyin, Jing and Zhaoqing temples, one of the Four Buddhist Temples in Hangzhou. It offers a quieter alternative to the city's better-known monasteries.
    Tip: Dress respectfully and check local opening hours, as smaller temples may keep limited schedules.
  10. Tiger Cave Kiln
    Excavations at this archaeological site helped identify a place of origin for Southern Song dynasty Guan ware, the "official" ceramics made for the imperial court. The site and associated ceramic sites are under the control of the Hangzhou Southern Song Guan Kiln Museum.
    Tip: Combine your visit with the Southern Song Guan Kiln Museum to see the ceramics in context.

Practical tips

  • Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer the most comfortable weather for temple and garden visits.
  • Many temples and museums keep shorter hours than parks; confirm opening times before setting out, especially for smaller sites.
  • Group West Lake–area sights (Hupao Spring, Temple of the Qian Kings, Hu Xueyan Residence) to minimize travel time.
  • Dress modestly when visiting active religious sites such as Lingyin and Haichao temples.
  • Use public transport or taxis between the western scenic areas and the historic districts, as the attractions are spread across the city.
