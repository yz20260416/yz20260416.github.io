Hangzhou Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province, is one of China's most celebrated destinations, famed for its poetic West Lake scenery, ancient Buddhist temples, and refined tea culture. As a former imperial capital and the southern terminus of the Grand Canal, the city blends centuries of history with modern convenience. This guide covers the ten attractions visitors should prioritize, with practical details on tickets, hours, and how to make the most of each site.

  1. West Lake (Xihu)

    Hangzhou's signature attraction and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, West Lake is a 6-square-kilometer freshwater lake framed by causeways, pagodas, and willow-lined shores. Classic views include Su Causeway, Broken Bridge, and the Three Pools Mirroring the Moon. Walking and cycling the perimeter are free; boat rides and some gardens charge admission.

    TicketFree to enter the lake area; boat cruises about CNY 55–70; some garden islands charge CNY 20–55
    HoursOpen 24 hours; boats typically 8:00–17:00
    TipsVisit at dawn for misty scenery and fewer crowds. The "Ten Scenes of West Lake" are best seen in different seasons—Lingering Snow on Broken Bridge in winter, Lotus in the Breeze in summer.

  2. Lingyin Temple and Feilai Feng

    One of China's oldest and most important Buddhist temples, founded in 328 AD and nestled in forested hills west of West Lake. The adjacent Feilai Feng (Peak Flown from Afar) features hundreds of Buddhist carvings cut into limestone cliffs, some dating to the 10th century.

    TicketFeilai Feng scenic area CNY 45; temple admission an additional CNY 30
    HoursApproximately 7:00–18:00 (varies by season)
    TipsArrive early to avoid tour groups. Dress modestly inside temple halls, and note that photography is restricted in some worship areas.

  3. Leifeng Pagoda

    A five-story octagonal pagoda on the south shore of West Lake, rebuilt in 2002 on the site of the original 10th-century structure. Escalators and elevators make it accessible, and the upper levels offer panoramic lake views. Exhibits inside display relics from the original pagoda's underground chamber.

    TicketAbout CNY 40
    HoursRoughly 8:00–20:00 (seasonal variation)
    TipsGo near sunset for the best light over the lake. The pagoda is tied to the famous Legend of the White Snake, adding cultural context to the visit.

  4. Xixi National Wetland Park

    China's first national wetland park, a 11.5-square-kilometer network of waterways, reed beds, and traditional villages about 5 kilometers west of the city center. Boat rides through the channels are the highlight, and the park is a haven for birdwatchers.

    TicketPark admission about CNY 80; boat rides extra (CNY 60–100)
    HoursApproximately 8:00–17:30
    TipsAllow at least half a day. Spring and autumn are ideal; summer brings lush greenery but also heat and mosquitoes.

  5. China National Tea Museum and Longjing Tea Village

    Located in the Longjing (Dragon Well) tea-growing hills, the museum explains China's tea history and production. Nearby Meijiawu and Longjing villages let visitors walk through terraced tea fields, taste freshly picked Dragon Well tea, and learn about roasting techniques.

    TicketMuseum free; tea village visits free, tea tastings and purchases vary
    HoursMuseum about 9:00–16:30, closed Mondays
    TipsSpring (late March to April) is harvest season and the most rewarding time to visit. Buy tea only from reputable shops and be wary of aggressive sales at roadside stalls.

  6. Hefang Street (Historical District)

    A restored Qing-era commercial street near Wushan Hill, packed with traditional pharmacies, silk shops, teahouses, and snack vendors. It is a lively place to sample Hangzhou specialties such as beggar's chicken and lotus root stuffed with glutinous rice.

    TicketFree
    HoursOpen all day; shops generally 9:00–22:00
    TipsVisit in the evening when lanterns are lit. Combine with a walk up Wushan Hill for city views.

  7. Six Harmonies Pagoda (Liuhe Pagoda)

    An octagonal brick-and-wood pagoda originally built in 970 AD on the north bank of the Qiantang River. Its seven exterior stories (13 interior levels) offer views of the river and the Qiantang Bridge. The site also includes a small park and exhibits on pagoda architecture.

    TicketAbout CNY 20 (park); pagoda entry may be included or small extra
    HoursApproximately 6:30–18:00
    TipsTime a visit with the Qiantang tidal bore (especially around the 18th day of the 8th lunar month) for a dramatic natural spectacle.

  8. Grand Canal (Hangzhou Section) and Gongchen Bridge

    The southern end of the UNESCO-listed Beijing–Hangzhou Grand Canal runs through Hangzhou. The historic Gongchen Bridge area has been revitalized with museums, workshops, and riverside promenades, and canal boat tours offer a relaxed way to see traditional waterfront neighborhoods.

    TicketFree to walk; canal boat tours roughly CNY 30–100 depending on route
    HoursPublic areas open all day; boat services daytime hours
    TipsEvening boat rides are atmospheric. The nearby Canal Museum (free) provides useful historical context.

  9. Songcheng Park and "The Romance of the Song Dynasty" Show

    A theme park recreating Song dynasty life, with street performances, folk crafts, and a large-scale evening stage production that is one of China's best-known cultural shows. It suits families and travelers interested in immersive historical entertainment.

    TicketPark plus show roughly CNY 300–320; park-only tickets cheaper
    HoursPark about 9:00–21:00; main show typically early evening
    TipsBook show tickets in advance, especially on weekends and holidays. Arrive a couple of hours early to explore the park.
