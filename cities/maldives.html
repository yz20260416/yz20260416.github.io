Best Time to Visit Maldives

The Maldives is a tropical island nation of roughly 1,200 coral islands grouped into 26 atolls in the Indian Ocean, famed for overwater villas, turquoise lagoons and world-class diving. Visit during the dry northeast monsoon (December to April) for calm seas, clear skies and the best snorkelling conditions, though the wetter months offer lower rates and excellent manta and whale shark sightings.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Male City and Fish Market — The compact capital offers colourful markets, the 17th-century Hukuru Miskiy mosque and a lively local ferry scene worth a half-day stop.
  2. Banana Reef — One of the Maldives' first protected dive sites, known for coral gardens, overhangs and schools of batfish and fusiliers.
  3. Manta Point, North Male Atoll — A cleaning station where reef manta rays gather seasonally, offering reliable snorkelling and diving encounters.
  4. Ari Atoll — A large atoll famed for year-round whale shark sightings and manta rays, with numerous resort islands and dive sites.
  5. Vaadhoo Island (Sea of Stars) — A Raa Atoll island where bioluminescent plankton light up the shoreline on dark nights, best seen from April to October.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR, Rf); US dollars widely accepted at resorts
  • Language: Dhivehi (English is widely spoken in resorts and tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: MVT (UTC+5), with many resorts operating on UTC+6
  • Visa: Most nationalities receive a free 30-day tourist visa on arrival with a valid passport, confirmed accommodation and onward ticket.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit the Maldives?
December to April offers the driest, sunniest weather and calmest seas, while May to November brings more rain, lower prices and strong manta and whale shark activity.
Do I need a visa to enter the Maldives?
Most visitors receive a free 30-day tourist visa on arrival, provided they hold a valid passport, pre-booked accommodation and a return or onward ticket.
Can I visit the Maldives on a budget?
Yes, guesthouses on local islands such as Maafushi and Dhigurah offer affordable stays and excursions, though alcohol is not permitted on inhabited local islands.
