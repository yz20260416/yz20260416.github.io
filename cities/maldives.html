Best Time to Visit Maldives

The Maldives is an archipelagic nation of 26 atolls stretching across the equator in the Indian Ocean, with Malé—traditionally called the "King's Island"—as its capital and most populated city. Visitors come for its low-lying coral islands, white sand beaches, and world-class diving, best enjoyed during the dry northeast monsoon from December to April.

Local Character

Malé, the capital, was traditionally known as the "King's Island," the seat from which ancient royal dynasties ruled the archipelago—a detail worth knowing because it explains why this small, densely packed city, rather than any resort island, remains the country's historical and administrative heart. Documented outside contact began around 947 CE with Arab travellers, and by the 12th century Islam had reached the islands partly through the influence of Arab and Persian Indian Ocean traders; this trade-driven conversion shaped Maldivian identity and is still visible in the capital's old coral-stone mosques and Friday prayers. The country's fishing heritage, long the dominant economic activity before tourism, is the reason Maldivian daily life and cuisine centre on tuna—visitors eating at local cafés will notice smoked, dried, and curried tuna dishes that trace directly to this livelihood. With an average elevation of just 1.5 metres and a highest natural point of only 2.4 metres, the Maldives is the world's lowest-lying country, which gives even a short visit an awareness of how closely island life is tied to the sea.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Malé Old Friday Mosque — A 17th-century coral-stone mosque with intricate carved decoration, reflecting the Islamic heritage that reached the islands by the 12th century.
  2. Malé Fish Market — A bustling harbour market where the day's tuna catch is landed and sold, showing the fishing trade that remains the country's second-largest economic sector.
  3. Ihavandhippolhu Atoll — The northernmost atoll in the Maldivian chain, marking the top of the 26-atoll stretch that runs from here to Addu in the south.
  4. Addu Atoll — The southernmost atoll of the archipelago, sitting near the equator and offering a quieter counterpoint to the central atolls.
  5. Mount Villingili — A modest rise cited by some sources as the country's highest point at about 5.1 metres, a striking illustration of the Maldives' extreme low elevation.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR, Rf)
  • Language: Dhivehi (English is widely used in tourist areas)
  • Time Zone: MVT (UTC+5)
  • Visa: Most international visitors receive a free 30-day tourist visa on arrival, provided they hold a valid passport, return ticket, and confirmed accommodation.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit the Maldives?
December to April, the dry northeast monsoon season, offers the sunniest and driest conditions, though it is also the busiest and most expensive period.
How many atolls make up the Maldives?
The country's chain consists of 26 atolls, stretching from Ihavandhippolhu Atoll in the north to Addu Atoll in the south across the equator.
How far is the Maldives from the Asian mainland?
The islands lie about 750 kilometres (470 miles) southwest of India and Sri Lanka, near the southeastern boundary of the Arabian Sea.
