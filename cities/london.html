Local Character

London's ancient core, the City of London, was founded by the Romans as Londinium and still retains its medieval boundaries, a square mile that today operates as Europe's major financial centre. A visitor should care because walking its compact street plan reveals Roman foundations beneath modern towers, making it the most historically layered square mile in Britain. To its west, the City of Westminster has hosted national government and parliament for centuries, so the short stretch between the two 'cities' packs royal, parliamentary and commercial history into a single walk. London's growth exploded in the 19th century, when it became the world's largest city, and the name 'London' then spread to the metropolis around the City, historically split among Middlesex, Essex, Surrey, Kent and Hertfordshire. Since 1965 Greater London has been governed by 33 local authorities and the Greater London Authority. The city's diversity encompasses over 300 languages, and the London Underground, opened in 1863, remains the world's oldest rapid transit system, a working museum of Victorian engineering that visitors use daily.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Tower of London — A historic royal fortress on the Thames holding the Crown Jewels and nearly a millennium of history.
  2. British Museum — A vast collection of world art and artefacts, from the Rosetta Stone to Egyptian mummies, free to enter.
  3. Westminster Abbey — The Gothic abbey where monarchs have been crowned and buried for centuries, beside the Houses of Parliament.
  4. Tate Modern — Britain's national gallery of modern art, housed in a converted riverside power station.
  5. London Eye — A giant observation wheel on the South Bank offering sweeping views across the Thames and the city.

FAQ

How do I get around London?
The London Underground, the world's oldest rapid transit system, plus buses and trains cover the city; contactless payment cards work on most services.
When is the best time to visit London?
May, June and September offer the best balance of mild weather and manageable crowds, while July and August are warm but busiest.
Are London's museums free?
Many major museums and galleries, including the British Museum and Tate Modern, offer free general admission.
