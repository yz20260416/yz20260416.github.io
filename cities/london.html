Best Time to Visit London

London is a sprawling, history-rich capital where royal palaces, world-class museums, and vibrant neighbourhoods sit alongside cutting-edge food and theatre. Visit in late spring or early autumn for mild weather and manageable crowds, though the city's cultural calendar offers reasons to come year-round.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. British Museum — A vast free museum housing world treasures from the Rosetta Stone to Egyptian mummies.
  2. Tower of London — A historic fortress on the Thames where the Crown Jewels and centuries of royal intrigue reside.
  3. Westminster Abbey — A Gothic masterpiece of coronations and royal weddings that is also the resting place of many famous figures.
  4. The Shard — London's tallest skyscraper offers sweeping views over the city from its viewing galleries.
  5. Camden Market — A lively labyrinth of street-food stalls, vintage shops, and crafts beside the Regent's Canal.

Practical Information

  • Currency: British Pound (GBP, £)
  • Language: English
  • Time Zone: GMT (UTC+0), with BST (UTC+1) in summer
  • Visa: Many international visitors can enter visa-free for short stays, but requirements vary by nationality, so check the UK government website before travelling.

FAQ

What is the best way to get around London?
Use the Tube and buses with a contactless card or Oyster card, as public transport is extensive and driving is best avoided.
How much does a typical day in London cost?
Expect roughly £80-150 per person per day for budget-to-midrange accommodation, meals, and transport, with many museums free to enter.
Do I need cash in London?
Card and contactless payment are accepted almost everywhere, so cash is rarely needed but useful for small market stalls.
