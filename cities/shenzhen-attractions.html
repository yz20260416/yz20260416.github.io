Top Attractions in Shenzhen

Shenzhen has transformed from a small border town into one of China's most dynamic cities, and its attractions reflect that mix of modern ambition and older heritage. Visitors can ride a giant observation wheel over Qianhai Bay, wander historic villages and border streets, explore world-class museums and art districts, or escape to forested mountains and reservoirs. This guide covers ten highlights most likely to appeal to international travelers.

  1. Bay Glory — This giant cantilevered observation wheel at Qianhai Bay, operated by OCT, opened to the public in April 2021 and offers sweeping views over the water and city skyline. It is one of Shenzhen's most striking modern landmarks.
  2. Chung Ying Street — A unique street on the Hong Kong–Shenzhen border within Sha Tau Kok and Shatoujiao, where one side belongs to Hong Kong and the other to mainland China. It is a fascinating spot for understanding the region's cross-border history.
  3. Dafen Village — Since 1989 this suburb of Buji in Longgang has grown into an artists' village specializing in replicas of masterworks and outsourced original art. It is a lively, unusual destination for art lovers and curious browsers.
  4. Fenghuang Village — Also known as Fenghuang Ancient Village, this historic urban village in Fuyong, Bao'an, was founded by descendants of the Southern Song scholar-general Wen Tianxiang. More than 60 Ming and Qing dynasty buildings remain, including the six-storey Wen Tower built in the late 18th century.
  5. Guangming Culture and Art Center — Completed in 2020, this 130,000-square-meter public facility in Guangming District houses a 1,500-seat theater, a 500-seat concert hall, an art gallery, a library, and exhibition halls. It is a major hub for culture and the arts in the city.
  6. Hongfa Temple — A Buddhist temple set within Fairylake Botanical Garden in Luohu District, offering a peaceful retreat amid greenery. It is one of Shenzhen's most visited religious sites.
  7. MOCAUP (Shenzhen Museum of Contemporary Art and Urban Planning) — Opened in 2016 in Futian District and designed by Coop Himmelb(l)au, this museum features permanent exhibitions on Shenzhen's urban planning, landmarks, and its history in the reform and opening-up era. The striking architecture alone is worth the visit.
  8. Mount Yangtai — Also known as Mount Yangtai Forest Park, this mountain at the junction of Bao'an and Nanshan Districts rises to 587.3 meters, the highest peak in western Shenzhen. It was rated one of Shenzhen's eight scenic spots by the city government in 2008.
  9. OCT Harbour — This large retail and entertainment complex covers roughly 1.25 square kilometers and features a manmade lake and canals, hotels, and a shopping mall. Opened in 2011, it is a popular spot for strolling, dining, and waterfront views.
  10. Shenzhen Art Museum — Founded in 1976 as the Shenzhen Exhibition Hall and renamed in 1987, this public art museum now has its main new venue in Longhua District alongside its earlier Donghu venue in Luohu. It is a key stop for contemporary and modern Chinese art.

Practical tips

  • Check opening days and hours in advance, as museums and cultural centers in Shenzhen often close on Mondays and may require advance booking.
  • For Chung Ying Street, bring your passport and allow extra time for border-area procedures, as access is controlled.
  • Visit Mount Yangtai and Hongfa Temple in the cooler morning hours, and wear comfortable shoes for walking and hiking.
  • Ride Bay Glory near sunset for the best views over Qianhai Bay, and confirm ticket prices online beforehand.
  • Combine OCT Harbour with nearby Nanshan District sights for a full day of waterfront dining and entertainment.
