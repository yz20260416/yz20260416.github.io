Guangzhou Travel Guide: Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Guangzhou, the capital of Guangdong Province and the largest city in southern China, blends a 2,000-year trading history with a modern skyline and some of the country's best food. Known as Canton to English speakers, the city rewards visitors with Lingnan gardens, riverside promenades, colonial-era architecture on Shamian Island, and easy access via Baiyun International Airport and high-speed rail from Hong Kong and Shenzhen. This guide covers the ten attractions most worth your time, with ticket prices, opening hours, and practical tips for visiting.

  1. Canton Tower

    At 600 meters, Canton Tower is the city's defining landmark, a twisted lattice structure on the south bank of the Pearl River. Observation decks at 450 and 488 meters offer sweeping views of the skyline and river, and the tower is especially photogenic after dark when its LED facade lights up. The 488-meter outdoor deck includes a "bubble tram" and a Ferris wheel on the roof.

    TicketObservation decks: approximately CNY 150–228 depending on level; rooftop attractions cost extra
    HoursDaily, roughly 9:00–22:30 (last entry around 22:00); hours vary seasonally
    TipsVisit 30–60 minutes before sunset for both daylight and night views; buy tickets online to skip queues; the tower is a short walk from Canton Tower Station (Metro Lines 3 and APM).

  2. Chen Clan Ancestral Hall (Chenjiaci)

    Built in the 1890s as a clan academy, this is one of the finest examples of Lingnan architecture in China. Its halls are covered in intricate wood carvings, stone reliefs, brick carvings, and colorful ceramic figurines along the roof ridges. It now houses the Guangdong Folk Art Museum, with displays of embroidery, paper cutting, and ivory carving.

    TicketCNY 10
    HoursTuesday–Sunday, 9:00–17:00 (closed Mondays)
    TipsAllow 1–1.5 hours; go early to avoid tour groups; the hall is at Chen Clan Academy Station (Metro Line 1) and pairs well with a visit to nearby Liwan District.

  3. Shamian Island

    A compact sandbank island that served as the British and French concession from the mid-19th century until World War II. Tree-lined avenues are flanked by colonial villas, churches, and consulates, making it one of the most pleasant places in the city for a stroll. It is also a popular spot for wedding photos and riverside picnics.

    TicketFree (island is open to the public)
    HoursOpen 24 hours; most buildings are exterior-only viewing
    TipsVisit on a weekday morning for quieter streets; combine with the Pearl River cruise docks nearby; the island is reachable via Huangsha Station (Metro Line 1) or Culture Park Station (Line 6).

  4. Yuexiu Park and the Five Rams Statue

    Guangzhou's largest urban park, centered on Yuexiu Hill, contains the iconic Five Rams Statue (the city's symbol), the Zhenhai Tower (now the Guangzhou Museum), and remnants of the Ming-era city wall. It is a green, hilly escape in the middle of the city and a favorite with locals for morning exercise.

    TicketPark: free; Guangzhou Museum (Zhenhai Tower): CNY 10
    HoursPark: daily, roughly 6:00–21:00; museum: 9:00–17:30, closed Mondays
    TipsEnter from the Jiefang North Road gate for the shortest route to the Five Rams Statue; wear comfortable shoes for the slopes; Yuexiu Park Station (Metro Line 2) is at the main entrance.

  5. Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall

    Built in 1931 in memory of the revolutionary leader Sun Yat-sen, this octagonal hall combines traditional Chinese roof forms with a modern concrete structure. The interior seats thousands and hosts concerts and events; exhibits trace Sun's life and the 1911 revolution. The landscaped grounds and the hall's blue-tiled roof are a photographer's favorite.

    TicketCNY 10 for the main hall and exhibitions
    HoursDaily, 8:00–18:00
    TipsCheck the schedule for performances; the hall is a 10-minute walk from Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall Station (Metro Line 2) and close to Yuexiu Park, so plan them together.

  6. Temple of the Six Banyan Trees and Flower Pagoda

    A working Buddhist temple founded in the 6th century, named for the banyan trees planted by the poet Su Shi. Its centerpiece is the nine-story Flower Pagoda, which visitors can climb for views over the old city. The temple remains active, with incense burning throughout the courtyards.

    TicketCNY 5 (pagoda climb included)
    HoursDaily, 8:00–17:30
    TipsDress modestly and keep quiet in worship areas; the pagoda stairs are steep and narrow; the temple is near Ximenkou Station (Metro Line 1) in Liwan District.

  7. Sacred Heart Cathedral (Shishi Catholic Church)

    Completed in 1888, this Gothic Revival cathedral is one of only a handful of all-granite churches in the world, built from stone quarried on the site. Its twin spires and rose windows stand in sharp contrast to the surrounding dense neighborhood, and it remains an active parish.

    TicketFree
    HoursGenerally open to visitors daily, roughly 8:30–17:30; closed during Mass times
    TipsPhotography is restricted during services; the cathedral is a short walk from Yide Road Station (Metro Line 6) and pairs with a walk through the nearby dried-seafood market streets.

  8. Guangdong Museum

    A striking modern building in Zhujiang New Town, shaped like a lacquered box, housing major collections of Chinese ceramics, calligraphy, Lingnan art, and regional history. Its temporary exhibitions are often excellent, and the building itself is an architectural highlight of the new city center.

    TicketFree with advance online reservation (ID or passport required)
    HoursTuesday–Sunday, 9:00–17:00 (closed Mondays; last entry around 16:00)
    TipsBook tickets on the museum's official WeChat account a few days ahead, especially on weekends; the museum is next to Huacheng Square and close to Canton Tower, making a convenient pairing.
