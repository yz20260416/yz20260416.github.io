Top Attractions in Guangzhou

Guangzhou, the bustling capital of Guangdong Province, blends centuries of trading history with striking modern architecture. From the soaring Canton Tower to tranquil temple grounds and leafy urban parks, the city offers a wide range of experiences for international visitors. Here are ten of the most compelling attractions to include on a Guangzhou itinerary.

  1. Canton Tower

    This 600-metre multipurpose observation tower in Haizhu District became operational in 2010 in time for the Asian Games and briefly held the title of tallest tower in the world. Its viewing decks and distinctive twisted form make it the city's signature landmark.

    Tip: Visit around sunset for daylight views that transition into the city's illuminated skyline.

  2. Baiyun Mountain (Guangdong)

    The Guangzhou portion of Baiyun Mountain is the city's classic green escape, a scenic hill area overlooking the urban sprawl. It is a popular spot for hiking and cable-car rides with panoramic views.

    Tip: Go early in the morning on weekdays to avoid crowds and enjoy cooler hiking weather.

  3. Nengren Temple (Guangzhou)

    Located on Mount Baiyun, Nengren Temple is a historic Buddhist temple set within the mountain's peaceful surroundings. It pairs naturally with a visit to Baiyun Mountain's trails and viewpoints.

    Tip: Combine your temple visit with the Baiyun Mountain cable car for an easy half-day outing.

  4. Guangzhou Opera House

    Designed by Zaha Hadid and opened in 2010, this striking riverside opera house is a landmark of contemporary architecture in Zhujiang New Town. Its futuristic form makes it a highlight even for visitors who skip a performance.

    Tip: View it at night when the facade is lit; check the schedule if you want to catch a show.

  5. Huacheng Square (Flower City Square)

    Guangzhou's largest city square stretches along the central axis of Zhujiang New Town, with Haixinsha Island to its south. The Opera House, Guangdong Museum, and Guangzhou Library all cluster around it, making it a convenient cultural hub.

    Tip: Use the underground APM line to hop between the square's museums and nearby sights.

  6. CITIC Plaza

    Completed in 1997, this 80-storey, 391-metre office skyscraper in Tianhe District was once the tallest concrete building in the world. It remains a defining feature of Guangzhou's modern skyline.

    Tip: Photograph it from Huacheng Square or the Tianhe sports area for the best skyline framing.

  7. Canton Fair Complex

    Situated on Pazhou Island, this vast convention centre is possibly the largest in the world and hosts the renowned Canton Fair. Even outside trade events, its scale and riverside setting are impressive.

    Tip: Avoid visiting during fair seasons unless attending; check the Canton Fair calendar before booking hotels nearby.

  8. Lotus Hill (Lianhuashan)

    This 4A-level national park in Panyu covers 2.33 square kilometres and rises to a 108-metre peak, with Shizi Lake to its west. Its name comes from a local legend of a dragon in the South Sea.

    Tip: Allow a full day; it is about 30 km from central Guangzhou, so plan transport in advance.

  9. Sanyuan Palace

    This Taoist temple in Yuexiu District is dedicated to the Three Great Emperor-Officials. It offers a quieter, traditional counterpoint to the city's modern districts.

    Tip: Visit in the morning and dress modestly as a mark of respect at an active place of worship.

  10. Our Lady of Lourdes Chapel, Shamian Island

    This 20th-century Gothic church sits on Shamian Island in Liwan District, a historic neighbourhood known for its colonial-era streetscape. The chapel is a photogenic stop while exploring the island on foot.

    Tip: Pair it with a leisurely walk around Shamian Island; weekday mornings are calmest for photos.

Practical tips

  • Guangzhou's summers are hot and humid; spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons for sightseeing.
  • Use the metro to reach most major sights; the APM line serves the Zhujiang New Town cluster (Opera House, Huacheng Square, CITIC Plaza).
  • Check the Canton Fair calendar, as hotel prices and crowds rise sharply during the spring and autumn sessions.
  • Dress modestly when visiting temples such as Sanyuan Palace and Nengren Temple.
  • Allow a full day for outlying sights like Lotus Hill and Baiyun Mountain, and start early to beat the heat.
