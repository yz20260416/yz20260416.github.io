Melbourne 3-Day Itinerary

Melbourne rewards travelers who slow down and wander. Australia's cultural capital blends laneway street art, world-class coffee, Victorian-era architecture, and a food scene shaped by waves of migration. This 3-day itinerary balances the city's greatest hits with neighborhood exploration, assuming you're based in the CBD or nearby (most attractions are walkable or a short tram ride away). It works best for first-time visitors with three full days on the ground.

Day 1 — The City Core: Laneways, Markets & the Yarra

Time Activity Notes
8:00 AM Breakfast at a laneway café Try Patricia Coffee Brewers (Little Bourke St) for a standing-room flat white, or Hardware Société for a sit-down brunch (book ahead on weekends).
9:30 AM Laneway street art walk Self-guided loop through Hosier Lane, AC/DC Lane, and Union Lane. Free; go early to avoid crowds.
11:00 AM Queen Victoria Market Australia's largest open-air market. Graze the deli hall; the market is closed Mondays and Wednesdays.
1:00 PM Lunch at the market or nearby Grab a borek or hot jam doughnut at the market, or sit down at Chin Chin (Flinders Lane) for modern Thai — arrive early or expect a wait.
2:30 PM National Gallery of Victoria (NGV) Free general entry to the NGV International on St Kilda Road; the stained-glass ceiling is a highlight. Closed Tuesdays.
4:30 PM Royal Botanic Gardens & Shrine of Remembrance Walk from the NGV through the gardens to the Shrine for city views from the balcony.
6:30 PM Southbank promenade & dinner Stroll the Yarra at golden hour. Dinner options: Supernormal (Asian-inspired, Flinders Lane), Maha (modern Middle Eastern), or casual dumplings in Chinatown.
9:00 PM Optional: rooftop bar Naked in the Sky (Fitzroy) or Rooftop Bar at Curtin House for skyline views.

Day 2 — Neighborhoods: Fitzroy, Carlton & the Museum

Time Activity Notes
8:30 AM Breakfast in Fitzroy Lune Croissanterie (Rose St) for cult-favorite croissants — queue early; or Industry Beans for coffee and a full breakfast.
10:00 AM Fitzroy & Collingwood wander Browse Brunswick Street boutiques, Gertrude Street galleries, and Smith Street vintage shops. Stop at Rose Street Artists' Market if visiting on a weekend.
12:30 PM Lunch on Smith or Gertrude Street Huxtaburger for burgers, Shop 225 for pizza, or Lentil as Anything-style community cafés nearby (check current status).
2:00 PM Melbourne Museum & Carlton Gardens Excellent natural history and First Peoples galleries; the adjacent Royal Exhibition Building is a UNESCO site. Allow 2 hours.
4:30 PM Lygon Street, Carlton Melbourne's Italian heartland — espresso, gelato, and bookshops. Brunetti for pastries.
6:30 PM Dinner in Carlton or back in the CBD DOC Pizza & Mozzarella Bar (Carlton) for authentic Italian, or Flower Drum (CBD) for a splurge Cantonese dinner — book well ahead.
8:30 PM Evening option Catch a show at the Arts Centre Melbourne, or a film at the historic Astor Theatre (check listings).

Day 3 — St Kilda, the Bay & Farewell

Time Activity Notes
8:30 AM Breakfast in St Kilda St Kilda Pier Kiosk area cafés, or Monarch Cakes for a classic slice. Take tram 96 or 16 from the CBD (~25 min).
9:30 AM St Kilda Pier & foreshore Walk the pier to the breakwater for penguin-viewing at dusk (return later if keen) and bay views.
10:30 AM Acland Street & Luna Park Art-deco cake shops on Acland Street; Luna Park's heritage entrance is free to visit (rides extra).
12:00 PM Lunch Donovans (waterfront, book ahead) or Cicciolina on Acland Street for Mediterranean plates.
1:30 PM Optional: Brighton Bathing Boxes Short train ride (Sandringham line) to Brighton Beach for the famous colorful huts — 30–45 min round trip plus stroll.
