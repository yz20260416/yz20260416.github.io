Local Character

Lijiang's character is rooted in the Naxi people, who migrated into the area during the Northern and Southern Dynasties period and shaped its towns and trade. One detail a generic guide misses: Baisha Old Town, just north of the main old town, served as the political, commercial and cultural center for the Naxi and neighboring ethnic groups for 450 years, from 658 to 1107 AD. Visitors should care because Baisha predates the famous Old Town and holds the Dabaoji Palace, built in 658 AD, whose Baisha Frescoes reward a half-day trip. A second detail: Baisha was once the southwest's center of silk embroidery and a key stop on the Ancient Southern Silk Road, also called the Ancient Tea and Horse Road, which ran from Burma through Lijiang. That history explains why embroidery studios and market culture remain part of local life, and why the old trade route still shapes how the town connects to the wider region. Lijiang is also a multi-ethnic settlement of 22 minorities besides the Han, so festivals, food and crafts vary from valley to valley.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Old Town of Lijiang — The UNESCO World Heritage old town, known for its mix of historical architecture styles and complex ancient water-supply system.
  2. Yulong Snow Mountain — A major snow-capped landmark and one of Lijiang's most recognized tourist attractions.
  3. Tiger Leaping Gorge — A dramatic river gorge counted among Lijiang's signature natural attractions.
  4. Baisha Old Town — The former Naxi political and cultural center for 450 years, home to the Dabaoji Palace and its Baisha Frescoes.
  5. Laojun Mountain — A scenic mountain area listed among Lijiang's widely recognized tourist attractions.

FAQ

What makes Lijiang's Old Town special?
It is a UNESCO Heritage Site containing a mixture of historical architecture styles and a complex, ancient water-supply system.
How many World Heritage Sites does Lijiang have?
Lijiang is the only prefecture-level city with three World Heritage Sites.
When is the best time to visit Lijiang?
March, April and October offer the best balance of mild, dry weather and manageable crowds, while July and August are wettest and busiest.
