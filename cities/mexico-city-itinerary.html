Mexico City 3-Day Itinerary

Mexico City is one of the largest and most culturally dense capitals on earth — a place where Aztec ruins sit beneath colonial palaces, world-class museums line leafy boulevards, and some of the hemisphere's best food is served from street carts and white-tablecloth dining rooms alike. Three days is enough to hit the essentials without rushing, provided you plan around the city's two constants: altitude (2,240 m / 7,350 ft) and traffic. This itinerary balances history, art, food, and neighborhood wandering, with realistic travel times and restaurant picks for every meal.

Day 1 — Historic Center & Zócalo

TimeActivityNotes
8:30 AM Breakfast at Café El Jarocho (Centro branch) or Panadería Rosetta (Roma Norte, if staying nearby) Start light — altitude can affect appetite. Try a guayaba pastry and café de olla.
9:30 AM – 12:00 PM Zócalo & Templo Mayor Begin at the vast main square, then tour the Templo Mayor ruins and museum (the Aztec main temple). Arrive early to beat tour groups.
12:00 – 1:30 PM Metropolitan Cathedral and National Palace (Diego Rivera murals) Free entry to the Palacio Nacional murals; bring ID. Cathedral is across the square.
1:30 – 3:00 PM Lunch at El Cardenal (Palma St. branch) Classic CDMX institution — order the concha with nata, mole, or chiles en nogada (seasonal). Reserve ahead.
3:00 – 5:30 PM Palacio de Bellas Artes + Alameda Central walk Catch the murals inside (Rivera, Siqueiros) and the art-deco interior; stroll the park afterward.
6:00 – 8:00 PM Rooftop sunset at Terraza Catedral or Balcón del Zócalo Great views over the square as lights come on.
8:30 PM Dinner at Azul Histórico (Centro) Regional Mexican cuisine in a beautiful courtyard; try the tlayuda or mole sampler.

Day 2 — Chapultepec, Polanco & Anthropology

TimeActivityNotes
8:00 AM Breakfast at Lardo (Condesa) or Panadería Rosetta Fuel up before the museum marathon; Lardo's baked eggs are a local favorite.
9:00 AM – 12:30 PM Museo Nacional de Antropología One of the world's great museums. Focus on the Aztec (Sala Mexica) and Maya halls; the Stone of the Sun is unmissable. Closed Mondays.
12:30 – 2:00 PM Walk through Chapultepec Park to Castillo de Chapultepec Optional interior visit; the terrace views over the city are worth the climb.
2:00 – 3:30 PM Lunch at Contramar (Roma Norte) or Máximo Bistrot Contramar's tuna tostadas and pescado a la talla are iconic — book days ahead. Máximo is a modern Mexican standout.
3:30 – 5:30 PM Polanco stroll: Avenida Presidente Masaryk, Museo Soumaya (free) or Jumex Soumaya's shimmering facade and eclectic collection are free; Jumex for contemporary art.
6:00 – 8:00 PM Relax in Condesa: Parque México, Amsterdam Avenue loop Art-deco architecture, leafy streets, and café culture — a perfect decompression hour.
8:30 PM Dinner at Rosetta (Roma Norte) or Nicos (Clavería) Rosetta for refined Italian-Mexican; Nicos for traditional home-style Mexican in a quieter neighborhood.

Day 3 — Coyoacán, Frida & Xochimilco (or San Ángel)

TimeActivityNotes
8:00 AM Breakfast at Mercado de Coyoacán tostadas stand or Café El Jarocho (Coyoacán) Try tostadas de tinga and fresh juice; the market wakes up early.
9:00 – 11:30 AM Museo Frida Kahlo (La Casa Azul) Book timed tickets online in advance — slots sell out. Arrive at opening to avoid crowds.
11:30 AM – 1:00 PM Wander Coyoacán: Plaza H
