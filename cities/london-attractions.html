Top Attractions in London

London rewards visitors who look beyond the obvious. This guide draws together ten standout sights and experiences, from the world's oldest scientific zoo and the hallowed turf of Lord's to a pedestrian bridge with a wobbly past and a quirky art installation hiding in plain sight. Each entry includes a short description and a practical tip to help you plan your day.

  1. London Zoo
    Opened in 1828, London Zoo is the world's oldest scientific zoo, originally founded as a collection for scientific study and opened to the public in 1847. At its peak it attracted over three million annual visitors and exhibited more than 900 species.
    Tip: Book tickets online in advance and arrive at opening time, as weekends and school holidays get busy.
  2. Lord's Cricket Ground and Pavilion
    Known as the "Home of Cricket," Lord's is owned by Marylebone Cricket Club and houses the world's oldest sporting museum. Its Victorian Grade II* listed pavilion, designed by Thomas Verity, is one of the sport's most recognisable buildings.
    Tip: Ground and pavilion tours run on non-match days; check fixtures before you travel, as match tickets sell out quickly.
  3. Marble Arch
    This 19th-century white marble triumphal arch was designed by John Nash in 1827 as the state entrance to Buckingham Palace's courtyard. It was later relocated to its present site at the corner of Hyde Park when the palace was expanded in the 1840s.
    Tip: Combine a visit with a walk through Hyde Park; it's a short stroll from Oxford Street and Bond Street.
  4. Millennium Bridge
    This steel pedestrian suspension bridge over the Thames links Bankside with the City of London and opened in June 2000. Londoners nicknamed it the "Wobbly Bridge" after pedestrians felt it sway on opening day.
    Tip: Walk it early in the morning for the best views of St Paul's Cathedral and the river; it's free and open at all hours.
  5. Museum Mile
    Museum Mile is home to 14 museums in the area between Bloomsbury and the Embankment, running through Woburn Place, Russell Square, Southampton Row, Kingsway and the Aldwych. Institutions along the route include the British Museum, the Charles Dickens Museum, Somerset House and the Courtauld Gallery.
    Tip: Many museums here offer free entry; allow a full day and pick two or three to avoid museum fatigue.
  6. Westminster Abbey's Poets' Corner
    Poets' Corner in the southern transept of Westminster Abbey is where many of Britain's poets, playwrights and writers are buried or commemorated. Geoffrey Chaucer was the first poet interred there in 1400, and Shakespeare received a monument in 1740.
    Tip: Book abbey tickets online and visit on a weekday morning to avoid the worst crowds.
  7. Portobello Road
    This street runs almost the length of Notting Hill, and on Saturdays it hosts Portobello Road Market, one of London's notable street markets known for second-hand clothes, pastries and antiques. The Portobello Film Festival has been held around the road every August since 1996.
    Tip: Come on a Saturday for the full market, but arrive before 10am to beat the crowds; nearby Ladbroke Grove is the closest Tube station.
  8. Mansion House
    The Mansion House is the official residence of the Lord Mayor of London and a Grade I listed building designed by George Dance in the Palladian style. Built primarily in the 1740s, it hosts some of the City's most formal official functions, including two annual white-tie dinners.
    Tip: It sits in the heart of the City near Bank station; check whether open days coincide with your visit, as access is limited.
  9. London Noses (Seven Noses of Soho)
    These plaster of Paris reproductions of artist Rick Buckley's nose protrude incongruously from walls around London. Created in 1997, about 35 were originally attached to buildings including the National Gallery and Tate Britain, though only around 10 survived by 2011.
    Tip: Treat it as a free walking treasure hunt around Soho and the West End; look up at eye level and above on older buildings.
  10. Eccleston Yards
    Eccleston Yards is an open-air mall in Belgravia between Eccleston Place, Eccleston Street, Ebury Street and Elizabeth Street. It comprises 19 units of shops, businesses, restaurants and fitness studios, on the site of the former Eccleston Place Power Station.
    Tip: It's about a seven-minute walk from London Victoria Station, making it an easy stop before or after a train.

Practical tips

  • Book major attractions such as London Zoo and Westminster Abbey online in advance to save time and secure entry.
  • Start early: popular spots like Portobello Road Market and the Millennium Bridge are far quieter before 10am.
  • Use contactless payment or a travel card on the Tube; many attractions on this list are within a short walk of central stations.
  • Check opening days and event schedules before visiting — Lord's, Mansion House and Westminster Abbey all have restricted access on certain days.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: several of these sights, including Museum Mile and the Soho nose hunt, are best explored on foot.
