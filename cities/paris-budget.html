Paris Travel Cost Guide

Paris has a reputation as an expensive city, but the reality is more nuanced: the French capital can be enjoyed on a shoestring, a comfortable mid-range budget, or with full luxury. Costs vary dramatically depending on where you sleep, how you eat, and whether you prioritize Michelin-starred dinners or picnic baguettes by the Seine. This guide breaks down realistic 2024–2025 daily and weekly costs across three travel styles — budget, mid-range, and luxury — covering accommodation, food, transport, and activities, so you can plan a trip that matches both your tastes and your wallet.

Daily Cost Breakdown by Travel Style

Category Budget (per day) Mid-Range (per day) Luxury (per day)
Accommodation €30–€70 (hostel dorm, budget hotel on the outskirts) €120–€250 (3–4★ hotel, boutique guesthouse, central arrondissement) €400–€1,000+ (5★ palace hotels, suites with Eiffel Tower views)
Food €25–€40 (bakery breakfasts, street crêpes, supermarket picnics, one casual bistro meal) €60–€110 (café breakfast, bistro lunch, dinner at a mid-range restaurant with wine) €200–€500+ (hotel breakfast, gastronomic lunches, Michelin-starred dinners with wine pairings)
Transport €8–€12 (Navigo Easy t+ tickets, walking, occasional Vélib' bike) €15–€25 (Navigo Semaine or day passes, occasional taxi/G7) €50–€150+ (private transfers, chauffeur service, taxis)
Activities €10–€25 (free churches and parks, one museum, free walking tour tip) €30–€60 (2–3 museums, Seine cruise, guided tour) €100–€300+ (private guides, skip-the-line experiences, opera, champagne tastings)
Total (approx.) €75–€145/day €225–€445/day €750–€1,950+/day

Figures are per person, in euros, and reflect typical 2024–2025 prices. Weekly estimates: budget roughly €500–€900; mid-range €1,500–€3,000; luxury €5,000+.

Accommodation in Detail

  • Budget: Hostels (St. Christopher's Canal, Generator) run €30–€60 per dorm bed; private budget rooms in the 10th–20th arrondissements start around €70–€90. Book 2–3 months ahead for summer.
  • Mid-range: Central 3★ hotels and boutique stays average €150–€250 per night; prices spike during fashion weeks, trade fairs, and Roland-Garros.
  • Luxury: Palace hotels (Ritz, Le Bristol, Shangri-La) start around €800–€1,200 per night; expect premiums for Eiffel Tower or garden views.

Food in Detail

  • Budget: A croissant and coffee costs €4–€6; a jambon-beurre sandwich €5–€7; a formule midi (two-course lunch) at a neighborhood bistro €15–€20. Supermarkets (Monoprix, Franprix) and markets (Marché d'Aligre) keep costs low.
  • Mid-range: Dinner for two with wine at a good bistro: €80–€130. Cafés charge more for terrace seating — standing at the bar is cheaper.
  • Luxury: Michelin-starred tasting menus range €150–€400 per person before wine; grand café terraces (Deux Magots, Flore) charge €8–€12 for a coffee.

Transport in Detail

  • Metro: A single t+ ticket is €2.15; a carnet of 10 is €17.35. The Navigo Easy card (€2) loads tickets; the Navigo Semaine (€30.75) covers Monday–Sunday unlimited travel in zones 1–5 — excellent value for a week.
  • Airport transfers: RER B from CDG to central Paris is €11.80; Roissybus €16.60; taxis €56–€65 flat rate to the Right Bank.
  • Bikes & walking: Vélib' bikes cost €5/day; central Paris is highly walkable — many visitors cover 8–12 km on foot daily.

Activities in Detail

  • Budget: Many churches (Notre-Dame exterior, Sacré-Cœur), parks (Luxembourg, Buttes-Chaumont), and markets are free. Museums are free on the first Sunday of the month; EU residents under 26 enter most national museums free.
  • Mid-range: The Paris Museum Pass (€62 for 4 days) covers 50+ museums and monuments, including the Louvre and Versailles. Seine cruises run €15–€20.
  • Luxury: Private after-hours Louvre tours, hot-air balloon rides, and Michelin cooking classes range €150–€500+.

Practical Money-Saving Tips

  • Book accommodation early: Prices for central hotels can double 4–6 weeks before peak season (June–September, December holidays).
  • Eat your main meal at lunch: The formule midi at bistros is often 30–40% cheaper than the same dishes at dinner.
  • Skip taxis: The metro and walking are almost always faster and far cheaper in central Paris; use G7 app if you truly need a cab.
