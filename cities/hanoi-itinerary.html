Hanoi 3-Day Itinerary

Hanoi is Vietnam's thousand-year-old capital, a city where French colonial villas stand beside ancient temples, motorbikes stream past serene lakes, and the aroma of pho and coffee drifts from every corner. Three days is enough to experience its historic core, its vibrant food culture, and a taste of the surrounding countryside — if you plan your time well. This itinerary balances must-see landmarks with authentic local experiences, organized by neighborhood to minimize travel time. All prices are approximate and in Vietnamese dong (VND); expect to walk a lot and cross streets with confidence.

Day 1 — The Old Quarter & Hoan Kiem Lake

TimeActivityNotes & Food
7:30–9:00 Breakfast: Pho Thin (13 Lo Duc St) or Pho Gia Truyen (49 Bat Dan St) Classic beef pho; arrive early, Bat Dan often sells out by mid-morning. ~60,000–80,000 VND.
9:00–11:30 Guided walk through the Old Quarter's 36 streets — Hang Bac (silver), Hang Ma (paper goods), Ma May heritage house Free walking tours depart from Hoan Kiem Lake area; otherwise explore independently.
11:30–13:00 Lunch: Bun Cha Huong Lien (24 Le Van Huu) — the "Obama bun cha" spot Grilled pork with vermicelli and herbs. ~60,000 VND. Alternative: Banh Mi 25 (25 Hang Ca).
13:00–15:00 Hoan Kiem Lake & Ngoc Son Temple; cross the red Huc Bridge Temple entry ~30,000 VND. Great people-watching; visit on weekends for the pedestrian-only streets.
15:00–16:30 Egg coffee at Cafe Giang (39 Nguyen Huu Huan) — the original Rich, custard-like coffee invented in Hanoi. ~35,000 VND.
18:00–20:00 Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre (57B Dinh Tien Hoang) Book tickets in advance (~100,000–150,000 VND). Show lasts ~50 minutes.
20:00–21:30 Dinner: Cha Ca Thang Long (21 Duong Thanh) or street food on Ta Hien street Turmeric fish with dill, grilled at your table. Ta Hien is the famous "beer corner" — busy and lively.

Day 2 — History, Temples & French Quarter

TimeActivityNotes & Food
8:00–9:00 Breakfast: Banh cuon at Banh Cuon Gia Truyen (12 Hang Ga) or Banh Mi Pho Hue Steamed rice rolls with pork and mushroom. ~40,000 VND.
9:00–11:00 Temple of Literature (Van Mieu — Quoc Tu Giam) Vietnam's first university (1070). Entry ~30,000 VND. Go early to avoid tour groups.
11:00–12:30 Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum & One Pillar Pagoda Free entry; closed Monday & Friday afternoons. Dress modestly (shoulders/knees covered), no photos inside. Check hours before going.
12:30–14:00 Lunch near Ba Dinh: Bun Bo Nam Bo (67 Hang Dieu) or a bun cha spot on Chau Long St Dry vermicelli bowls with beef, herbs, and peanuts.
14:00–16:00 Hoa Lo Prison Museum ("Hanoi Hilton") Entry ~30,000 VND. Sobering, well-presented history of colonial and wartime imprisonment.
16:00–18:00 Stroll the French Quarter: Opera House, Metropole Hotel, Trang Tien Street; coffee at Cafe Nang or The Note Coffee Colonial architecture and wide boulevards — a contrast to the Old Quarter.
18:30–20:30 Dinner: Bun Cha Dac Kim (1 Hang Manh) or Green Tangerine (48 Hang Be) for French-Vietnamese fusion Bun cha Dac Kim is a Hanoi institution; expect crowds and cash payment.
