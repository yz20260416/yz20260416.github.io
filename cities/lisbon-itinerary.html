Lisbon 3-Day Itinerary

Lisbon is one of Europe's most captivating capitals — a city of seven hills where pastel-tiled façades tumble down toward the Tagus River, vintage trams rattle through narrow alleys, and the scent of grilled sardines drifts from corner tascas. Over three days you can cover the essentials without rushing: the historic heart of Alfama, the grand squares of Baixa, the viewpoints (miradouros) that make Lisbon famous, the cultural riches of Belém, and the lively nightlife of Bairro Alto and Cais do Sodré. This itinerary balances must-see landmarks with neighborhood wandering, and includes restaurant picks for every budget. Wear comfortable shoes — Lisbon is hillier than it looks — and pace yourself on the famous custard tarts.

Day 1 — Alfama, Baixa & the Riverfront

Theme: Medieval Lisbon and the historic center. Start early to beat the crowds and the heat.

Time Activity Notes
8:30–9:30 Breakfast at a pastelaria (try Pastéis de Belém style tarts or a pastel de nata with espresso) Most cafés open by 8:00; grab a quick bite near your hotel.
9:30–11:30 Castelo de São Jorge Arrive at opening (9:00) to avoid lines. Panoramic views over Alfama and the river. ~€15 entry.
11:30–13:00 Wander Alfama's lanes; visit the Miradouro de Santa Luzia and Miradouro das Portas do Sol Free viewpoints; the Santa Luzia terrace is tiled and photogenic.
13:00–14:30 Lunch in Alfama Try Taberna Sal Grosso (book ahead; modern Portuguese, ~€25–35 pp) or Pateo 13 for grilled fish in a courtyard.
14:30–16:00 Walk down to Baixa; see Praça do Comércio and the triumphal arch The riverfront square is Lisbon's grandest; great for photos and a coffee stop.
16:00–17:30 Ride Tram 28 (or walk) through Graça and back; optional stop at the Miradouro da Senhora do Monte Tram 28 is iconic but crowded — go early or late, and watch for pickpockets.
19:30–22:00 Dinner with live fado in Alfama Clube de Fado (book well ahead, ~€50+ pp with show) or A Baiuca for a more intimate, affordable experience.

Day 2 — Belém, LX Factory & Bairro Alto

Theme: Monuments, museums, and Lisbon's creative and nightlife scenes.

  • 9:00 — Take Tram 15E or the train from Cais do Sodré to Belém (15 min).
  • 9:30–11:00 — Mosteiro dos Jerónimos: Manueline masterpiece; book tickets online to skip queues (~€12).
  • 11:00–12:00 — Torre de Belém (exterior view is often enough; interior tickets sell out) and the Padrão dos Descobrimentos.
  • 12:00–13:00 — Pastéis de Belém (the original custard tart bakery, since 1837) — expect a line, but it moves fast. Eat them warm with cinnamon.
  • 13:00–14:30 — Lunch at O Prado (contemporary Portuguese, ~€30 pp) or a casual tasca near the river.
  • 14:30–16:00 — Optional: Museu Nacional dos Coches (coaches) or the MAAT riverside museum for architecture and design fans.
  • 16:30–18:00 — Head to LX Factory (Alcântara): converted industrial complex with bookshops (Ler Devagar), boutiques, and street art. Coffee at a rooftop bar.
  • 19:00–20:30 — Dinner in Bairro Alto or Cais do Sodré. Try Time Out Market for variety (many stalls, ~€15–25 pp) or Sea Me for excellent seafood (~€35 pp).
  • 21:00 onward — Bar-hop in Bairro Alto (tiny bars, lively crowds) or dance at Pink Street in Cais do Sodré. Note: many Bairro Alto bars are standing-room only and open late.

Day 3 — Sintra Day Trip or Modern Lisbon

Theme: Choose your adventure — a fairy-tale day trip or a deeper dive into Lisbon's modern side.

Option A: Sintra (recommended for first-timers)

  • 8:00 — Train from Rossio Station to Sintra (~40 min, ~€2.30 each way with Viva Viagem card).
  • 9:00–12:00 — Palácio da Pena: book timed tickets online in advance; the palace is a steep climb or short bus ride from town.
  • 12:00–13:30 — Lunch in Sintra town; try Incomum (modern Portuguese, ~€25 pp) or a simple queijada and sandwich.
  • 13:30–15:30 — Quinta da Regaleira (initiation wells and gardens) or Moorish Castle for views.
  • 16:00 — Return train to Lisbon; rest and freshen up.
  • 19:30 — Farewell dinner: Belcanto (two Michelin stars, book months ahead, ~€150+ pp) for a splurge, or Taberna da Rua das Flores (creative small plates, ~€30 pp, no reservations — arrive early).

Option B: Modern Lisbon & Markets

  • 9:30 — Calouste Gulbenkian Museum: world-class art collection in a serene garden setting (~€10).
  • 12:00 — Lunch at Mercado de Arroios or a neighborhood tasca.
  • 14:00 — Explore Príncipe Real: boutiques, the Embaixada concept store, and the Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara.
