Lisbon Weather by Month

Lisbon enjoys one of the mildest climates in Europe, thanks to its position on the Atlantic coast and the warming influence of the Gulf Stream. The city basks in roughly 2,800 hours of sunshine per year, with warm, dry summers and gentle, occasionally rainy winters. That said, "mild" doesn't mean "uniform" — the Atlantic breeze, the hills, and the proximity to the ocean all shape the weather in ways worth understanding before you pack. This guide breaks down what to expect each month, so you can choose the ideal time for sightseeing, beach days, or budget-friendly exploring.

Best Months to Visit

  • Best overall: May, June, and September. These months combine warm, sunny days (22–27 °C / 72–81 °F), minimal rain, and manageable crowds. September is especially appealing for beach lovers, as the Atlantic has had all summer to warm up.
  • Best for hot-weather lovers: July and August. Expect highs near 29 °C (84 °F), cloudless skies, and vibrant street life. Be prepared for peak prices, crowded attractions, and the need to book restaurants and hotels well in advance.
  • Best for budget travelers: November through February. Prices drop significantly, and you'll have major sights largely to yourself. Just pack a rain jacket and layers — this is the wettest stretch of the year.
  • Best for festivals and atmosphere: June. The city comes alive with the Festas de Lisboa (June festivals), especially the huge street party on the night of June 12–13 in honor of Santo António.

Practical Tips

  • Pack for microclimates. Lisbon's hills and ocean proximity mean temperatures can vary noticeably between neighborhoods and throughout the day. Layers are your friend in every season.
  • Don't underestimate the Atlantic breeze. Even on hot summer days, the wind off the water can feel cool — especially in Belém and along the riverfront. A light jacket or wrap is handy for evenings.
  • Rain is concentrated, not constant. Most winter rainfall arrives in short, heavy bursts rather than all-day drizzle. A compact umbrella and waterproof shoes will keep you comfortable.
  • Watch the UV index. From May to September, sunshine is intense. Sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses are essential, particularly if you're exploring the exposed hilltop viewpoints (miradouros).
  • Book ahead in high season. If you're visiting between mid-June and early September, reserve accommodation and popular restaurants early — and expect queues at major attractions like the Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower.
  • Embrace the shoulder seasons. April–May and September–October often deliver the best balance of pleasant weather, reasonable prices, and thinner crowds.
  • Check the forecast for day trips. If you're heading to Sintra's hills or the beaches of Cascais, note that conditions can differ from central Lisbon — Sintra is cooler and mistier, while Cascais can be windier.
