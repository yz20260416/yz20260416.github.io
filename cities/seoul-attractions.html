Top Attractions in Seoul

Seoul blends centuries of royal history with modern skylines, and its best sights reflect that mix. From UNESCO-listed palaces and preserved hanok neighborhoods to world-class museums, riverside landmarks, and Olympic-era stadiums, the city offers something for every traveler. This guide highlights ten attractions most likely to interest international visitors, each with a practical tip to help you plan your visit.

  1. Changdeokgung
    A former royal palace established in 1405 and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, considered among the best preserved of all Korean palaces and once called the "East Palace" alongside neighboring Changgyeonggung.
    Tip: Book the separate Secret Garden (Huwon) tour in advance, as entry is by timed guided slot only.
  2. Deoksugung
    A former royal palace that served as the main palace during the Korean Empire (1897–1910), notable for its blend of traditional Korean and Western architecture and home to the Daehan Empire History Museum and an MMCA branch.
    Tip: Visit in late afternoon to catch the changing of the royal guard ceremony and see the palace lit for evening.
  3. Bukchon Hanok Village
    A residential neighborhood in Jongno District filled with restored traditional Korean houses (hanok), many dating to the late 19th and early 20th centuries, preserved during Seoul's rapid redevelopment.
    Tip: Go early in the morning to avoid crowds and keep quiet, as these are private homes still lived in by residents.
  4. Gwanghwamun Square
    A public square in Sejongno in front of Gyeongbokgung, historically the site of royal administrative buildings known as the Street of Six Ministries and today featuring statues of Admiral Yi Sun-sin and King Sejong the Great.
    Tip: Combine your visit with Gyeongbokgung Palace next door and the nearby museums; evenings are lively and well lit.
  5. National Museum of Korea
    South Korea's flagship museum of history and art, established in 1945 and relocated to Yongsan District in 2005, with wide-ranging exhibitions and education programs.
    Tip: Admission to the permanent galleries is free; allow at least half a day and check for special exhibitions.
  6. Seoul City Wall (Hanyang Doseong)
    A defensive wall first built by King Taejo of the Joseon dynasty to protect the capital, designated a Historic Site of South Korea in 1963 and now a popular walking route around downtown Seoul.
    Tip: Walk a section such as the Naksan or Namsan trail; wear sturdy shoes and go in cooler morning hours.
  7. 63 Building (63 SQUARE)
    A 249.6 m skyscraper on Yeouido overlooking the Han River, opened in 1985 as the tallest building outside North America at the time and one of the world's tallest gold-clad buildings.
    Tip: Head up for river and city views around sunset, then stroll the nearby Han River park.
  8. Seoul Olympic Stadium (Jamsil Olympic Stadium)
    A multi-purpose stadium in the Songpa District built as the main venue for the 1988 Summer Olympics and the 1986 Asian Games, and the largest stadium in South Korea.
    Tip: Pair a visit with the nearby Lotte World area and Jamsil riverside parks; check event schedules before going.

Practical tips

  • Many palaces and museums are closed on Mondays or specific weekdays—check opening days before planning your route.
  • Buy a T-money transit card for subways and buses; Seoul's metro connects nearly all of these attractions.
  • Visit palaces and hanok villages early morning for fewer crowds and better photos.
  • Palace admission is inexpensive, and several museums (including the National Museum of Korea's permanent galleries) are free.
  • Wear comfortable shoes—historic districts and the City Wall trails involve considerable walking and uneven ground.
