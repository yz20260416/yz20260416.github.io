Local Character

Melbourne's traditional custodians are the Boonwurrung, Woiwurrung and Wurundjeri peoples of the Kulin nation, who know the place as Narrm or Naarm — a reminder that the city's identity predates the 1835 free-settler founding by tens of thousands of years. The city takes its name from William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne, a quirk visitors might miss but which explains the very British flavour of much of the inner-city nomenclature. Melbourne gave rise to Australian rules football, Australian impressionism and Australian cinema, so a visitor who catches a match at the Melbourne Cricket Ground or browses the National Gallery of Victoria is engaging with cultural forms born here rather than imported. The gold-rush boom of the 1850s made late-19th-century Melbourne one of the wealthiest metropolises in the world, leaving the World Heritage–listed Royal Exhibition Building as a tangible relic of that era. Locals are known as Melburnians, and the laneway street art and live-music scene give the central city a character shaped by everyday creative use of its back streets.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Royal Exhibition Building — A World Heritage–listed 19th-century exhibition hall that survives as a monument to the city's gold-rush-era wealth.
  2. Melbourne Cricket Ground — Australia's iconic sporting stadium, the birthplace of Australian rules football and a must for match-day atmosphere.
  3. National Gallery of Victoria — The city's flagship art museum, central to the story of Australian impressionism and cinema.
  4. Port Phillip Bay coastline — The bay's northern and eastern shorelines frame much of the city and offer waterfront walks and views.
  5. Melbourne laneways — The CBD's back streets are famed for street art and intimate live-music and theatre venues.
