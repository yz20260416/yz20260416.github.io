Local Character

Sanya's character is shaped by its geography and history. Known in ancient times as Yazhou and sometimes called Tianya Haijiao ('the end of the sky and ocean'), it served as a place of exile for officials out of favor with rulers—a quirk that gives the city a sense of remoteness and resilience. Visitors should care because this history explains the laid-back, frontier feel that persists despite modern development. Another detail is the small Utsul community, administratively classified as Hui, whose presence adds a distinct cultural layer to the local fabric. A visitor might notice their unique spoken Chinese pronunciation, 'Huíhuī,' and their separate identity, offering a glimpse into the region's ethnic diversity. These historical and cultural threads make Sanya more than just a beach destination.

Top 5 Attractions

  1. Guanyin of Nanshan — The thirteenth tallest statue in the world, a majestic Buddhist figure located in Sanya.
  2. Phoenix Island — An artificial island in Sanya Bay featuring a seven-star resort and a complex of five-star hotels.
  3. Sanya Coral Reef National Nature Reserve — A protected marine area spanning 4,000 hectares, ideal for diving and coral viewing.
  4. Tianya Haijiao — Known as 'the end of the sky and ocean,' this scenic spot marks Sanya's historic remoteness.
  5. Yalong Bay — A popular beach destination known for its clear waters and luxury resorts.

FAQ

What is the best time to visit Sanya?
The best time is from November to March when the weather is dry and warm, though crowds are higher.
What is the local cuisine like?
Sanya offers fresh seafood and Hainanese specialties, influenced by its coastal location and tropical climate.
Is Sanya suitable for family travel?
Yes, Sanya has many family-friendly resorts, beaches, and attractions like the Nanshan Buddhist statue.
