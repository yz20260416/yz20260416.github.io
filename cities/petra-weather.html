Petra Weather by Month

Petra sits in the mountainous desert of southern Jordan at roughly 800–1,350 meters (2,600–4,400 ft) above sea level, which gives it a climate that surprises many visitors: summers are hot but not scorching, winters can be genuinely cold, and spring and autumn are close to ideal. Rainfall is scarce and concentrated between November and March, often arriving in short, intense bursts that can trigger flash floods in the narrow Siq. Because the site is vast and largely shadeless, the month you choose shapes your experience as much as the temperature itself. The guide below breaks down typical conditions month by month so you can plan around heat, cold, rain, and crowds.

Figures are long-term monthly averages for Petra and should be read as typical ranges, not guarantees — individual days can run several degrees hotter or colder, and annual rainfall varies considerably.

Best Months to Visit

  • Top picks — March to May and September to November: Daytime highs of roughly 19–28 °C (66–82 °F), cool nights, and mostly dry skies make these the most comfortable months for exploring the Siq, the Treasury, and the long climb to the Monastery.
  • Best of all — April, May, and October: These months combine near-perfect temperatures with manageable crowds and long daylight hours. April adds wildflowers and green hillsides; October offers reliably clear skies.
  • Budget and solitude — December to February: Expect cold mornings (near freezing at dawn), a real chance of rain, and occasional snow on the surrounding peaks — but also the fewest visitors and the lowest hotel rates. Pack layers and a waterproof shell.
  • Avoid if heat-sensitive — July and August: Midday temperatures in the low-to-mid 30s °C (low 90s °F) with intense, unshaded sun make afternoon exploring strenuous. If you visit then, start at opening time and plan a siesta.

Practical Tips

  • Start early, year-round. Gates typically open around 6:00–6:30 a.m.; the first two hours offer the softest light, coolest air, and thinnest crowds — essential in summer and a delight in every season.
  • Dress in layers. Desert days and nights diverge sharply: a 25 °C (77 °F) afternoon in spring can drop to 8 °C (46 °F) after sunset. A warm fleece or jacket is worth carrying even in shoulder season.
  • Watch for flash floods. During rain events (mostly November–March), the Siq and other narrow gorges can flood with little warning. Follow guide and site-staff instructions and never enter a flooded or closing canyon.
  • Respect the heat in summer. Carry at least 2–3 liters of water per person, wear a hat and high-SPF sunscreen, and use the shaded tea tents and Bedouin stalls along the route to rest.
  • Protect against cold mornings in winter. Dawn temperatures near 3–5 °C (37–41 °F) plus wind chill make gloves and a hat genuinely useful, especially for the early climb to the Monastery or a Petra by Night visit.
  • Plan around daylight. Winter days are short (sunset around 4:30–5:00 p.m.); prioritize the Treasury, Siq, and Monastery if your time is limited, and save the High Place of Sacrifice for a full day.
  • Check the forecast before hiking. Wadi rum and the Petra back-trails (such as the route to Little Petra) are exposed; avoid them during rain or high-wind warnings.
  • Book Petra by Night accordingly. This candlelit walk runs on select evenings; winter nights are cold, so bring warm clothing, while summer evenings are pleasantly mild.
