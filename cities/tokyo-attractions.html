Top Attractions in Tokyo

Tokyo rewards visitors who look beyond the obvious, and this guide gathers ten standout spots drawn from the city's landmarks, neighborhoods, and historic sites. From the electronics and anime culture of Akihabara to the solemn state rooms of Akasaka Palace, these attractions cover the traditional, the pop-cultural, and the scenic. Use the practical tips to plan timing and transport around your itinerary.

  1. Akihabara — This Chiyoda ward neighborhood, nicknamed "Akihabara Electric Town," centers on the area around Akihabara Station and is famed for electronics and otaku culture. Its name derives from Akibagahara, and the district spans Sotokanda and Kanda-Sakumachō.
    Tip: Visit on a weekday afternoon for a calmer browsing experience; many shops stay open into the evening.
  2. Akasaka Palace — Built in 1909 as the Imperial Palace for the Crown Prince, this Moto-Akasaka, Minato building now serves as Japan's state guest house for visiting dignitaries. It took on its present function in 1974.
    Tip: Check the official schedule in advance, as public access is limited to designated visiting days.
  3. AnimeJapan — This annual anime consumer show has been held at Tokyo Big Sight since March 2014, created from the merger of the Tokyo International Anime Fair and the Anime Contents Expo. It is organized by the AnimeJapan Executive Committee with support from industry associations.
    Tip: Events are seasonal and ticketed; confirm dates and purchase tickets early, as the show is extremely popular.
  4. Hanayashiki — This Asakusa amusement park in Taitō has operated since 1853 and claims the title of Japan's oldest amusement park. It is run by a Bandai Namco Holdings subsidiary, and its unofficial mascot is the Panda Car.
    Tip: Combine a visit with nearby Sensō-ji; ride tickets can be bought individually, so you need not commit to a full day.
  5. Jimbōchō Book Town — Located in Kanda-Jinbōchō, Chiyoda, this district is known for its dense concentration of used bookstores and also houses headquarters of major Japanese publishers and several university campuses. It is alternately called Kanda Book Town.
    Tip: Go on a weekday to browse without crowds, and bring cash for smaller independent shops.
  6. Jingūbashi (Harajuku Bridge) — This bridge over the Yamanote Line links Harajuku Station with the entrance to Meiji Shrine. Its wide pedestrian area made it famous as a gathering spot for cosplayers and fashion performers, turning it into a tourist attraction.
    Tip: Visit on a Sunday afternoon when street-fashion and cosplay activity is at its liveliest.
  7. Cat Street — This roughly half-mile street meanders between Harajuku and Shibuya and is described by Time Out Tokyo as the spiritual home of Tokyo's street fashion culture. Highlights include a Tadao Ando-designed building housing Armani Casa.
    Tip: Walk it in the late afternoon, then continue into Shibuya for dinner and the crossing.
  8. Godzilla Head — This 80-ton landmark on the Hotel Gracery Shinjuku's Godzilla Terrace, atop the Shinjuku Toho Building, depicts Godzilla with occasional glowing eyes and smoky breath. Unveiled in 2015, the design is based on Godzilla vs. Mothra (1992).
    Tip: The terrace is accessible from the hotel; check access hours and combine it with a walk through Kabukichō.
  9. Hibiya Godzilla Square — This Tokyo square features the largest Godzilla statue in Japan, based on the Shin Godzilla design, with an original 1954 screenplay contained within its base. It replaces an earlier Heisei-design statue commissioned in 1995.
    Tip: It is a quick photo stop near Hibiya; pair it with a stroll through nearby Hibiya Park.
  10. Diamond and Flower Ferris Wheel — This 117-metre Ferris wheel in Kasai Rinkai Park, Edogawa, is named for its light shows, which resemble a sparkling diamond or flower. Completed in 2001, it was then the world's second tallest Ferris wheel and Japan's tallest until the following year.
    Tip: Ride near sunset for daylight views followed by the illuminated light show; the park is a short walk from Kasai-Rinkai Park Station.

Practical tips

  • Group attractions by ward: Akihabara and Jimbōchō are both in Chiyoda, while Harajuku's Jingūbashi and Cat Street pair naturally with Shibuya.
  • Many neighborhood attractions such as Akihabara and Cat Street are free to wander; paid sites like Hanayashiki and the Ferris wheel are best booked or timed in advance.
  • For limited-access sites like Akasaka Palace, verify opening days on the official website before traveling across the city.
  • Seasonal events such as AnimeJapan require early ticket purchase and sell out quickly.
  • Use the Yamanote Line as your spine for the day, hopping off at Harajuku, Shinjuku, and Akihabara to link several of these stops efficiently.
