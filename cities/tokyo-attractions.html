Tokyo's Top 10 Must-See Attractions

Tokyo blends centuries-old temples with futuristic skylines, world-class museums, and vibrant neighborhoods. Whether you have three days or a week, these ten attractions cover the city's essential experiences—from the historic Senso-ji temple to the observation decks of Shibuya Sky. Below you'll find descriptions, current ticket prices (in Japanese yen, with approximate USD equivalents), opening hours, and practical tips to help you plan your visit.

  1. Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)

    Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 628, is famous for the Kaminarimon Gate with its giant red lantern and the bustling Nakamise-dori shopping street leading to the main hall. It's one of the city's most photogenic and atmospheric sites.

    • Ticket price: Free (the temple grounds and main hall)
    • Opening hours: Temple grounds open 24 hours; main hall 6:00–17:00 (6:30–17:00 October–March)
    • Visiting tips: Arrive before 8:00 to see the temple without crowds and catch Nakamise-dori shops opening. Visit at night for beautifully illuminated gates and far fewer visitors.

  2. Tokyo Skytree (Sumida)

    At 634 meters, Tokyo Skytree is Japan's tallest structure and offers sweeping views of the city and, on clear days, Mount Fuji. The Tembo Deck (350m) and Tembo Galleria (450m) provide different vantage points, and the base houses a large shopping and dining complex.

    • Ticket price: Tembo Deck ¥2,100–2,400 (approx. $14–16); combined Deck + Galleria ¥3,100–3,400 (approx. $21–23); prices vary by date
    • Opening hours: 10:00–21:00 (last entry 20:00); hours may vary seasonally
    • Visiting tips: Book online in advance for discounts and to skip ticket lines. Go about an hour before sunset to see the city in daylight, at dusk, and after dark in one visit.

  3. Meiji Jingu Shrine (Shibuya)

    A serene Shinto shrine dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken, set within a 70-hectare forest of some 100,000 trees donated from across Japan. The contrast with nearby Harajuku's busy streets is striking.

    • Ticket price: Free (the Inner Garden charges ¥500, approx. $3.50)
    • Opening hours: Sunrise to sunset (roughly 6:00–17:00, varying by season)
    • Visiting tips: Enter via the massive torii gate near Harajuku Station. Combine with a walk through Yoyogi Park or a visit to the nearby Nezu Museum. Weekday mornings are quietest.

  4. Tokyo National Museum (Ueno)

    Japan's oldest and largest museum, holding over 100,000 objects including samurai armor, ukiyo-e prints, ceramics, and Buddhist sculpture. It's the best single place to understand Japanese art and history.

    • Ticket price: ¥1,000 (approx. $7) for general admission; special exhibitions cost extra
    • Opening hours: 9:30–17:00 (until 21:00 on Fridays and Saturdays); closed Mondays (or the following day if Monday is a holiday)
    • Visiting tips: Allow at least three hours. Pick up an English map at the entrance and prioritize the Honkan (Japanese Gallery). Pair with a stroll through Ueno Park and its other museums.

  5. teamLab Planets TOKYO (Toyosu)

    An immersive digital art museum where visitors walk barefoot through water and mirrored rooms filled with projected light and sound. It consistently ranks among the world's most-visited art museums and is a favorite for families and photographers.

    • Ticket price: ¥3,800–4,800 (approx. $26–33) for adults, depending on date; reduced rates for children
    • Opening hours: 9:00–22:00 (last entry 21:00); hours vary by season
    • Visiting tips: Timed tickets must be purchased online in advance—weekend slots sell out. Wear shorts or trousers that can be rolled up (some rooms have water up to knee level), and lockers are provided for shoes and bags.

  6. Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

    A 58-hectare park combining Japanese, English, and French garden styles, renowned for cherry blossoms in spring and vivid foliage in autumn. It's a peaceful retreat in the heart of Shinjuku's skyscraper district.

    • Ticket price: ¥500 (approx. $3.50); free for children under 15 and seniors 65+
    • Opening hours: 9:00–16:00 (last entry 15:30); closed Mondays (or the following day if Monday is a holiday) and during New Year holidays
    • Visiting tips: Cherry blossom season (late March–early April) is spectacular but crowded—arrive at opening. Note that alcohol is not permitted, and tripods are restricted in some areas.

  7. Shibuya Crossing and Shibuya Sky

    The world's busiest pedestrian scramble is a quintessential Tokyo experience, best viewed from above. Shibuya Sky, an open-air observation deck atop Shibuya Scramble Square at 229 meters, offers a dramatic panorama of the crossing and the city beyond.

    • Ticket price: Shibuya Crossing free; Shibuya Sky ¥2,200–2,500 (approx. $15–17) for adults, with online advance discounts
    • Opening hours: Shibuya Sky 10:00–22:30 (last entry 21:20); hours may vary seasonally
    • Visiting tips: Book Shibuya Sky online for a sunset slot—it's the most popular time. For the crossing itself, the second-floor Starbucks in the QFRONT building and the Magnet by Shibuya109 rooftop offer good vantage points.

  8. Tsukiji Outer Market

    Although the wholesale fish market moved to Toyosu in 2018, the Outer Market remains a lively warren of stalls selling fresh seafood, tamagoyaki, knives, and street food. It's the best place in Tokyo for a breakfast of sushi or grilled scallops.

    • Ticket price: Free to enter; food and goods priced individually
    • Opening hours: Most shops 5:00–14:00; many close on Sundays and some Wednesdays
    • Visiting tips: Go early (before 9:00) for the best selection and smaller crowds. Bring cash—many stalls don't accept cards. Don't eat while walking; stand near the stall instead.

  9. Imperial Palace East Gardens (Chiyoda)

    The landscaped East Gardens occupy part of the former Edo Castle grounds and are open to the public free of charge. You can see stone walls, moats, and the foundation of the former castle keep, all within walking distance of Tokyo Station.

    • Ticket price: Free
    • Opening hours: 9:00–17:00 (until 16:00 October–February); closed Mondays and Fridays, and during New Year holidays
    • Visiting tips: Free English-guided tours of the palace grounds run twice daily (advance registration recommended). Combine with a walk around the palace moat to see the famous Nijubashi Bridge.

  10. Odaiba and teamLab Borderless (Koto)

    Odaiba is a waterfront entertainment district with shopping malls, a Ferris wheel, and the relocated teamLab Borderless digital art museum (now at Azabudai Hills). It's a family-friendly area with views of Rainbow Bridge and Tokyo Bay.

    • Ticket price: teamLab Borderless ¥3,800–4,800 (approx. $26–33); Odaiba public areas free
    • Opening hours: teamLab Borderless 10:00–21:00 (last entry 20:00); Odaiba attractions vary
    • Visiting tips: Take the Yurikamome monorail from Shimbashi for scenic bay views. Reserve teamLab tickets online well ahead. Weekday visits are calmer than weekends.

Practical Tips for Visiting Tokyo

  • Get a transit IC card: A Suica or PASMO card (or the mobile version in Apple Wallet) works on trains, subways, and buses across the city and saves time at ticket machines.
  • Buy a subway pass: The Tokyo Subway 24/48/72-hour Ticket (¥800/1,200/1,500) offers unlimited rides on Tokyo Metro and Toei lines—good value if you plan multiple trips per day.
  • Book popular attractions online: Shibuya Sky, teamLab, and Tokyo Skytree often sell out or cost more at the door. Advance online tickets are usually cheaper.
  • Carry cash: Many small restaurants, market stalls, and temples accept cash only. Convenience store ATMs (7-Eleven, Japan Post) reliably accept foreign cards.
