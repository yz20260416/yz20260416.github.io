Dublin 3-Day Itinerary

Dublin is a compact, walkable capital where medieval cathedrals, Georgian squares, world-class museums, and legendary pubs sit within a few square miles of each other. Three days is enough to cover the essentials — Trinity College and the Book of Kells, Dublin Castle, St. Patrick's Cathedral, the Guinness and Jameson stories, and a night or two of traditional music — while still leaving room for a day trip to the coast or the Wicklow Mountains. This itinerary balances headline sights with neighborhood wandering, and builds each day around a sensible geographic cluster so you spend your time exploring rather than crossing the city. Book timed-entry tickets (Book of Kells, Guinness Storehouse, Kilmainham Gaol) online in advance, especially in summer.

Day 1 — Trinity College, Medieval Dublin & the Liberties

Time Activity Notes
9:00 Trinity College & the Book of Kells Experience Enter via the Front Gate on College Green. Book the first slot of the day; the Long Room (Old Library) is the highlight. Allow 1.5–2 hours.
11:15 Dublin Castle & Chester Beatty Library 10-minute walk. State Apartments and Chapel Royal; the Chester Beatty (free) has superb Islamic and East Asian manuscripts. Don't miss the garden behind the library.
13:00 Lunch — The Fumbally or Fallon & Byrne The Fumbally (Fumbally Lane, Liberties) for seasonal café food; Fallon & Byrne (Exchequer St) for a deli lunch if you're staying central.
14:30 St. Patrick's Cathedral & Christ Church Cathedral Both within 10 minutes of each other. St. Patrick's is the larger and more storied; Christ Church has the medieval crypt. Combo tickets exist for some sites — check on arrival.
16:30 Teeling Whiskey Distillery or a Liberties wander Teeling (Newmarket Square) runs guided tours with tastings. Alternatively, walk the Liberties' old market streets and pop into St. Audoen's Church.
19:30 Dinner — The Winding Stair or Bull & Castle The Winding Stair (Orchard Lane, by Ha'penny Bridge) for modern Irish cooking; Bull & Castle (Lord Edward St) for a classic Victorian pub dinner.
21:30 Traditional music — The Cobblestone Smithfield. One of the city's best genuine sessions; arrive early for a seat and expect a lively, local crowd.

Day 2 — Guinness, Kilmainham & the Phoenix Park Side

Time Activity Notes
9:30 Guinness Storehouse St. James's Gate. Pre-book the first entry slot; the Gravity Bar pour at the top is included. Allow 2 hours.
12:00 Kilmainham Gaol 20-minute walk or short bus ride. Guided tours only and they sell out — book online well ahead. Deeply moving history of 1916 and the War of Independence.
13:30 Lunch — The Old Royal Oak or a picnic in the park The Old Royal Oak (Kilmainham) is a proper locals' pub with good pub grub. Alternatively, grab supplies and eat in the Phoenix Park.
15:00 Phoenix Park, Áras an Uachtaráin & Dublin Zoo (optional) One of Europe's largest enclosed city parks. See the Papal Cross, the Wellington Monument, and herds of fallow deer. The Zoo is excellent if traveling with children.
17:30 National Museum of Ireland — Decorative Arts & History (Collins Barracks) Free. Superb collections of Irish silver, glass, and furniture in a dramatic former barracks. Closes at 5pm some days — check hours and swap with the park if needed.
19:30 Dinner — L. Mulligan Grocer or Fish Shop L. Mulligan Grocer (Stoneybatter) for Irish craft beer and seasonal plates; Fish Shop (Queen St) for superb seafood in a tiny room — book ahead.
21:30 Pints in Stoneybatter Try Walsh's or The Glimmer Man for a low-key, local end to the day.

Day 3 — Museums, Merrion Square & a Coastal Escape

Time Activity Notes
9:30 National Gallery of Ireland Free. Strong on Irish painters (Yeats, Lavery) plus Caravaggio and Vermeer. Merrion Square entrance; allow 1–1.5 hours.
11:00 Merrion Square & the Georgian Doors Stroll the square, see the Oscar Wilde statue, and admire the famous brightly painted doors on Fitzwilliam and Merrion Squares.
12:00 National Museum of Ireland — Archaeology (Kildare St) Free. The Ardagh Chalice, Tara Brooch, and the bog bodies are unmissable. 1–1.5 hours.
13:30 Lunch — Bewley's Grafton Street or KC Peaches Bewley's for the historic café setting and theatrical interior; KC Peaches for quick, healthy salads and sandwiches.
15:00 Afternoon choice: Howth or Malahide (DART) Take the DART north (~30 min). Howth: cliff walk (allow 2 hours), fishing harbor, and seafood. Malahide: castle and gardens. Alternatively stay central and do the Jameson Bow St. Distillery tour.
18:30 Return to the city; sunset at Ha'penny Bridge or along the Liffey Cross the Ha'penny Bridge and walk the south quays toward Temple Bar — look, don't necessarily drink there.
19:30 Farewell dinner — Chapter One or Etto Chapter One (Parnell Square) for a Michelin-starred splurge; Etto (Merrion Row) for a relaxed, excellent wine-and-small-plates finale. Book both well in advance.
